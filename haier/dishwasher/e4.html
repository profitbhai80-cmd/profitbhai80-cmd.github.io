Haier Dishwasher Error Code E4

Quick answer

Error codeE4 — Haier Dishwasher
What it meansE4 on a Haier DW9/DW12/HDW12 dishwasher means the drain hose is crimped or bent, or it is positioned too high: the machine cannot drain because the hose run itself is at fault. The manuals signal it with 10 short buzzer signals every 30 seconds and 'E4' on the time-remaining indicator, and they give only those two checks — straighten the hose, set its free end within the 40–100 cm installation range, power-cycle, and contact the authorised service centre if E4 persists. There is no sensor or board diagnosis for E4 on these families.
Most common causeThe drain hose is crimped or bent, or it is routed too high for the drain pump to lift the water out — a hose-routing fault, not a failed part.
Safe to check yourself?Yes — this is one of the safest home fixes in the library. Switch the dishwasher off, pull it out only as far as its hoses allow (never force it or disconnect a hose while water is in the tub), inspect the full drain-hose run for crimps, bends and height, straighten and re-route it, then power-cycle. Do not open the machine's panels, touch the drain pump's electrics, or run cycles on standing water.

Applies to — read this first

On the Haier DW9/DW12/HDW12 dishwasher families we checked, E4 is consistent — and it is a plumbing problem, not an electronics problem. The manuals give E4 only two checks: the drain hose is crimped or bent, or the drain hose is too high. There is no sensor, board or heater diagnosis for E4 on these families, which is good news: the fix is usually visible the moment you look at the hose run.

The installation spec behind 'too high' is specific: on the DW12 family the drain hose's free end must sit between 40 and 100 cm high and must not be immersed in water. Above 100 cm the pump cannot lift the water out; looped 'very high' is itself an E4 cause, not a fix. The other trap is height in the opposite direction: a drain hose that is too LOW is E3, not E4 — and a water-supply problem is E5. Read the code exactly, then check only the hose fault that matches it.

What the machine does when E4 fires

The buzzer emits 10 short warning signals every 30 seconds and the time-remaining indicator displays 'E4'. The machine is telling you the drain side cannot clear — on these families that is the hose's crimp/bend or its height, not a failed heater or board. Water may remain in the tub until the hose fault is corrected; do not keep restarting cycles on standing water.

Common causes

  1. The drain hose is crimped or bent — kinked where it passes through cabinetry, trapped behind the machine, or squashed by the machine's own weight.
  2. The drain hose is positioned too high — its free end sits above the 40–100 cm installation range, so the drain pump cannot lift the water out.
  3. The drain hose run is otherwise obstructed — the manuals' E4 checks are limited to crimp/bend and height, so anything beyond a visible hose fault (for example a blocked pump or siphon) is service-centre work, not a home diagnosis.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the dishwasher off. If water is standing in the tub, do not disconnect any hose — leave the water where it is until the hose fault is found.
  2. Trace the full drain-hose run from the back of the machine to the drain point (sink, standpipe or siphon trap). Look for a crimp, a sharp bend, a squash where the hose passes through cabinetry, or a section trapped behind the machine.
  3. Straighten any crimp or bend and re-route the hose so it runs smoothly with no kink. Set the hose's free end at a height between 40 and 100 cm and make sure it is not immersed in standing water.
  4. Push the machine back carefully without re-trapping the hose, then power the dishwasher off, wait a few minutes, power it on and try a short cycle. If E4 clears, the hose was the fault.
  5. If E4 persists with a straight, correctly-heighted hose — or if water has leaked onto the floor or electrics — stop and contact the nearest authorised service centre with your model and serial number (on the plate inside the door). Do not dismantle the drain pump or open the machine's panels.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. E4 on these families has no deeper home diagnostic in any manual — hose crimp/bend, hose height, power-cycle, then the service centre. Never run repeated cycles on standing water to 'force' a drain.

When to call a technician

  • E4 persists after the hose is straight and its free end sits at 40–100 cm — the fault is beyond the manuals' two checks (pump, siphon or internal drain fault).
  • Water has leaked onto the floor or near electrics — switch off at the wall and call the service centre; do not power back on over wet electrics.
  • The drain hose is damaged, split or perished — it needs replacement by the service centre, not a tape repair.
  • You own an XF model — your E4 is a leak code, not a hose-height code; check for leaking water first.

Safety first

E4 is a plumbing fault, not an electrical fault — but the dishwasher is still a mains appliance with water inside it. Switch it off before inspecting the hose, never disconnect a drain hose while the tub holds water, and never open the machine's panels or touch pump electrics. If water has leaked onto the floor or into electrics, switch off at the wall, mop up, and call the authorised service centre — do not power the machine back on over wet electrics. If E4 persists after the hose is straight and correctly heighted, stop and call the service centre rather than dismantling the drain system.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E4 mean on a Haier dishwasher?

E4 means the drain hose is crimped or bent, or it is positioned too high. The DW9-UFE3, DW12-TFE1/TFE2, HDW12-SFE3 and HDW12-TFE3SS manuals all give it identically: when the buzzer emits 10 short warning signals every 30 seconds and the time-remaining indicator shows 'E4', check whether the drain hose is crimped or bent, and whether the drain hose is too high.

How high should the drain hose be?

On the DW12 family the installation manual specifies the drain hose's free end at a height between 40 and 100 cm, not immersed in water. Below that range you risk siphoning (which is E3's 'too low' fault); above 100 cm the pump cannot lift the water and you get E4. 'Very high' is not better — it is the fault.

How do I clear Haier dishwasher E4?

Switch the dishwasher off, inspect the full drain-hose run from the machine to the drain point, straighten any crimp or bend, and re-route the hose so its free end sits within 40–100 cm and is not immersed. Then power the dishwasher off, wait a few minutes, power it on and try again — that power-cycle is the manuals' own next step. If E4 persists with a straight, correctly-heighted hose, contact the nearest authorised service centre.

Is Haier dishwasher E4 the same as washer E4?

No — same brand, same letter, different appliance. Haier washing-machine E4 means water filling was not reached in 8 minutes (a fill fault). Haier dishwasher E4 on the DW/HDW families means the drain hose is crimped/bent or too high (a drain fault). Never carry a code meaning across appliances.

What if my Haier dishwasher is a newer XF model?

Newer Haier XF models (for example XF 4A4M4BX) use E4 differently: their troubleshooting table gives E4 as a water leak — check the drain pipe is not bent, trapped or blocked, the filter is not clogged, and the siphon is not clogged. If you own an XF model, your E4 is a leak code, not a hose-height code, and you should check for leaking water first and call the service centre if water is present.
