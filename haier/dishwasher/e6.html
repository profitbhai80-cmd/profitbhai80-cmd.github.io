Haier Dishwasher Error Code E6

Quick answer

Error codeE6 — Haier Dishwasher
What it meansE6 = water overflow trouble (overflow alarm). The dishwasher has detected too much water — the float/overflow switch has tripped — and the drain pump keeps running to clear it. Verified in two Haier service manuals: the DW12-PFE8 Series manual (§15-3, §15-4-6) and the DW12-TFE3 manuals (§15.2, §16.5). E6 is not the door (E1), the leak (E3) or the water supply (E5).
Most common causeWater in the bottom case — a leak or an overfill — has tripped the float/overflow switch, so the drain pump runs continuously. The manuals' own checks are the floater, the overflow switch, the leak position, and the drain-pump / tub / turbidity-sensor connections.
Safe to check yourself?Yes — switch off and unplug, check for water in the bottom case and mop it up, and check the floater moves freely and reset it. Changing the overflow switch or the PCB is service work; if E6 returns after the float is reset, contact the authorised service centre.

DW1004S001V0), §15-3 'Trouble display code' (p.42) + §15-4-6 'E6 Alarm : Water overflow trouble' (p.50); Haier DW12-TFE3 Service Manual — Domestic (37 pp, Edition 2008-05, Models DW12-TFE3/DW12-TFE3ME, Bom FA06D0E03/FA06D0E04), §15.2 trouble-shooting table (p.27) + §16.5 'Water overflow trouble' (p.32); Haier DW12-TFE3 Series Service Manual (53 pp, Qingdao Haier, 2010), §15.2 (p.42) + §16-5 'Water Overflow Trouble' (p.47)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E6 is an overflow alarm on the electronic DW12 families with a display-code table — DW12-PFE8 and DW12-TFE3. The user manuals for the DW9/DW12/HDW12 families print only the bare line 'When the buzzer emits 10 short warning signals every 30 seconds and the time remaining indicator display E2 or E6, contact the nearest authorized service center' — the service manuals are what expand it into a fault. If your dishwasher is mechanical (a knob, no display) or an XS model, this page is not your code.

The ranking pages get E6 wrong four different ways, and the PFE8 §15-3 map corrects all four: removeandreplace says 'locate the water supply valve, and shut off the supply' — water supply is E5's alarm, not E6's; dishwasherusa says 'E6 – Door Not Closed Properly' — the door is E1's alarm (§15-4-1); applianceforever says 'Water Leakage Detected' — leakage is E3's alarm; appongtho (Vietnamese table) says the temperature sensor is broken — the sensor is E2's NTC alarm (§15-4-2). E5 is the opposite problem to E6: E5 means the machine cannot get water in, E6 means it has too much water. The shared overflow-breakdown analysis — the same diagnostic flow in the HDW101 (2003), DW12-BFE (2005-7) and DW12-TFE1/TFE2 service manuals, and in the DW9-AFM p.17 flowchart — checks, in order: whether water is deposited in the bottom case, whether water is flowing out of the overflow conduit, whether the water level switch is closed, whether the water overflow switch is OK, and finally whether the program controller is broken. That flow is the overflow fault's diagnosis, not an E6 row: none of those manuals prints an E6 code.

What the machine does when E6 fires

The display shows E6 and the buzzer emits 10 short warning signals every 30 seconds, and the drain pump keeps on working — the machine is trying to clear the excess water that tripped the overflow switch. The cycle does not run normally while E6 is shown.

Common causes

  1. Water in the bottom case — a leak from the tub, a hose or a connection has filled the base and tripped the float (the manuals' first check: 'whether water in the bottom case').
  2. Floater stuck or not seated well — the float that trips the overflow switch is jammed in the raised position.
  3. Overflow switch faulty — the switch itself has failed and stays tripped even with the base dry.
  4. A leaking connection — the drain pump, the tub with water cup, or the turbidity sensor connection is letting water into the base.
  5. Program controller / PCB fault — the manuals' last resort: if the float, switch and connections are all sound, the controller is broken ('Change the PCB').

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the dishwasher off and unplug it before any check — E6 involves water and mains-powered electronics together.
  2. Check whether water is in the bottom case: look under and around the machine and mop up any pooled water, then find where it came from.
  3. Check the floater moves freely and reset it if it is stuck up — a stuck float keeps E6 showing with no water present.
  4. Check the overflow breakdown in order (the manuals' shared flow): water deposited in the bottom case, water flowing out of the overflow conduit, the water level switch closed, and the water overflow switch OK.
  5. Find the leak position: check the drain pump, the tub with water cup, and the turbidity sensor connections for leaks; reset or replace the leaking part.
  6. If the float and switch are sound and there is no leak, the program controller is the manuals' last step — 'Change the PCB' is service work, so contact the authorised service centre.

The steps above are the manuals' own checks, in the manuals' order — floater, overflow switch, leak position, connections, then the PCB. This page does not invent a button-press reset or a power-cycle ritual: no manual documents one for E6. If E6 returns after the bottom case is dry and the float is reset, the overflow switch or the PCB needs service.

When to call a technician

  • E6 returns after the bottom case is dry and the floater has been reset — the overflow switch or the PCB is faulty.
  • Water keeps appearing in the bottom case — an active leak, not a one-off overfill.
  • Any burning smell, visible damage, or water inside the door or control area — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

E6 involves water and mains-powered electronics together: switch the machine off and unplug it before checking the bottom case or the floater, and never dismantle the overflow switch or the PCB yourself — that is qualified-technician work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E6 mean on a Haier dishwasher?

E6 means water overflow trouble (overflow alarm): the float/overflow switch has tripped and the drain pump keeps running. It is verified in two Haier service manuals: the DW12-PFE8 Series manual (§15-3, §15-4-6) and the DW12-TFE3 manuals (§15.2, §16.5).

Why does the drain pump keep running on E6?

That is the manuals' own phenomenon phrase: 'drain pump keep on working' (PFE8 §15-4-6; TFE3 §16.5). The machine is clearing the excess water that tripped the overflow switch — it is not a drain fault (that is E4).

How do I reset Haier dishwasher E6?

Switch off and unplug, check for water in the bottom case and mop it up, check the floater moves freely and reset it, then restart. The manuals name no button-press reset for E6; if the code returns, the overflow switch or the PCB needs service.

Is E6 overflow or a leak?

E6 is the overflow alarm; E3 is the water-leakage alarm in the same §15-3 table. They are related — a leak can fill the bottom case and trip the overflow switch — but they are different codes: E3 = leakage, E6 = overflow. E5 is different again: it is the water-supply (inlet) alarm, the opposite problem — too little water, not too much.

Which Haier dishwashers show E6?

The electronic DW12 families with a display-code table: DW12-PFE8AAA/PFE8SAAA and DW12-TFE3/TFE3ME. It does NOT apply to the mechanical DW9-AFM (its service manual has no E-code table), the DW9-TFE3-F (flowcharts only), or the XS family (its §19 table is E2/E3/E4/E8/E18 only, no E6).
