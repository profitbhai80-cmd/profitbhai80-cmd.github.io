Haier Dishwasher Error Code E5

Quick answer

Error codeE5 — Haier Dishwasher
What it meansE5 on a Haier DW9/DW12/HDW12 dishwasher is a water-supply fault: the tap, the house supply and pressure, a crimped or bent supply hose, or a clogged supply-hose filter. The manuals signal it with 10 short buzzer signals every 30 seconds and 'E5' on the time-remaining indicator. Work the four supply checks in the manuals' own order, power-cycle, and contact the authorised service centre if E5 persists. The XS family has no E5 — its fill fault is E2.
Most common causeThe water supply is not reaching the machine properly: tap off or hose disconnected, low house pressure, a crimped supply hose, or a clogged supply-hose strainer.
Safe to check yourself?Yes — the four supply checks (tap, pressure, hose, strainer) are safe home checks. Do not open the machine's panels or touch inlet-valve electrics.

Applies to — read this first

If E3 and E4 are about water leaving the machine, E5 is about water coming in — the supply-side partner to the drain codes. The manuals give exactly four checks, in a fixed order, and this page follows that order. It also flags a printing quirk no ranking page mentions: the DW9/DW12 printings append a stray 'the drain hose is too low' line after the E5 checks. That is E3's check, repeated by a layout bleed — it is not a fifth E5 cause.

The stray 'drain hose is too low' line printed after the E5 checks is a layout bleed from E3's row, not a fifth cause. Treating it as a cause would send an E5 owner to re-route a drain hose while their supply hose sits crimped.

What the machine does when E5 fires

The machine announces E5 with 10 short buzzer signals every 30 seconds and 'E5' on the time-remaining indicator, and it cannot fill — the cycle cannot proceed because the water it needs is not arriving at sufficient pressure or volume. Unlike a drain fault, the tub stays dry (or nearly so); the fault is upstream of the machine, in the supply path.

Common causes

  1. The water tap for the dishwasher is turned off, or the supply hose is not connected properly.
  2. The house water supply is off or its pressure is too low for the machine to fill.
  3. The water supply hose is crimped or bent, choking the flow into the machine.
  4. The filter (strainer) for the water supply hose is clogged — it sits in the Aquastop or at the supply-hose connection, where debris collects.
  5. A kink where the hose passes behind the machine, re-forming every time the machine is pushed back.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Check the water tap for the dishwasher is fully turned on and the supply hose is connected properly at both ends.
  2. Check the house supply: is water reaching other taps normally, and is the pressure sufficient? If the whole house is affected, the fault is upstream of the machine.
  3. Trace the supply hose and straighten any crimp or bend, especially where it passes behind the machine — then push the machine back carefully without re-kinking it.
  4. Check and clean the supply-hose strainer, which the manuals place in the Aquastop or at the supply-hose connection. Turn the tap off before removing it, and refit it before turning the tap back on.
  5. Power the dishwasher off, wait a few minutes, power it on and try again — the manuals' own next step. If E5 persists with the supply path clear, contact the nearest authorised service centre with your model and serial number.

Work the four checks in the manuals' order — tap, house pressure, hose, strainer — and stop as soon as the code clears. Do not treat the stray 'drain hose is too low' print line as a fifth check; that is E3's row, not E5's.

When to call a technician

  • E5 persists with the tap on, house pressure normal, the hose straight and the strainer clean.
  • Water leaks at the tap, hose connection or Aquastop — turn the tap off and call the service centre.
  • The inlet valve itself is suspected — valve electrics are service-centre work, not a home check.
  • Any sign of water near the machine's electrics — switch off at the wall and call the service centre.

Safety first

E5 is a supply fault, so the main risks are water and electrics together: turn the tap off before removing the supply-hose strainer, mop up any spill before powering the machine, and never open the machine's panels or touch inlet-valve electrics. If water has reached the electrics, switch off at the wall and call the authorised service centre.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E5 mean on a Haier dishwasher?

On DW9/DW12/HDW12 families E5 is a water-supply fault. The manuals give four checks in order: the tap is on and the hose connected; the house supply is on with sufficient pressure; the supply hose is not crimped or bent; the supply-hose filter is not clogged.

Is Haier dishwasher E5 a heating problem?

No. No DW manual gives E5 as heating — the XS heating code is E8, and the blog list that calls E5 'ineffective water heating' has its codes rotated one off the manual rows. E5 is the supply code.

Where is the supply-hose strainer?

The same manuals place the strainer in the Aquastop or at the supply-hose connection. Turn the tap off before removing it to clean it, and refit it before turning the tap back on.

My Haier dishwasher is an XS model and will not fill — is that E5?

No. XS models have no E5; a fill problem on XS shows as E2 (does not fill with water). Follow the XS E2 meaning, not this page.

What is the stray 'drain hose is too low' line after the E5 checks?

A printing quirk in the DW9/DW12 manuals: the line is E3's check, repeated by a layout bleed after the E5 row. It is not a fifth E5 cause, and no ranking page flags it.
