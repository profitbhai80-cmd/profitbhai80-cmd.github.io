Haier Dishwasher Error Code E9

Quick answer

Error codeE9 — Haier Dishwasher
What it meansE9 = a touch button on the control panel registered as pressed for longer than 30 seconds — caused by water or some substance on the button. In plain terms: a stuck or wet touch key. It is NOT a software fault, and it is not a dishwasher-wide failure — it is the control panel reacting to one button.
Most common causeWater or another substance (spilled detergent, food, steam condensation) sitting on a touch button, keeping it registered as pressed for over 30 seconds.
Safe to check yourself?Yes — dry and clean the touch panel and the code should clear. The manuals name no user procedure beyond removing the water/substance from the button; if E9 persists on a dry, clean panel, contact service.
Checked againstHaier User Manual, Dishwasher HDBL655AFS (also covers HDBL655AFW, HDBL655AFB; 24" built-in, US, July 2015), section "ERROR CODES", p.31 + Haier User Manual, HDBL865ATS — 24" Built-In Dishwasher (trilingual EN/FR/ES), section "ERROR CODES", p.32

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E9 is a touch-panel code, not a water or heating code. On the Haier HDBL655 and HDBL865 24-inch built-in dishwasher families, E9 fires when one of the touch buttons registers a press longer than 30 seconds — and the manuals' own cause is "Water or substance on the button." If your dishwasher has mechanical push buttons or a rotary knob, this page is not your code. These manuals' error table contains only four codes: E1, E4, E8 and E9.

What the machine does when E9 fires

The display shows E9 and the dishwasher stops responding to normal button input — the control sees one touch button held down for over 30 seconds and flags it rather than running the cycle.

Common causes

  1. Water splashed on the touch panel during loading or unloading — the manuals' own named cause.
  2. Detergent, rinse aid, food or grease on a button, holding it registered as pressed.
  3. Steam condensation on the panel from a hot cycle or an open dishwasher in a cold kitchen.
  4. A button genuinely stuck mechanically under the panel film (rare — check the wet causes first).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Wipe the whole touch panel dry with a clean, soft cloth — then clean any film of detergent or grease off the buttons, since "substance" is the manuals' second named cause.
  2. Press each button once and make sure it responds and releases normally — a button that stays registered is your culprit.
  3. If the panel was wet from steam, leave the dishwasher open a few minutes to air out, then close and restart.
  4. Do not invent a power-cycle ritual for E9 — the manuals prescribe none; the fix is removing the water/substance from the button.
  5. If E9 persists on a dry, clean panel with every button responding normally, contact Haier service — Haier's own line is 1-877-377-3639 (in both manuals).

The home scope is the panel itself: dry it, clean it, confirm each button releases. The manuals define E9's cause but prescribe no power-cycle or reset ritual — and no manual names a user-serviceable repair past a clean panel.

When to call a technician

  • E9 returns on a dry, clean panel with every button responding normally — the touch panel or its connection may be faulty.
  • Multiple buttons register presses you did not make — a panel fault, not a wet-button fault.
  • Any burning smell, visible panel damage, or water inside the door — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

E9 is a control-panel code with no hazardous user steps: you only wipe the panel and press buttons. Never remove the door panel to reach the touch assembly — that is service work on mains-connected electronics.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E9 mean on a Haier dishwasher?

E9 means a touch button registered as pressed for longer than 30 seconds, caused by water or some substance on the button. It is verified verbatim in two Haier manuals: the HDBL655AFS/AFW/AFB user manual (p.31) and the HDBL865ATS user manual (p.32).

Is E9 a software fault?

No. A top-ranking aggregator calls it a "Software Fault," but both Haier manuals define E9 as a >30-second button touch caused by water or substance on the button — a wet-button condition, not a software failure.

How do I clear Haier dishwasher E9?

Dry and clean the touch panel — remove the water or substance from the button. The manuals name this as the cause and prescribe no power-cycle ritual; if the code persists on a dry, clean panel, contact service.

Does every Haier dishwasher have E9?

No. E9 is documented only for the HDBL655 and HDBL865 24-inch built-in families. The HDC2406TW, DW12/DW9, HDW15, HDW9, XS and QDT125SSLSS families have no E9 row in their manuals.

What is the Child Lock, and does it relate to E9?

Child Lock is a control-panel lockout on the same touch panel: press STEAM and SCRUB ZONE at the same time on the HDBL865, or hold Delay Start for 3 seconds on the HDBL655, to activate or cancel it. It is not E9 — but a locked panel and a stuck button can both make the dishwasher ignore input, so check both.
