Haier Dishwasher Error Code E40

Quick answer

Error codeE40 — Haier Dishwasher
What it meansE40 = flow-meter fault - the water-flow measurement is wrong or failing. On newer HDW displays the same fault shows as F40. Sub-codes: E41 = flow meter fault (same family); E42 = flow rate too high. The official guidance is book-a-service only (F&P: switch OFF and ON again; if it persists, call Customer Care). Caveat: F&P does not document the E41/E42 sub-codes - those come from the Haier article alone.
Most common causeThe flowmeter - the turbine + reed-switch device that meters inlet water - is giving a wrong or no reading. The manuals name no component-level sub-cause beyond the fault itself.
Safe to check yourself?Limited: the official article gives no homeowner steps ("Please book a service."); the F&P manual's only user action is one OFF/ON cycle. No manual documents turbine-inspection or reed-switch DIY.
Checked againstHaier official "Haier Dishwasher Fault Codes" support article (support.haier.com.au, AU/NZ) + Fisher & Paykel DW24U6I1 User Guide, p.29 "FAULT CODES AND DESCRIPTIONS"

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E40 is the HDW two-digit platform's water-measurement fault (newer displays show F40): the flowmeter that meters the inlet water is faulty. The official article's rows are "F40 E41 Flow meter fault. Please book a service." and "E42 Flow rate too high." Note the wording split: Haier says "flow meter fault", F&P says "F40, Water flow error" - the same component described two ways. Do not merge E40 with Hisense F40 (inlet valve leakage - different brand) or with invented sub-code stories (see below).

What the machine does when E40 fires

The display shows E40 (or F40 on newer panels); the water-flow measurement the machine uses to meter the fill is wrong or failing, so the fill cannot be controlled correctly.

Common causes

  1. Flow meter fault - the official article's own row (F40/E41).
  2. Flow rate too high - the official article's own E42 row.
  3. The manuals name no further sub-cause; one US repair site reports F40 flow-meter errors are often a detached impeller magnet - third-party repairer note, not a manual claim.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the dishwasher OFF and ON again (the F&P manual's only user action); if the fault persists, call Customer Care.
  2. The official article's routing: "Please book a service."

The steps above are the manuals' full response - nothing more exists in either document. This page does not invent turbine-inspection or reed-switch multimeter steps (appliancemode suggests them; no manual documents them).

When to call a technician

  • E40/E41/E42 persists after one OFF/ON cycle - the flowmeter (turbine, reed switch, wiring) needs service.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

E40 is a measurement fault inside a mains-powered dishwasher: never open the machine or probe the flowmeter circuit yourself - that is qualified-technician work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E40 mean on a Haier dishwasher?

E40 = flow-meter fault - the water-flow measurement is wrong or failing ("F40 E41 Flow meter fault" in Haier's official article; "F40, Water flow error" in the F&P DW24U6I1 guide). On newer displays it shows as F40.

What are E41 and E42?

Sub-codes from the Haier official article alone: E41 = flow meter fault (same family as F40); E42 = flow rate too high. F&P does not document them, and the article gives no further E41 detail - any site describing E41's exact signal behavior is guessing.

Is Haier dishwasher E40 the same as Hisense F40?

No. Haier E40/F40 = flow-meter fault. Hisense F40 = inlet valve leakage. Different brands, different faults - do not cross-read them.

Can I fix the flowmeter myself?

No manual documents any homeowner flowmeter procedure. The F&P manual's only user action is one OFF/ON cycle; the official article routes to "Please book a service."
