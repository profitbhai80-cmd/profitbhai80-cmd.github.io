Haier Washing Machine Error Code E5

Quick answer

Error codeE5 — Haier Washing Machine
What it meansE5 means two different things depending on your model family. On HLT364XXQ and GWT900AW/GWT700AW/GWT800AW models it is the water level sensor fault — the manuals say to call service. On HWM70-A models it is water temperature too high (over 60°C): the digital tube displays E5 and rings, and after cooling you press Start/Pause to resume.
Most common causeDepends on the family: a failed water level sensor (HLT/GWT families — call service) or incoming water that is too hot, over 60°C on HWM70-A (let it cool, then press Start/Pause).
Safe to check yourself?Only the temperature branch, and only partly: letting the water cool, checking hose connections, and pressing Start/Pause are user-safe. The level-sensor branch has no safe home fix — the manuals' remedy is after-sales service.

Applies to — read this first

E5 is a split-personality code: it means two different things on two different Haier washer families, and the remedy flips with the meaning. On HLT364XXQ and GWT900AW/GWT700AW/GWT800AW models, E5 is the water level sensor fault — and the manuals' only remedy is to call service. On HWM70-A models, E5 is water temperature too high (over 60°C) — and that one IS user-fixable: let the water cool, then press Start/Pause. Check your model family on the rating label before you do anything — see how to find your model number.

The same fault wears different codes on other families, which is why the model check matters: the water level sensor fault is E6 on the template top-load families (HWM70-0588H and relatives) and FA on B14979 front-loads — neither family has an E5 row. The HLTW500AXW temperature branch is a variant of the temperature meaning (hot water too cold, or cold water too hot, with a hose-connection check), not the HWM70-A over-60°C definition. Do not confuse E5 with Haier dishwasher E5 (a water-supply code on a different appliance).

What the machine does when E5 fires

HLT/GWT branch: the display shows E5 for a water level sensor fault and the manuals give no user remedy — the machine is telling you to call service. HWM70-A branch: the digital tube displays E5 and rings while the water temperature is over 60°C; the machine waits until the water cools, then resumes when you press Start/Pause.

Common causes

  1. HLT/GWT families: the water level sensor has failed — the manuals state no other cause and no home check for this branch.
  2. HWM70-A family: the incoming water is too hot — over 60°C, per the HWM70-A manual. The same manual's safety section warns not to use water hotter than 50°C inside the tub.
  3. HLTW500AXW temperature branch: the hot and cold hoses are swapped (hot to cold, cold to hot), or the hot-water heater is not supplying hot water — the manual's own checks for its E5 water-temperature problem.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Identify your model family on the rating label first — HLT/GWT, HWM70-A, or something else. See our guide to finding your model number. The fix depends entirely on this step.
  2. If your model is HLT364XXQ or GWT900AW/GWT700AW/GWT800AW (level-sensor branch): power-cycle once — unplug, wait a few minutes, replug — to rule out a glitch. If E5 returns, stop: the manuals' remedy is after-sales service, and they forbid user repair. Do not open the machine.
  3. If your model is HWM70-A (temperature branch): turn the hot tap down or off and let the water in the tub cool. Check that the hot and cold hoses are connected to the proper valves (hot to hot, cold to cold) — swapped hoses are the HLTW500AXW manual's own check for its temperature branch.
  4. After cooling, press the Start/Pause knob to resume — the HWM70-A manual's own remedy. If E5 returns with cool water and correctly connected hoses, the temperature sensing needs a technician.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. The two branches must not be mixed: never apply the temperature-branch fix (cool + restart) to a level-sensor E5, and never call a technician for a temperature E5 before letting the water cool. If a check needs the machine opened or a part tested, it is no longer a safe home check — skip to the technician section.

When to call a technician

  • E5 returns after a power cycle on an HLT/GWT model — the water level sensor fault is confirmed as a service matter.
  • E5 returns with cool water and correctly connected hoses on an HWM70-A model — the temperature sensing itself is faulty.
  • Any water leaking near electrical parts, burning smell, or sparking — switch off immediately and call a qualified technician.
  • E5 on a B14979 front-load or a template top-load model — that model has no E5, so check the code again against its own manual rather than applying this page.

Safety first

Unplug the machine before touching hoses or moving it, and close the tap before disconnecting any hose — water and electricity are a dangerous mix. Never use water hotter than 50°C inside the tub (HWM70-A safety section) and never open the machine or attempt sensor repairs yourself: both manuals forbid user repair. If you see water leaking near electrics, switch the machine off and call a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E5 mean on a Haier washing machine?

It depends on the family. On HLT364XXQ and GWT900AW/GWT700AW/GWT800AW models, E5 means the water level sensor fault — call service. On HWM70-A models, E5 means the water temperature is too high (over 60°C): the digital tube displays E5 and rings, and after cooling you press Start/Pause. Same code, different fault — check your rating label.

How do I fix Haier washer E5 on an HLT or GWT model?

You don't — not at home. Both manuals' only remedy for the water level sensor fault is 'Call service,' and both manuals forbid user repair ('Do not repair it by yourself'). Power-cycle once to rule out a glitch; if E5 returns, call after-sales service.

How do I fix Haier washer E5 on an HWM70-A model?

Let the water cool down, check that the hot and cold hoses are connected to the proper valves (hot to hot, cold to cold), then press the Start/Pause knob to resume — the manual's own remedy. If E5 returns with cool water and correctly connected hoses, the temperature sensing needs a technician.

Some sites say Haier E5 is a water overflow or drainage error. Is that right?

No. Overflow protection is F2 on the HLT-family table, and drainage is E1 on Haier top-loads — neither is E5. The verified E5 meanings are the water level sensor fault (HLT/GWT) and water temperature too high (HWM70-A).
