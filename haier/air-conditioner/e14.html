Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code E14

Quick answer

Error codeE14 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansE14 = indoor fan motor failure / malfunction (indoor unit DC fan motor faulty). Causes: fan motor wire break, main motor wire break, or detection error from a defective indoor PCB (M1); DC motor wiring interrupted, motor failure, electronic board damaged (M3). Split E14 carries no LED1 flash count — it shows on the indoor display. E14 is the INDOOR fan; the outdoor fan fault is a different code, F8.
Most common causeAn indoor fan motor or indoor PCB fault (M2 p.44 flowchart): a broken fan-motor wire, a failed DC motor, or a damaged indoor board.
Safe to check yourself?Limited — one power-cycle per M1. The CN8/CN9 checks (60-115VAC at CN9, pulse at CN8) are technician-only. Never pull out or insert the CN9 motor terminal while the unit is on — M4 warns the motor would be damaged.
Checked againstHaier Split Type Room Air Conditioner Operation Manual (models AS25/35/50/68/71...HRA — Pearl / Pearl Premium / Pearl Nordic / Revive Plus / Expert Nordic, R32), Troubleshooting Codes table; Haier Ductless Split Air Conditioner Service Manual (US models AW09/12/18/24ES2VHA + 1U09/12/18/24ES2VHA, R410A), Error Codes & Problem Solving (p.43) (p.43 + p.44 flowchart); Haier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units) (Table A pp.28-29); Haier HSU09XHK / HSU12XHK Service Manual (Order No. AC1205S001V0), §7.2 (§7.2 pp.32-34)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E14 is a split-wall/ductless code — it means the INDOOR fan motor on the EU wall-split R32 families, the US ductless split families, the EU mono/multi range and the HSU EK-Series ON/OFF wall units (M1/M2/M3/M4). It does NOT exist on portable models or HDU models, and on cabinet 14kW units the fan motor fault is EA, not E14. On the HSU-22HV03 family E14 means something else entirely (outdoor environment resistor fault). Check the model and series, not just the letters.

The ranking pages get E14 wrong by omission and by invention. heatingnewsjournal has no E14 row at all — it assigns E14's meaning to E7 ('fan motor fault'). decorexpro has no E14 anywhere and calls E8 'overload of the electric motor of the indoor unit' — E8 is wired-controller communication; the fan motor is E14. appliancepartspros covers neither E14 nor E7. smartacsolutions promises E14 solutions in its heading and contains no E14 content beneath it. Arlington gets the meaning right but invents the fix: 'move the fan manually until it starts spinning freely' and 'replace the fan capacitor' appear in no manual read for this DC motor — and M4 explicitly warns against pulling or inserting the CN9 motor terminal while the unit is on. F8 is the OUTDOOR DC fan motor fault (LED1 9 flashes, M2 p.45) — acerrorcode's F8 row ('High pressure abnormal') hides that distinction.

What the machine does when E14 fires

The indoor display shows E14, and the indoor fan motor stops — operation stops due to the fan motor wire break (M1). The unit cannot move air, so heating or cooling effectively stops.

Common causes

  1. Fan motor wire break — operation stop (M1).
  2. Main motor wire break — operation stop (M1).
  3. Detection error due to defective indoor unit PCB (M1).
  4. DC motor wiring interrupted (M3).
  5. Motor failure (M3).
  6. Electronic board damaged (M3).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Try one power-cycle first: turn the unit off, wait 30 seconds, then disconnect from power; reconnect and wait out the 3-minute restart protection (M1).
  2. If E14 returns, call a technician — the fan-motor checks are technician-only.
  3. The technician checks whether terminals CN8 and CN9 on the indoor PCB are well inserted (M4).
  4. The technician electrifies the machine again, turns it on in cooling operation, and measures whether there is voltage 60VAC-115VAC between the red wire and the black wire of terminal CN9 on the indoor mainboard; when the fan motor is running, whether there is a voltage pulse between the middle wire and the black side wire of terminal CN8 (M4 pp.33-34).
  5. The indoor mainboard is damaged — replace it; the indoor motor is damaged — replace it (M4).
  6. Never pull out or insert the terminal of the motor (CN9) while the unit is on — M4 p.33 warning, quoted verbatim: "When the unit is on, don't pull out or insert the terminal of the motor (CN9), or else the motor would be damaged."

Both flowcharts (M2 p.44, M4 pp.33-34) are technician-only: voltage and pulse measurements, then motor or indoor-PCB replacement. No manual gives an owner repair for E14 beyond one power-cycle. Do NOT repeat Arlington's invented steps — 'move the fan manually until it starts spinning freely' and 'replace the fan capacitor' — neither appears in any manual read, and M4 explicitly warns against touching CN9 while the unit is on.

When to call a technician

  • E14 returns after one power-cycle — the indoor fan motor or indoor PCB is faulty.
  • The indoor fan does not run at all, or runs then stops with E14 showing.
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

The fan-motor checks involve live voltage (60-115VAC at CN9) and the CN9 motor terminal: never pull out or insert the CN9 terminal while the unit is on — M4 warns the motor would be damaged. All of it is qualified-technician work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E14 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

E14 means the indoor fan motor has failed. M1: 'Indoor unit fan motor failure'; M2: 'Indoor fan motor malfunction'; M3: 'Indoor unit DC fan motor faulty'.

How do I fix Haier split E14?

Try one power-cycle first (off, wait 30 seconds, disconnect, reconnect — M1; the unit will not restart for 3 minutes). If E14 returns, a technician must check the fan motor and indoor PCB (M2 p.44): M4's checks are the CN8/CN9 terminal seating, 60-115VAC between the red and black wires of CN9, and the voltage pulse at CN8. Do not pull or insert the CN9 motor terminal while the unit is on — M4 warns this damages the motor.

Is E14 the outdoor fan or the indoor fan?

Indoor. E14 is the indoor (blower) fan motor. The outdoor fan motor fault is a different code, F8 ('Outdoor DC fan motor fault', LED1 9 flashes, M2 p.45). One list page's F8 row ('high pressure abnormal') hides this distinction.

Should I spin the fan by hand or replace a fan capacitor?

No. One guide (Arlington) tells E14 owners to spin the fan manually and replace a fan capacitor, but no Haier manual read gives either step for this DC fan motor — M2/M4 diagnose it by voltage and pulse checks and by replacing the motor or the indoor PCB. Spinning it by hand and capacitor replacement are invented.

Does E14 mean the same thing on every Haier AC?

No. On split-wall/ductless models E14 is the indoor fan motor failure (M1/M2/M3/M4). On cabinet 14kW units the fan motor fault is EA, not E14; portable and HDU models have no E14 at all; and on the HSU-22HV03 family E14 means an outdoor environment resistor fault while the indoor fan fault is E19. Check the model/series, not just the letters.
