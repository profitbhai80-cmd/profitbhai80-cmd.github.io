Haier Portable Air Conditioner Error Code FL

Quick answer

Error codeFL — Haier Portable Air Conditioner
What it meansFL = full water — the internal water tank is full of condensed water; the unit has shut itself off and lit the Water Full indicator (M5/M6/M7). FL is NOT a fault: no part has failed, no repair is needed, the tank is simply full and must be drained. PORTABLE ONLY.
Most common causeThe internal water tank has filled with condensed water — fastest in high humidity or in Dry/dehumidify mode, which pulls far more water from the air than cooling. Nothing has broken.
Safe to check yourself?Yes — drain the tank using the manual's 5 steps (unplug, pan under the LOWER drain, remove cap and rubber plug, drain, re-insert plug and cap, plug in). No part and no technician are needed for FL itself.

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because FL is a STATUS INDICATOR, not a component fault. FL means 'Full water' — the internal water tank has filled with condensed water. FL is listed under the 'Error Codes' heading in the portable owner manuals, and users search it as an error code, so it gets a page — but this page states plainly and early that FL is NOT a fault: no part has failed, no repair is needed, the tank is simply full and must be drained. Do NOT invent a fault meaning for FL (no sensor fault, no pump fault, no float fault). Portable E4 = anti-frozen protection — NOT the tank (acerrorcode and nordicdesignblog both give FL's job to E4). Split-wall and HDU series have NO FL code at all (their drainage fault is E0). Pre-2017 portables showed a full tank as 'E', 'STOP' or E4 (tank float) — not FL. This page is titled 'Portable' and scoped to portable only.

The ranking pages either omit FL or misattribute it. acerrorcode — the #1 Haier list page — has NO FL row anywhere, neither its main table nor its portable table; it assigns FL's job to E4 ('Tank float is stuck') and carries a second full-tank row ('STOP — Tank must be emptied'), so a reader cannot work out which display their unit will actually show. nordicdesignblog's generic cross-brand guide mentions FL only in 'How to Clear P1, FL, or E4 Full-Tank Codes': it attributes FL to 'LG, Hisense, Black+Decker' and omits Haier — Haier is a major FL user; it lists E4 as a full-tank code — wrong for Haier 2017+ (E4 = anti-frozen protection); it gives Frigidaire's P1 reset steps as the universal fix for all three codes; and it blurs the upper continuous-drain port with the lower FL drain port. smartacsolutions' 2017+ table is right on FL ('Water Chassis Overflow — drain the water from the unit and restart it') but the fix is one line, no procedure, no auto-shutoff, no manual citation; its 2016 table has no FL at all. A Home Depot-hosted FAQ PDF of unidentified brand adds an unverified 'micro-switch inside the tank was slightly displaced during shipping' claim — it appears in no Haier portable manual.

What the machine does when FL fires

The Water Full indicator ('FL' on the control panel display) illuminates when the internal water tank has filled with condensed water. The air conditioner automatically turns off, and the Water Full indicator illuminates to alert you of this condition (M5/M6/M7). In extreme humidity, the water tank in the chassis will fill up and 'FL' will show on the LED display (AM manual).

Common causes

  1. The internal water tank has filled with condensed water (M5/M6/M7).
  2. FL returns whenever the tank refills — fastest in high humidity or in Dry/dehumidify mode, which pulls far more water from the air than cooling.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Unplug or disconnect power (QPCA10-QPCA11 manual, §'Internal Water Tank', verbatim).
  2. Place a shallow 1 quart or larger pan (not included) below the lower drain located on the back (near the bottom) of the air conditioner.
  3. Remove the cap and the rubber plug and drain the water tank.
  4. Re-insert the drain plug and screw the cap snugly into place.
  5. Plug in or reconnect power.
  6. For extended Dry-mode use, avoid FL entirely: connect a 1/2 inch outer diameter flexible drain hose to the UPPER drain port and drain to a floor drain — the UPPER port is the continuous drain; the LOWER drain is the one used to clear FL. Do not confuse the two ports.
  7. AM-series variant (model-variant note, not the main procedure): put a water wedge below the upper drain port at the back of the unit, screw off the drain port cover and connect a 12mm external diameter PVC drain hose to allow water flow into the water wedge; after water has stopped flowing, plug the water stopper back in and screw the drain port cover back on tightly.

FL is the one portable code with a real manual procedure, quoted verbatim above from the QPCA10-QPCA11 manual. Do NOT present nordicdesignblog's generic cross-brand steps (Frigidaire P1 procedure, 'unplug for 30 seconds if needed to clear the display') as Haier manual steps. Do NOT present the thdstatic FAQ's 'micro-switch inside the tank was slightly displaced during shipping' claim — it is unverified, from an unidentified brand's FAQ, and appears in no Haier portable manual. If FL persists with an empty tank, the manuals give no further owner step — contact a qualified technician rather than opening the unit.

When to call a technician

  • FL shows with an empty tank — the manuals give no further owner step; contact a qualified technician rather than opening the unit.
  • Water leaks from the unit even after draining — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

FL itself needs no repair — drain the tank and the unit restarts. Unplug the unit before draining ('Disconnect power before servicing or cleaning'); pressing the Power button does NOT disconnect the appliance from the power supply. If FL persists with an empty tank, do not open the unit — contact a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does FL mean on a Haier portable air conditioner?

FL means 'Full water': the internal water tank has filled with condensed water. Verified word-for-word in three Haier portable owner manuals (QPFA08/QPFA10; QPWA14YZMW/QPCA14 p.14; QPCA10-QPCA11), and the AM09/AM12-series manual prints the same FL behaviour.

Is FL a fault? Do I need a repair?

No. FL is not a component fault — nothing has broken. When the tank fills, the unit automatically turns off and the Water Full indicator lights up. Drain the tank using the manual's steps and the unit restarts. No part and no technician are needed for FL itself.

How do I clear FL on a Haier portable AC?

Per the QPCA10-QPCA11 manual: (1) unplug the unit; (2) put a shallow 1-quart-or-larger pan below the lower drain on the back, near the bottom; (3) remove the cap and the rubber plug and drain the tank; (4) re-insert the drain plug and screw the cap snugly into place; (5) plug in and restart. Use the LOWER drain — the upper port is the continuous drain for Dry mode.

Why does FL keep coming back?

FL returns whenever the tank refills, which happens fastest in high humidity or in Dry/dehumidify mode (Dry mode pulls far more water from the air than cooling). For long Dry-mode sessions, connect a drain hose to the upper continuous-drain port so the tank never fills. If FL shows with an empty tank, the manuals give no further owner step — contact a qualified technician rather than opening the unit.

Is FL the same as E4?

No. On 2017-and-newer Haier portables, FL is the full-tank display and E4 is anti-frozen protection — different displays for different conditions. (On 2016-and-older portables the full tank was shown as E4, 'STOP' or 'E' instead — that is why some older guides say E4 means the tank is full. On your current model, trust FL.)
