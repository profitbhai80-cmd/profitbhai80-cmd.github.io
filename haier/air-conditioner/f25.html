Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F25

Quick answer

Error codeF25 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF25 = compressor delivery / exhaust / discharge temperature sensor failure — the temperature probe on the compressor's delivery (discharge) pipe has failed. The indoor display shows F25 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 13 times. 'Delivery', 'exhaust' and 'discharge' are three manual wordings for the same pipe/probe (M3 'delivery', M2 'exhaust', M7/M8 'discharge') — not three different sensors. No manual gives a lockout rule, a resistance value, or a fix step for F25, and no manual states the F25 cause explicitly — do not copy the F6 cause wording onto F25.
Most common causeThe compressor delivery (discharge / exhaust) temperature probe is faulty. No manual states the F25 cause explicitly — no 'open or short circuit' cause is given for F25.
Safe to check yourself?No — no fix step exists in any manual read for a persistent F25. Switch the unit off and call a technician to check or replace the outdoor delivery probe. Do not use the E1 (indoor sensor) fix for F25 — the sensors are in different units.
Checked againstHaier Ductless Split Air Conditioner Service Manual (US models AW09/12/18/24ES2VHA + 1U09/12/18/24ES2VHA, R410A), Error Codes & Problem Solving (p.43) (p.43); Haier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units) (Table A pp.28-31); Haier 1U26MADFRA Room Air Conditioner Installation Manual (Sept 2024, R32, AU/NZ), Outdoor Unit Troubleshooting (p.25) (p.25); Haier AS35SBBHRA-MW Service Manual (Version V1, 2024-11-30) §10.4.1 p.31

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F25 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means the delivery (discharge) temperature sensor on the US ductless split families, the EU mono/multi range, the AU/NZ R32 split families and the AS35 wall-split family (M2/M3/M7, identical LED count of 13). The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) reuses F25 for a completely different fault — U-V-W compressor phase overcurrent — while the delivery-probe fault sits under cabinet F13. The old fixed-speed lamp table has no F25 at all. Check the model and series, not just the letters — and do not confuse the delivery SENSOR fault (F25) with the delivery high-temperature PROTECTION (F4).

The ranking pages get F25 right and everything around it wrong. acerrorcode gets F25 right in one row ('Discharge temperature sensor fault') but gives no LED count, no F4 distinction — its own F4 row says 'Compressor overheat/discharging temperature protection', so a reader cannot tell whether F25 is a sensor fault or another overheat protection — and its F14 row duplicates F25's meaning under the wrong code ('Compressor discharging temp. sensor failure' — F14 is the 4-way valve). Arlington gets the LED1 13 right but its only fix is 'Try the exact solutions as suggested for E1' — E1 is the INDOOR room sensor, a different sensor in a different unit, and no manual gives an F25 resistance value; its 'open or short circuit' cause is not stated in any manual for F25; and its F4 section tells the reader to 'check the discharge temperature sensor' for F4, collapsing the F25/F4 distinction. decorexpro and heatingnewsjournal have no F25 content at all. A watch-list collision: lioncityaircon.sg uses a different Haier code system (E01-E124) where E23 is the compressor discharge temperature sensor fault — the same fault as F25 under a different code; do not confuse its E23 with F23 (phase overcurrent).

What the machine does when F25 fires

The indoor display shows F25 in place of the set temperature, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 13 times. The outdoor board cannot read the delivery-pipe temperature at all — it is a sensor failure, not an overheat warning.

Common causes

  1. Compressor delivery temperature probe faulty (M3).
  2. Exhaust temperature sensor failure (M2).
  3. Abnormal of compressor discharge sensor (M7).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Call a technician — the outdoor delivery probe is on the outdoor unit, and its check or replacement is technician work. No manual gives the procedure, a resistance value, or a plug check for F25, so this page does not invent one.

There is NO F25 flowchart and NO F25 fix step in any manual: the full extent of the manual evidence is the M2 p.43 table row, the M3 Table A row, the M7 p.25 row and the AS35 §10.4.1 grouping. The F4 flowchart on M2 p.49 is for discharge-temperature OVERHEAT protection, not for the F25 sensor failure — do not present F4's steps as F25 steps. Do NOT follow guides that tell you to use the E1 (indoor sensor) fix for F25, and do not copy the F6 'open or short circuit' cause wording onto F25 — no manual states it for F25. F25 is a one-row code in every manual: keep it short, do not pad with invented diagnostics.

When to call a technician

  • F25 returns after a restart — the outdoor delivery probe needs checking or replacing.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 13 times — delivery sensor (F21 defrost is 10, F7 suction is 11, F6 ambient is 12).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

The outdoor delivery probe is on the outdoor unit: checking or replacing it is qualified-technician work. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F25 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F25 means the compressor delivery (discharge / exhaust) temperature sensor has failed — the probe on the compressor's delivery pipe is faulty. The indoor display shows F25 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 13 times (M2 p.43; M3 Table A; M7 p.25; AS35 §10.4.1). 'Delivery', 'exhaust' and 'discharge' are three manual wordings for the same probe.

Is F25 the same as F4?

No. F4 is the delivery/discharge HIGH TEMPERATURE protection — the pipe is genuinely too hot (above 230°F per M2, above 110°C per the AS35 manual — the same threshold). F25 is a SENSOR failure — the board cannot read the pipe temperature at all. Same pipe, different fault: do not use F4's overheat steps for F25, and do not treat F25 as an overheat warning.

How is F25 different from F6, F7 and F21?

They are four different outdoor sensors: F6 ambient (12 flashes), F7 suction / compressor intake (11), F21 defrost (10) and F25 delivery / discharge (13). Same outdoor unit, four probes, four codes — never merge them. And do not confuse F25 with F14: one list page puts the discharge-sensor meaning under F14, but F14 is the 4-way valve.

Can I fix Haier F25 myself?

No fix step exists in any manual read for a persistent F25. Unlike F1-F4, F25 has no diagnostic flowchart at all — the manuals give only the table row. Do not follow guides that tell you to use the E1 (indoor sensor) fix for F25: the sensors are in different units, and no manual gives an F25 resistance value. Switch the unit off and call a technician to check or replace the outdoor delivery probe.

Is F25 the same on a Haier cabinet air conditioner?

No. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) reuses the number F25 for a completely different fault: 'U-V-W compressor phase overcurrent / Module input overcurrent' — that is what Table A F23 means on a split. On a cabinet unit the delivery-probe fault sits under F13, not F25. Check whether your unit is a split or a cabinet before using any F-code meaning. Also do not confuse F25 (delivery sensor, 13 flashes) with F6 (ambient, 12), F7 (suction, 11) or F21 (defrost, 10).
