Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F28

Quick answer

Error codeF28 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF28 is an outdoor-unit compressor-drive fault, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 19 times — but the three Haier manuals word it differently, and this page shows all three honestly rather than picking one: M2 calls it a 'loop of the station detect error'; M3 calls it 'compressor overcurrent at fixed frequency (software threshold)'; M7 calls it a 'module PWM select circuit error'. M3 adds the lockout rule: the alarm goes out 3 times in an hour and locks the machine.
Most common causeHonestly, the manuals disagree on wording: M2's loop-detect error, M3's fixed-frequency overcurrent, M7's PWM select circuit error — all three describe the same 19-flash outdoor compressor-drive fault. The M2 p.50 / AS35 §10.4.11 flowchart groups F28 with F11 (loss of synchronism): the rotor position cannot be detected normally, when the compressor wiring is wrong or the connection is poor, or when the compressor is damaged.
Safe to check yourself?No — no fix step exists in M2, M3 or M7 for F28. Switch the unit off and call a technician; compressor wiring, PCB/IPM and compressor diagnosis is technician work on the outdoor unit.

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F28 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it is an outdoor compressor-drive fault on the US ductless split families, the EU mono/multi range and the AU/NZ R32 split families (M2/M3/M7, identical LED count of 19). The three manuals do NOT agree on the wording — M2 'loop of the station detect error', M3 'compressor overcurrent at fixed frequency (software threshold)', M7 'module PWM select circuit error' — and this page shows all three, attributed, rather than picking one as 'the' meaning. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) carries a DIFFERENT F28 — 'No charge/faulty amperometric control'. The old fixed-speed lamp table has no F28 at all. Check the model and series, not just the letters.

The ranking pages each copy only one manual's wording. acerrorcode, the #1 list page, prints one row — 'Loop of the station detect error' (the M2 wording) — and nothing else: no LED1 count, no cause, no fix, no diagnostic; M3's overcurrent wording and M7's PWM wording never appear; no lockout rule, no flowchart note, no cabinet trap. Arlington gets the meaning and LED1 19 right but its three causes/solutions (loose compressor wiring, bad PCB/IPM, bad compressor) are generic invented steps, not a manual procedure; M3's overcurrent wording, M7's PWM wording, M3's lockout rule and the M2 p.50 flowchart grouping are all never mentioned. decorexpro has no F28 content at all — but it gives F11 two flash counts, 6 and 19, both wrong (F11 is 18; 19 is F28). Extra collision: M7's 'Module PWM select circuit error' wording for F28 matches cabinet Table B's F19 row ('DC voltage or AC voltage low / PWM selection circuit error in the power module') — the same wording under a different code number in the cabinet table.

What the machine does when F28 fires

The indoor display shows F28 in place of the set temperature, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 19 times. The outdoor unit's compressor drive is at fault. After the alarm goes out 3 times in an hour, the machine locks (M3).

Common causes

  1. Loop of the station detect error (M2 p.43).
  2. Compressor overcurrent at fixed frequency, software threshold (M3 Table A).
  3. Module PWM select circuit error (M7 p.25).
  4. Flowchart grouping (M2 p.50 / AS35 §10.4.11): the compressor rotor position cannot be detected normally — compressor wiring wrong or connection poor, or compressor damaged.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Call a technician — no fix step exists in M2, M3 or M7 for F28. The only quoted diagnostic conditions come from AS35 §10.4.11: the compressor rotor position cannot be detected normally, when the compressor wiring is wrong or the connection is poor, or when the compressor is damaged.

No power-cycle reset time and no 'check the wiring, then the PCB, then the compressor' procedure exists in M2/M3/M7 for F28 — Arlington's three generic steps are the site's own template, not manual steps. The M2 p.50 flowchart's step-by-step content was not verified in this research — do NOT reproduce invented flowchart steps. Do NOT present F11's loss-of-synchronism meaning as F28's sole meaning, and do NOT present any one manual's wording as 'the' meaning while hiding the other two. F28 is a one-row code in every manual, and the three rows disagree on wording: keep it short, show all three wordings attributed, and carry no invented DIY steps.

When to call a technician

  • F28 returns after a restart, or the machine has locked out after three trips in an hour.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 19 times — F28 (F11 is 18, F2 is 24).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

F28 diagnosis involves compressor wiring, the PCB/IPM and the compressor on the outdoor unit: that is qualified-technician work. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F28 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

It depends on which Haier manual you read, and the page must say so: the US service manual (M2 p.43) calls F28 a 'loop of the station detect error'; the European Technical Book (M3 Table A p.30) calls it 'compressor overcurrent at fixed frequency (software threshold)'; the AU/NZ installation manual (M7 p.25) calls it a 'module PWM select circuit error'. All three agree on one thing: it is an outdoor-unit compressor-drive fault, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 19 times.

Why do different websites say different things about F28?

Because they each copy only one manual's wording. acerrorcode and Arlington both print only the M2 wording ('loop of the station detect error') and never mention the M3 or M7 wordings. No single wording is 'the' meaning — the code is F28 with 19 flashes in all three manuals.

Is F28 a loss-of-synchronism fault?

Not exactly. Haier's service manual groups F28 (19 flashes) with F11 (18 flashes) under one 'Loss of Synchronism Detection' flowchart (M2 p.50; AS35SBBHRA-MW §10.4.11), because both relate to the compressor rotor position not being detected normally. But the diagnostic tables list F28 separately (overcurrent / loop detect / PWM circuit, depending on the manual) and F11 separately ('Loss of compressor synchronism detection'). Do not read F28 as loss-of-synchronism alone.

Can I fix Haier F28 myself?

No fix step exists in M2, M3 or M7 for F28. The only quoted diagnostic conditions come from the AS35 service manual §10.4.11: the compressor rotor position cannot be detected normally, when the compressor wiring is wrong or the connection is poor, or when the compressor is damaged. Do not follow guides that list generic 'check wiring, replace PCB, replace compressor' steps as a manual procedure — they are invented. Switch the unit off and call a technician.

Is F28 the same on a Haier cabinet air conditioner?

No. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) gives F28 a completely different meaning: 'No charge / faulty amperometric control'. Check whether your unit is a split or a cabinet before using any F-code meaning. Also do not confuse F28 (19 flashes) with F11 (loss of synchronism, 18 flashes) or F2 (failed to start / overcurrent, 24 flashes).
