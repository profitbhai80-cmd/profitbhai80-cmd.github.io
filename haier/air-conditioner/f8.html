Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F8

Quick answer

Error codeF8 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF8 = outdoor DC fan motor fault — the outdoor unit's DC fan motor is not running correctly; the outdoor board raises F8 when it receives no rotation feedback signal from the fan motor within 2 minutes. The indoor display shows F8 (US/EU models; AU/NZ M7 models store it as a trouble record) and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 9 times.
Most common causeA damaged fan motor or a damaged module PCB — the M2 p.45 / §10.4.4 flowchart's decision, made from resistance checks on the outdoor PCB.
Safe to check yourself?Limited — switch the unit off, clear visible debris from the outdoor fan, and call a technician. The flowchart's checks (fan-terminal resistances, CN21 pin tests at 310V) are live-voltage technician work.
Checked againstHaier Ductless Split Air Conditioner Service Manual (US models AW09/12/18/24ES2VHA + 1U09/12/18/24ES2VHA, R410A), Error Codes & Problem Solving (p.43) (p.43 + p.45 flowchart); Haier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units) (Table A pp.28-31); Haier 1U26MADFRA Room Air Conditioner Installation Manual (Sept 2024, R32, AU/NZ), Outdoor Unit Troubleshooting (p.25) (p.25); Haier AS35SBBHRA-MW Service Manual (Version V1, 2024-11-30) §10.4.4 pp.35-36

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F8 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means the OUTDOOR DC fan motor on the US ductless split families, the EU mono/multi range, the AU/NZ R32 split families and the AS35/AS50 wall-split families (M2/M3/M7, identical LED count of 9). The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) carries the same fan-motor wording under a different label, F09. The old fixed-speed lamp table has no F8 at all. Check the model and series, not just the letters — and do not confuse the outdoor fan (F8) with the indoor fan (E14).

The ranking pages get F8 wrong in two ways. acerrorcode, the #1 list page, says F8 is 'High pressure abnormal' — that is F20 (LED1 5), not F8; it also puts F8's own meaning under F2 ('Outdoor DC motor failure'). globalwizahub, the only dedicated F8 article, says F8 is 'Temperature sensor malfunction' — wrong on every element: the temperature sensors are F6/F7/F21/F25, and it invents an 'Average Repair Cost $50-$200' and a 'Typical Resolution Time' with no source. Arlington gets the meaning and LED1 9 right but its steps are generic fan steps (clean the fan, move it manually, check wiring) — NOT the M2 p.45 flowchart (no 2-minute feedback rule, no resistance checks, no module-PCB-vs-motor decision) — and it calls itself 'Official Haier', which it is not. F20 is high-pressure protection (LED1 5); F2 is compressor overcurrent (LED1 24); E14 is the INDOOR fan motor — none of them is F8.

What the machine does when F8 fires

The indoor display shows F8 in place of the set temperature (US/EU models), and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 9 times. With no rotation feedback from the outdoor fan motor within 2 minutes, the outdoor board raises F8 and the unit cannot run the outdoor fan.

Common causes

  1. No rotation feedback signal received from the fan motor within 2 minutes (AS35 §10.4.4).
  2. Module PCB damaged — fan-terminal resistances greater than 10MΩ / more than 40kΩ (AS35 §10.4.4).
  3. Fan motor damaged — CN21 pin resistances close to 0mΩ (AS35 §10.4.4).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off and clear any visible debris from the outdoor fan, then call a technician — the diagnostic steps are live-voltage technician work.
  2. The technician removes the fan motor after switching off the power and restarts the unit — if the LED becomes light/flash, the motor is at fault (AS35 §10.4.4 flowchart).
  3. The technician tests the resistances between the black line and red line, and between the black line and white line, of the fan terminal of the outdoor PCB: greater than 10MΩ / more than 40kΩ — the module PCB is damaged, replace it with a new one (AS35 §10.4.4).
  4. The technician tests the fan motor terminal resistance between pin 1 (310V) and pin 4 (0V), or pin 5 (15V) and pin 4 (0V), of CN21: close to 0mΩ — the fan motor is damaged, replace it with a new one (AS35 §10.4.4).

The M2 p.45 / §10.4.4 flowchart is a technician procedure: live-voltage resistance checks (310V at CN21) that decide between the module PCB and the fan motor. Do NOT present Arlington's generic steps (clean the fan, move it manually, check wiring) as manual steps — they are the site's own additions, not M2 p.45 steps. Do NOT present globalwizahub's temperature-sensor fixes (reset, clean filters, check vents) — they belong to the wrong fault entirely.

When to call a technician

  • F8 returns after a restart — the module PCB or the fan motor needs diagnosis.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 9 times — outdoor DC fan motor (E14, the indoor fan, has no LED count).
  • The outdoor fan does not spin, or spins then stops with F8 showing.
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

F8 diagnosis involves live-voltage resistance checks on the outdoor unit's PCB (310V at the CN21 fan-motor terminal): that is qualified-technician work. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F8 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F8 means the outdoor DC fan motor is faulty — the outdoor board gets no rotation feedback signal from the fan motor within 2 minutes. The indoor display shows F8 (US/EU models) and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 9 times (M2 p.43 + flowchart p.45; M3 Table A pp.28-31; M7 p.25).

Is F8 a temperature sensor fault?

No. One dedicated F8 article (globalwizahub) says F8 means 'temperature sensor malfunction' — that is wrong. The temperature sensors are F6 (ambient), F7 (suction), F21 (defrost) and F25 (delivery). F8 is the outdoor fan motor. Do not follow temperature-sensor fixes (clean filters, check vents) for F8.

Is F8 a high-pressure fault?

No. The #1 list page (acerrorcode) says F8 is 'High pressure abnormal' — that is wrong; high-pressure protection is F20 (LED1 5). F8 is the fan motor.

Can I fix Haier F8 myself?

Only the owner-safe part: switch the unit off, clear visible debris from the outdoor fan, and call a technician. The manual's diagnostic steps are technician work — the board checks whether a rotation-feedback signal arrives within 2 minutes, then tests resistances on the outdoor PCB (fan-terminal resistances and the CN21 pin tests) to decide whether the module PCB or the fan motor is damaged and needs replacing. Those checks involve live voltages (310V) and must not be done by the owner.

Is F8 the same on a Haier cabinet air conditioner?

No. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) has no code F8: the same fan-motor wording appears there under F09. Also do not confuse F8 (outdoor fan) with E14 (indoor fan motor malfunction) — different fans, different codes. Check whether your unit is a split or a cabinet before using any F-code meaning.
