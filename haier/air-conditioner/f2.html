Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F2

Quick answer

Error codeF2 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF2 = failed to start compressor / compressor overcurrent — an instantaneous overcurrent protection trip of the compressor. The indoor display shows F2 (on US models); the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 24 times. Wording varies by manual and is attributed: M3 says 'Failed to start compressor / Overcurrent'; M2 says 'Overcurrent of the compressor'; M7 says 'Instantaneous over-current protection of the compressor'. M3 adds the lockout rule: the alarm goes out 3 times in an hour and locks the machine.
Most common causeA faulty power module (if the fault appears before the compressor starts), an abnormal power supply voltage, or an incorrect refrigerant charge (if the fault appears after running) — the M2 p.46 shared flowchart.
Safe to check yourself?No — no owner fix exists in any manual. Switch the unit off and call a technician. Note: on newer R32 models F2 is a 'Trouble record' code (M7), so a unit may log F2 without showing it on the indoor panel — the outdoor LED1 count (24 flashes) is the confirmation.
Checked againstHaier Ductless Split Air Conditioner Service Manual (US models AW09/12/18/24ES2VHA + 1U09/12/18/24ES2VHA, R410A), Error Codes & Problem Solving (p.43) (p.43 + p.46 flowchart); Haier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units) (Table A p.31); Haier 1U26MADFRA Room Air Conditioner Installation Manual (Sept 2024, R32, AU/NZ), Outdoor Unit Troubleshooting (p.25) (p.25)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F2 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means compressor overcurrent on the US ductless split families, the EU mono/multi range and the AU/NZ R32 split families (M2/M3/M7, identical LED count of 24). The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) has NO F2 — the same 'Failed to start compressor / Overcurrent' wording sits under F24 there. The old fixed-speed lamp table's F2 (heat exchanger of the external module sensor off) is a different, unverified code set. Check the model and series, not just the letters.

The ranking pages give F2 four different wrong meanings. acerrorcode says F2 is 'Outdoor DC motor failure' — that is F8. decorexpro says F2 is the outdoor heat-exchanger temperature sensor disconnected. heatingnewsjournal says F2 is 'Overpressure protection' — that is F20/F39. howtodiscuss says F2 is the compressor being out of sync — that is F11. Local service-centre templates lump F1/F2 together as an indoor sensor fault — indoor sensors are E1/E2. And the three overcurrent codes share one flowchart in M2: F22 = AC overcurrent (LED1 3 flashes), F2 = compressor overcurrent (24 flashes), F23 = single-phase / U-V-W compressor phase overcurrent (25 flashes) — count the outdoor LED flashes to tell them apart.

What the machine does when F2 fires

The indoor display shows F2 (on US models); the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 24 times. After the alarm goes out 3 times in an hour, the machine locks (M3).

Common causes

  1. Power module faulty — the error appears before the compressor starts (M2 p.46 flowchart).
  2. Power supply voltage abnormal — the compressor starts normally but the fault appears after running (M2 p.46 flowchart).
  3. Refrigerant charge incorrect — the compressor starts normally but the fault appears after running (M2 p.46 flowchart).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Call a technician — there is no owner fix in any manual for F2.
  3. The technician follows the M2 p.46 flowchart: is the error displayed before the compressor starts? Yes — the power module is faulty, replace the power module.
  4. The compressor starts normally but malfunctions are reported after it has operated for a period of time — is the power supply voltage normal? No: correct the power supply. Yes: check the refrigerant charge (M2 p.46).

No power-cycle reset time, no 'clean the coils' step and no compressor-replacement step exists in the manuals for F2 — Arlington's 'restart after 1-3 minutes' and 'repair or replace the compressor' are the site's own additions, not manual steps. Note the M7 display-type nuance honestly: on newer R32 models F2 is a trouble-record code, so a unit that logs F2 without showing it on the indoor panel still needs the outdoor LED1 count (24 flashes) checked by a technician.

When to call a technician

  • F2 returns after a restart, or the machine has locked out after three trips in an hour.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 24 times — compressor overcurrent (F22 is 3 flashes, F23 is 25).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

F2 involves live voltage checks and the refrigerant system: power-module replacement, power-supply correction and refrigerant-charge work are qualified-technician work, and refrigerant handling requires a licensed technician. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F2 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F2 means the compressor has tripped on overcurrent — the European book words it 'Failed to start compressor / Overcurrent', the US service manual 'Overcurrent of the compressor', and the AU/NZ manual 'Instantaneous over-current protection of the compressor'. The outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 24 times.

What is the difference between Haier F2, F22 and F23?

All three are overcurrent codes sharing one flowchart in the US service manual, told apart by the LED1 flash count: F22 = AC overcurrent (3 flashes), F2 = compressor overcurrent (24 flashes), F23 = single-phase / U-V-W compressor phase overcurrent (25 flashes). Count the outdoor LED flashes to tell them apart.

Why does my Haier AC lock out on F2?

The European Technical Book states the alarm 'goes out 3 times in an hour and locks the machine'. After three trips in an hour the unit locks out by design — repeated resetting will not clear it; a technician must find the overcurrent cause.

Can I fix Haier F2 myself?

No owner fix exists in the manuals. The flowchart's only remedies are replacing a faulty power module, correcting the power supply, and checking the refrigerant charge — all technician work. Switch the unit off and call a technician.

Is F2 the same on a Haier cabinet air conditioner?

No. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) has no code F2 at all — and the 'Failed to start compressor / Overcurrent' wording appears there under F24, not F2. Check whether your unit is a split or a cabinet before using any F-code meaning.
