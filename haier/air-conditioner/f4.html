Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F4

Quick answer

Error codeF4 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF4 = compressor delivery/discharge (exhaust) high-temperature / overheat protection — the compressor's discharge temperature has risen past the protection threshold. The indoor display shows F4 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 8 times. M3 adds the lockout rule: the alarm goes out 3 times in an hour and locks the machine. Threshold discrepancy — the manuals do NOT agree on one number: M2 says the discharge temperature exceeds 230 °F (= 110 °C) shortly after the compressor starts; M8 says it exceeds 110 shortly after start; M3 says delivery temperature above 120°. Present both figures attributed per manual — do NOT treat either number as universal.
Most common causeRefrigerant loss (if the discharge temperature exceeds the threshold shortly after start-up) — the M2/M8 flowchart's first branch; otherwise a faulty exhaust temperature sensor or a faulty outdoor PCB.
Safe to check yourself?No — no owner fix exists in any manual. Switch the unit off and call a technician; refrigerant handling requires a licensed technician. Do not keep resetting a locked-out unit.
Checked againstHaier Ductless Split Air Conditioner Service Manual (US models AW09/12/18/24ES2VHA + 1U09/12/18/24ES2VHA, R410A), Error Codes & Problem Solving (p.43) (p.43 + p.49 flowchart); Haier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units) (Table A p.28); Haier 1U26MADFRA Room Air Conditioner Installation Manual (Sept 2024, R32, AU/NZ), Outdoor Unit Troubleshooting (p.25) (p.25); Haier HSU-09HSA03/R2(DB) / HSU-12HSA03/R2(DB) Service Manual (Edition 20070116), p.47 (p.47)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F4 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means compressor discharge overheat protection on the US ductless split families, the EU mono/multi range, the AU/NZ R32 split families and the HSU-HSA03 wall-split family (M2/M3/M7/M8, identical LED count of 8). The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) carries the same fault under a different label, F08. The old fixed-speed lamp table's F4 (airflow sensor off) is a different, unverified code set. Check the model and series, not just the letters — and check the manual for your model family before trusting any single temperature threshold.

The ranking pages give F4 no threshold at all. Arlington gets F4 right but gives no temperature threshold, no lockout note and no M2/M8 flowchart logic — its causes are dust on coils, compressor/fan overheating and low/wrong refrigerant, while the manuals' first branch is refrigerant loss on a 230 °F / 110 reading, not dust — and 'refill the gas' is presented as a homeowner step. acerrorcode gets F4 right in one row but its F14 row steals the discharge-sensor fault ('Compressor discharging temp. sensor failure' — that is F25; F14 = 4-way valve). decorexpro says F4 is the air-supply temperature sensor turning off — wrong. howtodiscuss says F4 is the airflow sensor off in its table and a 'refrigerator compressor shell roof' problem in its FAQ — both wrong. The threshold discrepancy is the point no ranking page carries: 110 °C / 230 °F in M2/M8 vs above 120° in M3 — Haier's own books disagree by 10 degrees, so do not treat either number as universal. F25 is the discharge-temperature SENSOR fault (LED1 13) — a faulty sensor, not the overheat trip; F13 (lack of refrigerant) is a possible CAUSE of F4, not the same code.

What the machine does when F4 fires

The indoor display shows F4 in place of the set temperature, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 8 times. After the alarm goes out 3 times in an hour, the machine locks (M3).

Common causes

  1. Loss of refrigerant — the discharge temperature exceeds the threshold shortly after the compressor starts (M2 p.49; M8 p.47).
  2. Exhaust temperature sensor faulty — the fault appears after running a while even though the measured temperature stays below the threshold (M2 p.49; M8 p.47).
  3. Outdoor PCB faulty — the sensor resistance is correct but the fault persists (M2 p.49; M8 p.47).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Call a technician — refrigerant handling requires a licensed technician; there is no owner fix in any manual for F4.
  3. The technician turns the unit on and checks the exhaust temperature of the compressor (M2 p.49).
  4. Does the temperature exceed 230F shortly after the compressor starts up? Yes — there is loss of refrigerant: check the refrigerant charge (M2 p.49; M8 uses 110).
  5. No — check the exhaust temperature sensor's resistance. Is it correct? No — the sensor is faulty, replace the sensor. Yes — the outdoor PCB is faulty, replace the PCB (M2 p.49).

No power-cycle reset time, no 'clean the coils/filters' step and no refrigerant-refill step for the homeowner exists in the manuals for F4 — Arlington's dust-cleaning and 'refill the gas' advice are the site's own additions, not manual steps. And the threshold discrepancy (110 °C in M2/M8 vs above 120° in M3) is shown as-is, not resolved by picking one number — the threshold varies by model family and book.

When to call a technician

  • F4 returns after a restart, or the machine has locked out after three trips in an hour.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 8 times — discharge overheat (F25, the sensor fault, is 13 flashes).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

F4 involves the refrigerant system and live outdoor-unit work: the refrigerant-charge check, exhaust-sensor test and outdoor-PCB replacement are qualified-technician work, and refrigerant handling requires a licensed technician. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F4 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F4 means compressor discharge high-temperature protection: the compressor's delivery (exhaust) temperature has risen past the protection threshold. The indoor display shows F4 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 8 times (M2 p.43; M3 Table A p.28; M7 p.25; M8 p.47).

What temperature triggers Haier F4?

It depends on the manual for your model family: the US ductless service manual (M2) and the HSU-HSA03 manual (M8) use 230 °F / 110 °C shortly after the compressor starts; the European Technical Book (M3) says delivery temperature above 120°. Haier's own books disagree by 10 degrees, so do not treat either number as universal — check the manual for your model.

What causes Haier F4?

Per the M2/M8 flowcharts, the first thing to check is refrigerant loss: if the discharge temperature exceeds the threshold shortly after start-up, the likely cause is leaked or low refrigerant ('Check refrigerant charge'). If the temperature stays below the threshold but the fault still appears after running a while, the exhaust sensor or the outdoor PCB is faulty.

Can I fix Haier F4 myself?

No owner fix exists in the manuals. The flowchart's only remedies are a refrigerant-charge check, an exhaust-sensor resistance test and replacement, and outdoor-PCB replacement — all technician work, and refrigerant handling requires a licensed technician. Switch the unit off and call a technician; cleaning filters or coils is not the manual's diagnostic.

Is F4 the same on a Haier cabinet air conditioner?

The fault is the same but the label is different: the cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) calls it F08, 'Compressor discharge temperature protection too high', with the same above-120° wording. There is no cabinet code F4 — check whether your unit is a split or a cabinet before using any F-code meaning. Also do not confuse F4 (the overheat trip) with F25 (the discharge-temperature sensor fault).
