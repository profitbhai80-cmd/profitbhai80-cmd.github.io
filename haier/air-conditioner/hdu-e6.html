Haier HDU Air Conditioner Error Code E6

Quick answer

Error codeE6 — Haier HDU Air Conditioner
What it meansE6 = pressure protection — a pressure switch in the refrigerant circuit has tripped. WHICH switch depends on the model family: LOW-pressure switch on most HDU models (HDU-42H03/H, HDU-28C03/M, HDU-28H03/H, HDU-18HA03/M(R1)); HIGH-pressure switch on HDU-14CL03/M and HDU-18CL03/M; BOTH switches report as E6 on HDU-09/12/18/28HF03/CF03 (M6 p.16; M9 No. 0010571462 p.16; M8 No. 0150506681 pp.15-16). A single flat 'E6 = low pressure' statement would be wrong for HDU-14/18CL03/M owners, and a flat 'E6 = high pressure' statement would be wrong for everyone else.
Most common causeA pressure switch in the refrigerant circuit has tripped — high-pressure switch acts 3 times in 30 minutes (high-pressure branch) or low-pressure switch acts in normal running (low-pressure branch). The manuals state only that the switch acted; they do not state a cause.
Safe to check yourself?No — there is no DIY fix for E6 in any HDU manual. Stop the operation and contact your sales dealer. Pressure-switch and refrigerant-circuit diagnosis is technician work.

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E6 is a PRESSURE fault with a model split — it means pressure protection on the HDU ceiling/ducted families, but WHICH pressure switch depends on the model family. Low-pressure switch: HDU-42H03/H (M6), HDU-28C03/M, HDU-28H03/H, HDU-18HA03/M(R1) (mcgrp mirrors of the M6 table, all reading 'low pressure pressure of pipe'). High-pressure switch: HDU-14CL03/M + HDU-18CL03/M (M9, No. 0010571462). Both switches report as E6: HDU-09/12/18/28HF03/CF03 (M8, No. 0150506681) — the table lists high-pressure abnormal (switch acts 3 times in 30 min) and low-pressure abnormal (switch acts in normal running) as E6 rows. E6 is a PRESSURE fault. E5 is a CURRENT fault (overload, CT check) — different protection, different code. E8/E9 are communication faults. Do not confuse with split Table A: there is NO indoor E6 in Table A (E6 exists only in cabinet Table B, where it means 'Outdoor unit high pressure' — a different series again). Do not confuse with the old lamp table E6 = 'No communication between units' (competitor/snippet only, contradicted by every HDU manual read). Do not confuse with F39/F40 (high/low pressure switch alarms, split outdoor table) — different codes. Do not confuse with Gree E6 — the site's own Gree page says Gree E6 is a communication failure between the indoor unit and the outdoor unit; that is a different brand's code, not Haier HDU E6.

The ranking pages give E6 no split at all. acerrorcode's E6 row 'Pressure protect' is generically right but empty — two words, no high/low split, no switch rules, no models, no flash count. A reader with an HDU-14CL03/M (high-pressure E6) and a reader with an HDU-42H03/H (low-pressure E6) both get the same two words. decorexpro says 'E6 indicates a lack of communication between the indoor and outdoor units of the air conditioner. This can happen due to a lack of power, mixed-up interconnect wires, blown fuses, or malfunctions of the electronic control boards.' — WRONG for HDU on every element: E6 is pressure protection; communication faults are E8 (wired controller) and E9 (indoor-outdoor). This is the old-lamp-table E6. Arlington has NO E6 section anywhere — the code is absent from a page calling itself the 'Full 30-Code Matrix' — and it calls itself 'Official Haier', which it is not. The SERP itself surfaces the high/low split (one manual says high, three say low) and no ranking page explains it.

What the machine does when E6 fires

When the CHECK lamp (red) flickers, an irregularity has occurred in the air conditioner (M6 p.16). The wired controller shows E6 and the remote-type flash count is 6 (cross-checked against the HSU-HVA103 lamp table, E6 = pressure protection tripped, 6 lamps). The low-pressure row shows 16 in the M8 OCR column — report the 6 as the cross-checked count and flag the 16 as M8-only/OCR-level.

Common causes

  1. High-pressure switch acts 3 times in 30 minutes (M8 pp.15-16, high-pressure branch).
  2. Low-pressure switch acts in normal running (M8 pp.15-16, low-pressure branch).
  3. The manuals state only that the pressure switch acted — they do not state a cause. The low-pressure branch does NOT automatically mean 'low refrigerant' (the manual says only that the low-pressure switch acted).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Stop the operation of the air conditioner and contact your sales dealer (M6 p.15-16; M8 p.17, verbatim instruction).
  2. Pre-service-call checks (M6 p.15 / M8 p.17, the general troubleshooting list — NOT E6-specific): 'Is the power source switch adjust cut in? Is city supply power in normal? ... Is the thermostat adjust as required? Isn't the air filter dirty? Isn't any doors or windows left open? Doesn't any obstacle exist at the air inlet or outlet?' These are the only owner checks the manual authorises before a service call.
  3. Note the manual's timing facts: the unit 'has a function of automatic restart system after recovering power stoppage' (M6 p.16), and cooling/heating 'is not operable for three minutes after the conditioner is stopped. Because the protecting circuit is activated' (M8 p.17).

There are NO per-code reset or DIY fix steps for E6 in any HDU manual. The page must say so plainly. Do NOT present any competitor's E6 fix as a manual step: decorexpro's wiring/fuse checks are built on the wrong (no-communication) meaning. Refrigerant work (checking charge, topping up gas) is technician work involving the sealed circuit — no HDU manual authorises an owner to do it, and the low-pressure branch does NOT automatically mean 'low refrigerant' (the manual says only that the low-pressure switch acted). The page must not state a cause the manuals do not state. Detection rules (M8 pp.15-16): 'High pressure switch acts 3 times in 30 min' (high-pressure branch — the high-pressure protection needs three switch actions in 30 minutes before it latches as a fault) and 'Low pressure switch acts in normal running' (low-pressure branch).

When to call a technician

  • E6 returns — the pressure switches and refrigerant circuit need diagnosis by a dealer/technician.
  • The unit stops cooling or heating and shows E6 on every start.
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

Pressure-switch and refrigerant-circuit diagnosis is technician work involving the sealed circuit. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power, and never top up refrigerant on the strength of an E6 code.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E6 mean on a Haier HDU air conditioner?

E6 means pressure protection — a pressure switch in the refrigerant circuit has tripped. Which switch depends on your model: on most HDU models (HDU-42H03/H, HDU-28C03/M, HDU-28H03/H, HDU-18HA03/M(R1)) it is the LOW-pressure switch; on HDU-14CL03/M and HDU-18CL03/M it is the HIGH-pressure switch; on HDU-09/12/18/28 models both switches report as E6. The red CHECK lamp flickers and the wired controller shows E6 (M6 p.16; M9 No. 0010571462 p.16; M8 No. 0150506681 pp.15-16).

Is E6 a communication fault?

No. One ranking article (decorexpro) says E6 means no communication between the indoor and outdoor units — that is the old fixed-speed lamp-table meaning, contradicted by every HDU manual read. On HDU units, communication faults are E8 (wired controller link) and E9 (indoor-outdoor link). E6 is pressure protection.

Does E6 always mean low refrigerant?

No. Low pressure CAN be caused by low refrigerant, but the manuals do not say that — they say only that the pressure switch acted. On HDU-14/18CL03/M models E6 is the HIGH-pressure switch, where low refrigerant is not the issue at all. Do not top up refrigerant on the strength of an E6 code; refrigerant work is sealed-circuit technician work.

Can I fix Haier HDU E6 myself?

The manuals give no DIY fix for E6. They instruct: stop the operation and contact your sales dealer. Pressure-switch and refrigerant-circuit diagnosis is technician work.

Is E6 the same on a Haier split air conditioner?

There is no indoor E6 in the split-wall diagnostic tables at all. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) has an E6, and it means 'Outdoor unit high pressure' — a different series again. Check whether your unit is an HDU duct/ceiling unit, and which HDU model family, before using any E6 meaning.
