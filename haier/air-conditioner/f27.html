Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F27

Quick answer

Error codeF27 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF27 = compressor locked rotor / compressor stall — the compressor rotor is locked (it tries to start, pulls high current and fails) or the compressor has stopped momentarily. The indoor display shows F27 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 7 times. M3 adds the lockout rule: the alarm goes out 3 times in an hour and locks the machine. All three manuals agree on the code, the meaning and the LED1 count (7).
Most common causeA locked compressor rotor — the compressor stalls instead of turning (M2/M3/M7). No manual gives a cause list beyond the locked-rotor/stall wording.
Safe to check yourself?No — no fix step exists in any manual read. Switch the unit off and call a technician; a locked compressor is outdoor-unit technician work. Do not try to free the rotor by 'mechanical ways' — that is not a manual procedure.
Checked againstHaier Ductless Split Air Conditioner Service Manual (US models AW09/12/18/24ES2VHA + 1U09/12/18/24ES2VHA, R410A), Error Codes & Problem Solving (p.43) (p.43); Haier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units) (Table A p.29); Haier 1U26MADFRA Room Air Conditioner Installation Manual (Sept 2024, R32, AU/NZ), Outdoor Unit Troubleshooting (p.25) (p.25)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F27 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means a locked compressor on the US ductless split families, the EU mono/multi range and the AU/NZ R32 split families (M2/M3/M7, identical LED count of 7). The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) carries a DIFFERENT F27 — 'Input current verification circuit failure' — and FlexFit/ducted systems use yet another numbering where outdoor 27 is a module current detect circuit fault. The old fixed-speed lamp table has no F27 at all. Check the model and series, not just the letters.

The ranking pages give F27 almost nothing. Arlington, the only ranking list page with a full F27 section, gets the meaning and LED1 7 right — but its 'mechanical ways' fix is invented (no manual step), M3's lockout rule is never mentioned, and there is no series split, no cabinet-F27 trap and no F2 distinction. acerrorcode has NO F27 row at all — its main table jumps F25 to F28. decorexpro has no F27 either (its F-codes stop at F12). F27 is a distinct code from F2 ('Failed to start compressor / Overcurrent', LED1 24) and from F11 ('Loss of compressor synchronism detection', LED1 18): a locked rotor can look like a failed start, but the manuals give it its own code and flash count.

What the machine does when F27 fires

The indoor display shows F27 in place of the set temperature, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 7 times. The compressor stalls instead of turning. After the alarm goes out 3 times in an hour, the machine locks (M3) — it will not keep retrying on its own.

Common causes

  1. Compressor has a locked rotor (M2).
  2. Compressor stopped momentary (M2).
  3. Locked compressor — alarm 3 times in an hour locks the machine (M3).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Call a technician — there is no owner fix step in any manual for F27, and no F27 flowchart exists. A locked compressor is outdoor-unit technician work.

There is NO F27 flowchart and NO fix step in any manual: the full extent of the manual evidence is the M2 p.43 table row, the M3 Table A row-7 lockout rule and the M7 p.25 row. M2's flowcharts (pp.44-50) cover E14, F8, F1, F22/F2/F23, F3, F19, F4, E7 and F11/F28 only. Do NOT follow guides that tell you to free the rotor by 'mechanical ways' — that is Arlington's invention, not a manual procedure. Do NOT present F2's overcurrent steps as F27 steps — they are different codes. F27 is a one-row code in every manual: keep it short, do not pad with invented diagnostics.

When to call a technician

  • F27 returns after a restart, or the machine has locked out after three alarms in an hour.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 7 times — locked rotor (F2 is 24, F11 is 18).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

A locked compressor is outdoor-unit work on live drive electronics: that is qualified-technician work. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F27 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F27 means the compressor has a locked rotor or has stopped momentarily — the compressor stalls instead of turning. The indoor display shows F27 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 7 times (M2 p.43; M3 Table A p.29; M7 p.25).

Will the unit keep trying after F27?

The European Technical Book says 'The alarm goes out 3 times in an hour and locks the machine' — after three F27 alarms within an hour the unit locks out and will not keep retrying on its own.

Can I fix Haier F27 myself?

No fix step exists in any manual read. Unlike F1-F4 and F11/F28, F27 has no diagnostic flowchart at all — the manuals give only the table row plus the lockout rule. Do not follow guides that tell you to free the rotor by 'mechanical ways': that is not a manual procedure. Switch the unit off and call a technician — a locked compressor is outdoor-unit technician work.

Is F27 the same as F2?

No. F2 is 'Failed to start compressor / Overcurrent' (LED1 24 flashes) — a start failure with overcurrent. F27 is a locked rotor / stall (LED1 7 flashes). They can look similar (a compressor that will not turn) but they are distinct codes with different flash counts.

Is F27 the same on a Haier cabinet air conditioner?

No. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) gives F27 a completely different meaning: 'Input current verification circuit failure'. Check whether your unit is a split or a cabinet before using any F-code meaning. Also do not confuse F27 (locked rotor, 7 flashes) with F11 (loss of synchronism, 18 flashes).
