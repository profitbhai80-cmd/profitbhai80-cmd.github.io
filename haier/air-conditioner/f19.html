Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F19

Quick answer

Error codeF19 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF19 = power voltage too high or too low — the power supply voltage is outside its limits and the over/under-voltage protection has tripped. The indoor display shows F19 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 6 times. The threshold differs by manual and measurement point: M3 (EU, 230V/50Hz mains) says above 270 V or below 187 V; M2 (US) tests above 390 V or below 150 V at the P & N terminals of the outdoor PCB (a DC-bus figure). M3 gives no lockout rule for F19 — do not import the '3 times in an hour' text, which belongs to a different fault.
Most common causeSupply voltage not as specified, a broken IPM module, or a broken outdoor PCB (HSU09VHJ §7.3.10) — the M2 p.48 flowchart's diagnostic split.
Safe to check yourself?No — there is no owner fix in any manual. Switch the unit off, check that the home's supply is normal (other appliances, breaker), and call a technician or electrician; the P & N terminal checks are live-voltage technician work.

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F19 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means power over/under voltage on the US ductless split families, the EU mono/multi range, the AU/NZ R32 split families and the AS35/HSU09VHJ wall-split families (M2/M3/M7, identical LED count of 6). The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) carries a DIFFERENT fault under F19 — low-only voltage plus a PWM selection circuit error — while cabinet F06 carries the high/low thresholds. The old fixed-speed lamp table has no F19 at all. Check the model and series, not just the letters — and check the manual for your model family before trusting any single voltage threshold.

The ranking pages give F19 no threshold at all. acerrorcode gets the meaning right in one row but gives no LED count, no threshold and no flowchart, and its F11 row steals F12's fault. Arlington gets the meaning and LED1 6 right but gives no voltage threshold anywhere — the one number a reader with a meter needs — and its fixes (check power cord, wall socket, main wiring) are the site's own additions, not the M2 flowchart's P & N / L1/L2 checks and 'Replace power module' remedy. decorexpro has no F19 section at all and misassigns F19's fault and flash count to F11 ('F11 flashing 6 times indicates that the mains voltage is abnormal' — 6 flashes is F19; F11 is loss of synchronism, 18 flashes). The haier-ac.pt ducted table gives yet another pair of figures (high voltage over 280VAC, low voltage lower than 155VAC) under flash-count codes 6 and 19, not F-codes — do not mix those into split-wall F19.

What the machine does when F19 fires

The indoor display shows F19 in place of the set temperature, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 6 times. The unit's over/under-voltage protection has tripped, so the unit will not run normally until the supply is corrected.

Common causes

  1. Supply voltage not as specified (HSU09VHJ §7.3.10).
  2. IPM module broken (HSU09VHJ §7.3.10).
  3. Outdoor PCB broken (HSU09VHJ §7.3.10).
  4. Unstable power supply — the compressor is operating normally, so the fault is the supply itself (M2 p.48 flowchart).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Check that the home's supply is normal — other appliances working, breaker not tripped — then call a technician or electrician; there is no owner fix in any manual for F19.
  3. The technician turns the unit on: is the compressor operating normally? Yes — 'The power supply is unstable.' (M2 p.48).
  4. No — the technician checks the P & N terminals of the power supply on the outdoor PCB: is the voltage >390V or <150V? Yes — 'Replace power module.' (M2 p.48).
  5. No — the technician verifies proper voltage to terminals L1 & L2 of the outdoor unit: 'Correct power source.' (M2 p.48).

No power-cycle reset time, no 'check the power cord / wall socket' step and no homeowner voltage test exists in the manuals for F19 — Arlington's cord/socket/wiring checks are the site's own additions, not manual steps. Do NOT present a single voltage threshold as the test: M2's >390V/<150V is a DC-bus test at the P & N terminals, M3's 270V/187V is an AC mains figure; they are not interchangeable. The flowchart is a technician procedure (live-voltage measurement, power-module replacement), not a homeowner repair.

When to call a technician

  • F19 returns after a restart — the supply voltage, IPM module or outdoor PCB needs diagnosis.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 6 times — voltage fault (F11 is 18 flashes, F28 is 19).
  • Other appliances in the home also misbehave — call an electrician about the supply.
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

F19 diagnosis involves live-voltage measurement on the outdoor unit's PCB (P & N terminals, L1/L2 terminals): that is qualified-technician work. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power, and never measure live terminals yourself.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F19 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F19 means the power voltage is too high or too low — the over/under-voltage protection has tripped. The indoor display shows F19 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 6 times (M2 p.43; M3 Table A pp.28-31; M7 p.25).

What voltage triggers Haier F19?

It depends on the manual for your model family, and the two figures measure different points: the European Technical Book (M3) says AC supply above 270 V or below 187 V; the US ductless manual (M2) tests above 390 V or below 150 V at the P & N terminals of the outdoor PCB (a DC-bus measurement). A third figure exists in a different Haier system (the ducted table: over 280VAC / lower than 155VAC). Do not treat any one number as universal — check the manual for your model, and do not test live terminals yourself.

What does the manual say to check for Haier F19?

The M2 p.48 flowchart (technician steps): turn the unit on and check whether the compressor is operating normally; if not, check the P & N terminals of the power supply on the outdoor PCB — if the voltage is above 390 V or below 150 V, replace the power module; otherwise verify proper voltage to terminals L1 & L2 and correct the power source. The HSU09VHJ manual lists the supposed causes as supply voltage not as specified, a broken IPM module, or a broken outdoor PCB.

Can I fix Haier F19 myself?

There is no owner fix in the manuals. The flowchart's checks are live-voltage measurements inside the outdoor unit and its remedy is power-module replacement — technician work. Switch the unit off, check that the home's supply is normal (other appliances, breaker), and call a technician or electrician. Do not open the outdoor unit to measure the P & N terminals yourself.

Is F19 the same on a Haier cabinet air conditioner?

No. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) calls F19 a different fault — 'DC voltage or AC voltage low / PWM selection circuit error in the power module' (low-only, plus a PWM circuit error, with a 3-times-an-hour lockout) — while cabinet F06 carries the high/low voltage fault with the same 270V/187V thresholds. Check whether your unit is a split or a cabinet before using any F-code meaning. Also do not confuse F19 (voltage) with F22/F2/F23 (compressor overcurrent) or F28 (PWM select circuit error).
