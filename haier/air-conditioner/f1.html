Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F1

Quick answer

Error codeF1 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF1 = power module / IPM protection — the outdoor unit's Intelligent Power Module has tripped its protection. The indoor display shows F1 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 2 times (M2 p.43; M3 Table A p.28; M7 p.25). M3 adds the lockout rule: the alarm goes out 3 times in an hour and locks the machine. F1 is not a communication error and not a refrigerant leak.
Most common causeA faulty power module (if the fault appears before the compressor starts) or a refrigerant pressure, blockage or charge problem (if the fault appears after the unit has run a while) — the M2 p.46 flowchart's diagnostic split.
Safe to check yourself?No — there is no owner fix in any manual. Switch the unit off and call a technician; the manual's remedies (power-module replacement, refrigerant pressure/charge check) are technician work. Do not keep resetting a locked-out unit.
Checked againstHaier Ductless Split Air Conditioner Service Manual (US models AW09/12/18/24ES2VHA + 1U09/12/18/24ES2VHA, R410A), Error Codes & Problem Solving (p.43) (p.43 + p.46 flowchart); Haier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units) (Table A p.28); Haier 1U26MADFRA Room Air Conditioner Installation Manual (Sept 2024, R32, AU/NZ), Outdoor Unit Troubleshooting (p.25) (p.25)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F1 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means IPM protection on the US ductless split families, the EU mono/multi range and the AU/NZ R32 split families (M2/M3/M7, identical code and LED count). The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) has NO literal F1 — its nearest analogue, F02, is 'IPM hardware overcurrent', a different label and a different fault. The old fixed-speed lamp table's F1 (indoor heat-exchanger sensor off) is a different, unverified code set. Check the model and series, not just the letters.

The ranking pages give F1 four different wrong meanings. appliancepartspros says F1 is a communication error — that is E7 (indoor-outdoor) or F3 (IPM-outdoor PCB), and its fix is the E7 fix. decorexpro says F1 is the indoor heat-exchanger sensor disconnected — that is E2. heatingnewsjournal says F1 is a refrigerant leak or insufficient charge — that is F13. howtodiscuss gives four contradictory F1 meanings in one article (temperature sensor; PCB communication failure; Ideal boiler low water pressure; pump connector/drainage hose) — all wrong. Arlington gets F1 right but cites no manual, gives no lockout note, and its solutions ('check for leaks', 5-15-minute rest) are not the M2 flowchart steps.

What the machine does when F1 fires

The indoor display shows F1 in place of the set temperature, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 2 times. After the alarm goes out 3 times in an hour, the machine locks (M3) — it will not keep running on repeated resets.

Common causes

  1. Power module faulty — the fault appears before the compressor starts (M2 p.46 flowchart).
  2. Refrigerant system pressure abnormal, with blockages (M2 p.46 flowchart).
  3. Improper refrigerant charge — the compressor starts normally but the fault appears after it has run a while (M2 p.46 flowchart).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Call a technician — there is no owner fix in any manual for F1.
  3. The technician ensures the refrigerant system pressure is normal with no blockages, then replaces the power module (M2 p.46).
  4. Diagnostic split (M2 p.46): is the error displayed before the compressor starts? Yes — the power module is faulty, replace the module. The compressor starts normally but malfunctions are reported after it has operated for a period of time — check for proper refrigerant charge.

No power-cycle reset time, no voltage-check step and no 'clean the coils' fix exists in the manuals for F1 — Arlington's 5-15-minute rest and leak check are the site's own additions, not manual steps. If F1 persists after a restart, the machine is locked out by design (M3) and needs a technician — do not tell yourself to keep resetting it.

When to call a technician

  • F1 returns after a restart, or the machine has locked out after three trips in an hour.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 2 times — the IPM protection trip.
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

F1 involves the outdoor power module and the refrigerant system: power-module replacement and refrigerant pressure/charge work are qualified-technician work, and refrigerant handling requires a licensed technician. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F1 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F1 means power module / IPM protection: the outdoor unit's Intelligent Power Module has tripped its protection. The indoor display shows F1 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 2 times (M2 p.43; M3 Table A p.28; M7 p.25).

Why does my Haier AC lock out on F1?

The European Technical Book states the alarm 'goes out 3 times in an hour and locks the machine'. After three trips in an hour the unit locks out by design — repeated resetting will not clear it; a technician must check the power module.

Can I fix Haier F1 myself?

No owner fix exists in the manuals. The M2 flowchart's only remedies are checking refrigerant pressure for blockages/charge and replacing the power module — both technician work. Switch the unit off and call a technician; do not keep resetting a locked-out unit.

Is Haier F1 a communication error?

No. Some guides say F1 means the indoor and outdoor units are not communicating, but that is E7 (indoor-outdoor) or F3 (IPM-outdoor PCB). F1 is the power module's own protection trip — a different fault in the same outdoor unit.

Is F1 the same on a Haier cabinet air conditioner?

No. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) has no code F1 at all; its nearest code is F02, 'IPM hardware overcurrent'. Same module, different label and different fault — check whether your unit is a split or a cabinet before using any F-code meaning.
