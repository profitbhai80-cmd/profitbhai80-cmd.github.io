Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F40

Quick answer

Error codeF40 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF40 = low pressure switch alarm — the outdoor unit's low-pressure switch is open (unplugged, faulty, or tripped by lack of refrigerant / low pressure). The outdoor error number is 43. On boards with two LEDs this reads as LED1 4 flashes + LED2 3 flashes (M×10+N rule, M3 p.29; 1U105 note: "The outdoor error code = (M*10+N). LED1 flash M times and LED2 flash N times"). Do NOT read it as a flat LED1 count.
Most common causeLow pressure switch unplugged, faulty, or tripped by lack of refrigerant (M3 Table A row 43) — the FlexFit manual adds dirt in the coils or air filter and incorrect refrigerant charge as the usual causes.
Safe to check yourself?No — there is no owner fix in any manual. Switch the unit off, check that filters/coils are not visibly blocked (only if safely accessible), and call a technician. Switch-continuity testing and any refrigerant removal/re-charge are technician work.
Checked againstHaier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units), p.31; Haier 3U24MS2VHB FlexFit Series Multi Zone Split System Service Manual, Console Technical Overview p.112 + p.35; Haier FlexFit 17400 BTU Dual-Zone Mini Split Service Manual, §"Outdoor Unit Error Codes", p.129; haier-ac.pt cassette/duct PDF (Cod.-Erros-Condutas-Alta-Pest.pdf); Haier 4U36MS2VHB Mid-Static Ducted Service Manual, p.92; Haier 1U105S2SS1FA outdoor troubleshooting (haier-ac.pt, Cod.-Erros-Consolas-Chao-Tecto.pdf)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F40 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means the low pressure switch alarm on the EU mono/multi range, the US FlexFit multi-zone/console families, and the cassette/convertible/duct families (M3/FlexFit/cassette/4U36/1U105, identical outdoor error number 43). It is NOT listed in M7 (the AU/NZ R32 installation manual jumps F35 → F43) or in M2 (the US ductless manual lists only 22 codes). The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) has NO F40 — the low pressure switch alarm sits there under F43. Ducted numeric 43 = low-pressure switch open = F40. Check the model and series, not just the letters.

What the machine does when F40 fires

The indoor display shows F40, and the outdoor unit's low-pressure switch is open. Per the 1U105 outdoor troubleshooting table, after the compressor has been running for 3 minutes, the switch is detected unconnected for 60 seconds — or unconnected for 30 seconds at standby. The error is Non-resumable: it will not clear until the fault is fixed and the power is cut and restored.

Common causes

  1. Low pressure switch unplugged (M3 Table A row 43).
  2. Low pressure switch faulty (M3 Table A row 43).
  3. Lack of refrigerant (M3 Table A row 43).
  4. Dirt in the coils or dirt in the air filter (FlexFit p.129 — the usual causes of low pressure).
  5. Incorrect refrigerant charge (FlexFit p.129).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Check that filters and coils are not visibly blocked (only if safely accessible), then call a technician — there is no owner fix in any manual for F40.
  3. The technician checks the continuity of the switch to ensure it is not open or shorted (FlexFit p.129).
  4. If the system trips on this error, it will be necessary to remove the refrigerant and re-charge to confirm low or high charge is not causing the problem (FlexFit p.129) — technician work; there are no pressure ports that can be accessed to measure low pressure in heat mode.

No power-cycle reset time, no pressure value in bar/psi, and no "clean the filter and restart" step exists in any manual for F40 — the FlexFit text names dirt in the coils/filter and incorrect charge as the USUAL causes, not as a guaranteed owner fix. Do NOT present F42's -45°C rule or F13's refrigerant steps as F40 steps. No flowchart exists for F40 in M2 (it is not listed there at all).

When to call a technician

  • F40 returns after a restart — the switch, the refrigerant charge, or the coils need diagnosis.
  • The error is Non-resumable — it will not clear until the fault is fixed and the power is cut and restored (1U105).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

F40 diagnosis involves switch-continuity testing and refrigerant removal/re-charge: that is qualified-technician work. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power, and never attempt refrigerant work yourself.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F40 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F40 means the low pressure switch alarm is active — the outdoor unit's low-pressure switch is open: unplugged, faulty, or tripped by lack of refrigerant / low pressure. The outdoor error number is 43 (M3 Table A p.31; FlexFit 3U24 p.112).

How is F40 detected?

Per the 1U105 outdoor troubleshooting table, after the compressor has been running for 3 minutes, the switch is detected unconnected for 60 seconds — or unconnected for 30 seconds at standby. The error is Non-resumable: it will not clear until the fault is fixed and the power is cut and restored.

Can I fix Haier F40 myself?

The manual's own testing procedure is a technician job: check the continuity of the low-pressure switch, and check for dirt in the coils or air filter and incorrect refrigerant charge. There are no pressure ports to measure low pressure in heat mode, so confirming a charge problem means removing and re-charging the refrigerant — technician work. Switch the unit off and call a technician.

Is F40 the same as F42?

No. F40 is the low-pressure SWITCH alarm (the switch itself is open). F42 is LOW SYSTEM pressure protection — the system temperature has fallen below -45°C. A faulty switch and a genuinely low system pressure are different faults with different codes (43 vs 45).

Is F40 the same on a Haier cabinet air conditioner?

The fault is the same, but the number is different: the cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) carries the low pressure switch alarm under F43, and has no F40. Check whether your unit is a split or a cabinet before using any F-code meaning.
