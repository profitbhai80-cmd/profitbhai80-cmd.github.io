Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F18

Quick answer

Error codeF18 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF18 = liquid pipe circuit 'D' temperature probe failure — the liquid-pipe temperature sensor on refrigerant circuit D of the multi-split outdoor unit is disconnected, broken, or poorly positioned. The indoor display shows F18 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 31 times (two-LED boards: LED1 5 + LED2 1 = code 51). Multi-split ONLY: circuits B–E exist only on multi-split outdoor units (3U/4U/5U); a single-split unit has only circuit A and can never show F18.
Most common causeSensor disconnected, broken, or poorly positioned (M3 Table A cause).
Safe to check yourself?No — no fix step exists in any manual. Switch the unit off, note which indoor unit / circuit D is affected, and call a technician; checking or replacing the outdoor liquid-pipe-D probe is technician work on the outdoor unit.

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F18 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means the liquid pipe circuit 'D' temperature probe has failed on the EU mono residential/multi range, the US FlexFit multi-zone R410A range (2U18/3U24/4U36MS2VHB) and the EU cassette/convertible/duct ranges on 3U/4U/5U outdoor units (M3/M8/M9, all agreeing on code, circuit D and LED1 31 flashes). F18 is NOT in M2 (US single-split, p.43) and NOT in M7 (1U26MADFRA, p.25) — expected, since both are single-split tables and circuits B–E exist only on multi-split outdoor units. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) uses F18 for a COMPLETELY DIFFERENT fault — loss of compressor synchronism detection (LED 38). Check the model and series, not just the letters.

What the machine does when F18 fires

The indoor display shows F18 in place of the set temperature, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 31 times (two-LED boards: LED1 5 + LED2 1 = code 51). The liquid-pipe temperature sensor on refrigerant circuit D of the multi-split outdoor unit has failed, so the outdoor unit cannot read that circuit's liquid-pipe temperature.

Common causes

  1. Sensor disconnected, broken, or poorly positioned (M3 Table A cause).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Note which indoor unit / circuit D is affected, and call a technician to check or replace the outdoor liquid-pipe-D probe — there is no owner fix step in any manual.
  3. Do not follow guides that tell you to use another sensor's fix for F18.

No power-cycle reset time, resistance value, 'check the plug' step, sensor-replacement procedure or 'clean the sensor' step exists in any manual for F18. No lockout rule ('3 times in an hour') is stated for the F18 row in any manual — do not claim one. No detection window (like F6's 60 seconds) is stated for F18 — the 60-second short/open-circuit window is verified only for F6/F7/F21/F25 (M3 rows 10–13); do not claim it for F18. Do not present other sensors' steps (e.g. E1 indoor-sensor steps, or F6 ambient-sensor steps) as F18 steps.

When to call a technician

  • F18 returns after a restart — the circuit-D liquid-pipe sensor needs diagnosis.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 31 times — liquid pipe circuit D (F10 is circuit A, 28; F16 is circuit B, 29; F17 is circuit C, 30).
  • A single-split unit seems to show F18 — double-check the code, or check whether the unit is actually a cabinet (cabinet F18 is loss of synchronism).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

No owner repair exists in any manual for this code. Never open the indoor or outdoor unit while it is connected to power, and never attempt refrigerant, gas or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F18 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F18 means the liquid pipe circuit 'D' temperature probe (sensor) has failed — the liquid-pipe temperature sensor on refrigerant circuit D of the multi-split outdoor unit is disconnected, broken, or poorly positioned. The indoor display shows F18 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 31 times (M3 Table A p.31; M8 FlexFit p.112; M9 haier-ac.pt p.428).

Can a single-split Haier show F18?

No. Circuits B–E exist only on multi-split outdoor units (3U/4U/5U, serving up to five indoor units). A single-split unit has only circuit A, so it can show F10 (liquid pipe A) but never F16/F17/F18. If your single-split unit seems to show F18, double-check the code — or check whether your unit is actually a cabinet (see next question).

Is F18 the same on a Haier cabinet air conditioner?

No. On the cabinet 14kW unit (M3 Table B), F18 means 'Loss of compressor synchronism detection' — an inverter/compressor circuit failure, not a sensor fault. Same code number, completely different fault. Check whether your unit is a split/multi-split or a cabinet before using any F-code meaning.

How is F18 different from F10, F16 and F17?

They are four different liquid-pipe sensors, one per refrigerant circuit: F10 = circuit A (LED 28), F16 = circuit B (29), F17 = circuit C (30), F18 = circuit D (31). The gas-pipe counterparts are F29–F32 (circuits A–D) and F26 (circuit E); liquid circuit E is F33. Never merge the circuits — each one points the technician at a different indoor unit's pipework.

Can I fix Haier F18 myself?

No fix step exists in any manual read. The manuals give only the table row plus M3's cause ('Sensor disconnected, broken, or poorly positioned'). Switch the unit off, note that it is circuit D, and call a technician to check or replace the outdoor liquid-pipe-D probe. Do not follow guides that tell you to use another sensor's fix for F18.
