Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F36

Quick answer

Error codeF36 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF36 = middle part of the condenser temperature sensor failure — the piping temperature probe 'TC' on the outdoor (condenser) unit is faulty: sensor disconnected, broken, or poorly positioned. The indoor display shows F36 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 39 times — the highest flash count in THIS batch (M3 Table A continues past F36 with higher-numbered rows, so 39 is not the highest in the full table). M7 does NOT list F36 at all — its table jumps F35 to F43.
Most common causePiping temperature probe 'TC' faulty — sensor disconnected, broken, or poorly positioned (M3 Table A).
Safe to check yourself?No — no fix step exists in any manual read. Switch the unit off and call a technician to check or replace the outdoor condenser/piping TC probe. Do not use other sensors' steps (E1 indoor, F6 ambient) as F36 steps.
Checked againstHaier Ductless Split Air Conditioner Service Manual (US models AW09/12/18/24ES2VHA + 1U09/12/18/24ES2VHA, R410A), Error Codes & Problem Solving (p.43) (p.43); Haier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units) (Table A p.31)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F36 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means the condenser (piping TC) temperature sensor on the US ductless split families and the EU mono/multi range (M2/M3, identical LED count of 39). The AU/NZ installation manual (M7) does NOT list F36 — its outdoor table jumps from F35 (38 flashes) to F43 (46). The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) carries the same 'TC' wording under a different code, F39 — while cabinet F42 is the high pressure switch alarm. The old fixed-speed lamp table has no F36 at all. Check the model and series, not just the letters — and do not confuse F36 with F39.

What the machine does when F36 fires

The indoor display shows F36 in place of the set temperature, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 39 times. The outdoor board cannot read the condenser/piping TC probe, so it cannot run the outdoor unit correctly.

Common causes

  1. Piping temperature probe 'TC' faulty (M3).
  2. Sensor disconnected (M3).
  3. Sensor broken (M3).
  4. Sensor poorly positioned (M3).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Call a technician — the outdoor condenser/piping TC probe is on the outdoor unit, and its check or replacement is technician work. No manual gives the procedure, a resistance value, or a plug check for F36, so this page does not invent one.

There is NO F36 flowchart and NO fix step in any manual: the full extent of the manual evidence is the M2 p.43 table row and the M3 Table A row-39 cause ('Sensor disconnected, broken, or poorly positioned'). M2's flowcharts (pp.44-50) cover E14, F8, F1, F22/F2/F23, F3, F19, F4, E7 and F11/F28 only. Do NOT present other sensors' steps (e.g. E1 indoor-sensor steps, or F6 ambient-sensor steps) as F36 steps — the sensors are different. Do NOT present cabinet F39's steps or meaning as F36's. F36 is a one-row code in both manuals that list it: keep it short, do not pad with invented diagnostics.

When to call a technician

  • F36 returns after a restart — the outdoor condenser/piping TC probe needs checking or replacing.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 39 times — condenser TC probe (F34 is 37, F35 is 38, F39 is the high pressure switch alarm).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

The outdoor condenser/piping TC probe is on the outdoor unit: checking or replacing it is qualified-technician work. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F36 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F36 means the middle part of the condenser temperature sensor has failed — the piping temperature probe 'TC' on the outdoor unit is faulty (disconnected, broken, or poorly positioned). The indoor display shows F36 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 39 times (M2 p.43; M3 Table A p.31).

How is F36 detected?

The European Technical Book lists the cause as 'Sensor disconnected, broken, or poorly positioned' — the outdoor board watches the condenser/piping TC probe and raises F36 when it reads faulty. No detection window (like F6's 60 seconds) is given for F36 in any manual read.

Can I fix Haier F36 myself?

No fix step exists in any manual read. Unlike F1-F4 and F11/F28, F36 has no diagnostic flowchart at all — the manuals give only the table row plus M3's short cause. Switch the unit off and call a technician to check or replace the outdoor condenser/piping TC probe.

Is F36 the same as F39?

No. Table A F39 is a 'High pressure switch alarm' — a pressure switch, not a temperature sensor. And the cabinet 14kW table's F39 is a 'Heat exchanger temperature sensor (TC) failure' — the same 'TC' wording family as Table A F36, but a different code number on a different unit type. Check whether your unit is a split or a cabinet, and use the code number, not just the wording.

Is F36 listed in every Haier manual?

No. The AU/NZ installation manual (M7, 1U26MADFRA, p.25) does not list F36 at all — its outdoor table jumps from F35 to F43. The US service manual (M2) and the European Technical Book (M3) both list it with 39 flashes. If your unit shows F36, use the M2/M3 meaning above; do not go looking for it in M7.
