Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code E5

Quick answer

Error codeE5 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansE5 = indoor unit ice (antifreeze / anti-frosting) protection — the indoor unit's heat-exchanger (coil) temperature has dropped too low and the unit has stopped to protect against icing. The indoor display shows E5 (M7 marks it 'Trouble record' rather than 'Directly display') and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 22 times (M7, p.25). M3's '16' is an alarm/row number, NOT a flash count — never quote 16 as a flash count.
Most common causeIndoor unit exchanger temperature too low (M3 Table A cause).
Safe to check yourself?No — there is no owner fix in any manual. Haier India's official page says these codes 'only can be solved by after-sale service engineers' and asks you to turn off the unit and call the Haier hotline for help. A single restart at the wall/MCB to see whether E5 returns is the most that page supports.
Checked againstHaier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units), pp.30–31; Haier 1U26MADFRA Room Air Conditioner Installation Manual (Sept 2024, R32, AU/NZ), Outdoor Unit Troubleshooting (p.25); Haier India official self-service page, 'How to do if indoor display error code? Such as E1 or F8 ect.' — 'E5 — Indoor anti-frosting protection'

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E5 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means indoor unit ice protection on the EU mono residential/multi range, the EU mono commercial range, the cabinet 14kW range (identical row) and the AU/NZ R32 split families (M3/M7, identical meaning, LED1 22 flashes on M7). The US ductless split manual (M2) does NOT list E5 at all — do not claim M2 corroboration. Other Haier families use E5 for completely different faults: HDU E5 is overload current, the old fixed-speed lamp table E5 is over-current limit protection, the cassette AB-series E5 is an indoor-unit address fault, and portable units have no E5 at all. Check the model and series, not just the letters.

The ranking pages get E5 wrong more often than right. appliancefaults.in (#1) has the meaning right but invents root causes (mechanical/electrical/software categories, a '60-second factory reset', citric-acid descaling of 'water-touching components') and never quotes M3 or M7. vcn.be (#2) and lcrypcore (#5) both say E5 is compressor overload/overcurrent — the HDU/old-lamp meaning, wrong for a split. acerrorcode (#3) says 'Over current protect limit' — also wrong for a split. heatingnewsjournal (#7–#8) says E5 is a full water tank / drainage fault — that confuses split E5 with the portable FL water-full fault; portable units have no E5. decorexpro says the compressor switched off due to excessive load — again the old lamp-table meaning.

What the machine does when E5 fires

The indoor display shows E5 in place of the set temperature, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 22 times. The indoor heat-exchanger temperature has dropped too low, so the unit has stopped to protect against icing.

Common causes

  1. Indoor unit exchanger temperature too low (M3 Table A cause).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Switch it back on once at the wall/MCB to see whether E5 returns (the most Haier India's page supports — it says 'turn off the unit'; the restart-once gloss is appliancefaults.in's, not Haier's).
  3. If E5 comes back, leave the unit off and call Haier authorised service — Haier India says these codes 'only can be solved by after-sale service engineers'.

No filter-cleaning step, no defrost-wait time, no 'let the ice melt' step, no refrigerant-charge step and no sensor-resistance value exists in any manual for E5. Do not present appliancepartspros' E2 'turn off and let it sit for several hours to allow the ice to melt / wash the filter' steps, vcn.be's 'let cool and reset', lcrypcore's 'one start attempt, watch household lighting' steps, or appliancefaults.in's '60-second factory reset' and citric-acid descaling as E5 steps — none of them is a manual E5 step.

When to call a technician

  • E5 returns after a restart — call Haier authorised service; there is no owner fix.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 22 times — ice protection (M3's 16 is an alarm number, not a flash count).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

No owner repair exists in any manual for this code. Never open the indoor or outdoor unit while it is connected to power, and never attempt refrigerant, gas or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications.

Frequently asked questions

What does E5 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

E5 means indoor unit ice protection (antifreeze / anti-frosting protection): the indoor heat-exchanger temperature has dropped too low and the unit has stopped to protect against icing. The indoor display shows E5 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 22 times (M3 Table A pp.30–31; M7 p.25; Haier India official page).

Is E5 an overcurrent or compressor overload fault?

No — not on a split. Overcurrent/overload is the HDU family's E5 ('The trouble of the overload current', HDU-42H03/H manual) and the old fixed-speed E5 ('Over current limit protection'). Two dedicated per-code E5 pages (vcn.be, lcrypcore) and the #3 list page (acerrorcode) give the overcurrent meaning for a split code — that is wrong for a split.

Is E5 a drainage or water-tank fault?

No. Heating News Journal says Haier E5 means a full condensate tank — that confuses split E5 with the portable water-full fault (portable code FL = full water; portable has no E5). On a split, E5 is ice protection, not drainage.

Can I fix Haier E5 myself?

No fix step exists in any manual read. Haier India's official page says these codes 'only can be solved by after-sale service engineers' and asks you to 'turn off the unit and call our Haier hotline for help'. Switch the unit off (and back on once at the wall/MCB to see whether E5 returns); if it comes back, leave it off and call Haier authorised service. Do not use the 'let cool and reset' compressor-overload fix, the '60-second factory reset', or the filter-washing steps other pages invent for E5.

How is E5 different from E2 and E4?

E2 is the indoor heat-exchange SENSOR failing — a broken sensor. E5 is the protection that trips when the exchanger temperature actually drops too low — ice, not a broken sensor. E4 is the indoor EEPROM error on a split — not anti-freeze. (On a portable, the letters swap: portable E4 IS the anti-freeze code and there is no portable E5.) Check your unit type — split, portable, HDU or cassette — before using any E-code meaning.
