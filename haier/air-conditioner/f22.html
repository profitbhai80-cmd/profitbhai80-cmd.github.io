Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F22

Quick answer

Error codeF22 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF22 = overcurrent protection of AC electricity for the outdoor model — the AC-side (mains) current into the outdoor unit is too high, and the outdoor board trips the protection. The indoor display shows F22 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 3 times. No manual gives a current threshold in amps or a lockout rule for F22.
Most common causeCompressor current too high — a damaged IPM module, a damaged compressor, or a power supply voltage too low or too high (AS35 §10.4.6); M3 adds 'faulty current control / phase sequence reversed (models ON OFF)'.
Safe to check yourself?No — no owner fix step exists in any manual. Switch the unit off and call a technician; the shared flowchart's checks (IPM module, compressor, supply voltage) are technician work on the outdoor unit.
Checked againstHaier Ductless Split Air Conditioner Service Manual (US models AW09/12/18/24ES2VHA + 1U09/12/18/24ES2VHA, R410A), Error Codes & Problem Solving (p.43) (p.43 + p.46 flowchart); Haier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units) (Table A pp.28-31); Haier 1U26MADFRA Room Air Conditioner Installation Manual (Sept 2024, R32, AU/NZ), Outdoor Unit Troubleshooting (p.25) (p.25); Haier AS35SBBHRA-MW Service Manual (Version V1, 2024-11-30) §10.4.6 p.38

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F22 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means AC overcurrent for the outdoor model on the US ductless split families, the EU mono/multi range, the AU/NZ R32 split families and the AS35 wall-split family (M2/M3/M7, identical LED count of 3). The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) carries a DIFFERENT F22: 'PFC circuit overcurrent / DC overcurrent at the power module'. The old fixed-speed lamp table has no F22 at all. Check the model and series, not just the letters.

The ranking pages give F22 almost nothing. Arlington, the only ranking guide with an F22 section, gets the LED1 3 right but its cause is a tautology ('detecting or requires overcurrent protection') that drops the 'AC electricity' qualifier, so it cannot separate F22 from F2/F23 — and its fixes (rest 5-10 minutes, check voltages, pro checks power supply/compressor/main PCB) are generic, not the M2 p.46 flowchart content, whose actual decision points (IPM module damaged, compressor damaged, supply voltage too low/high) are never named. acerrorcode gets F22's wording right in one row but gives no LED count, no flowchart, no cause and no fix — and its neighbouring rows are wrong on at least 12 codes, including F2 ('Outdoor DC motor failure' — that is F8) and F13 ('Communication failure between outdoor PCB and module' — that is F3). decorexpro and heatingnewsjournal have no F22 content at all: decorexpro's F-section ends at F12, heatingnewsjournal's list ends at F2.

What the machine does when F22 fires

The indoor display shows F22 in place of the set temperature, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 3 times. The AC-side current into the outdoor unit is too high, so the outdoor board trips the overcurrent protection.

Common causes

  1. Compressor current too high (AS35 §10.4.6).
  2. IPM module damaged (AS35 §10.4.6).
  3. Compressor damaged (AS35 §10.4.6).
  4. Power supply voltage too low or too high (AS35 §10.4.6).
  5. Faulty current control / phase sequence reversed (models ON OFF) (M3 Table A).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Call a technician — there is no owner fix step in any manual for F22, and no F22-only diagnostic exists.
  3. The technician follows the shared F22/F2/F23 flowchart (M2 p.46; AS35 §10.4.6): the compressor current is too high when the IPM module is damaged or the compressor is damaged, and the decision conditions include a power supply voltage too low or too high.

No power-cycle reset time, no current threshold in amps, no 'check the voltages' owner step and no component-replacement procedure exists in any manual for F22. Arlington's 'restart after 5-10 minutes / check voltages / pro checks power supply, compressor and main PCB' is the site's own generic list, not a manual step — and 'check the main PCB' is not a flowchart step at all. Do NOT present F1's IPM-protection steps or F19's voltage steps as F22 steps — related protections, different codes. F22 is a one-row code in every manual plus one shared flowchart: keep it short, do not pad with invented diagnostics.

When to call a technician

  • F22 returns after a restart — the IPM module, compressor or supply voltage needs diagnosis.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 3 times — AC overcurrent (F2 is 24, F23 is 25).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

F22 diagnosis involves the IPM module, the compressor and the power supply on the outdoor unit: that is qualified-technician work. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F22 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F22 means overcurrent protection of AC electricity for the outdoor model — the AC-side current into the outdoor unit is too high, so the outdoor board trips the protection. The indoor display shows F22 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 3 times (M2 p.43; M3 Table A; M7 p.25).

How is F22 different from F2 and F23?

All three are overcurrent codes sharing one diagnostic flowchart (M2 p.46), and only the flash count and label separate them: F2 = compressor overcurrent / failed to start (LED1 24 flashes), F22 = AC electricity overcurrent for the outdoor model (LED1 3), F23 = single-phase / U-V-W phase overcurrent of the compressor (LED1 25). Count the outdoor LED flashes to tell them apart.

What causes Haier F22?

Per the manuals, the compressor current is too high; the decision conditions are a damaged IPM module, a damaged compressor, or a power supply voltage that is too low or too high (AS35 §10.4.6). The European Technical Book adds 'faulty current control / phase sequence reversed (models ON OFF)' (M3 Table A). No manual gives a current threshold in amps.

Can I fix Haier F22 myself?

No owner fix step exists in any manual read. F22 has no F22-only diagnostic — only the shared F22/F2/F23 flowchart, whose checks (IPM module, compressor, supply voltage) are technician work on the outdoor unit. Do not follow guides that list generic 'restart 5-10 minutes / check the voltages' steps as a manual fix. Switch the unit off and call a technician.

Is F22 the same on a Haier cabinet air conditioner?

No. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) reuses the number F22 for a different fault: 'PFC circuit overcurrent / DC overcurrent at the power module'. Check whether your unit is a split or a cabinet before using any F-code meaning. Also do not confuse F22 (AC overcurrent, 3 flashes) with F2 (compressor overcurrent, 24) or F23 (phase overcurrent, 25).
