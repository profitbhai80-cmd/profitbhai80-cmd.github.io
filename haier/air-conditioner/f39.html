Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F39

Quick answer

Error codeF39 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF39 = high pressure switch alarm — the outdoor unit's high-pressure switch is open (unplugged, faulty, or tripped by excessive refrigerant / excessive pressure). The outdoor error number is 42. On boards with two LEDs this reads as LED1 4 flashes + LED2 2 flashes (M×10+N rule, M3 p.29; 1U105 note: "The outdoor error code = (M*10+N). LED1 flash M times and LED2 flash N times"). Do NOT read it as a flat LED1 count.
Most common causeHigh pressure switch unplugged, faulty, or tripped by excessive refrigerant (M3 Table A row 42) — the FlexFit manual adds dirt in the coils or air filter and incorrect refrigerant charge as the usual causes.
Safe to check yourself?No — there is no owner fix in any manual. Switch the unit off, check that filters/coils are not visibly blocked (only if safely accessible), and call a technician. Switch-continuity testing and any refrigerant removal/re-charge are technician work.
Checked againstHaier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units), p.31; Haier 3U24MS2VHB FlexFit Series Multi Zone Split System Service Manual, Console Technical Overview p.112 + p.35; Haier FlexFit 17400 BTU Dual-Zone Mini Split Service Manual, §"Outdoor Unit Error Codes", p.129; haier-ac.pt cassette/duct PDF (Cod.-Erros-Condutas-Alta-Pest.pdf); Haier 4U36MS2VHB Mid-Static Ducted Service Manual, p.92; Haier 1U105S2SS1FA outdoor troubleshooting (haier-ac.pt, Cod.-Erros-Consolas-Chao-Tecto.pdf)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F39 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means the high pressure switch alarm on the EU mono/multi range, the US FlexFit multi-zone/console families, and the cassette/convertible/duct families (M3/FlexFit/cassette/4U36/1U105, identical outdoor error number 42). It is NOT listed in M7 (the AU/NZ R32 installation manual jumps F35 → F43) or in M2 (the US ductless manual lists only 22 codes). The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) carries a DIFFERENT fault under F39 — "Heat exchanger temperature sensor (TC) failure" — while cabinet F42 is the high pressure switch alarm. Ducted numeric 42 = high-pressure switch open = F39 (do NOT read it as "F42"). Check the model and series, not just the letters.

What the machine does when F39 fires

The indoor display shows F39, and the outdoor unit's high-pressure switch is open. Per the 1U105 outdoor troubleshooting table, after the compressor has been running for 3 minutes, the switch is detected open-circuit for 30 seconds; the error locks if it occurs 3 times in 1 hour, and it is Non-resumable — it will not clear until the fault is fixed and the power is cut and restored.

Common causes

  1. High pressure switch unplugged (M3 Table A row 42).
  2. High pressure switch faulty (M3 Table A row 42).
  3. Excessive refrigerant (M3 Table A row 42).
  4. Dirt in the coils or dirt in the air filter (FlexFit p.129 — the usual causes of high pressure).
  5. Incorrect refrigerant charge (FlexFit p.129).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Check that filters and coils are not visibly blocked (only if safely accessible), then call a technician — there is no owner fix in any manual for F39.
  3. The technician checks the continuity of the switch to ensure it is not open or shorted (FlexFit p.129).
  4. If the system trips on this error, it will be necessary to remove the refrigerant and re-charge to confirm low or high charge is not causing the problem (FlexFit p.129) — technician work; there are no pressure ports that can be accessed to measure high pressure in cool mode.

No power-cycle reset time, no pressure value in bar/psi, and no "clean the filter and restart" step exists in any manual for F39 — the FlexFit text names dirt in the coils/filter and incorrect charge as the USUAL causes, not as a guaranteed owner fix. Do NOT present F41's 65°C threshold or F8's fan-motor steps as F39 steps. No flowchart exists for F39 in M2 (it is not listed there at all).

When to call a technician

  • F39 returns after a restart — the switch, the refrigerant charge, or the coils need diagnosis.
  • The error locks after 3 trips in 1 hour — the fault factor must be cleaned out and the power cut and re-offered (1U105).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

F39 diagnosis involves switch-continuity testing and refrigerant removal/re-charge: that is qualified-technician work. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power, and never attempt refrigerant work yourself.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F39 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F39 means the high pressure switch alarm is active — the outdoor unit's high-pressure switch is open: unplugged, faulty, or tripped by excessive refrigerant / excessive pressure. The outdoor error number is 42 (M3 Table A p.31; FlexFit 3U24 p.112).

How is F39 detected?

Per the 1U105 outdoor troubleshooting table, after the compressor has been running for 3 minutes, the switch is detected open-circuit for 30 seconds. The error locks if it occurs 3 times in 1 hour, and it is Non-resumable — it will not clear until the fault is fixed and the power is cut and restored.

Can I fix Haier F39 myself?

The manual's own testing procedure is a technician job: check the continuity of the high-pressure switch, and check for dirt in the coils or air filter and incorrect refrigerant charge. There are no pressure ports to measure high pressure in cool mode, so confirming a charge problem means removing and re-charging the refrigerant — technician work. Switch the unit off and call a technician.

Is F39 the same as F41?

No. F39 is the high-pressure SWITCH alarm (the switch itself is open). F41 is system high-pressure PROTECTION — the outdoor heat exchanger TC temperature is too high (over 65°C). A faulty switch and an overheating condenser are different faults with different codes (42 vs 44).

Is F39 the same on a Haier cabinet air conditioner?

No — this is the sharpest trap for this code. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) uses F39 for "Heat exchanger temperature sensor (TC) failure" (a sensor fault), and puts the high pressure switch alarm under F42. Check whether your unit is a split or a cabinet before using any F-code meaning.
