Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code E8

Quick answer

Error codeE8 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansE8 = communication error between the wired controller and the indoor unit — the wired controller and the indoor unit have not communicated for more than 4 consecutive minutes. The indoor display shows E8; M3 gives outdoor error 08. No manual read gives an LED1 flash count for E8 — do not claim 'LED1 flashes 8 times'. E8 only applies where a wired controller is fitted; a wall-split run from the wireless remote alone has no wired controller to lose communication with.
Most common causeLack of communication between the wired controller and the indoor unit for more than 4 consecutive minutes (M3 Table A).
Safe to check yourself?No fix step exists in any manual. On wired-controller units the alarm does not clear itself — it must be recalled via the procedure in your user manual (for the YR-E17 touchscreen controller: press the TIME key for 10 seconds, M3 pp.16–17). Beyond that, checking the controller connection and cable is technician work.
Checked againstHaier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units), pp.30–31; Haier FlexFit 3U24MS2VHB Service Manual, Error Codes table (p.112); Haier 4U36MS2VHB Service Manual, Error Codes table (p.92)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E8 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means a communication error between the wired controller and the indoor unit on the EU mono residential/multi range, the US FlexFit multi-zone/console range (3U24MS2VHB) and the US FlexFit mid-static ducted range (4U36MS2VHB). E8 is NOT in M2 (US ductless split, p.43) and NOT in M7 (1U26MADFRA, p.25) — do not claim M2/M7 corroboration. Other Haier families use E8 differently: HDU E8 is a main-control-board communication fault, cabinet E8 is a control-panel ↔ indoor-board communication fault, and the old fixed-speed lamp table E8 is an indoor-motor overload. Check the model and series, not just the letters.

The ranking pages all get E8 wrong. acerrorcode (#1) gives E8 the identical 'Communication trouble between indoor and outdoor unit' row as E9 — that row is E7's fault, not E8's. decorexpro (#2) says E8 is 'overload of the electric motor of the indoor unit' — the old unverified lamp table. heatingnewsjournal (#3) says E8 is overload protection or a compressor fault — the same old-lamp-table error. northnjhvac (#4) merges comms with overload and never says what communicates with what, and invents the statistic that 'filter cleaning alone solves E8 in about 30% of cases'. homeapricot (#6) and howtodiscuss (#9) repeat the old lamp-table motor-overload meaning. Arlington has NO E8 section at all.

What the machine does when E8 fires

The indoor display shows E8 in place of the set temperature, and M3 gives outdoor error 08. The wired controller and the indoor unit have not communicated for more than 4 consecutive minutes, so the controller cannot control the indoor unit.

Common causes

  1. No communication between the wired controller and the indoor unit for more than 4 consecutive minutes (M3 Table A).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Recall the alarm via the procedure in your user manual — for the YR-E17 wired touchscreen controller, press the TIME key for 10 seconds (M3 pp.16–17). Alarms on wired-controller units do not go out spontaneously.
  2. If E8 returns, switch the unit off and call a technician to check the wired-controller ↔ indoor-unit connection and cable and the controller itself.
  3. Do not follow guides that tell you to clean filters, reset for 5–10 minutes or listen for the fan motor for E8 — those steps belong to other faults.

No E8-specific flowchart, reset time, resistance value or fix step exists in any manual read. The connection-and-cable check is the logical check for a communication fault, NOT a quoted manual step. Do not present E7's indoor↔outdoor wiring steps or E1's sensor steps as E8 steps.

When to call a technician

  • E8 returns after recalling the alarm — the wired-controller connection or the controller itself needs diagnosis.
  • Your unit has no wired controller but shows E8 — check the code again; a comms fault on a remote-only unit is E7 (indoor↔outdoor), not E8.
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

No owner repair exists in any manual for this code. Never open the indoor or outdoor unit while it is connected to power, and never attempt refrigerant, gas or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E8 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

E8 means a communication error between the wired controller and the indoor unit: the two have not communicated for more than 4 consecutive minutes (M3 Table A pp.30–31; FlexFit 3U24MS2VHB p.112; 4U36MS2VHB p.92).

Is E8 the same as E7?

No. E7 is the communication error between the indoor and outdoor units; E8 is between the wired controller and the indoor unit. The #1 ranking list page gives both codes the identical 'communication trouble between indoor and outdoor unit' row — that row is E7's fault, not E8's.

My split uses only the wireless remote — can it show E8?

E8 is a wired-controller fault, so it applies where a wired controller is fitted. If your unit has no wired controller, check the code again and the model: a comms fault on a remote-only unit is E7 (indoor↔outdoor), not E8.

Can I clear Haier E8 myself?

No fix step exists in any manual read. The Technical Book says that on wired-controller units, alarms do not go out spontaneously and must be recalled via the procedure in your user manual (for the YR-E17 touchscreen controller: press the TIME key for 10 seconds). Beyond that, checking the wired-controller connection and cable is technician work; do not follow guides that tell you to clean filters or check the fan motor for E8 — those steps belong to other faults.

Is E8 the same on a Haier HDU or cabinet air conditioner?

No. On an HDU unit E8 is a main-control-board communication fault, and on a cabinet 14kW unit it is a control-panel ↔ indoor-board communication fault. The old fixed-speed table's 'indoor motor overload' meaning is also different — and unverified. Check your unit type before using any E8 meaning.
