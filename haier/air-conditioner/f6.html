Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F6

Quick answer

Error codeF6 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF6 = outdoor ambient temperature sensor (probe) failure — the outdoor unit's ambient temperature probe is in short circuit or open circuit. The indoor display shows F6 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 12 times. M3 adds the detection window: the short/open circuit is detected 'within last 60 seconds'. This is the thinnest code in the batch: no manual gives a lockout rule for F6, and no manual gives a flowchart or a fix step — this page says so honestly and stays short rather than padding with invented diagnostics.
Most common causeThe outdoor ambient temperature probe is in short circuit or open circuit (M3: 'Temperature probe in short circuit or open circuit within last 60 seconds').
Safe to check yourself?No — no fix step exists in any manual read. Switch the unit off and call a technician to check or replace the outdoor ambient probe. Do not use the E1 (indoor sensor) fix for F6 — the sensors are in different units.

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F6 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means the outdoor ambient sensor on the US ductless split families, the EU mono/multi range and the AU/NZ R32 split families (M2/M3/M7, identical LED count of 12). The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) carries the same ambient-sensor wording under a different label, F12 — while cabinet F06 is a voltage fault. The old fixed-speed lamp table has no F6 at all (it runs F0-F4/FF). Check the model and series, not just the letters.

The ranking pages get F6 mostly right and everything around it wrong. acerrorcode gets F6 right in one row — but its F66 row duplicates F6's meaning under a code with no manual support, and its neighbouring rows (F2, F8, F9-F16, E3/E5/E8/E9, portable E1) are wrong. decorexpro gets F6 right — its only correct F-code — and everything around it wrong. Arlington merges F6 with the unverified F66 without saying so, and its only solution is 'Try the exact solutions as suggested for Error Code E1' — E1 is the INDOOR room sensor, a different sensor in a different unit. askwhiz, a new thin hub, has no F6 content at all and its FAQ E2 answer ('outdoor unit sensor fault') is wrong. F66 ('Ambient temperature sensor fault' on acerrorcode and Arlington) has NO support in any manual read — it is not a second ambient-sensor code. The other outdoor sensors have their own codes: F7 suction (LED1 11), F21 defrost (10), F25 delivery (13) — do not merge them with F6 (12).

What the machine does when F6 fires

The indoor display shows F6 in place of the set temperature, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 12 times. The outdoor board cannot read the ambient temperature, so it cannot run the outdoor unit correctly.

Common causes

  1. Outdoor ambient temperature probe in short circuit (M3).
  2. Outdoor ambient temperature probe in open circuit (M3).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Call a technician — the outdoor ambient probe is on the outdoor unit, and its check or replacement is technician work. No manual gives the procedure, a resistance value, or a plug check for F6, so this page does not invent one.

There is NO F6 flowchart and NO fix step in any manual: the full extent of the manual evidence is the M2 p.43 table row, the M3 Table A row-12 cause ('Temperature probe in short circuit or open circuit within last 60 seconds'), and the M7 p.25 row. M2's flowcharts (pp.44-50) cover E14, F8, F1, F22/F2/F23, F3, F19, F4, E7 and F11/F28 only. Do NOT follow guides that tell you to use the E1 (indoor sensor) fix for F6, and do not follow decorexpro's generic 'check the connection / replace faulty sensors' — those are the sites' own additions, not manual steps.

When to call a technician

  • F6 returns after a restart — the outdoor ambient probe needs checking or replacing.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 12 times — ambient sensor (F7 suction is 11, F21 defrost is 10, F25 delivery is 13).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

The outdoor ambient probe is on the outdoor unit: checking or replacing it is qualified-technician work. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F6 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F6 means the outdoor ambient temperature sensor (probe) has failed — the probe is in short circuit or open circuit. The indoor display shows F6 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 12 times (M2 p.43; M3 Table A p.28; M7 p.25).

How does the unit detect Haier F6?

The European Technical Book says the fault is a 'Temperature probe in short circuit or open circuit within last 60 seconds' — the outdoor board watches the ambient probe and raises F6 when it reads shorted or open.

Can I fix Haier F6 myself?

No fix step exists in any manual read. Unlike F1-F4, F6 has no diagnostic flowchart at all — the manuals give only the table row. Do not follow guides that tell you to use the E1 (indoor sensor) fix for F6: the sensors are in different units. Switch the unit off and call a technician to check or replace the outdoor ambient probe.

Is F6 the same as F66?

No — F66 does not exist in any Haier manual read. Two list pages (acerrorcode, Arlington) print F66 with the same 'ambient temperature sensor' meaning as F6, but no manual supports it. If your unit shows F6, use the F6 meaning; ignore F66.

Is F6 the same on a Haier cabinet air conditioner?

No. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) has no code F6: the same ambient-sensor wording appears there under F12, and cabinet F06 is a voltage fault ('High DC voltage or AC voltage'). Check whether your unit is a split or a cabinet before using any F-code meaning. Also do not confuse F6 (outdoor ambient, 12 flashes) with F7 (suction, 11), F21 (defrost, 10) or F25 (delivery, 13).
