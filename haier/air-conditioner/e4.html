Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code E4

Quick answer

Error codeE4 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansE4 = indoor EEPROM error — EEPROM data error, defective EEPROM, or defective indoor PCB (M1). M3: 'EEPROM faulty indoor unit board'. M4's entire E4 instruction is one line: 'Replace the PCB of indoor unit'. Split E4 carries no LED1 flash count — it shows on the indoor display. No manual gives an owner fix, reset, or re-programming step for E4 — this page says so honestly and stays short rather than padding with invented cures.
Most common causeEEPROM data error, a defective EEPROM, or a defective indoor PCB (M1's own three causes).
Safe to check yourself?No — no manual gives an owner fix for E4. Try one power-cycle (M1); if E4 returns, the indoor PCB must be replaced by a technician.
Checked againstHaier Split Type Room Air Conditioner Operation Manual (models AS25/35/50/68/71...HRA — Pearl / Pearl Premium / Pearl Nordic / Revive Plus / Expert Nordic, R32), Troubleshooting Codes table; Haier Ductless Split Air Conditioner Service Manual (US models AW09/12/18/24ES2VHA + 1U09/12/18/24ES2VHA, R410A), Error Codes & Problem Solving (p.43) (p.43); Haier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units) (Table A pp.28-29); Haier HSU09XHK / HSU12XHK Service Manual (Order No. AC1205S001V0), §7.2 (§7.2 p.33)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E4 is a split-wall/ductless code — it means the INDOOR EEPROM on the EU wall-split R32 families, the US ductless split families, the EU mono/multi range and the HSU EK-Series ON/OFF wall units (M1/M2/M3/M4, identical meaning). On portable QPFA/QPCA models E4 is anti-frozen protection instead, and on HDU models E4 is an outdoor pipe temperature sensor fault — same letters, different faults. On cabinet 14kW units E4 has the same meaning as split. On the HSU-22HV03 family the indoor EEPROM fault is E18, not E4. Check the model and series, not just the letters.

The ranking pages get E4 wrong by treating a board fault as a compressor or airflow problem. appliancepartspros says E4 is 'compressor overload' and treats it as an airflow problem. decorexpro says E4 is 'compressor overheating' — the old lamp-table meaning. heatingnewsjournal says E4 is a 'low-pressure or suction pressure sensor problem' — the suction sensor is F7. smartacsolutions gives E4 as portable anti-freeze (2017+) / tank float (2016-), both presented unscoped; neither is split E4. acerrorcode's main-table E4 is garbled ('EEPROM wrong — being electrified to test E2 check sum') and its portable-table E4 is tank float — wrong (portable E4 = anti-frozen protection; tank float is FL). Arlington gets the meaning right but invents the cures — 'power-cycle clears a minor EEPROM glitch', 'energise it multiple times', 're-upload the instructions program into the EEPROM chip' — none of these appears in any manual read; M4 says replace the PCB. F12 is the OUTDOOR EEPROM error ('EEPROM outdoor unit motherboard faulty', M3 Table A; M2 gives F12 LED1 1 flash) — E4 (indoor) and F12 (outdoor) must never be merged; Arlington merges them into one 'E12, F12' section, and E12 does not exist in the split indoor tables at all.

What the machine does when E4 fires

The indoor display shows E4 in place of the set temperature. The fault is in the indoor board's memory, not in a sensor or the compressor, so no reset or sensor check clears it.

Common causes

  1. EEPROM data error (M1).
  2. Defective EEPROM (M1).
  3. Defective indoor PCB (M1).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Try one power-cycle: turn the unit off, wait 30 seconds, then disconnect from power; reconnect and wait out the 3-minute restart protection (M1).
  2. If E4 returns, call a technician — M4's only instruction is: 'E4: Indoor EEPROM error: Replace the PCB of indoor unit'.

No manual in the set gives any owner fix, reset, or re-programming step for E4. Do NOT repeat Arlington's invented cures — no 'energise multiple times', no 'reset settings to default', no 're-upload the instructions program into the EEPROM chip', and no 'power-cycle clears a minor EEPROM glitch' exists in these manuals. E4 is the thinnest code in this batch for owner action: one power-cycle, then a technician replaces the indoor PCB.

When to call a technician

  • E4 returns after one power-cycle — the indoor PCB must be replaced by a technician.
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

Indoor PCB replacement is qualified-technician work: the board is live mains electronics. Never open the indoor unit while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E4 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

E4 means the indoor unit's EEPROM has an error: EEPROM data error, defective EEPROM, or defective indoor PCB (M1). M3: 'EEPROM faulty indoor unit board'.

Can I fix Haier split E4 with a reset?

No manual says so. Try one power-cycle (off, wait 30 seconds, disconnect, reconnect, wait out the 3-minute restart protection — M1). If E4 returns, M4's only instruction is 'Replace the PCB of indoor unit' — a technician job. Blogs claiming a power-cycle clears a 'minor EEPROM glitch' or that a technician can 're-upload' the EEPROM program are inventing steps no Haier manual gives.

Is Haier split E4 the same as portable E4?

No. On split-wall units E4 is an indoor EEPROM (board) error; on portable QPFA/QPCA models E4 is anti-frozen protection. Same letters, different fault — check the model/series.

Is E4 a compressor problem?

No. Some blogs (appliancepartspros, decorexpro, heatingnewsjournal) say E4 means compressor overload, overheating, or low pressure, but every split manual read defines E4 as an indoor EEPROM error. Compressor overheating is the old fixed-speed lamp table's E4, not the split manuals'.

What is the difference between E4 and F12 on a Haier split AC?

Both are EEPROM errors on the same platform, on different boards: E4 is the INDOOR unit EEPROM (M1/M2/M3A/M4); F12 is the OUTDOOR unit EEPROM ('EEPROM outdoor unit motherboard faulty', M3A; M2 gives F12 LED1 1 flash). Do not merge them — one Arlington section merges E4's neighbour codes into a single 'E12, F12' entry, and E12 does not exist in the split indoor tables.
