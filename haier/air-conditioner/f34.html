Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F34

Quick answer

Error codeF34 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF34 = outdoor exchanger (coil / pipe) temperature protection — the outdoor unit's heat-exchanger temperature has tripped its protection, caused by heat-exchange problems or a temperature-probe failure. The outdoor error number is 37 (M7: outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 37 times). M7 marks F34 a "Trouble record" rather than "Directly display", and FlexFit adds that the protection applies in cooling mode. The three wordings (exchanger / coil / pipe) describe the same outdoor coil/pipe temperature protection, not three faults.
Most common causeHeat exchange problems / temperature probe failure (M3 Table A p.31).
Safe to check yourself?No — no fix step exists in any manual read. Switch the unit off and call a technician; checking the outdoor exchanger probe or heat exchange is technician work on the outdoor unit.
Checked againstHaier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units) (Table A p.31); Haier 1U26MADFRA Room Air Conditioner Installation Manual (Sept 2024, R32, AU/NZ), Outdoor Unit Troubleshooting (p.25) (p.25); Haier FlexFit Series Multi Zone Split System Service Manual, 3U24MS2VHB, Console Technical Overview (p.112)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F34 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means outdoor exchanger temperature protection on the EU mono/multi range, the AU/NZ R32 split families and the US FlexFit multi-zone/console families (M3/M7/FlexFit, identical outdoor error number 37). The US ductless split manual (M2) does NOT list F34 at all — its 22-code table has no F34 row. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) has no F34. The old fixed-speed lamp table has no F34. Check the model and series, not just the letters.

Ducted numeric 37 is NOT F34: in the 1U105/4U36 numeric tables, 37 = "Compressor overcurrent detected by the driver module/IPM" — never quote a numeric-37 row as an F34 meaning. F35 is the power module temperature SENSOR failure (38) — a sensor fault, not a protection; F36 is the piping temperature probe "TC" fault (39) — also a sensor fault. F34 is a temperature PROTECTION on the outdoor exchanger. E9 is indoor piping too high (a different, indoor protection).

What the machine does when F34 fires

The indoor display shows F34 (where directly displayed; M7 marks it a "Trouble record" rather than "Directly display"), and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 37 times. The outdoor unit's heat-exchanger (coil/pipe) temperature protection has tripped, caused by heat-exchange problems or a temperature-probe failure.

Common causes

  1. Heat exchange problems (M3 Table A p.31).
  2. Temperature probe failure (M3 Table A p.31).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Call a technician — checking the outdoor exchanger probe or heat exchange is technician work on the outdoor unit. No manual gives a procedure, a temperature threshold, or a coil-cleaning step for F34, so this page does not invent one.

There is NO F34 flowchart and NO fix step in any manual: the full extent of the manual evidence is the M3 Table A row-37 cause ("Heat exchange problems / temperature probe failure"), the M7 p.25 row ("Outdoor coil protection", LED1 37, Trouble record) and the FlexFit p.112 row ("Outdoor pipe temperature protection in cooling mode"). Do NOT present other codes' steps (e.g. F36's sensor cause, or F41's 65°C threshold) as F34 steps — the codes are different. Do NOT present ducted numeric-37's overcurrent diagnosis as F34's. F34 is a one-row code in every manual: keep it short, do not pad with invented diagnostics.

When to call a technician

  • F34 returns after a restart — the outdoor heat exchange and the exchanger temperature probe need checking.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 37 times — outdoor exchanger protection (F35 is 38, F36 is 39).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

The outdoor exchanger probe is on the outdoor unit: checking it is qualified-technician work. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F34 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F34 means the outdoor exchanger temperature protection has tripped — the outdoor unit's heat-exchanger (coil/pipe) temperature protection is active, caused by heat-exchange problems or a temperature-probe failure. The outdoor error number is 37, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 37 times (M3 Table A p.31; M7 p.25).

Is F34 the same as F36?

No. F34 is a temperature PROTECTION on the outdoor exchanger. F36 is a SENSOR fault — the piping temperature probe "TC" has failed (disconnected, broken, or poorly positioned). Same outdoor unit, different faults, different codes and flash counts (37 vs 39).

Can I fix Haier F34 myself?

No fix step exists in any manual read. The manuals give only the table row plus M3's short cause ("Heat exchange problems / temperature probe failure"). Switch the unit off and call a technician to check the outdoor heat exchange and the exchanger temperature probe.

Why does my manual not show F34?

The US ductless split service manual (M2) does not list F34 at all — its 22-code table has no F34 row. F34 is listed in the European Technical Book (M3), the AU/NZ installation manual (M7) and the FlexFit multi-zone service manual. If your unit shows F34, use the M3/M7 meaning above; do not go looking for it in M2.

My ducted Haier manual says 37 means compressor overcurrent — is that F34?

No. In the ducted/1U105 numeric tables, outdoor error 37 is "Compressor overcurrent detected by compressor driver module" — a different fault with the same number in a different numbering system. On a split-wall unit, F34 (outdoor error 37) is the outdoor exchanger temperature protection. Always match the code to your unit type, not just the number.
