Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F12

Quick answer

Error codeF12 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF12 = outdoor EEPROM error — the EEPROM on the outdoor unit's motherboard is faulty (its data is in error or the chip is damaged). The indoor display shows F12 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 1 time. No manual gives a lockout rule for F12 — the 'alarm goes out 3 times in an hour' text in M3 Table A belongs to the F1 row, not F12.
Most common causeEEPROM data error or a damaged EEPROM on the outdoor unit motherboard (M3: 'EEPROM outdoor unit motherboard faulty'; AS24 §10.3.2: 'When the data of EEPROM is error or the EEPROM is damaged').
Safe to check yourself?No — no fix step exists in any manual read. Switch the unit off and call a technician; EEPROM diagnosis and board work are technician work. Do not replace a fuse or 're-upload the program' yourself.
Checked againstHaier Ductless Split Air Conditioner Service Manual (US models AW09/12/18/24ES2VHA + 1U09/12/18/24ES2VHA, R410A), Error Codes & Problem Solving (p.43) (p.43); Haier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units) (Table A pp.28-31); Haier 1U26MADFRA Room Air Conditioner Installation Manual (Sept 2024, R32, AU/NZ), Outdoor Unit Troubleshooting (p.25) (p.25); Haier AS24TD2HRA Service Manual §10.3.2

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F12 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means the OUTDOOR EEPROM on the US ductless split families, the EU mono/multi range, the AU/NZ R32 split families and the AS24 wall-split families (M2/M3/M7, identical LED count of 1). The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) carries the same ambient-sensor wording under F12 — the sharpest trap for this code — while cabinet F06 is a voltage fault. The old fixed-speed lamp table has no F12 at all. Check the model and series, not just the letters — and do not confuse the outdoor EEPROM (F12) with the indoor EEPROM (E4).

The ranking pages get F12 mostly right and everything around it wrong. acerrorcode gets F12 right in one row ('Outdoor EEPROM fault') but gives no LED count and no E4 distinction — and its F11 row steals F12's fault ('Outdoor PCB EEPROM abnormal' — F11 is loss of synchronism, LED1 18/19). decorexpro says F12 is a 'short circuit in the headboard' with the fix 'replace the blown fuse' — wrong on meaning and fix: F12 is an outdoor EEPROM data/chip fault, and no fuse is involved. Arlington gets the meaning and LED1 1 right but merges F12 with non-existent E12 in one heading — E12 does not exist in M2 p.43 or M3 Table A — and its only solution is 'Try the exact solutions as suggested for Error Code E4' — E4 is the INDOOR EEPROM, a different chip on a different board, and its steps (including 're-upload the instructions program') are not manual F12 steps.

What the machine does when F12 fires

The indoor display shows F12 in place of the set temperature, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 1 time. The outdoor board's EEPROM data is in error or the chip is damaged, so the outdoor unit cannot run.

Common causes

  1. EEPROM data error (AS24 §10.3.2).
  2. EEPROM damaged (AS24 §10.3.2).
  3. EEPROM outdoor unit motherboard faulty (M3).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Call a technician — the outdoor EEPROM is on the outdoor unit's motherboard, and its diagnosis and board work are technician work. No manual gives the procedure, a reset step, or a board-replacement procedure for F12, so this page does not invent one.

There is NO F12 flowchart and NO fix step in any manual: the full extent of the manual evidence is the M2 p.43 table row, the M3 Table A row-1 cause ('EEPROM outdoor unit motherboard faulty'), the M7 p.25 row and the AS24 §10.3.2 detection method. M2's flowcharts (pp.44-50) cover E14, F8, F1, F22/F2/F23, F3, F19, F4, E7 and F11/F28 only. Do NOT follow guides that tell you to replace a fuse (decorexpro's invention for a different fault) or to 're-upload the program' yourself (Arlington's E4 cross-reference — company-engineer work). Do NOT present E4's indoor-EEPROM steps as F12 steps — the chips are on different boards in different units. F12 is a one-row code in every manual: keep it short, do not pad with invented diagnostics.

When to call a technician

  • F12 returns after a restart — the outdoor EEPROM or motherboard needs diagnosis by a technician.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 1 time — outdoor EEPROM (E4, the indoor EEPROM, has no LED count).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

The outdoor EEPROM is on the outdoor unit's motherboard: EEPROM diagnosis and board work are qualified-technician work. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F12 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F12 means the outdoor EEPROM has failed — the EEPROM chip on the outdoor unit's motherboard is faulty, its data is in error or the chip is damaged. The indoor display shows F12 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 1 time (M2 p.43; M3 Table A pp.28-31; M7 p.25).

Is F12 the same as E4?

No. E4 is the INDOOR EEPROM error and F12 is the OUTDOOR EEPROM error — different chips on different boards in different units. Both appear in the same manual table, which is why guides mix them up. Check whether your display shows E4 or F12 before following any fix.

Can I fix Haier F12 myself?

No fix step exists in any manual read. F12 has no diagnostic flowchart at all — the manuals give only the table row. Do not follow guides that tell you to replace a fuse (that is decorexpro's invention for a different fault) or to 're-upload the program' yourself (that is Arlington's E4 cross-reference, and it is company-engineer work). Switch the unit off and call a technician; EEPROM and board work is technician work.

Is F12 the same as F11?

No. F11 is loss of compressor synchronism (LED1 18 or 19 flashes) — a compressor-drive fault, not an EEPROM fault. One list page (acerrorcode) wrongly calls F11 'Outdoor PCB EEPROM abnormal'; that is F12's fault, not F11's.

Is F12 the same on a Haier cabinet air conditioner?

No. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) has no outdoor-EEPROM code F12: the same ambient-sensor wording appears there under F12 ('Outdoor unit ambient temperature probe (Ta) faulty'), and cabinet F06 is a voltage fault ('High DC voltage or AC voltage'). Check whether your unit is a split or a cabinet before using any F-code meaning.
