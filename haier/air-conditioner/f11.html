Haier Split Air Conditioner Error Code F11

Quick answer

Error codeF11 — Haier Split Air Conditioner
What it meansF11 = loss of compressor synchronism detection — the outdoor board cannot detect the position of the compressor rotor normally; the compressor has deviated from normal (synchronous) operation. The indoor display shows F11 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 18 times. M7 words it differently ('Compressor jam (only for spdu)') — same code, same 18 flashes, different wording, noted honestly below.
Most common causeCompressor wiring wrong or the connection poor, or the compressor damaged — the only two decision conditions in the M2 p.50 / §10.4.11 flowchart.
Safe to check yourself?No — there is no owner fix in any manual. Switch the unit off and call a technician; the flowchart's checks are compressor wiring and compressor diagnosis on the outdoor unit.
Checked againstHaier Ductless Split Air Conditioner Service Manual (US models AW09/12/18/24ES2VHA + 1U09/12/18/24ES2VHA, R410A), Error Codes & Problem Solving (p.43) (p.43 + p.50 flowchart); Haier European HVAC Technical Book 2024-25, DIAGNOSTIC Table A (Mono Residential and Multi Units) (Table A pp.28-31); Haier 1U26MADFRA Room Air Conditioner Installation Manual (Sept 2024, R32, AU/NZ), Outdoor Unit Troubleshooting (p.25) (p.25); Haier AS35SBBHRA-MW Service Manual (Version V1, 2024-11-30) §10.4.11 p.48

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because F11 is a split-wall/ductless Table A code — it means loss of compressor synchronism on the US ductless split families, the EU mono/multi range, the AU/NZ R32 split families and the AS35/AS50 wall-split families (M2/M3/M7, identical LED count of 18). The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) carries a COMPLETELY DIFFERENT meaning under the SAME code F11 — 'compressor intake (suction) temperature probe faulty', i.e. what Table A F7 means. The old fixed-speed lamp table has no F11 at all. Check the model and series, not just the letters.

The ranking pages get F11 wrong in three ways. acerrorcode, the #1 list page, says F11 is 'Outdoor PCB EEPROM abnormal' — that is F12 (LED1 1); it assigns the EEPROM meaning to two codes and the synchronism meaning to none. decorexpro gives F11 two flash counts, 6 and 19, and two meanings, mains voltage and control board — both wrong (6 flashes is F19, 18 is F11, 19 is F28). Arlington gets the flash count right but invents the causes (tripped breakers, blown fuses) — neither appears in the M2 p.50 flowchart, whose decision conditions are compressor wiring wrong/poor connection, or compressor damaged — and its solutions (power-cycle 1-3 minutes, check breakers, replace fuses) are invented. F28 shares F11's flowchart group (18/19 flashes) but has its own Table A meaning in M3 ('compressor overcurrent at fixed frequency') and its own wording in M7 ('Module PWM select circuit error') — do not claim F28 = loss of synchronism as its sole meaning.

What the machine does when F11 fires

The indoor display shows F11 in place of the set temperature, and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 18 times. The outdoor board cannot detect the compressor rotor position normally, so the compressor cannot run in synchronism.

Common causes

  1. Compressor wiring wrong or the connection poor (M2 p.50 / AS35 §10.4.11).
  2. Compressor damaged (M2 p.50 / AS35 §10.4.11).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off.
  2. Call a technician — there is no owner fix in any manual for F11.
  3. The technician checks the compressor wiring and connections: wrong wiring or a poor connection is one of the flowchart's two decision conditions (M2 p.50; AS35 §10.4.11).
  4. If the wiring is sound, the compressor itself is damaged and must be diagnosed by the technician (M2 p.50; AS35 §10.4.11).

No power-cycle reset time, no breaker check and no fuse check exists in the manuals for F11 — Arlington's breaker/fuse causes and fixes are invented; tripped breakers and blown fuses appear nowhere in the M2 p.50 / §10.4.11 flowchart. Do NOT present decorexpro's stabilizer or board-diagnostics advice — it belongs to the wrong codes (F19/F28) entirely. The flowchart is a technician procedure, not a homeowner repair.

When to call a technician

  • F11 returns after a restart — the compressor wiring or the compressor itself needs diagnosis.
  • The outdoor LED1 flashes 18 times — loss of synchronism (F28 is 19, F19 is 6).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

F11 diagnosis involves the compressor wiring and the outdoor unit's drive electronics: that is qualified-technician work. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F11 mean on a Haier split air conditioner?

F11 means loss of compressor synchronism detection — the outdoor board cannot detect the position of the compressor rotor normally, so the compressor has deviated from normal (synchronous) operation. The indoor display shows F11 and the outdoor PCB LED1 flashes 18 times (M2 p.43 + flowchart p.50; M3 Table A pp.28-31).

Is F11 an EEPROM fault?

No. The #1 list page (acerrorcode) says F11 is 'Outdoor PCB EEPROM abnormal' — that is wrong; the outdoor EEPROM fault is F12 (LED1 1 flash). F11 is a compressor synchronism fault.

Is F11 a mains voltage fault?

No. One guide (decorexpro) says 'F11 flashing 6 times = mains voltage abnormal' — that is wrong twice over: 6 flashes is F19 (power over/under voltage), and F11 flashes 18 times. Do not buy a voltage stabilizer for F11.

What is the difference between F11 and F28?

Honestly, the manuals disagree. M2's flowchart (p.50) groups LED1 18 (F11) and 19 (F28) under one heading, 'Loss of synchronism detection', because both mean the rotor position cannot be detected normally. But M3's Table A gives F28 its own meaning (compressor overcurrent at fixed frequency), and M7 calls F28 a 'Module PWM select circuit error'. Treat F11 as loss of synchronism; do not claim F28 means the same thing.

Is F11 the same on a Haier cabinet air conditioner?

No — this is the sharpest trap for this code. The cabinet 14kW table (M3 Table B) uses the same code F11 for a completely different fault: 'Compressor intake (suction) temperature probe faulty' — a sensor fault, i.e. what Table A F7 means. On a split, F11 is a compressor synchronism fault; on a cabinet, F11 is a sensor fault. Check whether your unit is a split or a cabinet before using any F-code meaning. Also do not confuse F11 (loss of synchronism, 18) with F27 (compressor stall / locked rotor, 7).
