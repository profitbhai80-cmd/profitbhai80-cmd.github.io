Haier HDU Air Conditioner Error Code E5

Quick answer

Error codeE5 — Haier HDU Air Conditioner
What it meansE5 = overload current / over-current protection — the board's current-transformer (CT) check has detected abnormal current, or the power supply is abnormal. The protection trips when the CT check is abnormal 3 times in 30 minutes (M6 p.16; M8 No. 0150506681 pp.15-16). HDU ONLY: on a split-wall unit E5 is indoor ice protection. E5 is a CURRENT fault, not a pressure fault.
Most common causeThe CT (current transformer) check reads abnormal 3 times in 30 minutes, or the power supply is abnormal. The manuals give no owner-level cause list beyond those two branches.
Safe to check yourself?No — there is no DIY fix for E5 in any HDU manual. Stop the operation and contact your sales dealer. Current and power-supply diagnosis involves live voltages and is technician work.
Checked againstHaier HDU-42H03/H Air Conditioner Instruction Manual (Manualshelf), p.16, §'When the CHECK lamp (red) flickers'; Haier HDU-42CF03/H + HDU-42HF03/H Duct Type Air Conditioner Indoor Unit Operation & Installation Manual, No. 0010576889, Malfunction table p.16; Haier HDU-09HF03 / HDU-12CF03 / HDU-18CF03 / HDU-18HF03 / HDU-28CF03 / HDU-28HF03 Duct Type Air Conditioner Indoor Unit Operation & Installation Manual, No. 0150506681, §'Trouble display', pp.15-16

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E5 is a CURRENT fault (too many amps) — it means overload current on the HDU-42H03/H, HDU-42CF03/H, HDU-42HF03/H and HDU-09/12/18/28HF03/CF03 ceiling/ducted families (M6 p.16; M7 No. 0010576889 p.16; M8 No. 0150506681 pp.15-16). E6 is a PRESSURE fault (pressure switch tripped) — different protection, different code. The split Table A E5 is 'Indoor unit ice protection', which is a temperature fault, not a current fault. Do not confuse E5 with the outdoor overcurrent codes F2/F22/F23 (compressor overcurrent, split outdoor table) — those are different codes on different boards. Do not confuse with Gree E5 — the site's own Gree page says Gree E5 means AC over-current protection (supply voltage unstable or too low, system load too high, indoor coil blocked or dirty); that is a different brand's code, and its cause list is Gree's, not Haier HDU's.

The ranking pages give HDU E5 no detail. acerrorcode's E5 row 'Over current protect limit' is RIGHT for HDU — but the table is framed as 'split-type and fixed-speed' codes, and in split Table A E5 = indoor ice protection. So the row misleads split owners while accidentally helping HDU owners, with no series split anywhere on the page, and no cause, no CT rule, no CHECK lamp, no flash count. decorexpro says 'Error E5 appears on the display when the compressor is switched off due to excessive load' — right in substance, wrong in causation: its causes (dirty radiator, excess refrigerant, failed outdoor fan, clogged capillary, clogged filter) are a chain built on its own WRONG E4 section (HDU E4 is the outdoor pipe temperature sensor, not compressor overheating). No CT check, no 3-in-30-minutes rule, no power-supply branch. Arlington has NO E5 section anywhere — its nearest content is an unlabelled section headed only 'LED1 flash blinks 21 times' that defers to F22 solutions, and F22 is a split outdoor overcurrent code, not an HDU E5 fix — and it calls itself 'Official Haier', which it is not.

What the machine does when E5 fires

The compressor is switched off on overload: the board's current-transformer (CT) check has detected abnormal current 3 times in 30 minutes, or the power supply is abnormal. The CHECK lamp (red) flickers and the wired controller shows E5; the remote-type flash count is 5 (M8 pp.15-16).

Common causes

  1. CT check abnormal 3 times in 30 minutes (M8 pp.15-16).
  2. Power supply abnormal (M8 pp.15-16).
  3. The manuals give no owner-level cause list beyond those two branches — dirty filters and blocked airflow can make a compressor work harder, but no HDU manual lists them as E5 causes, so this page does not present them as the manual's causes.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Stop the operation of the air conditioner and contact your sales dealer (M6 p.15-16; M8 p.17, verbatim instruction).
  2. Pre-service-call checks (M6 p.15 / M8 p.17, the general troubleshooting list — NOT E5-specific): 'Is the power source switch adjust cut in? Is city supply power in normal? ... Is the thermostat adjust as required? Isn't the air filter dirty? Isn't any doors or windows left open? Doesn't any obstacle exist at the air inlet or outlet?' These are the only owner checks the manual authorises before a service call.
  3. Note the manual's timing facts: the unit 'has a function of automatic restart system after recovering power stoppage' (M6 p.16), and cooling/heating 'is not operable for three minutes after the conditioner is stopped. Because the protecting circuit is activated' (M8 p.17) — do not mistake the 3-minute delay for a fault.

There are NO per-code reset or DIY fix steps for E5 in any HDU manual. The page must say so plainly. Do NOT present any competitor's E5 causes or fixes as manual steps: decorexpro's causal chain (dirty radiator, excess refrigerant, failed outdoor fan, clogged capillary tube, clogged indoor filter) is borrowed from its own WRONG E4 section — none of it is in any HDU manual. Arlington's nearest content defers to F22 solutions ('try the exact solutions as suggested for Error Code F22') under an unlabelled heading — F22 is a split outdoor overcurrent code, not an HDU E5 fix. acerrorcode gives no fix at all. Current/power-supply diagnosis is technician work involving live voltages; the manuals say all electrical repairs must be carried out by qualified electricians.

When to call a technician

  • E5 returns — the current transformer, compressor or power supply needs diagnosis by a dealer/technician.
  • The compressor switches off repeatedly on overload.
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

Current and power-supply diagnosis involves live voltages and is technician work — the manuals say all electrical repairs must be carried out by qualified electricians. Never open the outdoor unit while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E5 mean on a Haier HDU air conditioner?

E5 means overload current / over-current protection — the board's current-transformer (CT) check has detected abnormal current, or the power supply is abnormal. The protection trips when the CT check is abnormal 3 times in 30 minutes, and the compressor is switched off on overload. The red CHECK lamp flickers and the wired controller shows E5 (M6 p.16; M8 No. 0150506681 pp.15-16).

Is E5 an ice or freezing fault?

No — not on an HDU unit. On Haier split-wall units E5 means indoor ice protection (the indoor exchanger is too cold). On HDU duct/ceiling units E5 means over-current. Same code letters, different series, different meaning. Check whether your unit is an HDU duct/ceiling unit before using any E5 meaning.

What makes E5 trip?

Per the manual, two branches: the CT (current transformer) check reads abnormal 3 times in 30 minutes, or the power supply is abnormal. The manuals give no owner-level cause list beyond that — dirty filters and blocked airflow can make a compressor work harder, but no HDU manual lists them as E5 causes, so this page does not present them as the manual's causes.

Can I fix Haier HDU E5 myself?

The manuals give no DIY fix for E5. They instruct: stop the operation and contact your sales dealer. Current and power-supply diagnosis involves live voltages and is technician work — the manuals say all electrical repairs must be carried out by qualified electricians. Do not follow the F22-based steps some pages suggest for E5 — F22 is a different code from the split outdoor table.

How is E5 different from E6?

E5 is a CURRENT fault (too many amps, detected by the CT); E6 is a PRESSURE fault (a pressure switch has tripped). Different protections, different causes, different fixes — and on HDU units E6 additionally splits by model family (low-pressure switch on most models, high-pressure switch on HDU-14/18CL03/M). See the HDU E6 page.
