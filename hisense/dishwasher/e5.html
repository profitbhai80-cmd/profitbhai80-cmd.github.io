Hisense Dishwasher Error Code E5

Quick answer

Error codeE5 — Hisense Dishwasher
What it meansE5 means an overflow/leakage fault: the manual's cause column says 'Overflow occurs / Water leakage occurs.' When the system detects an overflow it shuts off the circulation pump and turns on the drain pump. On the H15DSS and HV60340UK families this is a documented code; on HSCE14FS/HSCE14FB there is no E5 — the equivalent overflow code there is E4.
Most common causeWater has overflowed in the tub or leaked into the base pan — an overfill event or a small leak the overflow system detected.
Safe to check yourself?Partially. You can safely do the manual's own steps: turn off the main water supply, switch off and unplug the machine, remove the water from the base pan, and visually check that the drain hose is correctly fitted and the inlet hose is not kinked or squashed. Finding and fixing an internal leak, the inlet valve, or the flood switch itself is technician work.
Checked againstHisense H15DSS Dishwasher User Manual (official PDF via hisense.co.za, 190520 DW-0359-450, 15 place settings); Hisense HV60340UK User's Operation Manual (manualslib, page 11); Hisense HSCE14FS/HSCE14FB Instruction Manual (manualspro transcription; confirms E5 does not exist on that family)

Applies to — read this first

Good news for diagnosis: on the H15DSS and HV60340UK families E5 is consistent — it is always the overflow/leakage fault ('Overflow occurs / Water leakage occurs'). What you must NOT do is borrow meanings from other Hisense dishwasher families or from third-party code lists: on HSCE14FS/HSCE14FB there is no E5 at all (the overflow fault there is E4), and 'door open' is E1 in these manuals — E5 is never a door or motor fault.

If your manual's table looks like this one, do not follow this page — look up E4 instead.

E5 and E6 sit in the same fault family and share the manual's overflow WARNING, but the manuals keep them as separate rows: E5 = 'Overflow/leakage' (possible causes: 'Overflow occurs/Water leakage occurs'), E6 = 'Water leakage' (possible cause: 'Water leakage occurs'). The wording suggests E5 additionally covers an overfill event while E6 is leakage-only, but the manuals never spell the difference out — treat both the same way.

What the machine does when E5 fires

When the system detects an overflow, the manual states: 'it shuts off the circulation pump and turns on the drain pump.' Expect the display to show E5 while the drain pump runs to clear the tub — this is the machine's protection response, not a second fault. The cycle will not continue normally until the overflow condition is resolved.

Common causes

  1. The manual's cause column: 'Overflow occurs / Water leakage occurs' — the overflow system has detected water where it should not be, in the tub beyond the fill level or in the base pan.
  2. An overfill event: too much water entered the machine (for example a faulty inlet valve letting water in when it shouldn't), tripping the overflow detection.
  3. A small leak into the base pan — from a door seal, a hose connection, or the sump — which the base float/flood sensor picks up.
  4. A drain hose that is not correctly fitted. The manual's installation section warns: 'The drain hose must be correctly fitted to avoid water leaks.'
  5. Oversudsing that leads to overflowing. The manual's own 'Suds in the tub' row notes that spilled rinse aid 'could cause oversudsing and lead to overflowing' — wrong detergent or rinse-aid spills can trip an overflow fault.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Turn off the main water supply to the dishwasher — this is the manual's own first instruction for an overflow event. Switch the machine off and unplug it from the wall socket.
  2. Remove the water from the base pan. The manual says it 'should be removed before restarting the dishwasher' — get access under the tub (you may need to remove the bottom plinth panel), and soak or drain the pooled water out completely. This is what clears the flood sensor.
  3. While you have access, visually check the hoses the manual names: the drain hose must be 'correctly fitted to avoid water leaks' (waste connection 50–100 cm from the floor, fixed with a hose clip), and the water inlet hose must not be kinked or squashed.
  4. Check for the overfill causes the manual documents: spilled rinse aid or wrong detergent causing oversudsing (the manual's own 'Suds in the tub' row links oversudsing to overflowing), and visible drips from the door seal or hose connections.
  5. Plug back in, restore the water supply, and run a short empty cycle. If E5 returns immediately, the overfill source or leak is still active — stop there and call a qualified technician; the manual's next step is 'calling a service.'

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. The manual gives no electrical or internal-repair steps for E5 — if a check needs a part opened up, a valve tested, or a sensor replaced, it is no longer a safe home check. Skip to the technician section.

When to call a technician

  • E5 returns right after you removed the base-pan water and restarted — the leak or overfill source is still active (likely a stuck inlet valve or an internal hose/connection leak).
  • You can see water coming from inside the cabinet, around the door seal, or under the machine during the fill — an active leak needs professional diagnosis.
  • The fault appeared after no visible spill or overfill, and the base pan keeps refilling — the flood switch itself or the inlet valve may be faulty, both internal parts.
  • Burning smells, tripped breakers, or water near the power cord — switch off at the wall and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

Never run the dishwasher with water sitting in the base pan — remove it before restarting, exactly as the manual instructs. Turn off the main water supply before calling a service, and keep the machine unplugged while you access the base area. Water plus mains electrics is the risk here: if you cannot see where the leak is coming from, or the fault returns immediately, stop and call a qualified technician rather than repeatedly restarting.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E5 mean on a Hisense dishwasher?

E5 means an overflow/leakage fault. Both the Hisense H15DSS manual and the HV60340UK manual define it the same way: 'E5 Overflow/leakage — Overflow occurs / Water leakage occurs.' When the system detects an overflow it shuts off the circulation pump and turns on the drain pump, per the manual's 'Before Calling for Service' section.

How do I reset Hisense dishwasher E5?

The manuals give no button reset for E5 — the manual's own overflow instruction is the procedure: turn off the main water supply, then remove the water from the base pan (unplug the machine, get access under the tub, and soak or drain it out) before restarting the dishwasher. If E5 returns right after that, the leak or overfill source is still active and it is a technician job.

Is Hisense dishwasher E5 the same as E4?

Not on the families this page covers: on H15DSS and HV60340UK, E5 is overflow/leakage while E4 is the temperature sensor. But watch your model: on HSCE14FS/HSCE14FB there is no E5 at all — the overflow fault there is E4 ('Overflow. | Some element of dishwasher leaks.'). Check your own manual's error table before following any code guide.

Some sites say Hisense E5 is a door or motor fault. Is that right?

No. Both manufacturer manuals define E5 strictly as overflow/leakage. The dishwasherusa site publishes two pages that contradict each other — one says E5 is a 'Motor Fault', the other says E5 is 'Door Not Closed Properly' — and 'door open' is already E1 in the manuals themselves. Neither meaning appears in any Hisense dishwasher manual, so both were invented.

What's the difference between E5 and E6?

The manuals list both codes but explain them only through the table rows: E5 = 'Overflow/leakage' ('Overflow occurs / Water leakage occurs'), E6 = 'Water leakage' ('Water leakage occurs'). The wording suggests E5 additionally covers an overfill event while E6 is leakage-only, but the manuals give both codes the same WARNING steps, so treat both the same way.
