Hisense Dishwasher Error Code E1

Quick answer

Error codeE1 — Hisense Dishwasher
What it meansDepends on the model family: a slow water-fill fault (HSCE14FS) or the door was opened during the cycle (HV60340UK, H15DSS).
Most common causeOn fill-fault models: tap not fully open, a kinked/restricted inlet hose or low water pressure. On door-code models: the door was opened mid-cycle.
Safe to check yourself?Yes — for the basic external checks below. Do not open the machine's body.
Checked againstHisense HSCE14FS/HSCE14FB user manual; Hisense HV60340UK manual; Hisense H15DSS manual

Applies to — read this first

E1 is one of the clearest examples of why the exact model matters: Hisense uses it for two different faults on different dishwasher families.

Model familyWhat E1 means here
HSCE14FS / HSCE14FB (freestanding, HSCE14FS control family)Longer inlet time — the dishwasher is taking too long to fill with water.
HV60340UK and H15DSS familiesDoor open — the door was opened while the dishwasher was operating.

Find your model number on the rating label if you are not sure which family your machine belongs to — see how to find your model number.

Common causes

  1. The water tap feeding the dishwasher is closed or only partly open (fill-fault models).
  2. The inlet hose is kinked, trapped, or its filter is clogged with grit or limescale.
  3. Household water pressure is very low, so filling takes longer than the control allows.
  4. The door was opened, or not fully latched, during the wash (door-code models).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the dishwasher off and close the water tap before touching any hose.
  2. If your model uses E1 for the door, first simply close the door firmly and restart the cycle — if the code clears, the door was the whole problem.
  3. For fill-fault models, check the supply tap under the sink is fully open.
  4. Pull the machine out only if it moves freely; straighten any kink in the inlet hose. Do not force a built-in unit.
  5. With the tap off and a towel ready, unscrew the inlet hose at the tap end and rinse its small mesh filter under a tap, then reconnect firmly.
  6. Run a short cycle and watch whether the machine fills normally and the code stays away.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. If a check needs a panel removed, a part tested, or a wire touched, it is no longer a safe home check — skip to the technician section.

When to call a technician

  • The code returns immediately even with a known-good water supply and a clean inlet filter.
  • You can hear no water entering at all on fill-fault models, or water enters but never stops.
  • There is any sign of leaking under or behind the machine.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

Always switch off and unplug the dishwasher before moving it or disconnecting hoses, and close the water tap first. Water and electricity are a dangerous mix — never work on a machine that is connected to power with wet hands or a wet floor.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E1 mean on a Hisense dishwasher?

It depends on the model family. In the HSCE14FS manual E1 means the machine is taking too long to fill with water. In the HV60340UK and H15DSS manuals E1 means the door was opened during operation. Check which family your model belongs to before trying fixes.

Can I keep running my dishwasher with E1 showing?

No. On fill-fault models the wash will be poor and the cycle cannot complete properly; on door-code models the cycle cannot run with the door unlatched. Find the cause first.

How do I clear Hisense dishwasher E1?

Fix the cause (open the tap, clean the inlet filter, or close the door properly), then start a fresh cycle. There is no special button sequence in the manuals for this code.

Why do other websites give a different meaning for E1?

Because Hisense genuinely uses E1 for two different faults on different model families. Sites that copy a single meaning for every Hisense dishwasher are wrong for half the machines.
