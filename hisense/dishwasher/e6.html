Hisense Dishwasher Error Code E6

Quick answer

Error codeE6 — Hisense Dishwasher
What it meansE6 means a water leakage fault: the manual's cause column says 'Water leakage occurs.' Water has leaked into the base pan, where the flood sensor picked it up. On the H15DSS and HV60340UK families this is a documented code; on HSCE14FS/HSCE14FB there is no E6 at all.
Most common causeWater has leaked into the base pan — a slow leak from a hose, seal, or connection, or an overfill that escaped the tub.
Safe to check yourself?Partially. You can safely do the manual's own steps: turn off the main water supply, switch off and unplug the machine, remove the water from the base pan, and visually check that the drain hose is correctly fitted and the inlet hose is not kinked or squashed. Tracing and fixing an internal leak, the inlet valve, or the flood switch is technician work.
Checked againstHisense H15DSS Dishwasher User Manual (official PDF via hisense.co.za, 190520 DW-0359-450, 15 place settings); Hisense HV60340UK User's Operation Manual (manualslib, page 11); Hisense HSCE14FS/HSCE14FB Instruction Manual (manualspro transcription; confirms E6 does not exist on that family)

Applies to — read this first

Good news for diagnosis: on the H15DSS and HV60340UK families E6 is consistent — it is always the water leakage fault ('Water leakage occurs'). What you must NOT do is borrow meanings from third-party code lists or other Hisense families: on HSCE14FS/HSCE14FB there is no E6 at all, and the manuals name no pressure switch or water sensor in their error tables — E6 is never a sensor fault.

E6 is the quieter sibling of E5. The manuals keep both codes: E5 = 'Overflow/leakage' ('Overflow occurs/Water leakage occurs'), E6 = 'Water leakage' ('Water leakage occurs'). The wording suggests E5 additionally covers an overfill event while E6 is leakage-only, but the manuals give both codes the same overflow WARNING steps, so treat both the same way.

What the machine does when E6 fires

Expect the display to show E6 while the machine refuses to run a normal cycle — the overflow system responds the way the manual describes for an overflow event: 'it shuts off the circulation pump and turns on the drain pump.' This is the protection response while water sits in the base pan, not a second fault.

Common causes

  1. The manual's cause column: 'Water leakage occurs' — water has leaked and reached the base pan, where the flood sensor picked it up.
  2. A slow leak from a hose connection, the door seal, or the sump that lets water drip down into the base over a cycle.
  3. A drain hose that is not correctly fitted. The manual's installation section warns: 'The drain hose must be correctly fitted to avoid water leaks.'
  4. A kinked or squashed water inlet hose causing abnormal pressure or dripping at the connection — the manual says to ensure the inlet hose is 'not kinked or squashed.'
  5. An overfill event that lets water escape the tub and run down into the base pan — the manual's base-pan note covers both 'an overfill or small leak.'

Safe checks, in order

  1. Turn off the main water supply to the dishwasher — the manual's own first instruction for an overflow/leak event. Switch the machine off and unplug it from the wall socket.
  2. Remove the water from the base pan. The manual says it 'should be removed before restarting the dishwasher' — get access under the tub (you may need to remove the bottom plinth panel), and soak or drain the pooled water out completely. This is what clears the flood sensor.
  3. Hunt the leak the way the manual's installation checks point you: confirm the drain hose is correctly fitted (waste connection 50–100 cm from the floor, fixed with a hose clip), the inlet hose is not kinked or squashed, and the hose connections are tight with no drips.
  4. Check the door seal and the filter/sump area for trapped food, damage, or debris that could let water escape the tub and run down into the base.
  5. Plug back in, restore the water supply, and run a short empty cycle while watching underneath. If E6 returns or the base pan refills, water is still leaking in — stop there and call a qualified technician; the manual's next step is 'calling a service.'

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. The manual gives no electrical or internal-repair steps for E6 — if a check needs a part opened up, a valve tested, or a sensor replaced, it is no longer a safe home check. Skip to the technician section.

When to call a technician

  • E6 returns right after you removed the base-pan water and restarted — water is still leaking in (a hose/connection leak, a stuck inlet valve, or a failed seal).
  • You can see a steady drip or pooling under the machine during the fill — an active leak needs professional diagnosis.
  • The base pan keeps refilling with no visible spill, or the fault appeared out of nowhere — the inlet valve or the flood switch itself may be faulty, both internal parts.
  • Burning smells, tripped breakers, or water near the power cord — switch off at the wall and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

Never run the dishwasher with water sitting in the base pan — remove it before restarting, exactly as the manual instructs. Turn off the main water supply before calling a service, and keep the machine unplugged while you access the base area. Water plus mains electrics is the risk here: if you cannot find the leak, or the fault returns immediately, stop and call a qualified technician rather than repeatedly restarting.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E6 mean on a Hisense dishwasher?

E6 means a water leakage fault. Both the Hisense H15DSS manual and the HV60340UK manual define it the same way: 'E6 Water leakage — Water leakage occurs.' Water has reached the base pan, where the flood sensor picked it up.

How do I reset Hisense dishwasher E6?

The manuals give no button reset — the manual's overflow instruction is the procedure: turn off the main water supply, then remove the water from the base pan (unplug the machine, get access under the tub, and soak or drain it out) before restarting the dishwasher. If E6 comes back right after that, water is still leaking in and it is a technician job.

Is E6 the same as E5?

The manuals list them as separate codes in the same fault family: E5 = 'Overflow/leakage' ('Overflow occurs/Water leakage occurs'), E6 = 'Water leakage' ('Water leakage occurs'). The wording suggests E5 additionally covers an overfill event while E6 is leakage-only — but the manuals give both the same WARNING steps, so treat both the same way.

Some sites say Hisense E6 is a pressure switch or water sensor fault. Is that right?

No. Both manufacturer manuals define E6 strictly as water leakage. The dishwasherusa site publishes two pages that contradict each other — one says E6 is a 'Pressure Switch Fault', the other says E6 is a 'Water Sensor Error' (with invented limescale advice) — and neither a pressure switch nor a water sensor appears anywhere in the Hisense dishwasher manuals' error tables. Both meanings were invented.

My Hisense dishwasher doesn't show E6 in its manual. Why?

Your machine is probably from a different family: the HSCE14FS/HSCE14FB manuals have no E6 at all (their error table is E1, E3, E4, E9, Ed only). E6 exists only on the H15DSS and HV60340UK families in the manuals we checked. Use your own manual's error table, not a generic list.
