Hisense Dishwasher Error Code E4

Quick answer

Error codeE4 — Hisense Dishwasher
What it meansDepends on the model family: a temperature sensor malfunction (H15DSS, HV60340UK) or overflow / anti-flood activation — water has leaked into the base (HSCE14FS/HSCE14FB).
Most common causeOn overflow models: water collected in the base pan from a slow leak, over-sudsing or a hose fault. On sensor models: a faulty or disconnected temperature sensor.
Safe to check yourself?Yes — the external checks below, but only with the machine unplugged. The overflow version involves water near electrics, so unplugging is non-negotiable.
Checked againstHisense H15DSS user manual (temperature sensor); Hisense HV60340UK User's Operation Manual, p.11 (temperature sensor); Hisense HSCE14FS/HSCE14FB instruction manual (E4 = "Overflow — some element of dishwasher leaks")

Applies to — read this first

E4 is one of the most misleading Hisense codes, because the two manual families use it for completely different faults. Check your model number first — see how to find your model number.

Model familyWhat E4 means hereCorrect first step
H15DSS and HV60340UK familiesTemperature sensor malfunctionPower off and restart; if it persists, the sensor needs a technician.
HSCE14FS / HSCE14FB familyOverflow — "some element of dishwasher leaks"; the anti-flood device has tripped on water in the baseUnplug, close the tap, and find water in the base pan (see overflow steps below).

Not a drainage problem

Some all-codes sites describe E4 as a drainage fault. The manuals say otherwise: it is a temperature sensor fault or an overflow/leak fault. Cleaning the drain filter will not fix either meaning of E4.

If your model shows E4 for overflow (HSCE14FS family)

On these models the anti-flood device has detected water in the base pan under the tub — often the pump runs and the machine refuses to start.

Common causes

  1. A slow leak from a door seal, spray-arm base, or a loosened inlet/drain hose.
  2. Over-sudsing from ordinary dish soap or too much detergent, causing foam to push water into the base.
  3. The machine was recently moved and a hose or clip shifted.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Unplug the machine from the wall and close the water tap. Never reach under a machine that is still connected to power.
  2. If the machine moves freely, slide it out and look for standing water in the base pan or on the floor behind it. Dry it up with towels.
  3. Check the inlet and drain hoses for loose clips or visible drips, and the door seal for damage or food debris.
  4. Leave the machine to dry for several hours with the door ajar — a damp anti-flood float keeps the code on until it is dry.
  5. Plug back in and run a short cycle. If the code clears and no new water appears in the base, the trip is reset.

If your model shows E4 for the temperature sensor (H15DSS/HV60340UK family)

Common causes

  1. A faulty or disconnected temperature (NTC) sensor or its wiring.
  2. A control-board fault reading the sensor incorrectly.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the dishwasher fully off, wait five minutes, and switch it back on — a one-off glitch can clear.
  2. Run a different program: if E4 appears only on hot programs, that points to the sensor circuit.
  3. If the code returns on every run, stop: testing or replacing the sensor means opening the machine, which is technician work.

When to call a technician

  • The code returns immediately even after the base is dry and the tap is open (overflow models).
  • Water keeps reappearing in the base after drying it out — there is an active leak.
  • E4 persists on temperature-sensor models after a restart — the sensor or control board needs testing.
  • Any burning smell, scorch marks, or tripped circuit breaker — stop and unplug immediately.

Safety first

Always switch off and unplug the dishwasher before moving it, reaching under it, or touching any hose, and close the water tap first. The overflow version of E4 means water may be sitting on or near electrical parts — never work on the machine while it is plugged in, never touch it with wet hands or while standing in water.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E4 mean on a Hisense dishwasher?

It depends on the model family. In the H15DSS and HV60340UK manuals E4 means a temperature sensor malfunction. In the HSCE14FS/HSCE14FB instruction manual E4 means overflow — water has leaked into the base and the anti-flood device has activated. Identify your model family first, because the fix is completely different.

Is Hisense dishwasher E4 a drainage problem?

No. The manufacturer manuals say E4 is either a temperature sensor fault or an overflow/leak — not a drain blockage. Some all-codes sites list it as drainage, which leads owners to the wrong fix.

How do I clear Hisense dishwasher E4?

On HSCE14FS-family models: unplug the machine, check for standing water in the base pan under it, mop it out and let it dry fully before plugging back in. On H15DSS/HV60340UK models (temperature sensor fault): switch the machine off and on again; if E4 persists, the sensor needs a technician to test or replace.

Is it safe to tilt the dishwasher to drain water from the base for E4?

Only with the machine unplugged and the water tap closed. Tilting a machine that is still connected to power or has a live supply is dangerous. If you are unsure, or water keeps reappearing, call a technician.
