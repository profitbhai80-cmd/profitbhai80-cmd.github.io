Hisense Refrigerator Error Code En

Quick answer

Error codeEn — Hisense Refrigerator
What it meansEn = WiFi module defective/malfunctioning. Call a service centre to fix the problem. No safe-mode or 'cannot operate normally' language attaches to En in either manual - unlike the fan codes, the manuals do not say the appliance stops working; do not claim it does.
Most common causeThe WiFi module is defective/malfunctioning - the manuals name no sub-cause beyond the fault itself.
Safe to check yourself?No - the manuals give no homeowner fix for En. Their full remedy: call a service centre to fix the problem. WiFi re-pairing, router resets, and app reinstalls are NOT the En remedy.
Checked againstHisense RB5P410SACCUK user manual (p.35) + Hisense doc 962974en-a2, INSTRUCTIONS FOR USE OF A REFRIGERATOR WITH FREEZER (troubleshooting section 28)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because Hisense En is a short, exact code: the WiFi module is defective/malfunctioning, verified in two Hisense-branded documents with one wording variant ('defective' in the RB5P410 manual, 'malfunctioning' in doc 962974en-a2). The two documents are distinct, not mirrors: 36 pages vs the RB5P410 manual's 40 pages; 'malfunctioning' vs 'defective' wording on En and F0; 962974en-a2 carries 2026-era Right to Repair content (Directive (EU) 2024/1799) and AdaptTech/ConvertActive/NoFrost Dual sections. Same residual caveat as F0/F2: both documents cover the same bottom-freezer platform, so this is a second Hisense document, not a second platform. Display note: En is shown as 'En' (capital E, lowercase n) - not 'EN' as an abbreviation and not 'E0'/'E8'.

What the machine does when En fires

The display shows En. The manuals do not say the appliance stops working: unlike the F0/F1/F2 fan codes, the En row has no 'cannot operate normally' language - only 'call a service centre to fix the problem'. The WiFi module fault is a connectivity fault, not a cooling fault.

Common causes

  1. WiFi module defective (RB5P410) / malfunctioning (doc 962974en-a2) - the manuals' own fault definition; no sub-cause is given.
  2. Wrong-symptom reading - a JustAnswer thread (RF749N4SWFE_BKSS) shows owners asking 'what EN means' with the fridge resetting itself to 8 degrees - no answer in the thread; the manual meaning (WiFi module) is unrelated to the temperature-reset symptom, and this page does not link the two.
  3. Wrong-procedure reading - RB3B-series Hisense manuals give WiFi connection troubleshooting (wrong password, 2.4 GHz only) for 'unable to connect to WiFi network', not for the En fault code.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Call a service centre to fix the problem - that is the manuals' full remedy.
  2. Do not try WiFi re-pairing, router resets, or app reinstalls for En - those are for 'unable to connect to WiFi' symptoms, not for a defective module showing En.
  3. Do not use demo-mode button holds (hold Mode 3 seconds, hold Power + WiFi 3 seconds) - JustAnswer forum advice for unresponsive controls after a failed app setup, not a manual En remedy.

The steps above are the manuals' full remedy, printed exactly. This page does not invent WiFi re-pairing, router, app-reinstall, or power-cycle steps - none exist in the manuals for En.

When to call a technician

  • En is displayed at all - the manuals' remedy is a service centre; there is no homeowner fix.
  • The fridge also resets itself to 8 degrees - that symptom is not the En fault; tell the technician about both, and do not treat the temperature reset as the WiFi fault.

Safety first

En is a module fault in a mains-powered refrigerator: never open panels or probe the control board or WiFi module yourself. Module replacement is technician work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does En mean on a Hisense refrigerator?

The WiFi module is defective/malfunctioning. Verified in the RB5P410SACCUK user manual and Hisense doc 962974en-a2.

Will my fridge stop cooling with En?

The manuals do not say so: unlike the F0/F1/F2 fan codes, the En row has no 'cannot operate normally' language - only 'call a service centre to fix the problem'. The WiFi module fault is a connectivity fault, not a cooling fault. Do not follow generic 'fridge not cooling' advice for En.

Can I fix En by reconnecting WiFi or reinstalling the app?

The manuals give no such step for En - only 'call a service centre'. WiFi connection troubleshooting (password, 2.4 GHz) is for 'unable to connect' symptoms, not for a defective module showing En.

Is En the same as 81?

Same fault, different code string: on the Gorenje-platform code set the WiFi module fault is shown as 81. On Hisense-branded models it is En. 81 has no published page yet (only the group document names it).

Is En the same as E0 or E8?

No. En is shown as 'En' (capital E, lowercase n) - a different code string from E0 and E8, with a different meaning (WiFi module, not sensor keys or a humidity sensor). Do not read En as E0 or E8.
