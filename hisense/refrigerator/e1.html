Hisense Refrigerator Error Code E1

Quick answer

Error codeE1 — Hisense Refrigerator
What it meansE1 = refrigerator (fresh-food) temperature sensor fault. On RM469/RS711/RB470 and doc 895744-a1 models the manual lumps E1 and E2 into one "temperature sensor error or fault" row; on RS5P535/LS110EA/RB5P410/HRF266 models E1 is specifically the refrigerator temperature sensor. The appliance keeps running in safe mode until a technician repairs it.
Most common causeThe refrigerator temperature sensor has failed - the manuals name no component-level sub-cause, only the fault and the remedy.
Safe to check yourself?No - the user manuals give no homeowner reset for E1. Their full remedy: call a service centre for repair. Sensor testing is a technician job.

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because Hisense E1 is worded two ways across the platforms - but unlike E0 and E3, the two wordings agree: on the fan platform (RM469/RS711/RB470/doc 895744-a1) E1 and E2 share one "temperature sensor error or fault" row, so E1 alone does not identify which sensor; on the sensor platform (RS5P535/LS110EA/RB5P410/HRF266/HS-309) E1 is specifically the refrigerator temperature sensor (E3 is the freezer sensor there). On every verified platform E1 is a sensor fault - no manual gives it a non-sensor meaning.

What the machine does when E1 fires

The display shows E1 and the appliance operates in safe mode - it automatically preserves or maintains the most suitable temperature for each compartment until a service technician repairs it.

Common causes

  1. Refrigerator temperature sensor defective - the manuals' own fault definition on every verified platform.
  2. Fan-platform lumping - on RM469/RS711/RB470/doc 895744-a1, E1 and E2 share one row, so the display alone does not identify which sensor failed.
  3. Wrong-appliance reading - Hisense AC E1 is a room/ambient temperature sensor fault on a different appliance with different remedies; do not follow AC guides for a refrigerator E1.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Call a service centre for repair - that is the manuals' full remedy. Until the appliance is repaired by a service technician, it will operate in safe mode.
  2. Do not invent a power-cycle fix: the user manuals give no homeowner reset for E1, and sensor replacement is a technician job (the HS-309 service instruction tests the sensor at the CN3 terminal with a multimeter).
  3. Check the platform note: on the sensor platform E1 is the refrigerator sensor and E3 is the freezer sensor; on the fan platform E1 and E2 share a row.

The steps above are the manuals' full remedy, printed exactly. This page does not invent power-cycle-then-DIY steps - none exist in the user manuals for E1.

When to call a technician

  • E1 is displayed at all - the manuals' remedy is a service centre in every case; there is no homeowner fix.
  • The fridge holds safe-mode temperatures but never clears E1 - the sensor needs replacing by a technician.
  • Any other fault code alongside E1 - switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

E1 is a sensor fault in a mains-powered refrigerator: never open the back panel or probe the control board or sensor wiring yourself. Sensor testing (HS-309: CN3 terminal, multimeter) is service-personnel work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E1 mean on a Hisense refrigerator?

A refrigerator (fresh-food) temperature sensor fault. On RM469/RS711/RB470/895744-a1 models the manual lumps E1 and E2 into one "temperature sensor error or fault" row; on RS5P535/LS110EA/RB5P410/HRF266 models E1 is specifically the refrigerator temperature sensor.

Will my fridge keep running with E1?

Yes, in safe mode: the manuals say the appliance "will operate in safe mode" and "automatically preserve/maintain the most suitable temperature for each compartment" until a service technician repairs it. Call a service centre; do not keep relying on safe mode.

Is E1 the same as E2?

On the fan platform (RM469, RS711, RB470, doc 895744-a1) E1 and E2 share one manual row, so the display alone does not tell you which sensor. On the sensor platform they are separate: E1 = refrigerator temperature sensor, E3 = freezer temperature sensor; E2 does not exist on RS5P535/LS110EA/HRF266.

Can I reset E1 myself?

The user manuals give no homeowner reset for E1 - only "call a service centre for repair". Do not invent a power-cycle fix; sensor replacement is a technician job (the HS-309 service instruction tests the sensor at the CN3 terminal with a multimeter).

Is E1 the same on a Hisense air conditioner?

No. Hisense AC E1 is a room/ambient temperature sensor fault on a different appliance with different remedies. Do not follow AC guides for a refrigerator E1.
