Beko Washing Machine Error Code E11

Quick answer

Error codeE11 — Beko Washing Machine
What it meansE11 = motor error: motor phase open circuit (ASKOLL motors) / motor triac open circuit or motor tacho open circuit (UMDC). Check the motor, the wiring, the motor triac on the control PCB, and the PCB. On the WTK platform E11 is the motor code — the platform where E06 does not exist.
Most common causeAn open circuit in the motor path: the motor phase (ASKOLL), or the motor triac or tacho (UMDC), or the wiring/PCB around them.
Safe to check yourself?Limited. The motor, wiring, triac and PCB checks are internal mains-voltage work — beyond a single power-cycle, E11 is technician territory. Your job is to confirm the code is really E11 and your model is the platform where E11 is the motor code.
Checked againstArçelik Beko B13 Washing Machine Service Handbook §11 (ASKOLL phase-open; UMDC triac/tacho open); ukwhitegoods Beko WMA table E11 row (motor error detected)

Applies to — read this first

E11 is the motor-open fault, and it plays a platform role no competitor states: on the WTK platform, where the B7S ARCD service document has no E6, E11 IS the motor code. The B13 table reads E11 as motor phase open circuit for ASKOLL motors, and motor triac open circuit / motor tacho open circuit for the UMDC drive. One honest note: the B13 OCR rendering scrambles the exact row boundary between E11 and E12, so this page presents the distinction as the table's motor theme confirmed by the WMA table, not as a flowchart-verified rule.

Competitors narrow E11 to a tacho-only fault or merge it with E06. The manuals say otherwise: E11 covers phase-open (ASKOLL) and triac-open or tacho-open (UMDC). E06 is the short/diode fault; E11 is the open fault — that is the table's split, and this page keeps it.

What the machine does when E11 fires

The motor misbehaves or gives up: the drum may fail to turn the drum at all despite the machine appearing to operate, rotate during wash phases but fail to reach spin speed, or run erratically. The WMA table's 'motor error detected' is a drive-system fault, not a load problem — redistributing laundry will not clear it.

Common causes

  1. Motor phase open circuit (ASKOLL motors).
  2. Motor triac open circuit (UMDC drive).
  3. Motor tacho open circuit — the speed sensor not reporting (UMDC drive).
  4. Wiring faults between the motor and the control PCB, or a control PCB fault.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the display reads E11, not E06: open-circuit (E11) vs short/diode fault (E06) point to different diagnoses.
  2. Confirm your platform: on WTK-platform machines E11 is the motor code; on B13/WMA machines E11 is the open-circuit motor fault. Do not borrow a different platform's table.
  3. Power-cycle once: unplug for a few minutes, plug back in, and try a short programme. If E11 returns, the open fault is genuine.
  4. If E11 persists, stop and call a technician. The WMA sequence — motor, wiring, motor triac on the control PCB, PCB — is mains-voltage internal work at potentially high current during spin.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. Do not measure motor resistance or tacho signals yourself: the manuals prescribe no test figures for E11, and blog-table 'typical' resistance values are not from the manufacturer sources above.

When to call a technician

  • E11 returns after a power-cycle and a retry.
  • The drum never rotates despite the machine appearing to run through cycle stages.
  • Any burning smell, visible wiring damage, or loud unusual noises — unplug immediately and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

Motor circuits operate at mains voltage and carry significant current — potentially around 10 amps at high-speed spin — and capacitors can store charge after unplugging. Never remove panels, never probe the motor or PCB, and never bypass a safety device to force the drum through.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E11 mean on a Beko washing machine?

E11 is a motor error: motor phase open circuit (ASKOLL motors), or motor triac open circuit / motor tacho open circuit (UMDC drive). The WMA check sequence is motor, wiring, motor triac on the control PCB, PCB.

Is Beko E11 the same as E06?

No. E06 = UMDC motor-triac short circuit / diode-mode fault. E11 = motor phase open (ASKOLL) or motor triac/tacho open (UMDC). Short vs open — different faults, different diagnoses.

Why does my machine show E11 and my manual has no E6?

Your machine is a WTK-platform model: the B7S ARCD service document has no E6, and E11 is the motor code on that platform. You are reading the right page — E11 is your motor fault.

Can I fix Beko E11 myself?

Beyond one power-cycle, no. E11's checks are motor, wiring, triac and PCB work at mains voltage, and the manuals prescribe no home test figures. If E11 returns after the power-cycle, call a technician.
