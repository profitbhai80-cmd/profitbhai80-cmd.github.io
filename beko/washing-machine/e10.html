Beko Washing Machine Error Code E10

Quick answer

Error codeE10 — Beko Washing Machine
What it meansE10 = door lock triac short circuit error; the door is detected as jammed. Check the door, the door lock, the lock wiring, the door lock triac on the control module, and the control module. This is the pair to E09 (triac open circuit).
Most common causeThe door lock triac has failed short, so the control reads the door as jammed; the lock mechanism or its wiring can also be the cause.
Safe to check yourself?Basic checks only: confirm nothing is jamming the door and the lock area is clean. The triac and control-module diagnosis is technician work.
Checked againstArçelik Beko B13 Washing Machine Service Handbook §11 row; ukwhitegoods Beko WMA table E10 row (door detected as jammed)

Applies to — read this first

E10 is the other half of the door-lock pair: where E09 is the triac failing OPEN (door cannot be locked), E10 is the triac failing SHORT, and the control reads the door as jammed. The B13 handbook's §11 table gives E10 the short-circuit row; the Beko-supplied WMA table agrees and adds the observable symptom — the door is detected as being jammed — with the check sequence: door, door lock, door lock wiring, door lock triac on the control module, control module.

Competitors merge E09/E10 into one 'door lock fault' row, which tells you nothing about mechanism. This page keeps the split the manuals keep: open vs short, cannot-lock vs jammed.

What the machine does when E10 fires

The control believes the door is jammed: the programme will not start (or halts), and the lock area is where the machine is looking. Unlike E09's 'cannot lock' state, E10's state reads as a jammed door — so physical inspection of the door and lock comes before any electronics diagnosis.

Common causes

  1. The door lock triac on the control module has failed short (the B13 mechanism).
  2. The door is genuinely jammed — an obstruction or misalignment in the door or lock.
  3. Damaged or shorted wiring in the door lock circuit.
  4. A fault in the control module itself.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Open the door and inspect the lock assembly area carefully for anything stuck in it, trapped lint or debris, or an obvious obstruction stopping the door from moving freely.
  2. Close the door firmly several times and listen: a shorted triac can read as jammed even when the mechanism moves, so note whether the lock sounds normal.
  3. Power-cycle: unplug for a few minutes, plug back in, and try again.
  4. If E10 persists, stop and call a technician. The WMA sequence — door lock, wiring, triac on the control module, control module — is internal work.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. Do not force the door or disassemble the lock housing yourself.

When to call a technician

  • E10 persists with the door and lock area visibly clear.
  • The door moves freely by hand yet the machine still reports a jam.
  • Any burning smell from the control area — unplug and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

Never force a door the machine reads as jammed: forcing the handle or the lock mechanism can break the latch or damage the locking assembly and turn a fault into a more expensive one. The door lock is a safety interlock — do not bypass it.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E10 mean on a Beko washing machine?

E10 is a door lock triac short circuit error: the triac has failed short and the control detects the door as jammed. The WMA check sequence is door, door lock, lock wiring, door lock triac on the control module, control module.

Is Beko E10 the same as E09?

No. E09 = door lock triac OPEN circuit (door cannot be locked, programme will not start). E10 = door lock triac SHORT circuit (door detected as jammed). They are paired codes, not the same fault.

My door moves freely but the machine says E10 — why?

Because E10 is an electrical fault as well as a mechanical one: a shorted triac makes the control read 'jammed' even when the mechanism is fine. If the door area is clear and E10 persists, the triac or control module is the suspect — technician work.

Can I clear Beko E10 by opening and closing the door?

Worth trying once: a real obstruction can cause a genuine jam reading, and closing the door firmly after clearing it can reset the state. But if E10 returns with a clear door area, stop — forcing the door risks breaking the latch.
