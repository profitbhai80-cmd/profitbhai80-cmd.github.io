Beko Washing Machine Error Code E06

Quick answer

Error codeE06 — Beko Washing Machine
What it meansE06 = motor triac fault on the UMDC drive: motor triac short circuit / motor triac diode-mode error. The machine may move a couple of times then end the programme, jump to spin during a wash, or leave the programme unfinished. This is a B13/WMA-platform code — on the WTK platform there is no E6, and the motor code there is E11.
Most common causeA shorted motor triac (or a triac diode-mode fault) in the motor drive circuit on the UMDC platform, with motor wiring or the control PCB involved.
Safe to check yourself?Limited. The WMA check sequence (motor, wiring, motor triac on the control PCB, PCB) is internal, mains-voltage work — beyond a single power-cycle, E06 is technician territory. Your job is to confirm the code is really E06 (not E11) and that your model is a B13/WMA-platform machine.
Checked againstArçelik Beko B13 Washing Machine Service Handbook §11 (p.97) E06 row; ukwhitegoods Beko WMA fault-code table (from information given by Beko) E6 row

Applies to — read this first

Scope first, because E06 is not a universal Beko code. The Arçelik Beko B13 Service Handbook names E06 for the UMDC drive (motor triac short circuit / diode-mode error), and the Beko-supplied WMA table agrees on the motor theme. But the Arçelik B7S ARCD service document for the WTK platform has NO E6 at all — on that platform the motor code is E11. If your machine is a WTK-platform model, this page is not your diagnosis; read our E11 page instead.

Competitor tables merge E06 and E11 into one motor row and one of them invents a universal-vs-BLDC split for the pair. The B13 split is UMDC triac short/diode (E06) vs ASKOLL phase-open or UMDC triac/tacho open (E11) — presented here as the table's distinction, not as a flowchart-verified rule, because the handbook's OCR rendering scrambles the exact row boundary. The WMA table confirms both codes as separate motor rows.

What the machine does when E06 fires

When E06 fires, the drive misbehaves before the machine gives up: the washing machine may move a couple of times and then the programme ends, the machine may jump to spin during a wash, or the programme simply stays unfinished. Those three behaviours are the WMA table's observable symptoms, and they are how owners usually notice the fault.

Common causes

  1. Motor triac short circuit on the UMDC drive (the B13 table's first mechanism).
  2. Motor triac diode-mode error on the UMDC drive (the B13 table's second mechanism).
  3. Faulty motor wiring between the motor and the control PCB.
  4. A fault in the motor control circuit or the control PCB itself.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the display really reads E06 and not E11: E06 is the UMDC triac fault, E11 is the motor phase/tacho open fault, and they need different diagnoses.
  2. Confirm your platform: if your machine is a WTK-platform model (the B7S ARCD service document covers these), stop — E06 does not exist for your machine and E11 is the motor code.
  3. Power-cycle once: switch the machine off, unplug it for a few minutes, plug it back in and try a short programme. If E06 returns, the triac fault is genuine.
  4. If E06 persists after the power-cycle, stop and call a technician. The WMA check sequence — motor, wiring, motor triac on the control PCB, PCB — is mains-voltage internal work, not a home repair.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. Do not test motor windings, triacs or tachometers yourself: the manuals prescribe no resistance figures for E06, and any numbers printed on blog tables (a tachometer or winding 'typical' reading) are not from the manufacturer sources above.

When to call a technician

  • E06 returns after a power-cycle and a retry.
  • The machine repeats the move-then-stop or jump-to-spin behaviour on every attempt.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or scorch marks near the motor or control board — unplug immediately and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

E06 is a motor-drive fault, and motor circuits run at mains voltage with capacitors that can hold charge after unplugging. Never remove the machine's panels, never probe the motor or control PCB, and never bypass a safety device to force the programme through.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E06 mean on a Beko washing machine?

On B13/WMA-platform machines E06 is a motor triac fault on the UMDC drive: motor triac short circuit or triac diode-mode error. The machine may move a couple of times then end the programme, jump to spin during a wash, or leave the programme unfinished.

Is Beko E06 the same as E11?

No. They are both motor codes but different faults: E06 = UMDC triac short/diode-mode fault; E11 = motor phase open circuit (ASKOLL) or motor triac/tacho open circuit (UMDC). Competitors that merge the two rows lose exactly this distinction.

My Beko shows E06 but my manual has no E06 — what now?

Your machine is likely a WTK-platform model: the B7S ARCD service document has no E6, and the motor code on that platform is E11. Do not apply this page's diagnosis; read the E11 page instead.

Can I reset Beko E06 myself?

You can power-cycle once and retry a short programme. If E06 returns, the triac fault is genuine and the manuals give no further user step — the WMA check sequence (motor, wiring, triac, PCB) is technician work.
