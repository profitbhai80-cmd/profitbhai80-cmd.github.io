Beko Washing Machine Error Code E04

Quick answer

Error codeE04 — Beko Washing Machine
What it meansE04 is a continuous-fill fault: the machine keeps taking in water, shows no washing movement, the door cannot be opened, and the programme never ends. The Arçelik service document's E4 flowchart and the Beko-supplied WMA table agree on the suspects: water valve, valve triac on the PCB, control board, wiring. First action: turn the inlet taps OFF — a failed-open valve keeps admitting water even with the machine switched off. Then the unplug test tells valve from board: still filling while unplugged means the valve itself is faulty; stopping when unplugged points to the triac or board (service work). Do not confuse with E08, the opposite fault (no water coming in).
Most common causeThe machine keeps taking in water — a stuck-open inlet valve, a shorted valve triac, or a faulty control board. Turn the taps off first.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. Turning the taps off and the unplug observation test are safe for the user. Valve replacement and any triac or control-board work are service-staff work — the machine must not be opened for those.

Applies to — read this first

E04 is a continuous-fill fault: the machine keeps taking in water and never gets to wash. Two manufacturer-linked sources agree exactly — the Arçelik B7S ARCD service document's E4 flowchart and the Beko-supplied WMA service table — and the single most useful fact on this page is the service document's own discriminator: if water keeps entering with the machine UNPLUGGED, the inlet valve itself is mechanically faulty; if it stops when unplugged, the valve triac or control board is at fault. Read that test before anything else, because the first safety step is different from every other fill fault: turn the taps OFF first.

E04 is a DIAGNOSTIC/SERVICE code, not a user-manual code: Beko user manuals (BM3WFU410415WPBB, BM1WFSU37233WPBB, BM3WFU41041W, B5WFU69415MB2 and others) print only Err, SC, E5, E8, E17, E29 etc. and have no E4 row — that absence is normal, not a contradiction. E04 surfaces in service/diagnostic mode. From the same sources: E08 is the OPPOSITE fill fault ('The machine may not be taking in water' — no water coming in); E07 is the level-sensor fault; E05 is the drain fault. Four ranking pages invert E04 and E08 — do not follow them. Also note the letter: F04 is not E04, and the Beko WTL120G top-loader's E4 (20-minute fill timeout) is a different product line with a different meaning.

What the machine does when E04 fires

The machine takes in water continuously and discharges it at the same time, shows no washing movement, the door cannot be opened, and the programme does not come to an end. This is the WMA table's observable symptom ('constantly filling and discharging water, no wash action') and the service flowchart's symptom list — the machine never progresses because the fill never completes.

Common causes

  1. The water inlet valve is stuck open mechanically (failed-open solenoid).
  2. The valve triac on the control board is short-circuited, keeping the valve powered.
  3. The valve triac diode is faulty.
  4. The control board is faulty and continues driving the valve.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Turn the inlet taps behind the machine OFF immediately — a failed-open valve admits water even with the machine switched off, and leaving the taps on risks flooding.
  2. Unplug the machine (switch off at the wall) and watch whether water is still entering the drum.
  3. If water is STILL entering with the machine unplugged, the inlet valve itself is mechanically faulty: leave the taps closed, do not run the machine, and call a qualified technician to replace the valve.
  4. If the water STOPS when the machine is unplugged, the fault is electrical (valve triac short circuit or control board): leave the machine unplugged and call a qualified technician — triac and board work is not a user repair.
  5. Do not keep restarting the machine or running programmes while E04 is present; the fill never completes, so the programme cannot progress and water may overflow.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the discriminator is clear. The taps-off step and the unplug observation are the only user steps; everything after the discriminator (valve replacement, triac/board work) is service-staff work. If a check needs the machine opened or a component tested electrically, it is no longer a safe home check — skip to the technician section.

When to call a technician

  • Water keeps entering the drum with the machine unplugged — mechanically faulty inlet valve.
  • Water stops when unplugged but the fault returns on power-up — valve triac or control board fault.
  • The machine fills and discharges at the same time without any wash action.
  • Any heavy leaking, water near electrical parts, or a burning smell — keep taps closed and power off and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

Turn the inlet taps off FIRST — a stuck-open valve can flood the property even with the machine switched off. Mop up any water immediately and keep water away from electrical parts. Never open the machine to reach the valve, triac or board while it is connected to power or water; if water is already leaking heavily, keep the taps closed and call a qualified technician (and a plumber if flooring is affected).

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E04 mean on a Beko washing machine?

E04 is a continuous-fill fault: the machine takes in water continuously, shows no washing movement, the door cannot be opened, and the programme does not come to an end. The Arçelik B7S ARCD service document's E4 flowchart and the Beko-supplied WMA table agree: check the water valve, the valve triac on the PCB, the PCB itself, and the wiring.

What should I do first when my Beko washer shows E04?

Turn the inlet taps off immediately. A valve failed open keeps admitting water even with the machine switched off, because mains pressure forces water through it — leaving the taps on risks flooding while you investigate. Only then unplug the machine and check whether water is still entering.

How do I tell whether it is the valve or the control board?

Use the service document's own test: unplug the machine and watch. If water keeps entering with the machine unplugged, the inlet valve itself is mechanically faulty (it needs replacing). If the water stops when unplugged, the fault is electrical — the valve triac or the control board — and that is service-staff work.

Is Beko washer E04 the same as E08?

No — they are opposites, and several ranking pages invert them. E04 = too much water coming in and not stopping (continuous fill). E08 = no water coming in ('The machine may not be taking in water', the user-manual row). If your machine will not fill at all, that is E08, not E04.
