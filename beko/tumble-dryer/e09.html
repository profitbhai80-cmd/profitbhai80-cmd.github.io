Beko Tumble Dryer Error Code E09

Quick answer

Error codeE09 — Beko Tumble Dryer
What it meansE09 = Compressor NTC: Open Circuit — the heat-pump compressor temperature sensor circuit is open. BX B7S / BLED heat-pump tumble dryer platform only. FAMILY-COUNT CAVEAT, stated honestly: verified in ONE genuine Arcelik/Beko service handbook covering TWO named platforms (BX B7S and BLED) — the code-to-meaning row is stated once in a table that applies to both named platforms. No second standalone dryer handbook was found publicly.
Most common causeAn open circuit in the compressor NTC wiring or sensor. There is no 'heater NTC' on this platform — do not diagnose the heating element for this code.
Safe to check yourself?No — the handbook gives no home fix for E09. A technician runs the service-test program; do not use the unsourced heater-test figures found on other sites.

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because Beko dryer E09 carries a family-count caveat this page states honestly and prominently: E09's meaning is verified in ONE genuine manufacturer document — the Beko/Arcelik service handbook below — which covers TWO named platforms (BX B7S and BLED) with separate service-test programs for each. That is the basis for the 2-family count: the code-to-meaning row is stated once in a table that applies to both named platforms. No second standalone dryer handbook was found publicly. The meaning itself ('Compressor NTC: Open Circuit') is not in doubt — it is verbatim from the handbook. Scope: E09 here is the BX B7S/BLED heat-pump dryer platform only. The compressor NTC is the heat-pump compressor temperature sensor — there is NO 'heater NTC' on this platform. Do NOT merge E09 with washer E09 (door lock).

What the machine does when E09 fires

The display shows E09 and the dryer stops — the compressor temperature sensor circuit is open. In the service test, Step 8 shows 'h:OC' for this fault, and the test cannot pass to the next step while an NTC or voltage error is present.

Common causes

  1. Open circuit in the compressor NTC wiring or sensor — the sensor-circuit fault the handbook's row names.
  2. Wrong-part diagnosis from other sites: the leading dryer guide renames the sensor a 'heater NTC' and prescribes heating-element coil tests (32.3 Ω / 73.6 Ω) and a self-resetting thermostat for a compressor-sensor fault. A heat-pump dryer heats via the compressor circuit; there is no 'heater NTC' on this platform.
  3. Restricted airflow listed as an open-circuit cause on other sites — no handbook support for that claim.
  4. Wrong-appliance reading — Beko washer E09 is a door interlock fault, not a compressor sensor fault.

Safe checks, in order

  1. E09 is a sensor-circuit fault, not a user-serviceable cleaning job — the handbook gives no home fix, so do not open the machine or probe the sensor wiring.
  2. A service technician runs the handbook's service-test program: Step 8 checks the door and compressor NTCs and shows 'h:OC' for a compressor NTC open circuit; if an NTC or voltage error occurs, the test is not allowed to pass to the next step.
  3. Step 15 runs the compressor: 'Crn' appears on display, the compressor starts to work, the compressor NTC value appears and starts to increase, and when it reaches 50–55 °C, 'C on' appears, the 'Sound Warning Led' starts to blink and the test may pass to the next step.
  4. Do NOT use the figures from other sites (15.2–19.5 kΩ; heater coils 32.3 Ω / 73.6 Ω; thermostat 0.1–1.0 Ω at 160 °C): they are unsourced, absent from the handbook, and for the wrong part.

The handbook gives NO E09-specific home procedure and NO resistance values, and its service test has NO heater steps (Steps 9–11 are fan, pump, motor; Step 15 is the compressor) — the heater test-mode steps on other sites appear borrowed from a different (resistive-heater) dryer platform. Anything beyond a technician's service test is not supported by the handbook.

When to call a technician

  • E09 is shown at all — the compressor NTC circuit is open and needs a technician with the service-test program.
  • The service test shows 'h:OC' at Step 8 and blocks progress — that is a technician diagnosis, not a cleaning job.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or other fault codes alongside E09 — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

E09 is a sensor fault in a mains-powered heat-pump dryer: never open the machine or probe the NTC wiring yourself. The service-test program is technician work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E09 mean on a Beko tumble dryer?

Compressor NTC: Open Circuit. The heat-pump compressor temperature sensor circuit is open.

What is the compressor NTC?

The temperature sensor on the compressor circuit of a heat-pump dryer. It is NOT a heater sensor — this platform has no 'heater NTC'.

Why do other sites say E09 is a heater fault?

The leading dryer guide renames the compressor NTC as a 'heater NTC' and prescribes heating-element tests (32.3 Ω / 73.6 Ω coils and a self-resetting thermostat). The handbook says 'Compressor NTC' — a heat-pump dryer heats via the compressor circuit, so those heater tests diagnose the wrong part.

Can I fix E09 myself?

The handbook gives no home fix. A technician runs the service-test program (Step 8 shows 'h:OC'; Step 15 runs the compressor to 50–55 °C) and checks the sensor harness and sockets.

What is the difference between E09, E10 and E11?

All three are the same compressor sensor in different fault modes: E09 open circuit, E10 short circuit, E11 overheat.
