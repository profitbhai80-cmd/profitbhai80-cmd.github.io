Beko Tumble Dryer Error Code E10

Quick answer

Error codeE10 — Beko Tumble Dryer
What it meansE10 = Compressor NTC: Short Circuit — the heat-pump compressor temperature sensor circuit is shorted. BX B7S / BLED heat-pump tumble dryer platform only. FAMILY-COUNT CAVEAT, stated honestly: verified in ONE genuine Arcelik/Beko service handbook covering TWO named platforms (BX B7S and BLED) — the code-to-meaning row is stated once in a table that applies to both named platforms. No second standalone dryer handbook was found publicly.
Most common causeA short circuit in the compressor NTC wiring or sensor. There is no 'heater NTC' on this platform — do not diagnose the heating element for this code.
Safe to check yourself?No — the handbook gives no home fix for E10. A technician runs the service-test program; do not use the unsourced heater-test figures found on other sites.

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because Beko dryer E10 carries a family-count caveat this page states honestly and prominently: E10's meaning is verified in ONE genuine manufacturer document — the Beko/Arcelik service handbook below — which covers TWO named platforms (BX B7S and BLED) with separate service-test programs for each. That is the basis for the 2-family count: the code-to-meaning row is stated once in a table that applies to both named platforms. No second standalone dryer handbook was found publicly. The meaning itself ('Compressor NTC: Short Circuit') is not in doubt — it is verbatim from the handbook. Scope: E10 here is the BX B7S/BLED heat-pump dryer platform only. The compressor NTC is the heat-pump compressor temperature sensor — there is NO 'heater NTC' on this platform. Do NOT merge E10 with washer E10 (door lock / water inlet — contested between sources) or Electrolux E10 (water inlet).

What the machine does when E10 fires

The display shows E10 and the dryer stops — the compressor temperature sensor circuit is shorted. In the service test, Step 8 shows 'h:SC' for this fault, and the test cannot pass to the next step while an NTC or voltage error is present.

Common causes

  1. Short circuit in the compressor NTC wiring or sensor — the sensor-circuit fault the handbook's row names.
  2. Wrong-part diagnosis from other sites: the leading dryer guide calls E10 a 'Heater NTC sensor short circuit error' in a related-code line under E09, and prescribes heating-element coil tests (32.3 Ω / 73.6 Ω) for a compressor-sensor fault. There is no 'heater NTC' on this platform.
  3. Wrong-appliance reading — Beko washer E10 is a door lock or water inlet fault (contested between sources); Electrolux E10 is a water inlet fault. Neither is the dryer meaning.

Safe checks, in order

  1. E10 is a sensor-circuit fault, not a user-serviceable cleaning job — the handbook gives no home fix, so do not open the machine or probe the sensor wiring.
  2. A service technician runs the handbook's service-test program: Step 8 checks the door and compressor NTCs and shows 'h:SC' for a compressor NTC short circuit; if an NTC or voltage error occurs, the test is not allowed to pass to the next step.
  3. Step 15 runs the compressor: 'Crn' appears on display, the compressor starts to work, the compressor NTC value appears and starts to increase, and when it reaches 50–55 °C, 'C on' appears, the 'Sound Warning Led' starts to blink and the test may pass to the next step.
  4. Do NOT use the figures from other sites (15.2–19.5 kΩ; heater coils 32.3 Ω / 73.6 Ω): they are unsourced, absent from the handbook, and for the wrong part.

The handbook gives NO E10-specific home procedure and NO resistance values, and its service test has NO heater steps (Steps 9–11 are fan, pump, motor; Step 15 is the compressor). Anything beyond a technician's service test is not supported by the handbook.

When to call a technician

  • E10 is shown at all — the compressor NTC circuit is shorted and needs a technician with the service-test program.
  • The service test shows 'h:SC' at Step 8 and blocks progress — that is a technician diagnosis, not a cleaning job.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or other fault codes alongside E10 — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

E10 is a sensor fault in a mains-powered heat-pump dryer: never open the machine or probe the NTC wiring yourself. The service-test program is technician work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E10 mean on a Beko tumble dryer?

Compressor NTC: Short Circuit. The heat-pump compressor temperature sensor circuit is shorted.

What is the compressor NTC?

The temperature sensor on the compressor circuit of a heat-pump dryer. It is NOT a heater sensor — this platform has no 'heater NTC'.

Why do other sites say E10 is a water-inlet or door-lock fault?

Those are WASHER E10 meanings (which disagree with each other — door lock per one source, water inlet per another) and an Electrolux code table pasted onto a Beko page. Dryer E10 is the compressor NTC short circuit.

Can I fix E10 myself?

The handbook gives no home fix. A technician runs the service-test program (Step 8 shows 'h:SC'; Step 15 runs the compressor to 50–55 °C) and checks the sensor harness and sockets.

What is the difference between E09, E10 and E11?

All three are the same compressor sensor in different fault modes: E09 open circuit, E10 short circuit, E11 overheat.
