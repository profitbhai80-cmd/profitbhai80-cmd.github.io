Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because Beko dryer E16 carries a family-count caveat this page states honestly and prominently: E16's meaning is verified in ONE genuine manufacturer document — the Beko/Arcelik service handbook below — which covers TWO named platforms (BX B7S and BLED) with separate service-test programs for each. That is the basis for the 2-family count: the code-to-meaning row is stated once in a table that applies to both named platforms. No second standalone dryer handbook was found publicly. The meaning itself ('Low Voltage (Lower than 165V)') is not in doubt — it is verbatim from the handbook, including the exact 165 V threshold. Scope: E16 here is the BX B7S/BLED heat-pump dryer platform only. On Beko dryers, voltage faults are E16/E17 — NEVER E08 (other brands put voltage on E08). Do NOT merge E16 with washer E29 (supply self-protection) or Cylinda fridge E08 (low voltage below 170 V — a different threshold on a different appliance).

What the machine does when E16 fires

The display shows E16 and the dryer stops — the mains supply has dropped below 165 V. In the service test, a voltage error blocks progress: Step 8 says 'If NTC and voltage error occurs, it is not allowed to pass to the next step.'

Common causes

  1. Low mains supply — the house voltage has dropped below 165 V. This is a supply fault, not a dryer fault.
  2. Internal power components / main board fault — only if the outlet reads a normal 220–240 Vac but E16 persists; a technician's check, not a first step.
  3. Wrong threshold from other sites: the leading dryer guide says 'typically below 170 Vac' — 5 V wrong. A supply at 167 V is above the handbook threshold and should NOT trigger E16; a supply at 160 V should.
  4. Wrong-appliance reading — Beko washer E29 is the washer's supply self-protection code, not E16.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Check the mains voltage at the outlet (normal range 220–240 Vac). If the supply is below 165 V, the fault is in the house supply — wait for it to stabilise or call an electrician.
  2. If the outlet reads a normal 220–240 Vac but E16 persists, the dryer's internal power components / main board are the technician's check — do not present board replacement as the first step.
  3. Do NOT use the 'below 170 Vac' threshold from other sites — the handbook says 'Lower than 165V'. And do NOT rely on the 'standby mode until the voltage stabilizes' claim — the handbook gives it no support; it says only that a voltage error blocks the service test.
  4. Do not open the dryer or probe the power components yourself — internal power work is a technician's job.

The handbook gives NO E16-specific home procedure beyond the table row and the one service-test line (Step 8). It gives no 'standby mode' behaviour, no board-replacement diagnostic path, and no other voltage figures — this page invents none. The 220–240 Vac normal range is the standard mains range used for the outlet check.

When to call a technician

  • E16 persists while the outlet reads a normal 220–240 Vac — the dryer's internal power components need a technician.
  • The service test is blocked at Step 8 by the voltage error — that is a technician diagnosis.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or other fault codes alongside E16 — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

E16 is a mains-supply fault: never open the dryer or probe its power components yourself. If the house supply is low, call an electrician; if the outlet is normal but E16 persists, call a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E16 mean on a Beko tumble dryer?

Low Voltage: the mains supply has dropped below 165 V.

Is 167 V low enough to trigger E16?

No. The fault triggers BELOW 165 V, so a supply at 167 V is above the threshold and should NOT trigger E16; a supply at 160 V should. Other sites saying 'below 170 V' are wrong by 5 V — under their threshold a 167 V supply would wrongly count as a fault.

What should I do for E16?

Check the voltage at the outlet. If it is below 165 V, the problem is the house supply — wait for it to stabilise or call an electrician. If the outlet reads a normal 220–240 Vac, the dryer needs a technician.

Is E16 the same as the voltage fault on other dryers?

No. Vestel and Daewoo dryers use E08 for voltage fluctuation; Beko uses E16 (low) and E17 (high). Same fault concept, different code.

Is dryer E16 the same as washer E29?

Related but not the same token: washers express supply problems as E29 (self-protection). Dryer E16 is the low-voltage code on BX B7S/BLED models.
