Beko Dishwasher Error Code E02

Quick answer

Error codeE02 — Beko Dishwasher
What it meansE02 means the water cut-off warning: the mains water is cut or the inlet tap is turned off, so the machine cannot take water in. The E:02 icon flashes on the programme indicator, the washing programme pauses, and the machine re-checks the inlet — when the water is restored, the icon disappears by itself and the programme resumes. All seven Beko manuals we checked use identical wording; do not confuse with E:01, which is the overflowing warning (leak/float).
Most common causeThe mains water supply is interrupted, the inlet tap is closed, the inlet hose is kinked, or the inlet filter/flow meter is blocked.
Safe to check yourself?Yes — the manual's own sequence is all external: restore the water, open the tap, check the hose and inlet filter. No reset is even needed; the code clears itself.
Checked againstBeko DFN26210W user manual (official PDF hosted on beko.com); Beko DDT38532XIHHW user manual (USA); Beko DSN28430X user manual; Beko DIN48430 user manual; Beko BDBU1430X37 user manual; Beko BDIS38122Q user manual; Beko BDIN38640C user manual

Applies to — read this first

E:02 is one of Beko's most consistent codes: freestanding (DFN), USA (DDT), integrated (DIN), and slim integrated (BDIS/BDIN) families all use the identical "Water cut-off warning" wording. What you must get right is the E:01/E:02 split, which the manuals place side by side: E:01 = overflowing warning (water leak, float switch); E:02 = water cut-off (no water coming in). Multiple ranking pages fuse or swap these two — the manuals do not.

The Beko service-manual detail (quoted via a white-goods thread): incoming water is monitored through a water counter (flow meter); if no pulses are detected during the Water Inlet step, a No Water condition occurs and the machine waits until a water signal is detected, then resumes — the mechanism behind the self-clearing behavior.

What the machine does when E02 fires

The E:02 icon flashes on the programme indicator and the washing programme pauses — the machine does not error out and lock. It keeps re-checking the water inlet on its own; when water is restored, the icon disappears after a while and the programme resumes by itself. No button sequence is needed.

Common causes

  1. The mains water supply is interrupted or the inlet tap is closed.
  2. The inlet hose is kinked, squashed, or trapped behind the machine.
  3. The small filter at the inlet hose connection is blocked with grit or limescale.
  4. The flow meter detects no water pulses during the Water Inlet step (service-manual mechanism).
  5. Water pressure is too low for the machine to register intake.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Check another tap in the house — if the mains water is cut, wait for it to be restored; the machine resumes by itself.
  2. Open the dishwasher's inlet tap fully.
  3. Straighten the inlet hose and check it is not squashed behind the machine.
  4. With the tap closed, disconnect the inlet hose at the tap end and rinse its small filter; reconnect firmly.
  5. Wait — the icon clears by itself once water flows; do not keep power-cycling. If E:02 stays on with a known-good supply and clean filter, the inlet valve or flow meter needs a technician.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the icon clears. E:02 is self-clearing by design — the most common mistake (and what the manuals' own wording prevents) is treating it like a hard fault and replacing parts. If a check needs the machine opened or a valve tested, it is no longer a safe home check — skip to the technician section.

When to call a technician

  • E:02 stays on with a confirmed water supply and a clean inlet filter.
  • The machine never detects water (flow meter or inlet-valve fault).
  • Any leak under the machine — note: a leak is the E:01 overflowing warning, a different fault with different checks.
  • Water enters but the programme never resumes.

Safety first

Always close the water tap before disconnecting the inlet hose, and switch the machine off at the wall. Water and electricity are a dangerous mix — mop up spills immediately and never work with wet hands on a powered machine. Inlet-valve electrical tests are service-staff work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E02 mean on a Beko dishwasher?

E:02 is the water cut-off warning: the mains water is cut or the inlet tap is turned off, so the machine cannot take water in. The programme pauses and resumes by itself once water is restored — no reset needed. Seven Beko manuals use identical wording.

Do I need to reset the dishwasher after E02?

No. The manuals state the E:02 icon disappears by itself "after a while" and the washing programme resumes once water is restored. Just fix the water supply and wait.

What is the difference between Beko E01 and E02?

E:01 = overflowing warning (too much water, leak, float switch); E:02 = water cut-off warning (no water coming in). The manuals place them side by side. Several ranking pages fuse or swap them — one calls E02 a heating failure, which no Beko manual supports.

Why does my Beko dishwasher show E02 but there is no leak?

There shouldn't be a leak with E02 — a leak under the machine is the E:01 overflowing warning. E02 is the opposite fault: water is not reaching the machine. Check the tap, the mains supply, the inlet hose, and the inlet filter.
