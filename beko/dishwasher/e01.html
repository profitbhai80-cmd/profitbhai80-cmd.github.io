Beko Dishwasher Error Code E01

Quick answer

Error codeE01 — Beko Dishwasher
What it means“Chassis water filling fault” — the anti-flood float switch detected water in the machine’s base. The error algorithm is activated and the cycle ends.
Most common causeA leak from a hose, seal or component, or the machine taking in too much water (overflow). Water can also reach the base during cleaning, moving or tilting the machine, or from excessive suds.
Safe to check yourself?Yes — for the basic external checks below. Do not open the machine’s body or reach the float switch.
Checked againstBeko BDIS16030 user manual; Beko BDIN38460 manual; Beko BDIN4S560WP manual; Beko Australia official dishwasher support

Applies to — read this first

E01 is the same fault on every Beko dishwasher family covered here, but it is shown differently depending on the model. Beko’s manuals also warn that “the error codes can change depend the model,” so check your own manual too.

Model typeHow this fault appears
Models with a digital display (e.g. BDIS16030 family)E01 Warning on the display
Models without a display (e.g. BDIN38460, BDIN4S560WP family)P1 blinks on the programme indicator and the power LED flashes — same “chassis water filling fault”, no E01 text

Find your model number on the rating label if you are not sure which type you have — see how to find your model number.

Common causes

  1. Water has leaked into the base/chassis from a hose, the door seal, or an internal component — the float switch trips and E01 is shown.
  2. The machine took in too much water (overflow). As long as the overflowing continues, the safety algorithm stays active and runs the drain pump to discharge the water — so a pump running at the start of a cycle is part of this fault, not a separate pump failure.
  3. Water got into the base during filter cleaning, while moving the machine, or after it was tilted on its side.
  4. Excessive suds (wrong detergent) pushed water down into the base tray.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the dishwasher off, unplug it, and close the water valve — these are the manual’s own first steps.
  2. Look under and around the machine for a visible leak. Clean the filters and check the drain hose and the drain connection/traps under the sink for obstructions, as Beko’s support advises before anything else.
  3. If the unit is freestanding and moves freely: pull it out, tilt it back no more than 45 degrees with a towel ready so the water in the base drains out. This resets the flood switch. Do not tilt a built-in unit you cannot move safely.
  4. Wipe up any spilled water, put the machine back, restore power, and run a short cycle while watching for leaks returning.
  5. If E01 shows continuously, stop: the manual’s next step is to contact an authorized service. Repeating the tilt may clear the symptom but will not fix an active leak.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. If a check needs a panel removed, a part tested, or a wire touched, it is no longer a safe home check — skip to the technician section.

When to call a technician

  • E01 returns continuously even after the base has been drained — the manual’s instruction at this point is authorized service.
  • Water keeps collecting in the base, which means there is an active leak you cannot reach.
  • Reaching the float switch or any internal part would require removing panels or the bottom plate.
  • The machine is still under warranty — let the manufacturer’s service handle it for free.

Safety first

Always switch off and unplug the dishwasher before moving it or tilting it, and close the water tap first. Water and electricity are a dangerous mix — never work on a machine that is connected to power with wet hands or a wet floor.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E01 mean on a Beko dishwasher?

E01 means “Chassis water filling fault.” The anti-flood float switch in the machine’s base detected water — from a leak or from the machine taking in too much water. The error algorithm is activated and the cycle ends. On models without a display, the same fault shows as a blinking P1 on the programme indicator with the power LED flashing.

Why does the drain pump keep running when E01 appears?

That is the designed safety response, not a pump fault. The manual says that as long as the overflowing continues, the safety algorithm stays active and tries to discharge the water. The pump running at the start of a cycle is the machine protecting itself.

How do I reset E01 on a Beko dishwasher?

There is no button reset in the manuals. Switch off and unplug the dishwasher, close the water valve, and clear the water from the base — on a freestanding unit that moves freely, tilt it back no more than 45 degrees with a towel ready, which resets the flood switch. If E01 shows continuously, the manual says to contact an authorized service.

Is E01 the same as P1 blinking on a Beko dishwasher?

Yes. Models with a digital display show E01; models without a display indicate the same “chassis water filling fault” by blinking P1 on the programme indicator while the power LED flashes. Beko’s own support confirms the P1 icon is the E01 error.

Why do other websites give different meanings for Beko E01?

Because most pages describe the symptom instead of the cause, or copy each other. Some claim E01 means the machine is not receiving enough water — that is the manual’s E02 (water outage), not E01. Others blame drainage, the door lock, or the power supply, which no Beko manual lists for E01. The manuals call it “chassis water filling fault”: water in the base tripping the flood switch.
