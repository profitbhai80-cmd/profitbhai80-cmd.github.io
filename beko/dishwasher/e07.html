Beko Dishwasher Error Code E07

Quick answer

Error codeE07 — Beko Dishwasher
What it meansE07 means the flowmeter is faulty. The flowmeter measures incoming water; when it fails, the error algorithm activates and the cycle ends. On display models the code shows as E07; on models without a display the same fault is P2 + F1 + F2 flashing. Eight Beko manuals print the identical §9.1 row. Do not confuse with E02 (water outage / filter blocked), which is a supply problem that clears when water returns — E07 is the measuring device itself, and the manual's remedy is to cut the electricity, close the valve, and contact authorized service.
Most common causeThe flowmeter that measures incoming water is faulty — the measuring device itself, not the water supply.
Safe to check yourself?Limited. The manual's only user steps are to cut off the electricity, close the valve, and contact authorized service when the error is continuous. No flowmeter inspection, testing or replacement is a user step.

Applies to — read this first

E07 is one of Beko's most consistent dishwasher codes: freestanding (BDFN), integrated (BDIN), slim (BDIS) and Argos-support families all print the identical §9.1 error-table row. What you must get right is the display split, which no ranking page mentions: display models show 'E07', while models without a display signal the same fault by P2 + F1 + F2 flashing. And the single most useful distinction on this page is E07 versus E02, from the same table: E02 is a water-supply problem that clears when water returns; E07 is the measuring device itself failing, and the cycle ends.

E07 exists only on the newer §9.1-table families. Older 2020-era DIN/DFN families use named warnings only ('Water cut-off warning', 'Overflowing warning') and have no E07 at all. From the SAME §9.1 table: E06 = temperature sensor faulty (P2 + P6 flashes) — a different component. E03, E04, E05, E08 and E15 do not exist in any Beko dishwasher manual despite ranking pages listing them; the flowmeter meaning belongs to E07 alone, not to any invented E15.

What the machine does when E07 fires

The error algorithm is activated and the cycle ends — E07 is not a pause-and-resume fault like E02. The machine stops because it can no longer measure incoming water, and the manual's remedy for a continuous error is to cut the electricity, close the valve, and contact authorized service.

Common causes

  1. The flowmeter (water counter) that measures incoming water has failed.
  2. The flowmeter wiring or its connection to the control board is loose or damaged.
  3. The control board is not receiving flowmeter pulses during the Water Inlet step.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the code: on a display model it reads E07; on a model without a display, P2, F1 and F2 flash together. If P2 + P6 flash instead, that is E06 (temperature sensor), a different fault.
  2. Note that the cycle ends — unlike E02, E07 does not clear by itself when water returns, because the measuring device itself is faulty.
  3. If the error is continuous, cut off the electricity to the dishwasher, as the manual instructs.
  4. Close the water valve, as the manual instructs.
  5. Get in touch with the authorized service and report the code plus the LED pattern. Do not attempt flowmeter testing or replacement yourself.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the situation is clear. E07 has no user-serviceable fix in the manual: the steps above are identification plus the manual's own shutdown-and-service sequence, not a repair sequence. If a check needs the machine opened or a part tested, it is no longer a safe home check — skip to the technician section.

When to call a technician

  • E07 is continuous across programmes — the flowmeter fault is persistent, not a one-off glitch.
  • The cycle ends every time rather than pausing and resuming (that pause-and-resume pattern is E02, not E07).
  • Any leak, burning smell, or water near electrical parts — keep the power off and the valve closed and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

Cut the electricity and close the water valve as the manual instructs, and do not open the dishwasher's base or side panels to reach the flowmeter — it is an internal, mains-connected component. If you notice any leak, burning smell, or water near electrics, leave the valve closed and the power off and call a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E07 mean on a Beko dishwasher?

E07 means the flowmeter is faulty. Eight Beko manuals print the identical §9.1 row: 'E07 Warning, P2 flashes, F1 and F2 flash, Flowmeter is faulty.' On models without a display, the same fault shows as P2 + F1 + F2 flashing.

What is the difference between Beko dishwasher E07 and E02?

E02 = water outage error / filter blocked: a supply problem — the programme pauses and resumes by itself when water is restored. E07 = flowmeter faulty: the device that measures incoming water has failed, the error algorithm activates and the cycle ends. Same table, different faults: check the tap and inlet filter for E02; E07 needs authorized service.

Can I fix Beko dishwasher E07 myself?

The manual's own steps are only: if the error is continuous, cut off the electricity, close the valve, and get in touch with the authorized service. The flowmeter is an internal part with no user-serviceable step in the manual — do not open the machine to reach it.

Why does my Beko dishwasher manual not mention E07?

Only the newer §9.1-table families (BDIS, BDIN, BDFN and Argos-support models) document E07. Older 2020-era families use named warnings only and have no E-codes at all — E07 genuinely does not apply to those machines. The manuals also note 'The error codes can change depend the model.'
