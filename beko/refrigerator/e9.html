Beko Refrigerator Error Code E9

Quick answer

Error codeE9 — Beko Refrigerator
What it meansE9 = icematic motor error - the ice-maker (icematic) motor has failed. On Cosmos SBS models (G91631NE-G91634NE) and FOUREVER 3-door models (G91629NWU). CAVEAT, stated honestly: both manuals are U4-platform documents with the same section structure and the same KN6/KN10 sockets - two product families, one control platform. Same caveat class as the published Hisense refrigerator F2 page.
Most common causeThe icematic motor has failed. The handbooks name no sub-cause beyond the motor itself - E9 is a MOTOR fault, not a sensor fault (that is E8).
Safe to check yourself?No - the handbooks give no user reset for E9. The service-test motor-rotation check is a service procedure - if you are not comfortable opening the control area or running a service test, call a service centre.
Checked againstArcelik BEKO COSMOS Service Handbook, section 13.7 (p.108) + Arcelik BEKO G91629NWU Service Manual, section 9.7 (p.41)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because Beko refrigerator E9 carries a platform caveat this page states honestly and prominently: both documents are U4-platform documents with the same section 13 / section 9 structure and the same KN6/KN10 sockets - two product families (SBS Cosmos vs FOUREVER 3-door wardrobe), one control platform. Same caveat class as the published Hisense refrigerator F2 page. E9 in this meaning exists only on the Cosmos/American U4 platform - not on bottom-mount models (where E09 means "freezer compartment is not cold enough", usually after a long power failure), not on Gorenje/ASKO E9 (refrigerator door sensor malfunction), and not on Beko washing machines (WM E09 is a door lock fault).

What the machine does when E9 fires

The handbooks give no user-facing behaviour for E9 beyond the fault itself. The ukwhitegoods transcription adds a timing detail ("Ice machine switch must change during initialisation or within 1 minute while the ice machine is rotating") that does NOT appear in either handbook's flowcharts - uncorroborated, cited as a transcription only.

Common causes

  1. Icematic motor failed - the handbooks' own fault definition.
  2. Wrong-code reading - the JustAnswer sensor-replacement advice is E8's meaning, not E9's; the water-supply cause in one AI answer belongs to neither E8 nor E9 on this platform; E09 (bottom-mount), Gorenje/ASKO E9 (door sensor) and Beko WM E09 (door lock) are different codes on different systems.

Safe checks, in order

  1. In service test, rotate the icematic motor in the pouring direction and check whether there is 12 V in the correcting direction (Cosmos handbook flowchart, p.246; corroborated by the G91629NWU section 9.7 flowchart).
  2. Service-test entry (Cosmos V3): Display+Vacation 2 seconds in the first 60 seconds after energising. The ASD V0 variant is Select+Set 3 seconds within 30 seconds of energising - do not conflate the two.
  3. Do NOT publish appliancemode's "Test the motor for continuity" / "Replace the ice maker assembly" as manual steps - they are its own additions, not handbook steps. Do NOT publish the JustAnswer sensor-replacement advice. Do NOT publish the American page's 1-minute switch-timing rule as a manual fact.

The steps above are the handbook flowchart's steps, printed exactly as far as the text layer goes. The ukwhitegoods ASD V0 transcription adds: "Ice machine failure. Ice machine switch must change during initialisation or within 1 minute while the ice machine is rotating. Check wiring. Replace ice maker. Check control module." - a transcription, not a manual, cited as such. The service-test work is a service procedure.

When to call a technician

  • E9 is displayed at all - the service-test motor check is a service procedure; there is no homeowner fix.
  • The icematic motor does not rotate in the pouring direction, or the 12 V check fails - the motor or its supply needs a technician.
  • Any burning smell, visibly damaged wiring, or other fault codes alongside E9 - switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

E9 is a motor fault in a mains-powered refrigerator: never open the control area or probe the icematic motor wiring yourself. The service-test motor check is service work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E9 mean on a Beko refrigerator?

The ice-maker (icematic) motor has failed. Verified in two Arcelik service manuals: the COSMOS handbook (section 13.7, models G91631NE-G91634NE) and the G91629NWU service manual (section 9.7). It is the motor that rotates the ice maker - not the sensor (that is E8).

Is E9 a sensor fault?

No. Two JustAnswer experts say E9 is an ice-maker sensor fault - that is wrong. E8 is the icematic air sensor; E9 is the icematic motor. Replacing the sensor will not fix E9.

Is E9 the same as E09?

No. E9 (no leading zero) is a Cosmos/American SxS service code: icematic motor error. E09 is a bottom-mount warning-table code that means "freezer compartment is not cold enough" (usually after a long power failure) - and no Beko manual defines its meaning. Different systems, different pages.

How do I check it?

Per the Cosmos handbook flowchart: in service test, rotate the icematic motor in the pouring direction and check whether there is 12 V in the correcting direction. This is a service procedure - if you are not comfortable opening the control area or running a service test, call a service centre.

Is E9 a water-supply problem?

No. One AI answer suggests checking the water inlet valve for E9 - that belongs to neither E8 nor E9 on this platform. E9 is a motor fault; the water supply is a separate system.
