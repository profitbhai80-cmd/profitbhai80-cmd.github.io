Midea Air Conditioner Error Code E4

Quick answer

Error codeE4 — Midea Air Conditioner
What it meansE4 on a Midea mini split means the indoor room temperature sensor (T1) is open circuit or short circuit: the sensor that measures room air at the indoor unit has failed, so its sampling voltage falls outside the guide's 0.06V–4.94V window. The Midea manuals name three causes — a wiring mistake, a faulty sensor, or a faulty indoor PCB — and the guide's tree checks connections, then sensor resistance, then the sensor, then the PCB. Power-cycle once; if E4 returns, the sensor circuit needs a qualified technician.
Most common causeThe indoor room-temperature sensor (T1) has gone open or short circuit, or its wiring to the indoor PCB is faulty — the control can no longer read room temperature.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home check is a single power-cycle: switch the unit off, wait, and switch it back on — if E4 clears it was a transient detection glitch. Anything beyond that (checking the sensor's connections, measuring its resistance, or testing the PCB) is technician work under the guide's own tree; never open the indoor unit's electrical covers yourself.
Checked againstMidea High Wall System Error Codes and Fault Diagnosis Guide (manuals.plus mirror of the Midea service document) — E4/E5/F1/F2/F3 Temperature sensor failure section, incl. 0.06V/4.94V decision condition and E4 = T1 mapping; Midea MSMBB-12CRDN1-MPOW Air Conditioner error table (manuals.plus) — E4 indoor room temperature sensor T1 open circuit or short circuit, timer lamp extinguished, operation lamp flash 5; Midea Air Conditioner Owner's Manual, document 16122500000082 (20150328) — current-generation indoor table: indoor room temperature sensor open/short circuit E4, timer OFF, running lamp 5 flashes; Midea manuals.plus current-generation cassette/ducted/underceiling indoor table — open/short circuit of T1 temperature sensor E4, timer extinguished, operation lamp 5 flashes; Midea Neola Series tables (manuals.plus) — fixed-speed split: T1 fault coded E5; inverter split: E4 = T1 (flash 5), E5 = T2 (flash 6); Midea MDV-D22Q1/N1-D Technical Manual (MDV COMMERCIAL — do not borrow) — indoor ambient temperature sensor error is E2 on MDV, not E4; Midea MSX-series tables (do not borrow) — E4 = starting current exceeded after four starts, a different platform's fault, not the T1 sensor

Applies to — read this first

Good news for diagnosis: on the Midea mini-split families we checked, E4 is consistent — it is always the indoor room temperature sensor (T1) open or short circuit, never a pressure, refrigerant or outdoor-sensor fault. What you must NOT do is borrow meanings from other Midea platforms: on MDV commercial tables the indoor ambient sensor is E2, and on MSX-series tables E4 is a starting-current fault — neither applies to the mini-split indoor display. The one real series split inside the mini-split family is the fixed-speed Neola swap (see the series table): on fixed-speed Neola models the same T1 fault is coded E5, not E4.

The High Wall guide lists the sensor codes together as E4/E5/F1/F2/F3 — E4 is only the T1 (indoor ambient) member of that group. E5 on current/inverter models is the T2 indoor coil sensor, a different sensor; do not treat an E5 reading as E4. The resistance checks and PCB tests in the guide are technician checks, quoted here only so you know what the technician will test.

What the machine does when E4 fires

The indoor unit displays E4 — on lamp-only models the timer lamp is OFF and the operation/running lamp flashes 5 times — because the T1 sampling voltage has gone outside the guide's 0.06V–4.94V window. With its room-temperature reading gone, the control cannot regulate the room to the setpoint; the manuals describe the display and the fault, not a separate shutdown sequence, so do not expect the unit to behave like a protection shutdown (E1/E3) — the symptom is lost temperature control, not a pressure or fan event.

Common causes

  1. A wiring mistake or loose connection between the T1 temperature sensor and the indoor main PCB — the first cause the High Wall guide names, and the first branch of its action tree.
  2. A faulty T1 sensor itself — the room-temperature thermistor gone open circuit or short circuit, so its sampling voltage falls below 0.06V or rises above 4.94V.
  3. A damaged sensor cable or poor sensor placement — the guide tells the technician to check the connections, the placement of the sensor, and the cable condition before condemning any part.
  4. A faulty indoor main PCB — the guide's final verdict when the connections are good and the sensor resistance is normal but E4 persists.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off, wait a minute, and switch it back on. If E4 clears, it was a transient detection glitch — keep an eye on it, because a repeat points to the sensor circuit.
  2. Note exactly what the display shows: E4 on a display model, or timer lamp OFF with the operation lamp flashing 5 times on a lamp-only model. If a different lamp count flashes, check the matching code instead (6 flashes is E5, the T2 coil sensor).
  3. If E4 returns after the restart, stop: the guide's next steps are checking the T1 sensor's connections to the indoor main PCB, then measuring the sensor's resistance against the guide's curve tables (and via Info Enquiry Mode) — all of which mean opening the indoor unit and handling its electronics. That is technician work. If the connections are good but the resistance is out of range, the sensor gets replaced; if the resistance is normal, the guide condemns the indoor main PCB.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. E4 has no deeper home diagnostic in any manual — power-cycle, identify the lamp pattern, then the technician's connection/resistance/PCB tree. Never open the indoor unit's electrical covers or test a live board.

When to call a technician

  • E4 returns after a power-cycle — the T1 sensor circuit fault is genuine, not a glitch.
  • The T1 sensor's sampling voltage is outside the guide's 0.06V–4.94V window, or its resistance does not match the curve tables — faulty sensor or wiring, per the guide.
  • Connections good and sensor resistance normal but E4 persists — the guide condemns the indoor main PCB.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or scorching near the indoor unit — switch off immediately and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

E4 involves mains-powered indoor electronics: never open the indoor unit's electrical covers or touch sensor or PCB terminals while investigating it. Switch the power off before any visible check, and never work on a live board. The sensor-resistance and PCB verdicts in the guide use live measurements and belong to a qualified technician. If E4 persists after a power-cycle, switch the unit off and call a qualified technician — never attempt refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E4 mean on a Midea mini split?

E4 means the indoor room temperature sensor (T1) is open circuit or short circuit: the sensor that measures room air at the indoor unit has failed, so its sampling voltage falls below 0.06V or rises above 4.94V and the LED displays E4. The Midea High Wall Fault Diagnosis Guide defines it exactly this way, and the MSMBB-12CRDN1-MPOW table and Owner's Manual 16122500000082 agree.

How do I clear Midea mini split E4?

Switch the unit off and back on — if E4 was a transient detection glitch it clears. If it returns, the fault is genuine: the manual's tree is connections first, then sensor resistance, then the sensor, then the indoor PCB. Connection, resistance and PCB checks are technician work; there is no deeper home fix.

Is Midea E4 the same as E5?

No — on current and inverter models they are different sensors: E4 = T1 indoor room (ambient) sensor, E5 = T2 indoor coil (evaporator) sensor. The one exception is the older fixed-speed Neola series, where the same T1 fault is coded E5 — so check your series before deciding which sensor is at fault.

Why can't my air conditioner control the room temperature with E4?

Because the control has lost its room-temperature reading: the T1 sensor is what tells the unit how warm the room actually is. With E4 the unit cannot regulate to your setpoint — it is flying blind on room temperature, which is why the fault needs a technician rather than a setting change.

What is the T1 sensor and where is it?

T1 is the indoor ambient (room) temperature sensor — the thermistor that samples the room air entering the indoor unit. It is an internal component wired to the indoor main PCB; checking its connections or resistance means opening the indoor unit, which is technician work, not a home check.
