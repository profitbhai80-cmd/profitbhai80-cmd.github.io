Midea Air Conditioner Error Code E2

Quick answer

Error codeE2 — Midea Air Conditioner
What it meansE2 on a Midea mini split means a zero-crossing signal detection error: the indoor PCB cannot detect the correct zero-crossing signal time interval. The Midea manuals name three causes — bad power-cable connections, a faulty power supply, or a faulty indoor PCB — and the guide states the PCB then cannot control the AC fan motor speed correctly. Check the power supply and connections, restart once, and if E2 returns the indoor PCB is defective and needs a qualified technician.
Most common causeBad power-cable connections, a faulty power supply, or a faulty indoor PCB that cannot detect the zero-crossing signal time interval.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home checks are: confirm the power supply is good (breaker/isolator on, supply stable) and restart the system — the manuals' own first steps. Anything beyond that (opening the indoor unit, testing the PCB) is technician work; if power and connections are good and E2 persists, the manuals condemn the indoor PCB.

Applies to — read this first

Good news for diagnosis: on the Midea mini-split families we checked, E2 is consistent — it is always the zero-crossing signal detection error, never a temperature-sensor or pressure fault. What you must NOT do is borrow meanings from other Midea platforms: on MDV ducted commercial systems E2 is the indoor ambient temperature sensor error, on the outdoor-unit display E2 is a pipe temperature sensor code, and on VRF network tables E2 is a communication error — none of those apply to the mini-split indoor display.

The current-generation cassette/ducted indoor table in the 20150328 owner's manual omits E2 entirely (it lists E0, E1, E3, E4, E5...). Do not use that table as an E2 source; the three sources in the series table below are the E2 authorities. E2 is an indoor-PCB fault in the end — the safe home checks only establish whether power and connections are good.

What the machine does when E2 fires

The indoor unit displays E2 and the system cannot run normally: without the zero-crossing timing reference the indoor PCB cannot control the AC fan motor speed correctly. The unit stops rather than running with uncontrolled fan speed.

Common causes

  1. A bad connection of the power cables — the first cause the High Wall Fault Diagnosis Guide names for E2.
  2. A faulty power supply (unstable or interrupted mains supply to the indoor unit) — the second cause the guide names.
  3. A faulty indoor PCB that can no longer detect the zero-crossing time interval — the guide's final verdict when power and connections are good.
  4. Because the PCB uses the zero-crossing timing to control the AC indoor fan motor speed, a persistent E2 can also show up as a fan that will not run at the right speed — that is a symptom of the same PCB timing fault, not a separate fan fault.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off, then check that the power supply is good: breaker/isolator on, supply stable, no tripped protection. The manuals' first check is the power supply and its connections.
  2. With the power OFF, check that the visible power connections are sound and fully seated. Do not open electrical covers or touch PCB terminals.
  3. Restart the system. If E2 clears and the fan runs normally, the fault was a transient supply or connection issue — keep an eye on it, because a repeat points to the PCB.
  4. If E2 returns with a good power supply and sound connections, stop: both the High Wall guide and the Pridiom flowchart condemn the indoor PCB at this point. Switch the unit off and call a qualified technician for the indoor PCB replacement.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. E2 has no deeper home diagnostic in any manual — power, connections, restart, then the indoor PCB verdict. Never open the indoor unit's electrical covers or test a live board.

When to call a technician

  • E2 returns after a restart with a good power supply and sound connections — the indoor PCB is defective per both manuals.
  • The indoor fan runs at the wrong speed or not at all alongside E2 — the PCB cannot control fan speed without the zero-crossing timing.
  • Any burning smell, tripped breaker, or damaged power cabling — switch off immediately and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

E2 involves mains-powered indoor electronics: never open the indoor unit's electrical covers or touch PCB terminals while investigating it. Switch the power off before checking any visible connection, and never work on a live board. If E2 persists after a power-cycle with a good supply, switch the unit off and call a qualified technician — the indoor PCB is the manuals' verdict, not a DIY part swap. Never attempt refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E2 mean on a Midea mini split?

E2 is the zero-crossing signal detection error: the indoor PCB cannot detect the correct zero-crossing signal time interval. The Midea High Wall Fault Diagnosis Guide defines it exactly this way, and the same meaning appears in the MSMBB-12CRDN1-MPOW error table and the Pridiom multi-zone service document.

How do I clear Midea mini split E2?

Check that the power supply is good (breaker/isolator on, supply stable) and that the power connections are sound, then restart the system. The manuals' own sequence is: power and connections good + restart fails = indoor PCB defective, which is a technician replacement — there is no deeper home fix.

Is Midea E2 a temperature sensor or low-pressure fault?

No. On the mini-split indoor display E2 is the zero-crossing signal error. The temperature-sensor E2 belongs to MDV commercial ducted systems, and the low-pressure E2 appears only on a thin SEO page that contradicts its own E2 section. The indoor room-temperature sensor fault on mini-splits is E4 (T1), not E2.

Why does my indoor fan misbehave with E2?

The High Wall guide states it directly: the PCB is unable to control the zero-crossing time interval and is therefore unable to control the AC fan motor speed correctly. The fan symptom is caused by the same PCB timing fault — fixing the fan alone will not clear E2.
