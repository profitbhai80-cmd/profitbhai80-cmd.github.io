Midea Air Conditioner Error Code E1

Quick answer

Error codeE1 — Midea Air Conditioner
What it meansE1 means a communication malfunction between the indoor and outdoor units: the indoor unit does not receive feedback from the outdoor unit. The trigger is precise — no feedback for 110 seconds, happening four times. Every Midea mini-split manual we checked defines E1 this way.
Most common causeA wiring fault between the indoor and outdoor units, or a faulty indoor or outdoor main PCB.
Safe to check yourself?Partially. You can safely do the manual's power-cycle reset and visually check the interconnecting wiring for damage with the power off. Voltage tests and board replacement are technician work.
Checked againstMidea High Wall System Error Codes and Fault Diagnosis Guide (service document); Midea Air Conditioner Owner's Manual (document 16122500000082, 20150328); Midea MDV-D22Q1/N1-D Technical Manual

Applies to — read this first

Good news for diagnosis: within the Midea mini-split families we checked, E1 is consistent — it is always the indoor/outdoor communication fault. What you must NOT do is borrow meanings from other Midea appliances or from the outdoor unit's own display: Midea dishwasher and washing-machine E1 is a water-inlet fault, Midea portable/window AC E1 is a room temperature sensor error, and a pre-2012 outdoor-unit E1 is an indoor room temperature sensor reading — none of those apply to the indoor display of a mini-split.

On newer Midea models the same fault may display as EL 01 — see our EC page for how EC/EL OC display variants work. Secondary lists claim older-generation splits (MS11D-HRN1, MSX series) used E1 for an EEPROM error; we have not verified that in any Midea manual, so this page covers the current documented meaning only.

What the machine does when E1 fires

The indoor unit stops normal operation and displays E1 — on some models the running lamp flashes twice with the timer lamp off. The unit will not run cooling or heating until communication is restored. This is a protection response, not a second fault.

Common causes

  1. A wiring mistake or damage on the interconnecting cable between the indoor and outdoor units — the manual lists this cause first.
  2. Corroded or loose signal terminals at the indoor or outdoor unit.
  3. A faulty indoor main PCB. The manual's test: if the S-to-N DC voltage shows a fixed value, the indoor board is faulty.
  4. A faulty outdoor main PCB. The manual's test: if the S-to-N reading shows fluctuating positive values only (never negative), the outdoor board is faulty.
  5. An installation wiring error: on 4-core interconnecting wiring, the W spade connection must be moved to 1(L) on the condensing unit — the service guide calls this out explicitly.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Shut off the power supply to the unit completely, wait 5 seconds, then turn it back on. If E1 is gone and does not return, it was a transient communication glitch — the manual's own first step.
  2. With the power OFF, visually inspect the interconnecting cable between the indoor and outdoor units for damage, and check that the signal terminals are tight and free of corrosion. Do not open any electrical covers.
  3. If the unit was recently installed or serviced, confirm the wiring order at both ends matches the installation manual — the service guide specifically calls out the 4-core W-spade-to-1(L) mistake on the condensing unit.
  4. If E1 returns immediately after the power cycle, stop: the manual's next steps are measuring the S-to-N DC voltage and interpreting the reading (fixed value = indoor board fault; fluctuating positive-only = outdoor board fault) — live electrical tests reserved for service staff.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. If a check needs an electrical cover removed, a live voltage measured, or a board touched, it is no longer a safe home check — skip to the technician section.

When to call a technician

  • E1 returns immediately after the power-cycle reset — the fault is in the wiring, a terminal, or one of the main PCBs.
  • The S-to-N DC voltage measurement is needed to tell the indoor board fault from the outdoor board fault — that is a live-electrical test for a technician.
  • Any sign of burnt, melted, or water-damaged wiring or connectors between the units.
  • The unit was recently installed and E1 has shown from day one — an installer wiring mistake is likely, and the installer should return to fix it.

Safety first

Mini-split indoor and outdoor units contain mains-voltage wiring and capacitors that hold charge after switch-off — never remove electrical covers or touch terminals yourself. Only the visual inspection and power-cycle steps above are safe at home. The manual's own DANGER wording stands: the unit has no user-serviceable electrical components. If communication keeps failing, switch the unit off and call a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E1 mean on a Midea mini split?

E1 is a communication malfunction between the indoor and outdoor units: the indoor unit does not receive feedback from the outdoor unit for 110 seconds, and this condition happens four times. Every Midea mini-split manual we checked defines it this way.

How do I reset Midea mini split E1?

Shut off the power supply and turn it back on 5 seconds later — that is the manual's own first step. Then check the wiring between the units with the power off. If E1 comes back, the fault needs the manual's S-to-N voltage test, which is technician work.

Some sites say Midea E1 is high-pressure protection. Is that right?

No. The Midea service manual defines E1 strictly as indoor/outdoor communication. High-pressure protection lives in the P-code family (P2/P6), not E1. Pages claiming E1 = high pressure are contradicting the manufacturer's own manual.

Is Midea mini-split E1 the same as Midea dishwasher E1?

No. Midea dishwasher and washing-machine E1 is a water-inlet fill fault; Midea portable/window AC E1 is a room temperature sensor error. Only the mini-split indoor display's E1 is the communication fault — never borrow meanings across appliances.
