Midea Air Conditioner Error Code E5

Quick answer

Error codeE5 — Midea Air Conditioner
What it meansE5 on a Midea air conditioner (current-generation indoor display, timer OFF, running lamp 6 flashes) means the evaporator coil temperature sensor (T2) is open circuit or short circuit. Pair it correctly: E4 = the indoor room temperature sensor (T1), E5 = the evaporator sensor (T2) — two different sensors, not one generic 'sensor fault'. Older-family tables map E5 differently (for example an outdoor sensor on some older blocks); those mappings must not be borrowed for the current-generation indoor display.
Most common causeThe evaporator coil temperature sensor (T2) circuit is open or shorted — wiring mistake, failed sensor, or faulty PCB, per the Midea High Wall guide's temperature-sensor section.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. A power-cycle and a visual check of obvious sensor wiring damage are the only safe home checks. Measuring and replacing the sensor is technician work.
Checked againstMidea Air Conditioner Owner's Manual, error table row 5; Midea R410A Middle Static Pressure Duct Type Owner's Manual, Table 7-3 row 5; Midea High Wall System Error Codes and Fault Diagnosis Guide, E4/E5/F1/F2/F3 section; Midea service manual, section 9.1

Applies to — read this first

Midea E5 is where the generic lists do real damage: they merge E4 and E5 into one 'indoor temperature sensor fault' and call it done. The manuals say otherwise — E4 is the room sensor (T1) and E5 is the evaporator coil sensor (T2), two different sensors with two different lamp patterns. This page is written for the current-generation indoor display (E5 = timer OFF, 6 flashes) and warns you off the older-family E5 mappings that blogs love to borrow.

The blogs' trap is the merge: 'E4/E5 = indoor temperature sensor fault' hides the T1/T2 split that the manuals make explicitly. If your code is E5 and you follow E4 advice, you are checking the wrong sensor.

What the machine does when E5 fires

The indoor display shows E5 with the timer lamp off and the running lamp flashing 6 times; with the evaporator sensor unreadable, the control cannot trust the coil temperature and the unit does not run normally.

Common causes

  1. The evaporator coil temperature sensor (T2) circuit is open or shorted — the manual's meaning.
  2. A wiring mistake at the sensor-to-PCB connection — the High Wall guide's first named cause.
  3. A faulty sensor itself, or a faulty PCB — the guide's remaining named causes.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Power the unit off, wait a few minutes, and power it back on once — to rule out a start-up glitch.
  2. With the power off, visually check the sensor area for obviously damaged or disconnected wiring — touch nothing inside the electrical enclosure.
  3. If the code returns, stop: sensor circuit diagnosis (resistance checks, sensor or PCB replacement) is technician work per the High Wall guide.

E5 is a circuit fault, not a settings fault — the home side is one clean restart and a visual check. The guide's next steps are resistance measurements at the board, which are not home work.

When to call a technician

  • E5 returns after a restart with no visible wiring damage.
  • Your unit is an older family where E5 has a different meaning — a technician with your model's manual should diagnose it.
  • Any burnt connectors, melted wiring or breaker trips — switch off and call a technician immediately.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

Sensor wiring lives inside the electrical enclosure. Switch power off at the breaker before any visual check, and never probe, disconnect or replace sensors or boards yourself.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E5 mean on a Midea air conditioner?

On the current-generation indoor display E5 means the evaporator coil temperature sensor (T2) is open circuit or short circuit — timer lamp off, running lamp 6 flashes, per the owner's manual, duct manual, High Wall guide and service manual.

Is Midea E5 the same as E4?

No. The manuals pair them as different sensors: E4 = indoor room temperature sensor (T1), E5 = evaporator coil sensor (T2). They are two different sensors with different lamp patterns — never merge them into one 'sensor fault'.

Can I fix Midea E5 by replacing a sensor myself?

No. The High Wall guide's fix path runs through the sensor-to-PCB connections, resistance checks and possibly the PCB — all technician work. The home side is one restart and a visual check.

Why do some sites say Midea E5 is an outdoor sensor fault?

They are borrowing from older/other Midea families where the code map differs. This page covers the current-generation indoor display, where the manuals agree E5 is the evaporator (T2) sensor — do not mix the mappings.

What if E5 keeps coming back?

Call a qualified technician. A returning E5 with intact wiring points to a failed sensor or a faulty PCB — both are beyond the home checks.
