Midea Air Conditioner Error Code P2

Quick answer

Error codeP2 — Midea Air Conditioner
What it meansP2 on a Midea mini split means high temperature protection of the compressor top: the protection trips when the compressor top reaches 120°C (248°F) and resets at 105°C (221°F). The Midea manuals' diagnostic chain runs from compressor operation and connection, through the protector, to the refrigerant system (coil temperature and pressure), gas charging, circuit blockage (capillary or welded points), and finally the outdoor PCB. The safe home steps are one power-cycle, an outdoor-airflow check, and noting whether the compressor runs — protector, refrigerant, blockage, and PCB work are technician work, and refrigerant work is never DIY.
Most common causeThe compressor top overheats past 120°C — blocked outdoor airflow, low refrigerant charge, a circuit blockage, a faulty top protector, or a faulty outdoor PCB.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home steps are: one power-cycle, a visual outdoor-airflow check (leaves, debris, clearance — no covers off), and noting whether the compressor runs when the code appears. Protector testing, refrigerant pressure and charge checks, blockage diagnosis, and PCB work are technician work.

Applies to — read this first

Good news for diagnosis: on the Midea mini-split families we checked, indoor-display P2 is consistent — it is always high temperature protection of the compressor top, never a drainage, condenser, or pressure fault. What you must NOT do is borrow the outdoor-display meaning: on the SAME Pridiom document's outdoor-unit table, P2 means low pressure protection (36K 1x4 units only), and on VRF commercial tables P2 is also low pressure — neither applies to the mini-split indoor display. Also do not confuse P2 with the two neighbouring protections in the same document: exhaust/discharge temperature protection (115°C/90°C) and condenser high-temperature protection (≥65°C) are separate protections with different thresholds.

What the machine does when P2 fires

When the compressor top temperature reaches 120°C, the protection trips (display P2) and the system stops the compressor to prevent damage; it resumes only when the top temperature falls back to 105°C. While P2 is active the unit will not cool or heat, and repeated trips mean the underlying heat cause is still present.

Common causes

  1. Extreme operating heat at the compressor top — the compressor-top temperature switch/protector trips at 120°C (248°F) and resets at 105°C (221°F).
  2. Blocked outdoor-unit airflow or a dirty condenser coil — the outdoor unit cannot reject heat, so the compressor top overheats (the manuals' airflow branch of the P2 chain).
  3. A refrigerant system problem — low charge or a leak, which the manuals check via coil temperature and pressure; low charge makes the compressor run hot.
  4. A blockage in the refrigerant circuit — the manuals name capillary or welded-point blockages as the branch when the abnormality persists after gas charging.
  5. A faulty compressor-top protector — the manuals check whether the protector itself is normal and replace it if not.
  6. A faulty outdoor PCB — the manuals' final branch when compressor, connection, protector, refrigerant, and blockage all check out.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Power-cycle once: switch the unit off, wait, and switch it back on. Note whether P2 returns immediately or only after the compressor has run for a while — that timing helps the technician.
  2. Check the outdoor unit's airflow and ventilation from the outside only: clear leaves, debris, or obstructions around the outdoor coil and confirm clearance. Do not open any cover.
  3. Note whether the compressor operates when the unit runs: the manuals' first diagnostic question is whether the compressor runs at all. Do not touch the compressor or its wiring — just observe from outside.
  4. If P2 returns with clear outdoor airflow, stop: the manuals' remaining steps are the connection check, the protector check, the refrigerant system check (coil temperature and pressure), gas charging, blockage diagnosis (capillary or welded points), and outdoor-PCB replacement — all qualified-technician work, and refrigerant work is never DIY. Switch the unit off and call a qualified technician, and tell them the code is P2 (compressor-top temperature protection, 120/105°C).

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. Only the first three steps are home steps; the rest of the manuals' chain is quoted in the series table so you can follow what the technician does — not so you can do it. Never keep restarting a compressor that keeps tripping on P2.

When to call a technician

  • P2 returns after a power-cycle with clear outdoor airflow — protector, refrigerant, blockage, or outdoor-PCB fault per the manuals.
  • The compressor does not run at all, or runs only briefly before P2 trips — a compressor or protector fault for a technician.
  • Oil stains, ice on refrigerant lines, or a burning smell around the outdoor unit — switch off immediately and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

P2 is a compressor-overheat protection on a mains-voltage, refrigerant-charged system: never open the outdoor unit's electrical box, never touch the compressor or its protector, and never check refrigerant pressure or charge yourself — refrigerant work requires a licensed technician and the correct recovery equipment. If P2 keeps returning, or if you notice a burning smell, oil stains, or ice on refrigerant lines, switch the unit off and call a qualified technician. Never bypass a protection device and never keep restarting a tripping compressor — repeated overheat trips damage the compressor.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does P2 mean on a Midea mini split?

P2 is high temperature protection of the compressor top: the protection trips when the compressor top reaches 120°C (248°F) and resets at 105°C (221°F). The MSMBB-12CRDN1-MPOW error table defines it exactly this way, and the Pridiom multi-zone service document gives the same thresholds and diagnostic chain.

How do I fix Midea mini split P2?

Power-cycle once, check the outdoor unit's airflow is not blocked (leaves, debris, clearance), and note whether the compressor runs. That is the safe home scope. The manuals' remaining steps — protector test, refrigerant coil temperature and pressure check, gas charging, blockage diagnosis, outdoor PCB — are all qualified-technician work, and refrigerant work is never DIY.

Is Midea P2 a drainage or water problem?

No — that claim is invented. No checked Midea mini-split manual defines any P-code as drainage; cassette water-level alarm is EE, not P2. Indoor P2 is compressor-top temperature protection. The drainage claim comes from a Singapore SEO page that also gets other codes wrong.

Some sites say P5 is compressor overheat. Is that P2?

Yes — that is the confusion to avoid. Compressor high-temperature protection IS P2, not P5. The manuals define P5 as indoor-units mode conflict (multi-zone only). Pages claiming P5 = compressor overheat have taken P2's meaning and pinned it to the wrong code.
