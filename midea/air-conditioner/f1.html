Midea Air Conditioner Error Code F1

Quick answer

Error codeF1 — Midea Air Conditioner
What it meansF1 on a Midea mini split means the outdoor room temperature sensor (T4) is open circuit or short circuit: the sensor that measures outdoor air at the outdoor unit has failed, so its sampling voltage falls outside the guide's 0.06V–4.94V window. The Midea manuals name three causes — a wiring mistake, a faulty sensor, or a faulty outdoor PCB — and the guide's tree checks connections, then sensor resistance, then the sensor, then the PCB. Power-cycle once; if F1 returns, the outdoor sensor circuit needs a qualified technician.
Most common causeThe outdoor room-temperature sensor (T4) has gone open or short circuit, or its wiring to the outdoor PCB is faulty — the control can no longer read outdoor ambient temperature.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home check is a single power-cycle: switch the unit off, wait, and switch it back on — if F1 clears it was a transient detection glitch. Anything beyond that (checking the outdoor sensor's connections, measuring its resistance, or testing the outdoor PCB) is technician work under the guide's own tree; never open the outdoor unit's electrical covers yourself.
Checked againstMidea High Wall System Error Codes and Fault Diagnosis Guide (manuals.plus mirror of the Midea service document) — E4/E5/F1/F2/F3 Temperature sensor failure section, incl. 0.06V/4.94V decision condition and F1 = T4 mapping; Midea Air Conditioner Owner's Manual, document 16122500000082 (20150328) — current-generation indoor table: outdoor room temperature sensor open/short circuit F1, timer ON, running lamp 2 flashes; Midea Air Conditioner Service Manual, section 9.3.5 (Scribd mirror) — E4/E5/F1/F2/F3 diagnosis and solution, same 0.06V/4.94V condition; Midea Neola Series Midwall Inverters table (manuals.plus) — F1, 2 flashes, outdoor temperature sensor T4 open circuit or short circuit

Applies to — read this first

On the current/inverter Midea high-wall families we checked, F1 is consistent — it is always the outdoor room (ambient) temperature sensor T4, open or short circuit. The giveaway on a lamp-only model is the timer lamp: F-series codes show the timer lamp ON, while the indoor E-series codes show it OFF. If your timer lamp is ON and the running lamp flashes twice, you are looking at F1, not at an indoor sensor fault.

What the machine does when F1 fires

The indoor unit displays F1 — on lamp-only models the timer lamp is ON and the running lamp flashes twice — because the T4 sampling voltage has gone outside the guide's 0.06V–4.94V window. The manuals describe the display and the fault, not a separate shutdown sequence; do not expect the unit to behave like a protection shutdown (E1/E3). The symptom is a lost outdoor-temperature reading, not a fan or pressure event.

Common causes

  1. A wiring mistake or loose connection between the outdoor T4 temperature sensor and the outdoor main PCB — the first cause the High Wall guide names.
  2. A faulty T4 sensor itself — the outdoor ambient thermistor gone open circuit or short circuit, so its sampling voltage falls below 0.06V or rises above 4.94V.
  3. A damaged sensor cable or poor sensor placement — the guide tells the technician to check the connections, the placement of the sensor, and the cable condition before condemning any part.
  4. A faulty outdoor main PCB — the guide's final verdict when the connections are good and the sensor resistance is normal but F1 persists.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off, wait a minute, and switch it back on. If F1 clears, it was a transient detection glitch — keep an eye on it, because a repeat points to the outdoor sensor circuit.
  2. Note exactly what the display shows: F1 on a display model, or timer lamp ON with the running lamp flashing twice on a lamp-only model. If a different flash count shows, check the matching code instead (3 flashes is F2, the outdoor coil sensor; 4 flashes is F3, the discharge sensor).
  3. If F1 returns after the restart, stop: the guide's next steps are checking the T4 sensor's connections to the outdoor main PCB, then measuring the sensor's resistance against the guide's curve tables — all of which mean opening the outdoor unit and handling its electronics. That is technician work. If the connections are good but the resistance is out of range, the sensor gets replaced; if the resistance is normal, the guide condemns the outdoor main PCB.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. F1 has no deeper home diagnostic in any manual — power-cycle, identify the lamp pattern, then the technician's connection/resistance/PCB tree. Never open the outdoor unit's electrical covers or test a live board.

When to call a technician

  • F1 returns after a power-cycle — the T4 sensor circuit fault is genuine, not a glitch.
  • The T4 sensor's sampling voltage is outside the guide's 0.06V–4.94V window, or its resistance does not match the curve tables — faulty sensor or wiring, per the guide.
  • Connections good and sensor resistance normal but F1 persists — the guide condemns the outdoor main PCB.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or scorching near the outdoor unit — switch off immediately and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

F1 involves mains-powered outdoor electronics: never open the outdoor unit's electrical covers or touch sensor or PCB terminals while investigating it. Switch the power off before any visible check, and never work on a live board. The sensor-resistance and PCB verdicts in the guide use live measurements and belong to a qualified technician. If F1 persists after a power-cycle, switch the unit off and call a qualified technician — never attempt refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F1 mean on a Midea air conditioner?

F1 means the outdoor room (ambient) temperature sensor T4 is open circuit or short circuit: the sensor that measures outdoor air at the outdoor unit has failed, so its sampling voltage falls below 0.06V or rises above 4.94V. The Midea High Wall Fault Diagnosis Guide defines it this way in its E4/E5/F1/F2/F3 section, and the Owner's Manual 16122500000082 lists it as outdoor room temperature sensor error, timer lamp ON, running lamp 2 flashes.

How do I clear Midea F1?

Switch the unit off and back on — if F1 was a transient detection glitch it clears. If it returns, the fault is genuine: the manual's tree is connections first, then sensor resistance, then the sensor, then the outdoor PCB. All of those checks involve the outdoor unit's electrics and are technician work; there is no deeper home fix.

Is Midea F1 the indoor ambient sensor?

No — on the current/inverter high-wall family F1 is the OUTDOOR ambient sensor (T4). The indoor ambient sensor is T1, coded E4. Several Singapore list pages mislabel F1 as indoor ambient; following them would send you to the wrong unit and the wrong sensor.

How do I tell F1 from F2 or F3?

By the running-lamp flash count with the timer lamp ON: F1 flashes twice (T4 outdoor ambient), F2 flashes three times (T3 outdoor coil), F3 flashes four times (T5 discharge). On a display model the code itself is shown; on a lamp-only model the flash count is the only distinction, so count carefully.

Why does the outdoor temperature sensor matter?

The control uses outdoor ambient temperature to manage the refrigeration cycle and its protections. With T4 gone open or short, the control has lost a reading it needs to run the outdoor unit safely, which is why a persistent F1 needs a technician rather than a setting change.
