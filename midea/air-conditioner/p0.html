Midea Air Conditioner Error Code P0

Quick answer

Error codeP0 — Midea Air Conditioner
What it meansP0 on a Midea mini split means IPM protection: when the voltage signal that the IPM (Intelligent Power Module) sends to the compressor drive chip is abnormal, the display LED shows P0 and the AC turns off. The Midea manuals name wiring mistakes, IPM malfunction, a faulty outdoor fan motor, compressor malfunction, a faulty outdoor PCB, or a surge. The only safe home steps are one power-cycle and checking the outdoor unit's airflow is not blocked — IPM continuity, compressor resistance, and PCB tests involve mains-voltage boards and charged capacitors and belong to a qualified technician.
Most common causeThe IPM (Intelligent Power Module) sends an abnormal voltage signal to the compressor drive chip — wiring mistake, IPM malfunction, faulty outdoor fan motor, compressor fault, faulty outdoor PCB, or a surge.
Safe to check yourself?Only barely. The safe home scope is: one power-cycle, and a visual check that the outdoor unit's airflow and ventilation are not blocked (leaves, debris, clearance) — no covers off, no wiring touched. Everything else in the manuals' P0 chain is technician work.

Applies to — read this first

Good news for diagnosis: on the Midea mini-split families we checked, indoor-display P0 is consistent — it is always IPM / inverter-module protection, never a temperature or pressure fault. What you must NOT do is borrow the outdoor-display meaning: on the SAME Pridiom document's outdoor-unit table, P0 means compressor-top temperature protection (the indoor P2 meaning), and on VRF commercial tables P0 is the inverter-compressor top sensor protection — neither applies to the mini-split indoor display. If your unit shows P0 on the indoor display, this page is the right one; an outdoor-board P0 is a different code.

What the machine does when P0 fires

When the IPM's voltage signal to the compressor drive chip goes abnormal, the display LED shows P0 and the AC turns off — the system stops the compressor rather than driving it with a faulty power module. The unit will not cool or heat while P0 is active.

Common causes

  1. A wiring mistake — the first cause the High Wall guide names, especially where jack-plug connections meet.
  2. An IPM malfunction — the Intelligent Power Module itself failing, so the voltage signal it sends to the compressor drive chip goes abnormal.
  3. A faulty outdoor fan motor — checked by the manuals via the E3 fan-speed method; a stalled outdoor fan also overheats the module.
  4. A compressor malfunction — the manuals check compressor winding resistances (U-V, V-W, U-W must all be equal; any winding-to-ground reading means a faulty compressor).
  5. A faulty outdoor PCB — the verdict when wiring, IPM, fan, and compressor all check good.
  6. A power surge — listed as a possible cause in the High Wall guide.
  7. Blocked outdoor-coil airflow or poor ventilation — the manuals require good outdoor-coil airflow as part of the P0 chain; a choked outdoor unit overheats the module.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Power-cycle once: switch the unit off, wait, and switch it back on. If P0 was a transient surge or control glitch, it may clear; if it returns immediately, do not keep cycling the unit.
  2. Check the outdoor unit's airflow and ventilation from the outside only: clear leaves, debris, or obstructions around the outdoor coil and confirm clearance. Do not open any cover or touch any wiring.
  3. If P0 persists with clear outdoor airflow, stop: the manuals' next steps are the wiring check, IPM continuity check, outdoor-fan check (E3 method), compressor resistance check, main-PCB replacement, and reactor check — all mains-voltage technician work on boards with charged capacitors. Switch the unit off and call a qualified technician, and tell them the code is P0 (IPM protection) so they bring the right diagnostics.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. Only the first two steps are home steps; the rest of the manuals' chain is quoted in the series table so you can follow what the technician does — not so you can do it.

When to call a technician

  • P0 returns immediately after a power-cycle with clear outdoor airflow — IPM, compressor, or outdoor PCB fault per the manuals.
  • Any burning smell, scorching, or tripped breaker around the outdoor unit — switch off immediately and call a qualified technician.
  • The outdoor fan does not run or the compressor will not start alongside P0 — both are branches of the manuals' P0 chain, for a technician.

Safety first

P0 is a mains-voltage inverter fault: never open the outdoor unit's electrical box, never touch IPM or PCB terminals, and never test compressor or module resistances yourself. The service documents warn that capacitors in the PCB and outdoor unit hold charge after power-off and must be discharged by a qualified person before any contact. If P0 persists after one power-cycle with clear outdoor airflow, switch the unit off and call a qualified technician. Never attempt refrigerant, compressor, or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications, and never bypass a protection device.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does P0 mean on a Midea mini split?

P0 is IPM protection: when the voltage signal that the IPM sends to the compressor drive chip is abnormal, the display LED shows P0 and the AC turns off. The Midea High Wall Fault Diagnosis Guide defines it exactly this way; the MSMBB-12CRDN1-MPOW table calls it 'IPM malfunction or IGBT over-strong current protection' and the Pridiom service document calls it 'Inverter module protection'.

How do I fix Midea mini split P0?

Try one power-cycle, and check the outdoor unit's airflow and ventilation are not blocked (leaves, debris, clearance). That is the full safe home scope. The manuals' remaining steps — wiring, IPM continuity, fan, compressor resistances, PCB, reactor — are all technician work on mains-voltage boards with charged capacitors.

Is Midea P0 a compressor overheating fault?

Not on the indoor display. Indoor P0 is IPM/inverter-module protection; compressor-top temperature protection is indoor P2. Confusingly, the outdoor-unit display uses P0 for compressor-top temperature — but that is the outdoor board's code, not the indoor display's. If your indoor display shows P0, the IPM chain is the diagnosis.

Can I test the IPM myself?

No. The manuals' IPM continuity check (P:U/P:V/P:W and N:U/N:V/N:W resistance readings) is done on mains-voltage boards, and the service documents warn that capacitors hold charge after power-off and must be discharged with a bulb or 1500–2000 ohm resistance first. IPM and compressor testing belongs to a qualified technician.
