Midea Air Conditioner Error Code E3

Quick answer

Error codeE3 — Midea Air Conditioner
What it meansE3 on a Midea mini split means the indoor fan speed is out of control: when the fan speed keeps too low (300 RPM) for a certain time, the unit stops and the LED displays E3. The Midea manuals name wiring mistakes, a blocked fan wheel or broken bearing, a faulty fan motor, a faulty PCB, or a surge. The safe home checks are the manual's own: power-cycle, then with the power OFF spin the fan by hand and check the motor connector — anything deeper (motor and PCB tests) is technician work.
Most common causeThe indoor fan stays too slow (around 300 RPM) for a set time — blocked fan wheel, broken bearing, faulty fan motor, bad wiring/connector, or a faulty indoor PCB.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home checks are the manual's own: power-cycle (off, on 5 seconds later), spin the indoor fan by hand with the power OFF to check it rotates freely, and check the fan-motor connector is seated with the power OFF. Motor winding-resistance and PCB voltage tests are technician work — never test a live board or motor.

Applies to — read this first

Good news for diagnosis: on the Midea mini-split families we checked, E3 is consistent — it is always the indoor fan speed out of control, never a pressure or refrigerant fault. What you must NOT do is borrow meanings from other contexts: on the outdoor-unit display E3 is a pipe temperature sensor code, on MDV commercial tables E3 is reserved, and some SEO pages call E3 'high pressure protection' — none of those apply to the mini-split indoor display. Note the pairing: indoor E3 and outdoor F5 are the same fault family (fan speed abnormal) on opposite ends of the system.

The High Wall guide lists the code as E3/F5(DP) or E3/E7(DB) — the E3 half is the indoor display; F5 is the outdoor display of the same fan-speed fault family. The safe home diagnostic is the manual's spin-by-hand test with the power OFF; the motor winding resistances and PCB voltage tests in the guide are technician checks, quoted here only so you know what the technician will test.

What the machine does when E3 fires

When the indoor fan speed stays too low (300 RPM) for a set time, the unit stops and the LED displays E3. The indoor blower will not run at speed — airflow stops or drops sharply — because the control has lost reliable fan-speed feedback.

Common causes

  1. A wiring mistake or loose fan-motor connector — the first cause the High Wall guide names, and the first branch of the Pridiom flowchart.
  2. A blocked fan wheel (fan blade) — debris or an obstruction stopping the indoor blower from spinning freely.
  3. A broken fan bearing — the manual's own example of why a fan does not rotate properly by hand.
  4. A faulty fan motor — confirmed by the manual's motor tests (winding resistances for AC motors, connector voltages for DC motors).
  5. A faulty indoor PCB — the verdict when the motor and connections are good but the fan still will not run at speed (the PCB test: pin1–pin2 must read at least 100V on a 208–240V supply, or 50V on a 115V supply, in fan mode at high speed after 15 seconds).
  6. A power surge — listed as a possible cause in the High Wall guide.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Shut off the power supply and turn it on 5 seconds later — the High Wall guide's first step. If E3 clears, it was a transient state; if it returns, continue.
  2. With the power OFF, rotate the indoor fan by hand. If it does not rotate properly, find the cause and clear it — the manual's own examples are a blocked fan or a broken bearing. Do not force a seized fan.
  3. With the power still OFF, check the fan-motor connector and its wiring: seated firmly, no loose or damaged wires. Correct any loose connection, then restart.
  4. If the fan spins freely, the connector is good, and E3 persists, stop: the manual's next steps are the fan-motor test and the main-PCB test, both technician work with live voltages. Switch the unit off and call a qualified technician — the verdict is a faulty fan motor or indoor PCB.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. Every safe step above is done with the power OFF except the restart itself. The manual's resistance and voltage tests are quoted in the series table for context only — they are not home steps.

When to call a technician

  • The indoor fan does not rotate freely by hand (blocked wheel or broken bearing) — do not force it; call a technician.
  • E3 persists with a free-spinning fan and a good connector — faulty fan motor or indoor PCB per the manuals.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or a fan motor that hums without spinning — switch off immediately and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

E3 involves mains-powered indoor electronics and a spinning blower: always switch the power OFF before touching the fan or any connector — never spin the fan or check connections on a live unit. Never open electrical covers or test PCB voltages yourself; the manual's motor and PCB tests use live mains voltages and belong to a qualified technician. If the fan does not rotate freely, the connector is damaged, or E3 persists after the safe checks, switch off and call a qualified technician. Never attempt refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E3 mean on a Midea mini split?

E3 means the indoor fan speed is out of control: when the fan speed keeps too low (300 RPM) for a certain time, the unit stops and the LED displays E3. The Midea High Wall Fault Diagnosis Guide defines it exactly this way, and the MSMBB-12CRDN1-MPOW table and Pridiom service document agree.

How do I fix Midea mini split E3?

Power off, then on again 5 seconds later (the manual's first step). With the power OFF, rotate the indoor fan by hand — if it does not spin freely, clear the obstruction or suspect a broken bearing. If it spins freely, check the fan-motor connector is seated. If E3 persists, the fan motor or indoor PCB needs a qualified technician.

Is Midea E3 a high-pressure protection fault?

No. High-pressure protection is not an E3 meaning on any checked Midea mini-split family — that claim comes from a thin SEO page that also contradicts itself on E2. E3 is strictly the indoor fan speed fault; the outdoor display's E3 is a pipe temperature sensor code, a different context.

What is the difference between E3 and F5?

They are the same fault family on opposite ends of the system: E3 is the indoor fan speed out of control, F5 is the outdoor fan speed out of control. The High Wall guide lists them together as E3/F5, and the MSMBB table gives F5 the outdoor lamp pattern (timer light, operation flash 6).
