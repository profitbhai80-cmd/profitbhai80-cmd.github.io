Midea Air Conditioner Error Code F4

Quick answer

Error codeF4 — Midea Air Conditioner
What it meansF4 = outdoor unit EEPROM parameter error. The outdoor PCB's main chip does not receive feedback from the EEPROM chip, so the board has lost its stored operating configuration. E0 is the same fault on the indoor PCB; F4 is the outdoor-PCB twin. It is a board/EEPROM fault, not a sensor or refrigerant fault.
Most common causeInstallation mistake or a faulty PCB (High Wall guide). The E0/F4 fault can also be caused by electrical interference during start-up.
Safe to check yourself?Partly - power off, wait 2 minutes, restart once. If F4 returns, the outdoor main PCB has likely been damaged and needs replacement by a technician. Do not invent multimeter or reprogramming steps.
Checked againstMidea High Wall System Error Codes and Fault Diagnosis Guide (manuals.plus) + Midea Air Conditioner Error Codes and Troubleshooting Guide (manuals.plus) + Midea Air Conditioner Error Codes Guide, MSMBB-12CRDN1-MP0W table (manuals.plus)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because Midea F4 has one meaning across three named Midea manufacturer documents - but the same code string means different things on other platforms, and generic lists get F4 wrong. On High Wall, Neola Midwall Inverter, MSMBB, and current-generation cassette/ducted/underceiling units F4 is the outdoor EEPROM parameter error, the outdoor twin of E0 (indoor PCB). On previous-generation Inverter LCAC indoor units F4 means "Other malfunction of master unit or slave unit" - a different, vaguer F4. Outdoor-display F4 and Midea VRF F4 are different codes again. Check your model family first.

What the machine does when F4 fires

The outdoor PCB's main chip does not receive feedback from the EEPROM chip, so the board does not know its operating configuration. The High Wall guide gives no separate A/C status for F4 - this page does not invent one.

Common causes

  1. Installation mistake - the High Wall guide's first possible cause.
  2. Faulty PCB - the High Wall guide's second possible cause; if a hard restart does not rectify the issue, the main PCB has likely been damaged and will need to be replaced.
  3. Electrical interference during start-up - the High Wall guide says the E0/F4 fault can be caused by electrical interference during start-up, so a hard restart is recommended.
  4. Wrong-code reading - F4 is NOT an indoor fan motor fault. Some generic error-code lists say F4 is an indoor fan motor fault; that is wrong for Midea. The fan-speed codes on Midea are E3 (indoor) and F5 (outdoor).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Try one hard restart: power off the unit, wait 2 minutes, then restart (High Wall guide, E0/F4 section).
  2. If the error is resolved, troubleshooting is complete - the manual says electrical interference at start-up can cause F4.
  3. If F4 returns, the outdoor main PCB has likely been damaged and needs replacement by a technician (High Wall guide: "Replace the indoor/outdoor main PCB"). EEPROM reprogramming or outdoor PCB replacement is technician work.
  4. Do not invent multimeter or reprogramming steps, and do not diagnose F4 as a fan motor fault - no manual supports either.

The steps above are the High Wall guide's full E0/F4 remedy, quoted in substance. This page does not invent multimeter or reprogramming steps.

When to call a technician

  • F4 returns after the 2-minute hard restart - the outdoor main PCB/EEPROM needs a technician.
  • Any burning smell, visibly damaged wiring, or other fault codes alongside F4 - switch off and call a qualified technician.
  • Your unit is a previous-generation Inverter LCAC unit showing F4 - on that platform F4 means "other malfunction of master unit or slave unit", not the outdoor EEPROM error; use the LCAC manual, not this page.

Safety first

F4 involves the outdoor unit's main PCB and EEPROM: never open the outdoor unit or probe the PCB yourself. PCB replacement is qualified-technician work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F4 mean on a Midea air conditioner?

The outdoor unit EEPROM parameter error: the outdoor PCB's main chip is not receiving feedback from the EEPROM chip, so the board has lost its operating configuration. Verified in three Midea manufacturer documents.

Is F4 the same as E0?

Same fault, different board. E0 is the EEPROM parameter error on the indoor PCB; F4 is the same error on the outdoor PCB. The High Wall guide states this explicitly.

Can I fix F4 myself?

Try one hard restart: power off, wait 2 minutes, restart. The manual says electrical interference at start-up can cause it. If F4 returns, the outdoor main PCB has likely been damaged and needs replacement by a technician.

Does F4 mean the fan motor is faulty?

No. Some generic error-code lists say F4 is an indoor fan motor fault - that is wrong for Midea. The fan-speed codes on Midea are E3 (indoor) and F5 (outdoor).

My cassette manual says F4 is a master/slave malfunction - which is right?

Both, on different platforms. On previous-generation Inverter LCAC indoor units F4 means "other malfunction of master unit or slave unit"; on High Wall, Neola inverter, MSMBB and current-generation cassette/ducted units F4 is the outdoor EEPROM error. Check your model family.
