Midea Air Conditioner Error Code F5

Quick answer

Error codeF5 — Midea Air Conditioner
What it meansF5 = outdoor fan speed malfunction / out of control (DC fan motor only). The outdoor fan motor is running too slowly (below about 300 RPM for a set time on the High Wall platform) or its speed feedback is lost - fan blade, fan motor, wiring, or outdoor PCB.
Most common causeWiring mistake; faulty fan wheel (fan blade); faulty fan motor; faulty PCB; or surge (Midea High Wall System Error Codes and Fault Diagnosis Guide, E3/F5 section).
Safe to check yourself?Partly - shut off power, turn on after 5 seconds. If F5 returns, power off and rotate the outdoor fan by hand: if it does not spin freely, clear the obstruction (leaves/debris) and restart. If it spins freely and F5 persists, it is the fan motor, wiring, or outdoor PCB - call a technician.
Checked againstMidea High Wall System Error Codes and Fault Diagnosis Guide (manuals.plus) + Midea Air Conditioner Error Codes and Troubleshooting Guide (manuals.plus) + Midea Air Conditioner Error Codes Guide, MSMBB-12CRDN1-MP0W table (manuals.plus) + Midea Air Conditioner Owner's Manual, 24000Btu/h type (manuals.plus)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because Midea F5 is the outdoor-fan twin of E3 - and it applies to DC fan motors only. F5 is verified in three named Midea manufacturer documents with the same meaning: the High Wall guide (E3/F5(DP), fan speed abnormal), the cassette/ducted troubleshooting guide (outdoor fan speed, DC fan motor only), and the MSMBB table (outdoor fan speed out of control), with the owner's manual as supporting confirmation. On units with an AC outdoor fan motor the code may not exist - do not diagnose an AC-fan unit by F5. Midea VRF F5 and outdoor-display F5 are different codes. On High Wall DB units the outdoor twin is coded E7, not F5.

What the machine does when F5 fires

When fan speed keeps too low (300 RPM) for a certain time, the unit will stop and the LED will display the failure (High Wall guide). In plain terms: the outdoor fan is not spinning at the speed the board commands, so the unit stops to protect the compressor/condenser.

Common causes

  1. Wiring mistake - the High Wall guide's first possible cause.
  2. Faulty fan wheel (fan blade) - the High Wall guide's second possible cause.
  3. Faulty fan motor - the High Wall guide's third possible cause.
  4. Faulty PCB - the High Wall guide's fourth possible cause.
  5. Surge - the High Wall guide's fifth possible cause.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Shut off the power supply and turn it on 5 seconds later (High Wall guide, E3/F5 section). If F5 clears, the fault may have been transient.
  2. If F5 returns, shut off the power supply and rotate the outdoor fan by hand. Does it rotate properly? If the fan blade does not spin freely by hand, clear any obstruction (leaves/debris) and restart the system.
  3. Are all the connections and wiring between the fan motor and PCB ok? If not, remedy and restart the system. If both the hand-spin check and the wiring are good, check the fan motor and the PCB.
  4. The manual's technician checks (quoted for the technician, not as homeowner DIY): release the UVW connector and measure the resistance of U-V, U-W, V-W - if the resistance is not equal to each other, the fan motor is faulty and needs to be replaced; power on and, when the unit is in standby, measure the voltage of pin 4-5 in the feedback signal connector - if the value is not 5V, change the PCB; rotate the fan by hand and measure the voltage of pin 1-5, pin 2-5 and pin 3-5 in the feedback signal connector - if any voltage is not a positive voltage fluctuation, the fan motor is faulty and needs to be replaced.
  5. Do not keep running the unit with F5 - without the outdoor fan the condenser overheats and the compressor will trip on protection.

The UVW-resistance and feedback-voltage checks are technician procedures quoted from the High Wall guide for the technician, not homeowner DIY. The homeowner-safe steps are the 5-second restart and the power-off hand-spin check.

When to call a technician

  • F5 returns after the 5-second restart and the fan spins freely by hand - the fan motor, wiring, or outdoor PCB needs a technician.
  • The fan does not spin freely by hand even after clearing debris - the fan motor or bearings need a technician.
  • Any burning smell, visibly damaged wiring, or other fault codes alongside F5 - switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

F5 involves the outdoor fan and live PCB wiring: never open the outdoor unit, reach into the fan, or probe the fan motor or PCB yourself. The hand-spin check is done only with the power supply shut off. UVW-resistance and feedback-voltage checks are qualified-technician work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F5 mean on a Midea air conditioner?

The outdoor fan speed has gone out of control (DC fan motor only): the outdoor fan is running too slowly or its speed feedback is lost. Verified in three Midea manufacturer documents.

Is F5 the indoor or the outdoor fan?

Outdoor. The indoor fan-speed fault is E3. The High Wall guide treats them as one fault with two codes: E3/F5(DP) - E3 indoor, F5 outdoor (on DB units the outdoor twin is coded E7).

Can I fix F5 myself?

Partly: shut off power, turn on after 5 seconds. If F5 returns, power off and spin the outdoor fan by hand - if it is stuck, clear leaves/debris and restart. If it spins freely and F5 persists, the fan motor, wiring, or outdoor PCB is faulty - call a technician.

Why does my unit show F5 but the fan looks fine?

The fault is speed feedback, not just rotation: the motor can spin yet fail the speed check (worn bearings, failing driver chip, or a PCB feedback fault). The manual's UVW-resistance and feedback-voltage checks are how a technician tells motor from board.

Does F5 mean the compressor is broken?

No. F5 is the outdoor fan, not the compressor (compressor drive errors are P4). But do not keep running the unit with F5 - without the outdoor fan the condenser overheats and the compressor will trip on protection.
