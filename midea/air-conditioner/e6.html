Midea Air Conditioner Error Code E6

Quick answer

Error codeE6 — Midea Air Conditioner
What it meansE6 = open or short circuit of the room or evaporator temperature sensor (T2). The unit cannot read the evaporator/room temperature, so it stops rather than run blind. This is the indoor-display E6; outdoor-display E6 is a different code.
Most common causeThe T2 (room/evaporator) temperature sensor is open or short-circuited, its connector or connection is faulty, or the indoor PCB is faulty (Pridiom Multi-Zone service manual check point).
Safe to check yourself?Partly - power-cycle once. If E6 returns, the T2 sensor or its connector is the suspect and needs a qualified technician (sensor resistance check / replacement). Do not open the unit to test the sensor yourself.
Checked againstMidea Air Conditioner Error Codes Guide, R series / 9V series / 9A series tables (manuals.plus) + Midea Air Conditioner Error Codes and Troubleshooting Guide, Neola / Mission / Aurora / Altimate Comfort midwall tables (manuals.plus) + Midea Inverter Air Condition, Pridiom Multi-Zone service manual (PWM093HX indoor / POM273HX outdoor) + Midea Split Wall-Mounted Type Service Manual CSNA-1304-A, Corona Series, section 9.1

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because Midea E6 has one meaning on the indoor display across four named Midea manufacturer documents - but the same code string means different things on other platforms, and the top-ranked page gets E6 wrong. On the indoor display E6 is the room/evaporator temperature sensor (T2) open or short circuit, across the R, 9V, 9A, Neola, Mission, Aurora, Altimate Comfort, Pridiom and Corona families. On Neola Midwall Inverters and on the High Wall diagnosis guide platform the same T2 sensor fault is coded E5, not E6 - E6 does not exist on those indoor tables. Outdoor-display E6 is a different code (PFC module protection or a coil-sensor fault, depending on the outdoor model), and Midea VRF E6 is "Reserved" - no meaning at all. Check your model family first.

What the machine does when E6 fires

The unit cannot read the evaporator/room temperature, so it stops rather than run blind. On the midwall families the indoor display flashes E6 6 times.

Common causes

  1. The room or evaporator temperature sensor (T2) is open-circuited - the manuals' first fault definition.
  2. The room or evaporator temperature sensor (T2) is short-circuited - the manuals' second fault definition.
  3. The indoor temperature and evaporator sensor connector or connection is faulty - the Pridiom manual's check point (repair the connector and reconnect).
  4. The indoor PCB is faulty - the Pridiom manual's next step after a good connector (replace the indoor PCB, then replace the sensor and check if E6 displays again).
  5. Wrong-code reading - communication between indoor and outdoor units is E1, not E6. Checking the communication cable will not fix E6.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Power-cycle once: switch the unit off, wait, and switch it back on. If E6 clears, the fault may have been transient.
  2. If E6 returns, the T2 sensor or its connector is the suspect. The Pridiom manual's procedure is: check whether the indoor temperature and evaporator sensor connector and connection are good - if not, repair the connector and reconnect; if they are good, replace the indoor PCB, then replace the sensor and check if E6 displays again.
  3. The High Wall guide's temperature-sensor method (same sensor family) is: check the connections of the sensor, the placement of the sensor, and the cable condition. If all is good, measure the resistance of the sensor using the Resistance curve table - a technician step.
  4. Do not invent multimeter values or board-swap steps beyond what the manuals state, and do not open the unit to test the sensor yourself.

The Pridiom check point is the only manual giving an E6 procedure. The High Wall sensor method is from the E4/E5/F1/F2/F3 section (same sensor family, same method), not an E6-specific section.

When to call a technician

  • E6 returns after one power-cycle - the T2 sensor, its connector, or the indoor PCB needs a technician.
  • Any burning smell, visibly damaged wiring, or other fault codes alongside E6 - switch off and call a qualified technician.
  • Your unit is a Neola Midwall Inverter or High Wall unit showing a T2 fault as E5 - that is the same sensor under a different code on your platform; use the E5 page, not this one.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

E6 is a sensor/PCB fault inside the indoor unit: never open the unit to test the sensor yourself. Sensor resistance checks and PCB work are qualified-technician work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E6 mean on a Midea air conditioner?

An open or short circuit of the room or evaporator temperature sensor (T2). Four Midea manufacturer documents define it identically across the R, 9V, 9A, Neola, Mission, Aurora, Altimate Comfort, Pridiom and Corona families.

Is E6 a communication error?

No. That is the #1-ranked page's mistake. Communication between indoor and outdoor units is E1. E6 is a temperature-sensor fault - checking the communication cable will not fix it.

Can I fix E6 myself?

Power-cycle once. If E6 returns, the T2 sensor or its connector is faulty - the manuals' procedure is connector check, then sensor/PCB by a technician. Do not open the unit to test the sensor yourself.

Why does my neighbour's Midea show E5 for the same sensor fault?

Series split: on Neola Midwall Inverter and High Wall platforms the T2 sensor fault is coded E5, not E6. Same sensor, different code number - check your model family.

Is E6 the same as E5?

No - but they are adjacent sensor codes: on most families E5 is the outdoor temperature sensor and E6 is the room/evaporator (T2) sensor. On inverter midwall families E5 takes the T2 meaning instead. See the E5 page.
