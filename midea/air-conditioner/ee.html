Midea Air Conditioner Error Code EE

Quick answer

Error codeEE — Midea Air Conditioner
What it meansEE on a Midea air conditioner (indoor display, timer OFF, running lamp 8 flashes) means water-level alarm malfunction — the indoor unit's water-level device has alarmed (drain tray / float / pump circuit on duct/cassette units fitted with one). Scope: this is the residential indoor-display water-level alarm. Commercial network EE and Aqua Thermal EE (EVI heat-exchanger sensor) are different systems and must not be borrowed. A wall-split without a water-level device showing EE points to a board/display misreport.
Most common causeThe indoor unit's water-level device has alarmed — the drain tray is full, the float is stuck, the drain line is blocked, or the water-level/pump circuit has failed.
Safe to check yourself?Yes for the drain-side checks on units with a water-level device — tray, float and drain line. The electrical side is technician work.

Applies to — read this first

Midea EE is a water-level alarm — but only on the residential indoor display the manuals describe. The traps are borrowing: commercial/service-guide EE rows and Aqua Thermal EE (an EVI heat-exchanger sensor) are different systems entirely. And if your wall-split has no water-level device at all, EE cannot be a real water alarm — it is a board or display misreport. Check the family before the drain.

What the machine does when EE fires

The indoor display shows EE with the timer lamp off and the running lamp flashing 8 times; the water-level device has alarmed and the unit stops normal operation until the drain condition is cleared.

Common causes

  1. The drain tray is full or the float / water-level device is stuck at the alarm position.
  2. The condensate drain line is blocked or disconnected.
  3. The condensate pump or its circuit has failed (on units fitted with one).
  4. On a wall-split without a water-level device: a board or display misreport, not a water fault.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Power the unit off and place a towel under the indoor unit's drain area.
  2. Check the drain tray is not full and the float / water-level device moves freely — not stuck up.
  3. Check the visible drain line for kinks, disconnections or blockage at the outlet.
  4. On units with a condensate pump, check the pump is actually lifting water when powered.
  5. If your wall-split has no water-level device at all, skip the drain work: call a technician for the board/display check.
  6. If EE persists with the drain path clear, call a qualified technician — the water-level device or its wiring needs professional diagnosis.

The drain path is the whole home diagnostic — tray, float, line, pump. One family check (does your unit even have a water-level device?) decides whether the drain work applies at all.

When to call a technician

  • EE persists with the drain tray, float, line and pump all clear.
  • The water-level device or its wiring is damaged.
  • EE on a wall-split without a water-level device — a board/display misreport needs professional diagnosis.
  • Water leaking from the indoor unit housing itself rather than the drain outlet — switch off and call a technician.

Safety first

EE is a water fault in a mains appliance. Power off before touching the drain tray or pump, mop up spills, and never work on a powered machine with wet hands. Never open the unit's panels or touch internal wiring.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does EE mean on a Midea air conditioner?

EE means water-level alarm malfunction — the indoor unit's water-level device has alarmed. The indoor display shows EE with the timer lamp off and the running lamp flashing 8 times, per the owner's manual, duct manual and current-generation indoor table.

How do I clear Midea EE myself?

Power off, then work the drain path: drain tray not full, float moving freely, drain line clear, and the condensate pump lifting water if one is fitted. Clearing the water condition clears the alarm.

Is Midea EE the same on commercial units?

No. Commercial network EE rows and the Aqua Thermal EE (an EVI heat-exchanger sensor fault) are different systems — do not borrow them for this residential indoor-display code.

My wall-split shows EE but has no drain pump — what does it mean?

It cannot be the water-level alarm: without a water-level device there is nothing to alarm. Your EE is a board or display misreport — call a technician rather than chasing drains.

EE is still there with the drain clear — what next?

Stop and call a qualified technician. With the drain path clear, the fault is in the water-level device, its wiring or the control — professional diagnosis, not more drain work.
