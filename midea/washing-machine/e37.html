Midea Washer-Dryer Error Code E37

Quick answer

Error codeE37 — Midea Washer-Dryer
What it meansE37 = drying intake (dry-in) thermistor fault — the temperature sensor at the drying air intake port does not register a temperature change; short circuit or open circuit of the sensor or its wire harness (M1 §6; M6 §5). WASHER-DRYER ONLY.
Most common causeShort circuit or open circuit in the intake-port temperature sensor or its wire harness. Dry temperature sensor: 4.8 kOhm ±10% (M1) / 4.8 kOhm ±8% at 25 °C (M6).
Safe to check yourself?No — sensor and harness work is technician work. There is no owner-level fix for E37 beyond noting the code and calling service.
Checked againstMidea MF210 Series Service Manual (midea.com), §6 'ERORR CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§6, E37 row + detail page); Midea MF100W70BA-W-IT Service Manual (midea.com, Model L01), §5 'MALFUNCTION CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§5 pp.13+ + §7 component values)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E37 is a washer-dryer drying-system code — it means a dry-in thermistor fault on the MF210 family and the MF100W70BA washer-dryer (M1 §6; M6 §5, word-for-word agreement). CRITICAL — the E37/E38 swap: M1's table says E37 = Thermistor (Dry-in); appliancemode's hub says E37 = 'Drying-out NTC sensor error' — the two sources SWAP E37 and E38. M1 (and now M6) is the manufacturer manual and wins; this page states the M1/M6 assignment explicitly and calls out the swap. Never confuse E37 with E3 (top-load lid open), E33 (water level sensor), or E38 (dry-out — the other sensor).

The ranking pages get E37 backwards. appliancemode's hub says E37 is a 'Drying-out NTC sensor error... open or short circuit in the drying outlet thermistor' — SWAPPED vs the manufacturer manuals (M1 and M6: E37 = dry-in, E38 = dry-out; appliancemode says the opposite — a reader following it tests the wrong sensor). Its drying NTC values '1.7 kOhm at 50 °C / 0.45 kOhm at 90 °C' are self-flagged 'examples' and do not match M1 (4.8 kOhm ±10%) or M6 (4.8 kOhm ±8%), and its dry heater '18 Ohm / 30 Ohm example' does not match M1 (44 Ohm ±10%). A JustAnswer MFL70 thread says E38 is a 'water level error code... pressure switch has malfunctioned' — WRONG vs M1/M6 (E38 is a dry-out thermistor fault, a drying-system code). No dedicated per-code E37 article exists.

What the machine does when E37 fires

During drying, the temperature sensor at the intake port does not receive the change of temperature — the board raises E37 (M1 §6; M6 §5).

Common causes

  1. Short circuit or open circuit of the temperature sensor around the intake port (M1 §6).
  2. Short circuit or open circuit of the sensor's wire harness (M1 §6).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Call service — there is no owner-level fix for E37 in the manuals.
  2. Technician check (M1 §6; M6 §5): the intake-port temperature sensor and its wire harness for short or open circuit.
  3. Technician value (M1; M6 §7): dry temperature sensor 4.8 kOhm ±10% (M1) / 4.8 kOhm ±8% at 25 °C (M6). Quote both.

Do NOT invent: no drying NTC values '1.7 kOhm at 50 °C / 0.45 kOhm at 90 °C' (appliancemode's self-flagged examples — do not match M1/M6); no power-cycle reset time; no 'water level error' diagnosis (the JustAnswer MFL70 thread's claim is wrong — E37 is a drying-sensor code, not a water-level code). Sensor and harness work is technician work; there is no owner-level fix for E37 beyond noting the code and calling service.

When to call a technician

  • E37 returns — the intake-port sensor or its harness needs diagnosis by a technician.
  • Drying finishes but clothes come out cold or damp.
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

E37 has no owner-level fix: sensor and harness work means opening the machine. That is technician work. Never open the machine while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E37 mean on a Midea washer-dryer?

E37 means a drying intake (dry-in) thermistor fault: the temperature sensor at the drying air intake port does not register a temperature change — a short circuit or open circuit of the sensor or its wire harness (M1 §6, M6 §5).

Is E37 the intake sensor or the exhaust sensor?

Intake (dry-in). Both manufacturer manuals say E37 = dry-in and E38 = dry-out. Some repair sites have them swapped — they have E37 as the drying-out sensor, which is wrong; following that, you test the wrong sensor.

What causes Midea washer-dryer E37?

Per the manuals: a short circuit or open circuit in the intake-port temperature sensor or its wire harness. The dry temperature sensor measures 4.8 kOhm ±10% (M1) / 4.8 kOhm ±8% at 25 °C (M6) — a technician tests it.

Is E37 the same as E38?

No. E37 = dry-in (intake port) thermistor; E38 = dry-out (exhaust port) thermistor. Two different sensors, two different codes — do not treat them as one.

Is E37 the same on a Midea top-load washer?

No. Top-load Midea washers (MAS70/80/120, MA100) have NO E37 code in any manual read, and washer-only front-loaders (MF110W60D) have no E37 either. Do not apply this page to a washer-only machine. (Also: E37 is not E3 — E3 on a top-load is the lid-open alarm.)
