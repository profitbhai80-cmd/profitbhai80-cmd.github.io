Midea Washer-Dryer Error Code E38

Quick answer

Error codeE38 — Midea Washer-Dryer
What it meansE38 = drying exhaust (dry-out) thermistor fault — the temperature sensor at the drying air exhaust port does not register a temperature change; short circuit or open circuit of the sensor or its wire harness (M1 §6; M6 §5). WASHER-DRYER ONLY. It is NOT a water-level error.
Most common causeShort circuit or open circuit in the exhaust-port temperature sensor or its wire harness. Dry temperature sensor: 4.8 kOhm ±10% (M1) / 4.8 kOhm ±8% at 25 °C (M6).
Safe to check yourself?No — sensor and harness work is technician work. There is no owner-level fix for E38 beyond noting the code and calling service.
Checked againstMidea MF210 Series Service Manual (midea.com), §6 'ERORR CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§6, E38 row + detail page); Midea MF100W70BA-W-IT Service Manual (midea.com, Model L01), §5 'MALFUNCTION CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§5 pp.13+ + §7 component values)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E38 is a washer-dryer drying-system code — it means a dry-out thermistor fault on the MF210 family and the MF100W70BA washer-dryer (M1 §6; M6 §5, word-for-word agreement). CRITICAL — the E37/E38 swap: M1's table says E38 = Thermistor (Dry-out); appliancemode's hub says E38 = 'Drying-in NTC sensor error' — the two sources SWAP E37 and E38. M1 (and now M6) is the manufacturer manual and wins; this page states the M1/M6 assignment explicitly and calls out the swap. Never confuse E38 with E3 (top-load lid open), E33 (water level sensor), or E37 (dry-in — the other sensor). Never accept the JustAnswer 'water level error' claim for E38 — it is a drying-sensor code, not a water-level code.

The ranking pages get E38 backwards. appliancemode's hub says E38 is a 'Drying-in NTC sensor error... open or short circuit in the drying inlet thermistor' — SWAPPED vs the manufacturer manuals (M1 and M6: E37 = dry-in, E38 = dry-out; appliancemode says the opposite — a reader following it tests the wrong sensor). Its drying NTC values '1.7 kOhm at 50 °C / 0.45 kOhm at 90 °C' are self-flagged 'examples' and do not match M1 (4.8 kOhm ±10%) or M6 (4.8 kOhm ±8%), and its dry heater '18 Ohm / 30 Ohm example' does not match M1 (44 Ohm ±10%). The only E38-specific result, a JustAnswer MFL70-D1211S thread, says E38 is a 'water level error code... pressure switch has malfunctioned' — WRONG vs M1/M6 (E38 = dry-out thermistor, a drying-system code, not a water-level code); its own AI summary reframes E38 as a 'water inlet or sensor issue' — a third meaning on the same page. No dedicated per-code E38 article exists.

What the machine does when E38 fires

During drying, the temperature sensor at the exhaust port does not receive the change of temperature — the board raises E38 (M1 §6; M6 §5).

Common causes

  1. Short circuit or open circuit of the temperature sensor around the exhaust port (M1 §6).
  2. Short circuit or open circuit of the sensor's wire harness (M1 §6).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Call service — there is no owner-level fix for E38 in the manuals.
  2. Technician check (M1 §6; M6 §5): the exhaust-port temperature sensor and its wire harness for short or open circuit.
  3. Technician value (M1; M6 §7): dry temperature sensor 4.8 kOhm ±10% (M1) / 4.8 kOhm ±8% at 25 °C (M6). Quote both.

Do NOT invent: no drying NTC values '1.7 kOhm at 50 °C / 0.45 kOhm at 90 °C' (appliancemode's self-flagged examples — do not match M1/M6); no power-cycle reset time; no pressure-switch / water-level diagnosis (the JustAnswer MFL70 thread's 'water level error code... pressure switch has malfunctioned' claim is wrong — E38 is a drying-sensor code). Sensor and harness work is technician work; there is no owner-level fix for E38 beyond noting the code and calling service.

When to call a technician

  • E38 returns — the exhaust-port sensor or its harness needs diagnosis by a technician.
  • Drying finishes but clothes come out cold or damp.
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

E38 has no owner-level fix: sensor and harness work means opening the machine. That is technician work. Never open the machine while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E38 mean on a Midea washer-dryer?

E38 means a drying exhaust (dry-out) thermistor fault: the temperature sensor at the drying air exhaust port does not register a temperature change — a short circuit or open circuit of the sensor or its wire harness (M1 §6, M6 §5).

Is E38 the intake sensor or the exhaust sensor?

Exhaust (dry-out). Both manufacturer manuals say E37 = dry-in and E38 = dry-out. Some repair sites have them swapped — they have E38 as the drying-in sensor, which is wrong; following that, you test the wrong sensor.

What causes Midea washer-dryer E38?

Per the manuals: a short circuit or open circuit in the exhaust-port temperature sensor or its wire harness. The dry temperature sensor measures 4.8 kOhm ±10% (M1) / 4.8 kOhm ±8% at 25 °C (M6) — a technician tests it. It is NOT a water-level or pressure-switch fault, despite what one JustAnswer thread claims.

Is E38 the same as E37?

No. E37 = dry-in (intake port) thermistor; E38 = dry-out (exhaust port) thermistor. Two different sensors, two different codes — do not treat them as one.

Is E38 the same on a Midea top-load washer?

No. Top-load Midea washers (MAS70/80/120, MA100) have NO E38 code in any manual read, and washer-only front-loaders (MF110W60D) have no E38 either. Do not apply this page to a washer-only machine. (Also: E38 is not E3 — E3 on a top-load is the lid-open alarm.)
