Midea Front-Load Washing Machine Error Code E62

Quick answer

Error codeE62 — Midea Front-Load Washing Machine
What it meansE62 = actual motor speed is 300 rpm higher than the target speed — the root reason is the SCR on the PCB is damaged (M5/M7 §5 pp.41-44). FRONT-LOAD ONLY: top-load Midea washers have no E62.
Most common causeThe SCR on the PCB is damaged, or the motor speed measuring device is damaged (M5/M7 §5).
Safe to check yourself?No — there is no owner-level fix for E62 in the manuals. The procedure is a technician replacement of the PCB first, then the motor. Unplug the machine and call service.
Checked againstMidea MF110W60D Service Manual (manua.ls, 71 pp.), §5 'MALFUNCTION CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§5 pp.41-44); Midea MF110W70BA10 Service Manual (manua.ls, Model L11, 71 pp.), §5 'MALFUNCTION CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§5 pp.41-44)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E62 is a FRONT-LOAD code — it means motor overspeed on the MF110W60D and MF110W70BA10 (Model L11) front-load families (M5 §5; M7 §5, word-for-word agreement). It does not exist on top-loaders. Never merge E60/E61/E62 — E60 = motor failed to start 5 times; E61 = no speed feedback 5 times (under 15 rpm for 3 s); E62 = actual speed 300 rpm above target (SCR damaged). E62 is distinct from E50 (inverter PCB abnormal signals) and E64 (inverter-to-main PCB communication). SCR vs TRIAC: the manuals say SCR; appliancemode says TRIAC — a TRIAC is in the SCR family, but this page quotes the manual's word (SCR) and notes the equivalence rather than silently renaming it. Never present E62 as a carbon-brush fault — the manuals' procedure is replace-PCB-then-motor, with no brush check.

The ranking pages give E62 almost nothing. appliancemode's hub is the ONLY page naming E62: it files E62 under 'Universal Motor Errors... carbon brushes' as a 'Motor TRIAC error... problem with the TRIAC on the main PCB', sharing one cause list across E60/E61/E62. Errors: the +300 rpm rule is never stated; it renames the manual's SCR as a TRIAC without noting the manual's wording; carbon brushes / tachometer / motor fuse are given as checks (no manual basis — the manuals say replace PCB, replace motor); one cause list is shared across three different codes; no named manual; the same hub invents E11, E20, E40, E52-E5D, E70 and E81.

What the machine does when E62 fires

The actual motor speed runs 300 rpm higher than the target speed: the SCR on the PCB is damaged, so the board raises E62 (M5/M7 §5).

Common causes

  1. The SCR on the PCB is damaged (M5/M7 §5).
  2. The motor speed measuring device is damaged (M5/M7 §5).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Unplug the machine and call service — there is no owner-level fix for E62 in the manuals.
  2. Technician step 1 — replace the PCB (M7 §5 pp.43-44): 'Disassemble the 2 screws of the top cover plate. Push back the top cover plate 15mm until it leave away from the control panel and then take it down. Disassemble the 2 screws on the control panel. After opening the drawer, press the blue siphon cap according to the text prompts, and take out the drawer at the same time. Disassemble the 2 screws on the control panel. Remove the terminals on the PCB. Disassemble the 2 screws on the PCB and take out it. Replace a new PCB and reassemble the washing machine.'
  3. Technician step 2 — replace the motor (M7 §5 pp.43-44): 'Disassemble the 4 screws of the rear cover. Remove the rear cover and then remove the belt. After the soft things are on the floor mat, the washing machine is dumped forward and downward. Disassemble the screw on the rear tub and remove the terminal connecting the motor. Take out the motor upwards and replace it with a new one.'

Do NOT invent: no carbon-brush check (no brushes in M5/M7's E62 procedure); no tachometer test; no motor resistance value for E62 in M5/M7 as read; no power-cycle reset time; no 'motor fuse' check (not in M5/M7). PCB and motor replacement is technician work; there is no owner-level fix for E62 beyond noting the code and calling service. Note on wording: the manuals say SCR, not TRIAC — some repair sites call the same part a TRIAC; the manual's word is SCR.

When to call a technician

  • E62 returns after a restart — the PCB or the motor speed measuring device needs diagnosis.
  • The drum spins far faster than the selected speed.
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

E62 diagnosis means opening the machine and replacing the PCB and motor: that is technician work. Never open the machine while it is connected to power, and do not open the motor looking for carbon brushes — the MF110 manuals give no brush check for E62.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E62 mean on a Midea front-load washing machine?

E62 means the actual motor speed is 300 rpm higher than the target speed: the root reason is the SCR on the PCB is damaged (M5/M7 §5).

What causes Midea front-load E62?

Per the MF110W60D and MF110W70BA10 service manuals: the SCR on the PCB is damaged, or the motor speed measuring device is damaged.

What should I check first for E62?

There is no owner-level fix in the manuals: the procedure is a technician replacement of the PCB first, then the motor if the fault persists. Unplug the machine and call service — do not open the motor looking for carbon brushes, because the MF110 manuals give no brush check for E62.

Is E62 the same as E60 or E61?

No. E60 = the motor failed to start 5 times; E61 = no speed feedback 5 times (speed under 15 rpm for 3 seconds); E62 = the actual speed is 300 rpm above the target speed (SCR on the PCB damaged). Three distinct codes with three distinct checks — do not treat them as one 'motor error'.

Is E62 the same on a Midea top-load washer?

No. Top-load Midea washers (MAS70/80/120, MA100) have NO E62 code in any manual read. Do not apply this page to a top-load machine. (Note: the manuals say SCR, not TRIAC — some repair sites call the same part a TRIAC; the manual's word is SCR.)
