Midea Top-Load Washing Machine Error Code E4

Quick answer

Error codeE4 — Midea Top-Load Washing Machine
What it meansE4 = unbalance — the outer tub hits the safety switch or cabinet seriously, so the machine can't spin smoothly (M4 §6). TOP-LOAD ONLY: front-load Midea washers have no E4 in any service manual read — their temperature-sensor codes are E34/E35.
Most common causeMachine unbalanced or rocking, clothes piled at one side, suspension failure, or safety-switch beam deformation — plus the owner-manual causes: machine not on a stable level surface, or the motor belt loosened.
Safe to check yourself?Yes — redistribute the clothes by hand, add 1-2 items to a single-item wash, and level the machine on a flat surface. Suspension, balance ring, safety-switch beam and PCB work is technician work.
Checked againstMidea MAS70/MAS80/MAS120 Top-Load Service Manual (manuals.plus), §6 'ERORR CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§6 + §4 + §7.6 + §8); Midea MA100W75/85/95 Owner's Manual (manualspro.net), troubleshooting table p.20 (p.20 + p.13); Midea MAE70-504TPS/01FM-AU-S1 User Manual (manuals.plus), troubleshooting table

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E4 is a TOP-LOAD code — it means unbalance on the MAS70/MAS80/MAS120 US top-load families and the MA100/MAE70 top-load families (M4 §6/§4/§7.6/§8; MA100 p.20 + p.13; MAE70 troubleshooting table). There is NO front-load E4 in M1/M5/M6 §5 — applianceteacher's 'front-load E4 = water temperature sensor' has no manual support (front-load temperature codes are E34/E35). E4 is NOT overflow (theblackurbantimes), NOT a sensor malfunction (homegeargeek), NOT low water flow (applianceteacher). E3 is lid open — the SAME safety switch, not closed in spin. E2 is the drain fault. E5 has NO manual support in M4 or MA100 — never merge E4/E5.

What the machine does when E4 fires

During the spin process, if clothing is piled up at one side of the spin barrel, it causes unbalance of the spin barrel resulting in striking. The computer inside the washer feels this unbalance and automatically corrects it (fill in water, wash) — but if the unbalance is not removed after continuous three corrections, the computer alarms E4 and the user must manually and evenly place the clothing inside the barrel (owner-manual family text, via removeandreplace transcription).

Common causes

  1. Machine is unbalanced — 'Check whether the machine is unbalance or rock' (M4 §6).
  2. Clothes are unbalanced — 'Check whether the clothes is placed evenly' (M4 §6).
  3. 'The suspension failure' — machine is balanced, but it still shakes seriously (M4 §6).
  4. 'Safety switch beam deformation' (M4 §6).
  5. Owner-manual causes (MA100 p.20): clothes piled largely at one side of the barrel or excessively placed; washer not placed in a stable condition or in an inclination state (ground not flat); motor belt loosened.
  6. Single-item wash — 'When washing a single clothes, it may easily cause great eccentricity and give alarm due to great unbalance' (MA100 p.13).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Manually balance clothes in the barrel by hands (MA100 p.20, verbatim owner step).
  2. Check whether the machine is unbalanced or rocking, and whether the clothes are placed evenly (M4 §6).
  3. Check the washer is set on a sturdy flat surface — adjust each leg until the machine is level side to side and front to back (M4 §7.6).
  4. If you washed a single item, add one or two more clothes to be washed together (MA100 p.13).
  5. Technician work (M4 §6, §7.6, §8): suspension rod assembly, tub seal and bearing kit and balance ring, safety-switch beam deformation, motor coupling, drive belt, clutch, motor capacitor, drive motor and PCB — and check whether the lever of the safety switch touches the outer tub if touching noise happens.

The E3/E4 link: one safety switch serves both codes — E3 when it is not closed in spin, E4 when the unbalanced tub hits it. A machine that alarms E4 and then E3 (or vice versa) is telling one story about the same switch. Do NOT invent: no power-cycle reset time for E4 in M4/MA100 as read (applianceteacher's 'turn off 30 seconds to 1 minute' is uncited); no 'load balancing kit' (tophomeapps filler); no water-level adjustment as an E4 fix. Owner actions are: redistribute the load by hand, add 1-2 items to a single-item wash, level the machine on a flat surface. Suspension rods, balance ring, safety-switch beam and PCB work is technician work.

When to call a technician

  • E4 returns after the load is redistributed and the machine is level — the suspension, balance ring or safety-switch beam needs diagnosis.
  • The machine shakes seriously even though it is balanced — per M4 §6 that points to suspension failure.
  • Touching noise from the safety switch lever against the outer tub (M4 §8).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

E4's owner checks are external only — redistribute the load and level the machine. Suspension, balance ring and safety-switch beam work means opening the machine: that is technician work. Never open the machine while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E4 mean on a Midea top-load washing machine?

E4 means the load is unbalanced: the outer tub hits the safety switch or cabinet seriously, so the machine can't spin smoothly (M4 §6).

What causes Midea top-load E4?

Per the MAS70/80/120 service manual: the machine is unbalanced, the clothes are unbalanced, the suspension has failed, or the safety-switch beam is deformed. Per the owner manual: clothes piled largely at one side, the machine not on a stable level surface, or the motor belt loosened.

What should I do first for E4?

The owner manual (MA100 p.20) says: 'Manually balance clothes in the barrel by hands.' Open the lid, spread the clothes evenly, and check the machine is level. If you washed a single item, add one or two more clothes (MA100 p.13) — a single item alone easily causes unbalance.

Will the machine fix unbalance by itself?

Up to a point: the computer feels the unbalance and automatically corrects it (fills in water and washes) up to three times in a row. If the unbalance is not removed after three continuous corrections, it alarms E4 and you must place the clothing evenly by hand.

Is E4 the same on a Midea front-load washer?

No. Front-load Midea washers (MF100/MF110/MF200/MF210) have NO E4 code in any service manual read — their temperature-sensor codes are E34/E35. Do not apply this page to a front-load machine. And E4 is not the same as E3 (lid open) — but the two share one safety switch: E3 means it is not closed in spin, E4 means the unbalanced tub is hitting it.
