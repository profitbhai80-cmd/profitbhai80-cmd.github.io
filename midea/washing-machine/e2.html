Midea Top-Load Washing Machine Error Code E2

Quick answer

Error codeE2 — Midea Top-Load Washing Machine
What it meansE2 = water not draining — the water in the tub did not drain out absolutely within 10 minutes (some models 15 minutes or more), so the PCB displays E2 (M4 §6). TOP-LOAD ONLY: front-load Midea washers have no E2 — their drain fault is E21.
Most common causeDrain hose hung too high or wrongly installed, drain valve blocked or not fully open (bad fit with the retractor), inner drain-pipe blocked or bent, drain pump filter blocked, retractor not opening the valve, drain pump faulty, or water level sensor / PCB failure.
Safe to check yourself?Yes — check the drain hose height and installation, check the hose end is not sealed in a pond, clean the drain pump filter, and check the drain valve rubber ring for dirt. Retractor, sensor and PCB work is technician work.
Checked againstMidea MAS70/MAS80/MAS120 Top-Load Service Manual (manuals.plus), §6 'ERORR CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§6 + §7.5 + §8); Midea MA100W75/85/95 Owner's Manual (manualspro.net), troubleshooting table p.20; Midea MAE70-504TPS/01FM-AU-S1 User Manual (manuals.plus), troubleshooting table

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E2 is a TOP-LOAD code — it means water not draining on the MAS70/MAS80/MAS120 US top-load families and the MA100/MAE70 top-load families (M4 §6/§7.5/§8; MA100 p.20; MAE70 troubleshooting table). The front-load family has NO E2 — its drain fault is E21 (6-minute rule: the water level doesn't change within 6 minutes of the pump starting). Never write 'E2 or E20' (E20 has no manual support in M1/M5/M6/M4). E1 is the fill fault (not E2's domain). E3 is lid open. E4 is unbalance. E5 has NO manual support in M4 or MA100.

The ranking pages miss the 10-minute rule entirely. appliancemode's top-load hub calls E2 a 'Drain Pump Error' and gives pump resistances of 16 Ohm @ 120 VAC / 140 Ohm @ 220-240 VAC — but it gives NO retractor test (M4 §8: 5-7 kOhm), is pump-centric on a mostly gravity-drain family (the pump exists only on up-drain models), and misses the drain-valve causes, the inner-pipe bending, and the 'water drained but E2 persists — PCB/sensor' branch. codigodeerror's dedicated E2 page is a long essay with no manual and no timeout rule, presents E2/E21 as interchangeable variants, and puts a front-load 'bottom filter behind a small cover in the lower front area' on a top-load code. removeandreplace's 2016 hub gives one line ('Draining not finished correctly') and also lists F0 and HU as top-load codes — neither appears in M4 or MA100. iFixit's guide is the strongest practical one (hose ~100 cm, pump ~16-17 Ohm) but is pump-centric, has no retractor checks and no timeout rule, and its 16 Ohm value is video-derived, not manual-derived. crownclassiccleaners gives Midea a 6-minute rule — WRONG for top-load E2 (M4: 10 min, some 15+); the 6-minute figure is the front-load E21 rule pasted onto E2.

What the machine does when E2 fires

If the water in the tub does not drain out absolutely within 10 minutes, the PCB displays E2 — the manual adds 'Remind the timeout. Some machine is 15min or more than 15min' (M4 §6). The drain phase has not finished as expected.

Common causes

  1. Drain-pipe hung too high (M4 §6).
  2. Drain pipe not installed correctly (M4 §6).
  3. Drain valve blocked by foreign bodies (M4 §6).
  4. Drain valve not fully open — bad fit with the retractor and drain valve (M4 §6).
  5. Inner drain-pipe blocked or obviously bent (M4 §6).
  6. Drain pump filter blocked by foreign bodies (M4 §6).
  7. Retractor not open — 'Tractor has sound but can't open the drain valve', or 'The retractor is not running, check whether the circuit is normal' (M4 §6).
  8. Drain pump has sound but can't drain normally (M4 §6).
  9. Water drained out absolutely but E2 still shows — PCB failure or water level sensor failure (M4 §6).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Check whether the drain-pipe is hung too high (M4 §6).
  2. Check the drain pipe is installed correctly (M4 §6).
  3. Check whether foreign bodies block the drain valve (M4 §6).
  4. Check whether the inner drain-pipe has obvious bending (M4 §6).
  5. Remove the drainage pump filter to check for foreign bodies — clean the drain pump filter (M4 §6; MA100 p.19).
  6. Check the owner-manual inspection items (MA100 p.20): if the water drain hose is placed very high; if the end of the water drain hose is sealed in a pond; if the internal rubber ring of the drain valve is blocked by dirt.
  7. Technician work (M4 §6): retractor fit and circuit, drain pump circuit, water level sensor and PCB — including the 'water drained but E2 persists' branch, where the sensor or PCB is replaced. The retractor / drain motor resistance is 5-7 kOhm (red-light blue, M4 §8).

A top-load Midea drains through a RETRACTOR (drain motor/tractor) that pulls the drain valve open — M4 §8: 5-7 kOhm — and only up-drain models also have a drain PUMP (M4 §7.5). Test the retractor first; the pump exists only on some models. Do NOT present a drain-pump-motor resistance as a manual value: no drain-pump-motor resistance is given in M4 as read (appliancemode's 16 Ohm / 140 Ohm table and iFixit's 16-17 Ohm are not M4 values). Do NOT invent: no power-cycle reset time and no valve/pump replacement procedure exist in any manual for E2. Owner actions are: check drain hose height and installation, check the hose end is not sealed in a pond, clear the drain pump filter, check the drain valve rubber ring for dirt. Retractor, sensor and PCB work is technician work.

When to call a technician

  • E2 returns after the hose, filter and valve checks — the retractor, sensor or PCB needs diagnosis.
  • The retractor has sound but can't open the drain valve, or is not running at all (M4 §6).
  • The tub is empty but E2 still shows — per M4 §6 that is a PCB or water level sensor fault, not a drain fault.
  • Any leak, burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

E2's owner checks are external only — drain hose, pump filter and drain valve rubber ring. Retractor, drain pump, sensor and PCB work means opening the machine: that is technician work. Never open the machine while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E2 mean on a Midea top-load washing machine?

E2 means the water is not draining: the water in the tub did not drain out absolutely within 10 minutes (some models 15 minutes or more), so the PCB displays E2 (M4 §6).

What causes Midea top-load E2?

Per the MAS70/80/120 service manual: the drain hose is hung too high or wrongly installed, the drain valve is blocked or not fully open (bad fit with the retractor), the inner drain-pipe is blocked or bent, the drain pump filter is blocked, the retractor is not opening the valve, the drain pump is faulty, or the water level sensor / PCB has failed.

What should I check first for E2?

The owner manual (MA100 p.20) says: 'Check the water level sensor and drain pump.' First check the drain hose height and that its end is not sealed in a pond, and clean the drain pump filter (MA100 p.19). Retractor, sensor and PCB work is technician work.

Is E2 the same on a Midea front-load washer?

No. Front-load Midea washers (MF100/MF110/MF200/MF210) have NO E2 code — their drain fault is E21, which triggers on a 6-minute rule, not 10 minutes. Do not apply this page to a front-load machine, and do not use the 6-minute figure for a top-load E2.

The tub is empty but E2 still shows — what does that mean?

M4 §6 covers exactly this: if the water in the tub is drained out absolutely and E2 still displays, the fault is the PCB or the water level sensor — replace the sensor, then the PCB. Call a technician; do not keep replacing drain parts.
