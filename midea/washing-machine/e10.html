Midea Front-Load Washing Machine Error Code E10

Quick answer

Error codeE10 — Midea Front-Load Washing Machine
What it meansE10 = water injecting problem during the wash cycle — the water level doesn't change within 3 minutes. The machine expects the level to rise as it fills, and if it doesn't move in 3 minutes it raises E10 (M1 §6; M5 §5 p.12).
Most common causeLow water pressure, a closed or partly closed tap, a kinked inlet hose, or a blocked inlet valve filter — the checks the user manuals list before any part is suspected.
Safe to check yourself?Yes — the owner steps are tap, pressure, hose and inlet-filter checks. Resistance testing of the inlet valve (3–6 kΩ) and the water level sensor (10–50 Ω) is technician work.
Checked againstMidea MF210 Series Service Manual (midea.com), §6 'ERORR CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§6, E10 row); Midea MF110W60D Service Manual (manua.ls, 71 pp.), §5 'MALFUNCTION CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§5 p.12); Midea MF200W120WB User Manual (manuals.plus), user error table; Midea MFE50/60/75 Owner's Manual (AU, manuals.plus), user error table; Midea WBMF310 Washer-Dryer User Manual (manua.ls), user error table; Midea Drum Washing Machine Service Manual (guidespdf), §3.2

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E10 is a FRONT-LOAD code — it means a water injecting problem on the MF210, MF110, MF200, MFE and WBMF310 front-load families (M1/M2/M3/M5/M6/M7, identical wording). The top-load family has NO E10 — its fill fault is E1 (water not supplied, 30-minute timeout). E33 is the front-load water-level-sensor fault — a different code from E10 even though the level sensor appears in E10's check procedure; E10 means the level isn't changing during fill, E33 means the sensor itself is faulty. E21 is the drain fault, not a fill fault. Check the machine type, not just the letters.

The ranking pages give E10 no precision. appliancemode, the #1 hub, renames the code 'Water Inlet Valve Error' — the manuals say 'Water injecting problem during wash cycle', broader than the valve alone — and gives no 3-minute rule, no >0.1 MPa pressure, no 3–6 kΩ / 10–50 Ω values, no named manual and no E1/E10 split. washerhouse, the only dedicated E10 page, is a translated content farm: its 'blow into the pressure hose, listen for 1–3 clicks' test, its citric-acid soak and its 'inlet valve cannot be repaired' claim have no manual support. removeandreplace's 2016 hub gives one line ('Inlet water overtime') with no 3-minute rule, no values and no named manual.

What the machine does when E10 fires

During the wash cycle, the machine fills and watches the water level. If the level doesn't change within 3 minutes, the board raises E10 — the fill phase is not completing as expected.

Common causes

  1. Tap not opened (M7 §3.2).
  2. Water pressure too low — must be over 0.1 MPa (M5 §5).
  3. Water pipe (inlet hose) kinked — straighten it (M2/M3/M6).
  4. Inlet valve filter blocked (M2/M3/M6; M7 §3.2).
  5. Inlet valve faulty — resistance should read 3–6 kΩ (M5 §5, technician check).
  6. Water level sensor faulty — resistance should read 10–50 Ω (M5 §5, technician check).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Check the water tap is actually opened (M7 §3.2).
  2. Check if the water pressure is high enough — the service manual requires over 0.1 MPa (M2/M3/M6; M5 §5).
  3. Straighten the water pipe (M2/M3/M6).
  4. Clean the inlet valve filter (M2/M3/M6).
  5. Technician checks (M5 §5, not owner steps): inlet valve resistance 3–6 kΩ OK; water level sensor resistance 10–50 Ω OK. Do not open the machine to test these yourself.

No power-cycle reset time, no other resistance value, no 'replace the valve' step and no citric-acid soak exists in any manual for E10. washerhouse's 'blow into the pressure hose, listen for 1–3 clicks' test and citric-acid soak are that site's own additions, not manual steps. Do NOT present E1's top-load steps as E10 steps — different machine type, different timeout. The page's owner actions are the tap/pressure/pipe/filter checks above; valve and sensor resistance testing is technician work.

When to call a technician

  • E10 returns after the tap, pressure, hose and filter checks — the inlet valve or level sensor needs diagnosis.
  • The water level still doesn't rise during fill even with the tap fully open.
  • Any leak, burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

E10's owner checks are external only — tap, hose and filter. Testing the inlet valve or the water level sensor means opening the machine: that is technician work. Never open the machine while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E10 mean on a Midea front-load washing machine?

E10 means a water injecting problem during the wash cycle: the water level doesn't change within 3 minutes. The machine expects the level to rise as it fills, and if it doesn't move in 3 minutes it raises E10 (M1 §6; M5 §5 p.12).

What should I check first for Midea E10?

The user manuals say: check the water pressure is high enough, straighten the water pipe, and clean the inlet valve filter — plus check the tap is actually opened (M2/M3/M6; M7 §3.2). The service manual adds that water pressure must be over 0.1 MPa (M5 §5).

How does a technician test the parts for E10?

By resistance: the inlet valve should read 3–6 kΩ and the water level sensor 10–50 Ω (M5 §5). Those are service-manual checks, not owner steps — do not open the machine to test them yourself.

Is E10 the same as E1?

No. E1 is a top-load-only code (water not supplied, 30-minute timeout). Front-load Midea washers have no E1 — their fill fault is E10 with the 3-minute no-change rule. Never use top-load E1 advice for a front-load E10.

Is E10 the same as E33?

No. E10 means the water level isn't changing while the machine fills; E33 is the water-level-sensor fault code itself. The level sensor is checked during E10 diagnosis (10–50 Ω), but a faulty sensor is reported as E33, not E10.
