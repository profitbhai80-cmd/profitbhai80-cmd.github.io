Midea Front-Load Washing Machine Error Code E61

Quick answer

Error codeE61 — Midea Front-Load Washing Machine
What it meansE61 = PCB cannot detect the speed feedback signal for 5 times — when the motor continues to rotate, the speed measured by the PCB is less than 15 rpm for 3 seconds (M5/M7 §5 pp.38-40). FRONT-LOAD ONLY: top-load Midea washers have no E61.
Most common causeThe terminals of the motor speed measuring device are loose, or the motor is damaged (M5/M7 §5).
Safe to check yourself?No — there is no owner-level fix for E61 in the manuals. The procedure is a technician check of the speed-measuring-device terminals, then the motor. Unplug the machine and call service.
Checked againstMidea MF110W60D Service Manual (manua.ls, 71 pp.), §5 'MALFUNCTION CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§5 pp.38-40); Midea MF110W70BA10 Service Manual (manua.ls, Model L11, 71 pp.), §5 'MALFUNCTION CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§5 pp.38-40)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E61 is a FRONT-LOAD code — it means no speed feedback on the MF110W60D and MF110W70BA10 (Model L11) front-load families (M5 §5; M7 §5, word-for-word agreement). It does not exist on top-loaders. Never merge E60/E61/E62 — E60 = motor failed to start 5 times; E61 = no speed feedback 5 times (under 15 rpm for 3 s); E62 = actual speed 300 rpm above target (SCR damaged). E61 is distinct from E50 (inverter PCB abnormal signals — its category (d) includes 'speed signal not detected', but E50 is the inverter-PCB code, E61 is the speed-feedback code) and from E64 (inverter-to-main PCB communication). Never present E61 as a carbon-brush / tachometer fault — the manuals' checked part is the motor speed measuring device's terminals.

The ranking pages give E61 almost nothing. appliancemode's hub is the ONLY page naming E61: it files E61 under 'Universal Motor Errors... carbon brushes' as an 'Abnormal motor speed signal error... irregular speed signal from the tachometer', sharing one cause list across E60/E61/E62. Errors: the under-15 rpm / 3-second / 5-times rule is never stated; one cause list is shared across three different codes (hiding all three manual procedures); carbon brushes are a check (no manual basis); 'tripped motor fuse protection' is not in M5/M7; the 'tachometer' framing is wrong — the manuals check the speed measuring device's TERMINALS; no named manual; the same hub invents E11, E20, E40, E52-E5D, E70 and E81.

What the machine does when E61 fires

The motor continues to rotate, but the speed measured by the PCB is less than 15 rpm for 3 seconds — and after the PCB cannot detect the speed feedback signal 5 times, it raises E61 (M5/M7 §5).

Common causes

  1. The terminals of the motor speed measuring device are loose (M5/M7 §5).
  2. The motor is damaged (M5/M7 §5).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Unplug the machine and call service — there is no owner-level fix for E61 in the manuals.
  2. Technician step 1 — check the motor speed measuring device terminals (M7 §5 p.39-40): 'Is the terminals of motor speed measuring device are loose? Reconnect the terminals.' Detail: 'Disassemble the 4 screws of the rear cover. Remove the rear cover and then dump the washing machine. Check the terminals of motor speed measuring device s and reconnect them if they are loose.'
  3. Technician step 2 — replace the motor (M7 §5): 'Replace the motor and make sure the terminals are ok.'

Do NOT invent: no carbon-brush check (no brushes in M5/M7's E61 procedure); no tachometer output test (the manuals check the speed measuring device's TERMINALS, not a tachometer voltage); no motor resistance value for E61 in M5/M7 as read; no power-cycle reset time; no 'motor fuse' check (not in M5/M7). Terminal and motor work is technician work; there is no owner-level fix for E61 beyond noting the code and calling service.

When to call a technician

  • E61 returns after a restart — the speed-measuring-device terminals or the motor needs diagnosis.
  • The drum turns very slowly or the speed never rises during spin.
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

E61 diagnosis means opening the machine and working on the motor terminals: that is technician work. Never open the machine while it is connected to power, and do not open the motor looking for carbon brushes — the MF110 manuals give no brush check for E61.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E61 mean on a Midea front-load washing machine?

E61 means the PCB cannot detect the speed feedback signal for 5 times: when the motor continues to rotate, the speed measured by the PCB is less than 15 rpm for 3 seconds (M5/M7 §5).

What causes Midea front-load E61?

Per the MF110W60D and MF110W70BA10 service manuals: the terminals of the motor speed measuring device are loose, or the motor is damaged.

What should I check first for E61?

There is no owner-level fix in the manuals: the procedure is a technician check of the speed-measuring-device terminals (reconnect if loose), then the motor (replace if the terminals are fine). Unplug the machine and call service — do not open the motor looking for carbon brushes, because the MF110 manuals give no brush check for E61.

Is E61 the same as E60 or E62?

No. E60 = the motor failed to start 5 times; E61 = no speed feedback 5 times (speed under 15 rpm for 3 seconds); E62 = the actual speed is 300 rpm above the target speed (SCR on the PCB damaged). Three distinct codes with three distinct checks — do not treat them as one 'motor error'.

Is E61 the same on a Midea top-load washer?

No. Top-load Midea washers (MAS70/80/120, MA100) have NO E61 code in any manual read. Do not apply this page to a top-load machine.
