Midea Washer-Dryer Error Code E36

Quick answer

Error codeE36 — Midea Washer-Dryer
What it meansE36 = water heater error — the water heater runs continuously for 10 minutes but the temperature changes by less than 3 °C (M1 §6; M6 §5). WASHER-DRYER ONLY: washer-only front-loaders and top-loaders have no E36.
Most common causeThe water heater or its NTC temperature sensor is faulty — heater M1: 27-33 Ohm / M6: 20-35 Ohm; NTC M1: 4-6 kOhm / M6: 4.8 kOhm ±8% at 25 °C.
Safe to check yourself?No — the manual says 'E36 ERROR can not be clear by consumer. Need service engineer.' The clear method (K1+K4 long press 3 seconds) is a service step, not an owner fix. Call service.
Checked againstMidea MF210 Series Service Manual (midea.com), §6 'ERORR CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§6, E36 row + detail page); Midea MF100W70BA-W-IT Service Manual (midea.com, Model L01), §5 'MALFUNCTION CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§5 pp.13+ + §7 component values)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E36 is a washer-dryer / heated-wash code — it means a water heater error on the MF210 family and the MF100W70BA washer-dryer (M1 §6; M6 §5, word-for-word agreement). It does not exist on washer-only front-loaders (MF110W60D §5 has no E36) or on top-loaders. Never confuse E36 with E3A (dry heater fault — the DRY heater, not the WASH heater; E36's rule is heater on 10 minutes with under 3 °C change, E3A's rule is under 3 °C change during the drying process) or with E34/E35 (water temperature sensor failure — one combined row in M1 and M6, no open/short split).

The ranking pages give E36 no detection rule. appliancemode's hub calls E36 a 'Heater Assembly Error... water temperature is not increasing as expected' — but its heater resistance '16 Ohm for 110V or 300 Ohm for 220V' is WRONG vs M1 (27-33 Ohm) and M6 (20-35 Ohm), self-flagged as an 'example... may vary by model'; it never states the 10-minute / under-3 °C rule, never mentions that E36 cannot be cleared by the consumer, gives no K1+K4 clear method, and refers the NTC check to its E34/E35 section, whose open/short split is itself unverified (M1 and M6 list E34/E35 as one combined row). No dedicated per-code E36 article exists.

What the machine does when E36 fires

The water heater runs continuously for 10 minutes, but the temperature changes by less than 3 °C — the board raises E36, and the manual adds that this error cannot be cleared by the consumer (M1 §6; M6 §5).

Common causes

  1. Water heater faulty — heater M1: 27-33 Ohm / 30±3 Ohm; M6: 20-35 Ohm (technician check).
  2. NTC temperature sensor faulty — NTC M1: 4-6 kOhm; M6: 4.8 kOhm ±8% at 25 °C (technician check).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Call a service engineer — the MF210 service manual says: 'E36 ERROR can not be clear by consumer. Need service engineer.'
  2. The manual's clear method is K1+K4 long press 3 seconds — a service step, not an owner fix.
  3. Technician checks (M1 §6; M6 §7): water heater resistance — M1 27-33 Ohm (30±3 Ohm), M6 20-35 Ohm; NTC — M1 4-6 kOhm, M6 4.8 kOhm ±8% at 25 °C. Quote both ranges; do not pick one as 'the' value.

Do NOT invent: no power-cycle reset time; no heater resistance '16 Ohm for 110V or 300 Ohm for 220V' (appliancemode's self-flagged guess — wrong vs M1/M6); no NTC check via the E34/E35 split (M1 and M6 list E34/E35 as ONE combined row with no open/short split). Heater and sensor work is technician work; there is no owner-level fix for E36 beyond noting the code and calling service.

When to call a technician

  • E36 returns — the water heater or NTC sensor needs diagnosis by a service engineer.
  • The wash water never gets hot even on a hot program.
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

E36 has no owner-level fix: the manual says a service engineer is needed. Heater and sensor work means opening the machine: that is technician work. Never open the machine while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E36 mean on a Midea washer-dryer?

E36 means a water heater error: the water heater runs continuously for 10 minutes but the temperature changes by less than 3 °C (M1 §6, M6 §5).

Can I clear E36 myself?

No. The MF210 service manual says 'E36 ERROR can not be clear by consumer. Need service engineer.' The manual's clear method is K1+K4 long press 3 seconds — a service step, not an owner fix. Call service.

What causes Midea washer-dryer E36?

Per the manuals: the water heater or its NTC temperature sensor is faulty (heater M1: 27-33 Ohm / M6: 20-35 Ohm; NTC M1: 4-6 kOhm / M6: 4.8 kOhm ±8% at 25 °C). A technician tests the heater and sensor.

Is E36 the same as E3A?

No. E36 is the WASH water heater error (heater on 10 minutes, under 3 °C change). E3A is the DRY heater fault (temperature changed under 3 °C during the drying process). Different heaters, different rules — do not treat them as one code.

Is E36 the same on a Midea top-load washer?

No. Top-load Midea washers (MAS70/80/120, MA100) have NO E36 code in any manual read, and washer-only front-loaders (MF110W60D) have no E36 either. Do not apply this page to a washer-only machine.
