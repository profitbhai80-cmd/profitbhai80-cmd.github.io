Midea Washer-Dryer Error Code E3A

Quick answer

Error codeE3A — Midea Washer-Dryer
What it meansE3A = dry heater fault / no drying — during the drying process the temperature changed by less than 3 °C, so the E3A alarm is given (M1 §6; M6 §5). WASHER-DRYER ONLY: the dryer is NOT heating enough — not overheating.
Most common causeDry heater or its circuit (dry heater 44 Ohm ±10% at 220-240 V), dry temperature sensor (4.8 kOhm ±10%), a blocked fan, or the PCB.
Safe to check yourself?No — heater, fan and PCB work is technician work. There is no owner-level fix for E3A beyond noting the code and calling service.
Checked againstMidea MF210 Series Service Manual (midea.com), §6 'ERORR CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§6, E3A row + detail page); Midea MF100W70BA-W-IT Service Manual (midea.com, Model L01), §5 'MALFUNCTION CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§5 pp.13+ checks)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E3A exists ONLY on washer-dryer models — it means a dry heater fault on the MF210/MF200D families and the MF100W70BA washer-dryer (M1 §6; M6 §5). Never present E3A as a general washer code. Never confuse it with E3 (top-load lid open — a different code on a different family) or with E36 (water heater error — the WASH heater, not the DRY heater; E36's rule is heater on 10 minutes with under 3 °C change, E3A's rule is under 3 °C change during the drying process). The forums' 'excessive heat / clogged vent' theory is the OPPOSITE of M1's rule: E3A means the dryer is NOT heating enough (too little temperature change), not overheating.

The ranking pages get E3A backwards. appliancemode's hub reduces E3A to a generic 'Drying heater assembly Error' — M1's detection rule (under 3 °C during drying) is never stated; its dry heater '18 Ohm / 30 Ohm example' does not match M1 (44 Ohm ±10% at 220-240 V); its drying NTC values '1.7 kOhm at 50 °C / 0.45 kOhm at 90 °C' are self-flagged 'examples' and do not match M1 (4.8 kOhm ±10%); it adds a 'tripped high-limit thermostat' and fan/capacitor checks (C-R ~80 Ohm, C-S ~90 Ohm, capacitor ~2.8 µF — all 'examples') with no manual basis in M1; and it SWAPS E37/E38 in the same section. The JustAnswer E3A ventless washer/dryer threads are mutually inconsistent: one says the dryer is overheating due to a clogged vent (blower malfunctioning), the other says excessive heat from restricted airflow — neither matches M1's definition, and one AI summary suggests unplugging for 10 minutes, which is not a manual step.

What the machine does when E3A fires

During the drying process the temperature changed by less than 3 °C — the dryer is not heating enough, so the E3A alarm is given (M1 §6).

Common causes

  1. Dry heater faulty — dry heater 44 Ohm ±10% at 220-240 V (M1 §6, technician check).
  2. Dry temperature sensor faulty — 4.8 kOhm ±10% (M1 §6, technician check).
  3. Heater connector/terminal faulty (M6 §5).
  4. Fan blocked (M6 §5).
  5. PCB faulty (M6 §5).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Call service — there is no owner-level fix for E3A in the manuals.
  2. Technician checks (M6 §5): heater connector/terminal, whether the fan was blocked, PCB.
  3. Technician values (M1 §6): dry heater 44 Ohm ±10% at 220-240 V; dry temperature sensor 4.8 kOhm ±10%.

Do NOT invent: no dry heater '18 Ohm for 110V or 30 Ohm for 220V' (appliancemode's self-flagged example — does not match M1's 44 Ohm ±10%); no drying NTC values '1.7 kOhm at 50 °C / 0.45 kOhm at 90 °C' (appliancemode's examples — do not match M1's 4.8 kOhm ±10%); no 'tripped high-limit thermostat' or fan/capacitor values (appliancemode adds these with no manual basis in M1); no unplug-for-10-minutes reset (a JustAnswer AI summary's suggestion, not a manual step); no 'clogged vent / overheating' diagnosis (the forums' theory contradicts M1's rule — E3A means too LITTLE temperature change). Heater, fan and PCB work is technician work.

When to call a technician

  • E3A returns — the dry heater, sensor, fan or PCB needs diagnosis by a technician.
  • Drying finishes but clothes come out cold or damp.
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

E3A has no owner-level fix: heater, fan and PCB work means opening the machine. That is technician work. Never open the machine while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E3A mean on a Midea washer-dryer?

E3A means a dry heater fault / no drying: during the drying process the temperature changed by less than 3 °C, so the E3A alarm is given (M1 §6, M6 §5).

Is E3A caused by overheating or a clogged vent?

No — that is the forums' theory, and it is the opposite of the manual's rule. E3A means the dryer is NOT heating enough (temperature changed under 3 °C during drying). The manuals' checks are the heater connector/terminal, whether the fan was blocked, and the PCB (M6 §5).

What causes Midea washer-dryer E3A?

Per the manuals: the dry heater or its circuit (dry heater 44 Ohm ±10% at 220-240 V), the dry temperature sensor (4.8 kOhm ±10%), a blocked fan, or the PCB. A technician tests these.

Is E3A the same as E36?

No. E36 is the WASH water heater error (heater on 10 minutes, under 3 °C change). E3A is the DRY heater fault (temperature changed under 3 °C during the drying process). Different heaters, different rules — do not treat them as one code.

Is E3A the same on a Midea top-load washer?

No. Top-load Midea washers (MAS70/80/120, MA100) have NO E3A code in any manual read, and washer-only front-loaders (MF110W60D) have no E3A either. Do not apply this page to a washer-only machine. (Also: E3A is not E3 — E3 on a top-load is the lid-open alarm.)
