Midea Top-Load Washing Machine Error Code E1

Quick answer

Error codeE1 — Midea Top-Load Washing Machine
What it meansE1 = water is not supplied — the water in the tub did not achieve the specified level within 30 minutes of the water supply starting, so the PCB displays E1 and the buzzer rings (M4 §6, §7.2). TOP-LOAD ONLY: front-load Midea washers have no E1 — their fill fault is E10.
Most common causeTap not open, water pressure too low, a blocked or damaged inlet valve, a loose fill-valve/PCB power cord, or PCB failure — plus the 'can't stop the water' variant (loose or leaking level-sensor pressure pipe, sensor failure, PCB failure).
Safe to check yourself?Yes — check the tap is open, check water pressure, and check the inlet hose/valve screens for blockage. Valve, sensor-pipe and PCB work is technician work.
Checked againstMidea MAS70/MAS80/MAS120 Top-Load Service Manual (manuals.plus), §6 'ERORR CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§6 + §7.2); Midea MA100W75/85/95 Owner's Manual (manualspro.net), troubleshooting table p.20; Midea MF11EW80BA10 manual FAQ (manuals.co.uk) — E1 = water supply issue

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E1 is a TOP-LOAD code — it means water is not supplied on the MAS70/MAS80/MAS120 US top-load families and the MA100 top-load family (M4 §6/§7.2; MA100 p.20). The front-load family has NO E1 — its fill fault is E10 (3-minute rule: water not reaching the specified level within 3 minutes). Never apply E10's 3-minute timing to a top-load E1, and never write 'front-load E1'. E2 is the drain fault (not E1's domain — the wsimg PDF pastes E2 drain content under E1). E3 is lid open (not overheating, not unbalance). E4 is unbalance (not overflow, not sensor malfunction). E5 has NO manual support in M4 or MA100 — never present it as a verified code.

The ranking pages miss the 30-minute rule entirely. appliancemode's top-load hub calls E1 a 'Water Inlet Valve Error' with 6 fix steps and a resistance table (1650 Ω @ 120 VAC / 3600 Ω @ 220–240 VAC) — plausibly manual-derived, but NO manual is cited — and it gives no 30-minute timeout rule, no 'can't stop the water' variant, and merges E4/E5 into one 'Unbalanced Load' section (E5 is a separate code on some newer top-load families — MLV47/MAV160, M12 §4.3: out of balance in spin — not the same fault as E4), invents F3 as a water-level-sensor code (M4 has no F3 — F8 is the level-sensor code) and FD as a lid-lock code (M4 has no FD). removeandreplace's 2016 hub gives one line ('Not enough water pressure') and misses the timeout, all M4 causes and the can't-stop-water variant. homegeargeek says 'E3: Overheating issue' (WRONG — E3 is lid open) and 'E4: Sensor malfunction' (WRONG — E4 is unbalance). theblackurbantimes gets E3/E4/E5 ALL WRONG vs M4/MA100. The wsimg PDF lists DRAIN faults under '(E1 Code)' — E2-type content pasted under E1.

What the machine does when E1 fires

If the water supply is not finished within 30 minutes of the water supply starting, the buzzer rings and 'E1' is displayed (M4 §7.2). The same E1 entry also covers the opposite failure: the machine can't stop the water (M4 §6).

Common causes

  1. Tap not open (M4 §6).
  2. Water pressure small (M4 §6).
  3. Inlet valve blocked (M4 §6).
  4. Loose fill-valve/PCB power cord (M4 §6).
  5. Inlet valve damage (M4 §6).
  6. PCB failure (M4 §6).
  7. 'Can't stop the water' variant (M4 §6): level-sensor pressure pipe loose or leaking; sensor failure; PCB failure.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Check the water tap is opened or not (MA100 p.20).
  2. Check the water pressure is high enough, and check the inlet hose and valve screens for blockage.
  3. Technician work (M4 §6): inlet valve, level-sensor pressure pipe, sensor and PCB — including the 'can't stop the water' variant, where the level-sensor pressure pipe may be loose or leaking, the sensor may have failed, or the PCB may have failed.

The manual evidence for E1 is a cause list, not a step-by-step owner procedure — it is quoted as a cause list above, and no steps are invented. No inlet-valve resistance value is given in M4/MA100 as read (appliancemode's 1650 Ω @ 120 VAC / 3600 Ω @ 220–240 VAC is uncited — it is not presented as a manual value here); no power-cycle reset time and no valve-replacement procedure are given. Owner actions are: check the tap is open, check water pressure, check the inlet hose/valve screens for blockage. Valve, sensor-pipe and PCB work is technician work.

When to call a technician

  • E1 returns after the tap, pressure and screen checks — the inlet valve or PCB needs diagnosis.
  • The machine keeps filling and won't stop — that is the 'can't stop the water' side of the same E1 entry: turn the water off and call a technician.
  • Any leak, burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

E1's owner checks are external only — tap, pressure, hose and screens. If the machine keeps filling and won't stop, turn the water supply off and call a technician. Valve, sensor-pipe and PCB work is technician work. Never open the machine while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E1 mean on a Midea top-load washing machine?

E1 means water is not supplied: the water in the tub did not reach the specified level within 30 minutes of the water supply starting, so the PCB displays E1 and the buzzer rings (M4 §6, §7.2).

What causes Midea top-load E1?

Per the MAS70/80/120 service manual: the tap is not open, water pressure is too low, the inlet valve is blocked or damaged, the fill-valve/PCB power cord is loose, or the PCB has failed. The manual also lists a 'can't stop the water' variant caused by a loose or leaking level-sensor pressure pipe, sensor failure, or PCB failure.

What should I check first for E1?

The owner manual (MA100 p.20) says: 'Check the water tap is opened or not.' Then check water pressure and the inlet hose/valve screens for blockage. Valve, sensor-pipe and PCB work is technician work.

Is E1 the same on a Midea front-load washer?

No. Front-load Midea washers (MF100/MF110/MF200/MF210) have NO E1 code — their fill fault is E10, which triggers on a 3-minute rule, not 30 minutes. Do not apply this page to a front-load machine.

My washer keeps filling and won't stop — is that E1 too?

Yes, that is the 'can't stop the water' side of the same E1 entry in M4 §6: the level-sensor pressure pipe may be loose or leaking, the sensor may have failed, or the PCB may have failed. Turn the water off and call a technician — do not confuse it with E2 (drain fault) or E4 (unbalance).
