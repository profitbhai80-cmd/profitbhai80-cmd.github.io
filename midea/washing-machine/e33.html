Midea Front-Load Washing Machine Error Code E33

Quick answer

Error codeE33 — Midea Front-Load Washing Machine
What it meansE33 = water level sensor failure — the machine detects that the frequency of the water level sensor is not within the normal range for 10 seconds and gives an alarm (M1 §6; M5 §5 p.28).
Most common causeA loose or damaged connector between the sensor and the internal wire, a damaged sensor, or a damaged computer board — in the M5 p.29 check order.
Safe to check yourself?Limited — the manual's checks are connector inspections and sensor/board replacement, which is technician work. No owner reset step or sensor value is given in the manuals read.
Checked againstMidea MF210 Series Service Manual (midea.com), §6 'ERORR CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§6, E33 row); Midea MF110W60D Service Manual (manua.ls, 71 pp.), §5 'MALFUNCTION CODES AND EXPLANATIONS' (§5 pp.28–29); Midea MF100T80B Service Manual FAQ (manua.ls) — E33 corroboration, EU top-load MFL80-T1301B family

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E33 is a FRONT-LOAD code — it means a water level sensor failure on the MF210 and MF110 front-load families (M1/M5, identical wording), with the same meaning corroborated on the EU top-load MFL80-T1301B family. E33 is a sensor fault (10-second frequency rule). E12 is an overflow event (16-second rule) — a different fault with a different diagnosis (stuck-open inlet valve, pressure hose, air chamber). E11 has NO manual support in M1/M5 — never present it as verified. E3 is the top-load lid-open code — it only looks similar to E33; never import E3 lid content onto this page.

The ranking pages merge E33 into the wrong fault. appliancemode's section 'E11, E12, E33' gives E33 the SAME causes/steps as overflow E11/E12 (stuck-open inlet valve, pressure hose, air chamber) — WRONG framing: E33 is a sensor-frequency fault (10 s rule), not an overflow event; its sensor values 'capacitance ~45 nF, ohmic ~25 Ω — examples and may vary' are hedged guesses, not manual values; it gives no 10-second rule and no named manual. The iFixit E3 guide is a neighbour-confusion risk only (E3 vs E33 look similar). One JustAnswer thread says E3 means 'the control isn't sensing the water temperature' — WRONG vs M4 (E3 = lid open); anecdotal, not adopted.

What the machine does when E33 fires

The machine detects that the frequency of the water level sensor is not within the normal range for 10 seconds and gives the E33 alarm (M1 §6; M5 §5 p.28).

Common causes

  1. Connector between the water level sensor and the internal wire terminal loose or faulty (M5 p.29).
  2. Water level sensor itself damaged (M5 p.29).
  3. Connector between the PCB terminal and the internal wire terminal loose (M5 p.29).
  4. Computer board damaged (M5 p.29).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Technician checks (M5 p.29, in order — not owner steps): check the connector between the water level sensor and the internal wire terminal.
  2. If the sensor itself is damaged, repair or change the sensor (M5 p.29).
  3. Check the connector loose between the PCB terminal and the internal wire terminal (M5 p.29).
  4. If all inspections are complete, suspect the computer board is damaged (M5 p.29).

Do NOT invent: no resistance or capacitance value for the E33 sensor check is given in M1/M5 as read — appliancemode's 'capacitance ~45 nF, ohmic ~25 Ω — examples and may vary' are hedged guesses, not manual values. M5's 10–50 Ω and 40–50 nF figures belong to the E10/E21 and E12 steps, not E33. No power-cycle reset time is given for E33. Sensor and board replacement is technician work.

When to call a technician

  • E33 returns after a restart — the sensor, its connectors or the computer board needs diagnosis.
  • A sensor or PCB connector is visibly loose or damaged (technician check).
  • Any burning smell or visible damage — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

E33's checks are connector inspections and sensor/board replacement — technician work. Never open the machine while it is connected to power.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E33 mean on a Midea front-load washing machine?

E33 means the water level sensor has failed: the machine detects that the sensor's frequency is not within the normal range for 10 seconds and gives an alarm (M1 §6; M5 §5 p.28).

How do I check Midea E33?

Per the MF110W60D service manual (M5 p.29), in order: check the connector between the water level sensor and the internal wire terminal; if the sensor itself is damaged, repair or change it; check the connector between the PCB terminal and the internal wire terminal; if all inspections are complete, suspect the computer board is damaged.

Is E33 the same as E12?

No. E33 is a sensor fault — the level sensor's frequency is out of range for 10 seconds. E12 is an overflow event with a 16-second rule and a different diagnosis (stuck-open inlet valve, pressure hose, air chamber). Do not use E12's fix steps for E33.

Is E33 the same as E3?

No. E3 is the top-load lid-open code; E33 is a front-load water level sensor failure. The codes only look similar — they are different faults on different families.

Can I fix E33 myself?

The manual's checks are connector inspections and a sensor/board replacement — technician work. No owner reset step or sensor value is given in the manuals read. Switch the machine off, check nothing is visibly loose only if you are comfortable, and call a technician for sensor or board replacement.
