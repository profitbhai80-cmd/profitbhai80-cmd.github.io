Midea Refrigerator Error Code EF

Quick answer

Error codeEF — Midea Refrigerator
What it meansEF = "Water tank installation failure" — the manual-fill water-dispenser tank (on models with an internal water tank) is not seated or detected correctly. It is a seating/installation fault, not a compressor, sensor or cooling fault: the fridge keeps refrigerating, but the dispenser side is flagged.
Most common causeThe water tank is not installed in place — pulled out partially, tilted, or its seat switch and connection terminals are not making contact.
Safe to check yourself?Partially: the manual's own first step — pull the water tank out and install it again — is a homeowner step. Steps 2–3 (cleaning terminals behind the tank seat, checking the seat switch) are technician-level.
Checked againstMidea Service Manual REF MDRE411FGE01 (midea.com official PDF), §9.7 "Error code and solutions" — BMF No Frost Series (Market Model MDRE411FG / HD-390RWEN BI, CE-BCD300WX-JT 22031020011081) + Midea Service Manual — SBS No Frost Series (Market Model MDRS925CIE46M / SA-BCD695WZ-JT / 92031050Z05922; midea.com official PDF), §8.6 "Error code and solutions"

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because EF is a service-manual-only code on a narrow set of models. It appears in two Midea service manuals — the BMF No Frost series (built-in bottom-freezer MDRE411FG / HD-390RWEN BI) and the SBS No Frost side-by-side series (MDRS925CIE46M) — for units with a manual-fill internal water tank. The French-door (MRF29D9BST, MRF29D3AST), quad-door (MDRF632FGF46APD), and most bottom-freezer user-manual fault tables carry NO EF row, and the MDRS925CIE46M user manual lacks it too (only its service manual defines it). If your model has no water tank, this page is not your code. Do not import the Midea heat-pump "EF: Clock chip error" — wrong appliance entirely.

What the machine does when EF fires

The display shows EF and the water-dispenser side is flagged. Refrigeration itself is unaffected — EF is an installation fault of the water tank, not a cooling fault.

Common causes

  1. The water tank is not installed in place — pulled out partially, tilted, or not pushed fully into its seat (the manuals' own step 1).
  2. The connection terminals behind the water tank seat are unplugged or have foreign matter in them (the manuals' step 2).
  3. The switch in the water tank seat is damaged and no longer detects the tank (the manuals' step 3).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Check whether the water tank is installed in place: pull the water tank out and install it again — the manuals' own first step, and the homeowner step.
  2. Check whether the connection terminals behind the water tank seat are plugged in place and whether there are foreign matters in it; after cleaning the terminal, plug it in again — technician-level, do not force anything.
  3. Check whether the switch in the water tank seat is damaged — if it is, it must be replaced by a qualified technician.
  4. Code-display rule from both manuals: error codes display within the first 30 minutes of each power-on; after that, enter error-code display mode to view them (BMF: keep pressing "SUPER COOL" and "SUPER FRZ" for 7 seconds, display "0", then press "FREEZER" to select mode "A6"; SBS: long-press "SUPER FRZ" and "FREEZER" for 7 seconds, display "0", then "FRIDGE" or "FREEZER" to select "A6").

The home scope is step 1 — reseat the tank. Steps 2–3 involve terminals and a switch behind the tank seat; the manuals' language (clean the terminal, replace the switch) is service work. If EF persists after a proper reseat, call a qualified technician.

When to call a technician

  • EF persists with the water tank correctly installed and seated.
  • The switch in the water tank seat is damaged — replacement is technician work.
  • Other fault codes appear alongside EF — then the machine has a fault beyond the tank installation.

Safety first

EF involves only the water tank and its seat — but never force connectors or probe terminals with power on. Leave panel-off terminal and switch work to a qualified technician, and switch the fridge off before any visible check.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does EF mean on a Midea refrigerator?

EF means "Water tank installation failure" — the manual-fill water-dispenser tank is not seated or detected correctly. It is verified verbatim in two Midea service manuals: the BMF No Frost Series manual REF MDRE411FGE01 (§9.7) and the SBS No Frost Series manual (§8.6).

Does EF stop the fridge from cooling?

No. EF is an installation fault of the water tank, not a cooling fault — the refrigerator keeps refrigerating. The dispenser side is what is flagged.

Which Midea models show EF?

The two families whose service manuals define it: the BMF No Frost bottom-freezer series (MDRE411FG / HD-390RWEN BI) and the SBS No Frost side-by-side series (MDRS925CIE46M). French-door, quad-door and most other bottom-freezer models have no EF row.

What is the official fix order for Midea EF?

The manuals' own 3-step procedure: (1) check whether the water tank is installed in place — pull it out and install it again; (2) check whether the connection terminals behind the water tank seat are plugged in place and free of foreign matter — clean and re-plug; (3) check whether the switch in the water tank seat is damaged — replace it.

EF vs the heat-pump EF — same code?

No. A Midea heat-pump/chiller "EF" (clock chip error) is a different code on a different appliance. For a refrigerator, EF is the water tank installation failure — do not follow heat-pump advice for a fridge.
