Midea Refrigerator Error Code F4

Quick answer

Error codeF4 — Midea Refrigerator
What it meansF4 on a Midea refrigerator means the sensor at the bottom of the refrigerating (fresh-food) ice maker has failed. F4 exists ONLY on French-door models with a refrigerating-chamber ice maker — combi, four-door, side-by-side and top-freezer manuals have no F-codes at all. The fridge itself keeps refrigerating; only ice production is affected, because the ice maker cannot judge harvest timing without its bottom sensor.
Most common causeThe bottom sensor of the fresh-food ice maker has failed, or its connection has been disturbed — the ice maker can no longer report its own temperature, so it cannot judge when to harvest.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home checks are: power-cycle once, toggle the ice maker off and back on, and check for jammed cubes blocking the sensor area of the ice maker. The fridge keeps cooling throughout, so food stays safe. Sensor replacement is technician work; the manuals say to contact maintenance personnel.

Applies to — read this first

This page is only for French-door Midea refrigerators with a refrigerating-chamber (fresh-food) ice maker. That scope is the whole point: no other Midea refrigerator family lists F4, and the ice-maker codes form a ladder in which every rung is a different fault. Read the ladder before you act, because F4 (sensor) and F5 (mechanical harvest failure) send you to different parts.

The aggregator page has no F4 section at all — F4 appears only as a parenthetical inside its EE section, which inverts the relationship: on French-door models F4 and EE are the two chamber-specific bottom-sensor faults, neither subordinate to the other. And its combined F5/F6/EP section lumps a turn-over failure, an initialization failure and the freezer counterpart into one fix, which cannot distinguish a sensor fault (F4) from a motor fault (F5).

What the machine does when F4 fires

The display shows F4 and the fresh-food ice maker stops producing ice: its bottom sensor reports the ice maker's temperature so the maker knows when to harvest, and when that sensor fails the maker cannot judge harvest timing. The refrigerator itself keeps refrigerating — food stays safe, only ice production stops. That reassurance matters: an ice-maker fault is not a cooling fault.

Common causes

  1. The bottom sensor of the refrigerating ice maker itself has failed.
  2. The sensor's connection has been disturbed, so its temperature reading never reaches the control.
  3. Jammed cubes blocking the sensor area of the ice maker, confusing its temperature reading.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Power-cycle the refrigerator once: unplug it, wait a few minutes, plug it back in, and see whether F4 returns.
  2. Toggle the ice maker off and back on using its own switch, then look into the ice-maker area for jammed cubes blocking the sensor area; clear any jammed cubes gently, without forcing the mechanism.
  3. Confirm the rest of the fridge is still cooling normally — with F4 it should be, since only ice production is affected.
  4. If F4 returns after the power-cycle with the ice maker clear, stop: the manuals' only further instruction is to contact maintenance personnel. Bottom-sensor replacement is technician work.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. Never force the ice maker by hand and never open the refrigerator's electrical covers. If the fridge itself stops cooling, that is a different fault — treat it as urgent and call a technician.

When to call a technician

  • F4 returns after a power-cycle with the ice maker clear of jammed cubes.
  • No ice production at all from the fresh-food ice maker while the fridge cools normally.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or water near electrical parts — unplug immediately and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

F4 involves mains-powered refrigerator electronics: never open the refrigerator's electrical covers or touch sensor terminals while investigating it. Unplug the refrigerator before any visible check, and never force the ice maker mechanism by hand — a forced harvest can damage it. Sensor replacement belongs to a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F4 mean on a Midea refrigerator?

F4 means the sensor at the bottom of the refrigerating (fresh-food) ice maker has failed. Three French-door manual families — MRF29D3, MRF29D9BST and MRF29D6AST — list it identically.

What is the difference between Midea refrigerator F4 and F5?

F4 is a sensor fault: the ice maker's bottom sensor has failed. F5 is a mechanical fault: the ice maker fails to turn over (release) its ice. A page that treats 'ice maker broken' as one fault sends F5 owners to replace a sensor and F4 owners to replace a motor.

Does F4 stop my fridge from cooling?

No. The refrigerator keeps refrigerating — food stays safe and only ice production stops. The ice maker cannot judge harvest timing without its bottom sensor, so it stops making ice instead of guessing.

Is F4 the same as EE?

No — they are counterparts. F4 is the fresh-food ice-maker bottom sensor; EE is the freezer ice-maker bottom sensor. Same kind of fault, different ice maker.

How do I clear Midea refrigerator F4?

Power-cycle once, toggle the ice maker off and on, and clear any jammed cubes from the sensor area. If F4 returns, the sensor fault is genuine and the manuals say to contact maintenance personnel.
