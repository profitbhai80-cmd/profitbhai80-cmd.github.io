Midea Refrigerator Error Code EP

Quick answer

Error codeEP — Midea Refrigerator
What it meansEP on a Midea refrigerator means the freezer ice maker fails to turn over (release) its ice — a mechanical harvest failure of the freezer maker. It is the freezer counterpart of F5 (the fresh-food maker's harvest failure). On single-ice-maker Midea families the same code is worded generically as the (only) ice maker failing to drop or separate its ice. And one warning up front: Daewoo's EP is a completely different fault (an EEPROM error) — this page is Midea only.
Most common causeThe freezer ice maker's harvest mechanism is jammed by bridged or stuck cubes, its drive motor has failed, or the release movement is obstructed — so finished ice cannot be turned out of the mould.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home checks are: confirm the code is EP (not E0 or EE) and that it is the freezer maker, power-cycle once, and clear a visible ice jam by hand from the freezer ice box. Motor, mechanism and board work is technician-only — the manuals say to contact maintenance personnel.

Applies to — read this first

This page is for Midea refrigerators, and the scope split is the whole point. On French-door dual-ice-maker models EP is specifically the FREEZER maker's harvest failure. On single-ice-maker families (MDRS925CIE46M, MRS26D5AST, MRQ23B4AST, MRF23I5AST, MRQ22D7AST) the same code is the only ice maker's harvest failure, worded generically. Check which kind of machine you own before you act — and never borrow Daewoo's EP, which is an EEPROM fault on another brand entirely.

EP vs F5 vs E0 — do not mix themEP = freezer maker harvest failure. F5 = fresh-food maker harvest failure. E0 = freezer maker initialization failure (never starts). EP and F5 are the same step on different makers; EP and E0 are different steps on the same maker.

Both ranking aggregator pages lump EP with F5 and F6 into one shared section with one shared fix list — so a freezer-harvest fault, a fresh-food harvest fault and a fresh-food initialization fault all get identical advice, and motor-resistance testing plus motor and board replacement are presented as home steps. This page separates EP, names its maker, and stops home work at a visible jam check.

What the machine does when EP fires

The display shows EP and the freezer ice maker cannot release its ice: the harvest (turn-over) movement fails, so finished cubes stay in the mould instead of dropping into the box. The refrigerator keeps cooling and freezing — the manuals say so explicitly — but freezer ice production stops until the harvest fault is fixed.

Common causes

  1. Cubes bridged or stuck in the freezer ice-maker mould, blocking the turn-over movement.
  2. The freezer ice maker's drive motor or harvest mechanism has failed.
  3. The mechanism is obstructed by ice buildup or a foreign object in the mould area.
  4. A fault in the ice-making board or its connection that stops the harvest signal from being read.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the code letter: EP is a harvest (turn-over) failure. If your display shows E0, that is the freezer maker's initialization failure, not this page; if it shows EE, that is the freezer maker's bottom sensor.
  2. Confirm which maker is affected: on dual-ice-maker models EP is the freezer maker, not the fresh-food maker (that is F5).
  3. Power-cycle once: switch the refrigerator off, wait a few minutes, switch it back on, and see whether EP returns.
  4. Remove the freezer ice box and look for bridged or stuck cubes or any visible obstruction in the mould area. Clear only what you can move by hand, without tools and without forcing the mechanism, then reseat the box.
  5. If EP returns after the power-cycle with nothing visibly jammed, stop: motor, mechanism and board diagnosis belongs to a qualified technician.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. EP has no deeper home diagnostic in any manual — code check, maker check, power-cycle, visible jam check, then the technician. Never force the harvest mechanism and never test motor resistance yourself.

When to call a technician

  • EP returns after a power-cycle with nothing visibly jammed in the freezer maker.
  • The freezer ice maker does not move during harvest, or moves part-way and stops.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or water leaking around the ice maker — switch off and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

EP involves a mains-powered refrigerator with a motorized ice maker: never open the ice maker housing, never force its harvest mechanism, and never test motor or board circuits yourself. Switch the unit off before removing the ice box, keep hands dry, and leave all electrical and sealed-system work to a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does EP mean on a Midea refrigerator?

EP means the freezer ice maker fails to turn over (release) its ice — a mechanical harvest failure. French-door manuals (MRF29D9BST, MRF29D6AST) word it exactly that way; single-ice-maker manuals word the same code generically as the ice maker failing to drop or separate its ice.

What is the difference between EP and F5?

Only which maker: EP is the freezer maker's harvest failure, F5 is the fresh-food maker's harvest failure. Same step, different maker, different part to fix.

What is the difference between EP and E0?

EP is a harvest failure: the freezer maker starts but cannot release its ice. E0 is an initialization failure: the freezer maker never starts at all. Different steps on the same maker.

Is Daewoo EP the same fault?

No. Daewoo's EP is an EEPROM read/write error — an electronics fault with nothing to do with ice makers. This page is Midea only; a Daewoo owner with EP needs a different page entirely.

Why do some websites give the same fix for F5, F6 and EP?

Because they lump all three into one section with one shared solution list. That hides the real distinctions — initialization versus harvest, freezer versus fresh-food — and presents motor and board replacement as home steps. On this page those are technician-only.
