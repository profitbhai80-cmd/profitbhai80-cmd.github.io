Midea Refrigerator Error Code C9

Quick answer

Error codeC9 — Midea Refrigerator
What it meansC9 on a Midea refrigerator means the sensor in the ice-making chamber fails — the MRF29D9BST English table words it as the temperature sensor in the ice-making chamber. C9 is NOT CC — CC is the chamber's defrost sensor, a different sensor with a different job. No dedicated C9 page existed anywhere before this one: the code appears only as a one-line manual row and a passing mention on aggregator pages.
Most common causeThe ice-making chamber's temperature sensor itself has failed, or its connection has been disturbed — so the control gets no usable reading of how cold the chamber is.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home checks are: confirm the code is C9 (not CC, F4 or EE), power-cycle once, and check the ice box is seated. Sensor testing and replacement are technician-only — the manuals say to contact maintenance personnel.

Applies to — read this first

This page is for Midea French-door and 4-door refrigerators with an ice-making chamber. The chamber has two sensors, and C9 is one of them: C9 is the (temperature) SENSOR, CC is the DEFROST sensor. The rest of the ladder matters too: F4 and EE are the fresh-food and freezer bottom sensors — C9 is none of those.

What the machine does when C9 fires

The display shows C9 and the control loses its temperature reading for the ice-making chamber: it cannot tell how cold the chamber is, so ice production from that chamber is no longer managed normally. The refrigerator itself keeps cooling and freezing — the manuals say so explicitly — but the chamber's ice making is out of reliable service until the sensor fault is fixed.

Common causes

  1. The ice-making chamber's temperature sensor itself has failed.
  2. The sensor's connection has been disturbed or damaged, so its reading no longer reaches the control.
  3. A fault in the ice-making board circuit that the control reads as a sensor failure.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the code letter: C9 is the chamber (temperature) SENSOR. If your display shows CC, that is the chamber defrost sensor — a different page and a different part.
  2. Power-cycle once: switch the refrigerator off, wait a few minutes, switch it back on, and see whether C9 returns.
  3. Remove the ice box and check it is seated correctly, then reseat it. Do not remove panels, do not touch sensor wiring, and do not test anything with a meter.
  4. If C9 returns after the power-cycle with the box seated, stop: sensor testing and replacement belongs to a qualified technician.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. C9 has no deeper home diagnostic in any manual — code check, power-cycle, box seating, then the technician. Never open the chamber's panels and never test sensor resistance yourself.

When to call a technician

  • C9 returns after a power-cycle with the ice box seated.
  • Ice production from the chamber stops, or runs erratically, while C9 is showing.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or water leaking around the ice maker — switch off and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

C9 involves a mains-powered refrigerator with an ice maker: never open the chamber's panels, never touch sensor wiring, and never test sensor circuits yourself. Switch the unit off before removing the ice box, keep hands dry, and leave all electrical and sealed-system work to a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does C9 mean on a Midea refrigerator?

C9 means the sensor in the ice-making chamber fails — the MRF29D9BST English table words it as the temperature sensor in the ice-making chamber. Three French-door manual families list it, with CC (chamber defrost sensor) as its neighbour.

What is the difference between C9 and CC?

C9 is the chamber's (temperature) SENSOR — how cold is the chamber? CC is the chamber's DEFROST sensor — has the chamber defrosted? Two sensors, two codes, two repairs.

Why does one French manual seem to call C9 a defrost sensor?

A French-language copy of the D9BST manual words C9 with a defrost term, which collides with CC's meaning. The English tables are consistent: CC is the defrost sensor, C9 is the (temperature) sensor. This page follows the English tables and discloses the conflict rather than hiding it.

What is the difference between C9, F4 and EE?

C9 is a chamber sensor. F4 is the bottom sensor of the fresh-food ice maker and EE is the bottom sensor of the freezer ice maker. Chamber versus bottom is the distinction that matters.

Does the refrigerator still cool with C9 showing?

Yes. The manuals say the refrigerator can still provide cooling and freezing functions in this fault case, but that you should contact maintenance personnel to ensure normal use.
