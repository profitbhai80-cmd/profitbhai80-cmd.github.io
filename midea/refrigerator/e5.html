Midea Refrigerator Error Code E5

Quick answer

Error codeE5 — Midea Refrigerator
What it meansE5 on a Midea refrigerator means the defrost sensor of the freezing (freezer) chamber has failed — the sensor that times the melt cycle keeping the freezer evaporator clear. It is not the freezer temperature sensor (that is E2): a failed defrost sensor lets frost build on the freezer evaporator, choking airflow, so the freezer and then the fresh-food compartment warm gradually with visible frost. Power-cycle once, check for heavy frost on the freezer back panel, never chip ice off with tools, and book a technician.
Most common causeThe freezer defrost sensor (or its detection circuit) has failed, so the defrost cycle no longer clears frost from the freezer evaporator.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home checks are: power-cycle once, look for heavy frost on the freezer evaporator area, and move food if temperatures are rising. Sensor and PCB work is technician work, and ice must never be chipped off with tools.

Applies to — read this first

E5 is the second freezer sensor, and the one owners confuse with E2. The distinction matters because the symptom chain is different: E2 is a wrong temperature reading, while E5 is a failed defrost cycle — frost builds, airflow chokes, and the compartments warm slowly. Four-door models carry E5 alongside E4 (the refrigerating-chamber defrost sensor); combi models carry E5 alone, with no E4. Either way, E5 is the freezer defrost sensor on every family checked.

The ranking aggregator gets E5's zone right but never distinguishes it from E2, and its resistance chart gives a 'freezer sensor' value without saying whether that row is the temperature sensor or the defrost sensor — two different parts. Measuring the wrong one against that chart proves nothing.

What the machine does when E5 fires

The display shows E5 and the freezer's defrost cycle stops being timed correctly. Without working defrost sensing, frost accumulates on the freezer evaporator instead of being melted away on schedule. As the frost thickens it chokes airflow, so the freezer warms gradually — and because the fresh-food compartment is cooled from the same system, it follows. The warming is slow and the frost is visible, which is the opposite pattern from E2's wrong-reading behaviour.

Common causes

  1. A failed freezer defrost sensor — the sensor that reports evaporator temperature for the defrost cycle no longer reads correctly.
  2. A failed defrost-sensor detection circuit — the wiring or connection between the sensor and the main PCB is broken or contaminated.
  3. A faulty main PCB that can no longer read the defrost circuit even with a sound sensor.
  4. Heavy frost already on the evaporator from an earlier defrost failure, which keeps the fault visible even after the sensor is the only failed part — the frost itself is a symptom, not the root cause.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Unplug the refrigerator, wait a few minutes, and plug it back in. If E5 clears, it was a transient detection glitch — keep an eye on it, because a repeat points to the defrost circuit.
  2. Look (do not touch with tools) for heavy frost on the freezer back panel or evaporator area. Heavy, uneven frost there is the classic E5 symptom chain and confirms you are on the defrost fault, not a temperature-reading fault.
  3. Never chip ice off the evaporator with a knife, screwdriver or any tool — you can puncture a refrigerant line or damage the evaporator. If frost must be cleared, that is a technician's job with the machine unplugged.
  4. Check the actual freezer and fresh-food temperatures with a thermometer. If either is rising, move perishables to another cold store — do not rely on the display while E5 is showing.
  5. If E5 returns after the power-cycle, stop: the sensor, its circuit and the PCB are technician checks. Book a technician and mention the frost you saw — it tells them which system to test first.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. E5 has no deeper home diagnostic in any manual — power-cycle, frost check, temperature and food check, then the technician. Never chip ice, never open the electrical covers.

When to call a technician

  • E5 returns after a power-cycle — the defrost circuit fault is genuine, not a glitch.
  • Heavy frost keeps returning on the freezer evaporator after any clearing.
  • Freezer or fresh-food temperatures keep rising despite the machine running.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or water leaking from electrical parts — unplug immediately and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

E5 involves mains-powered refrigerator electronics and a frosted evaporator that hides refrigerant lines. Never chip ice off with tools, never open the refrigerator's electrical covers or the main PCB housing, and never touch sensor or PCB terminals. Unplug the refrigerator before any visible check. Sensor, circuit and PCB diagnosis belongs to a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E5 mean on a Midea refrigerator?

E5 means the defrost sensor of the freezing (freezer) chamber has failed — the sensor (or its detection circuit) that times the melt cycle keeping the freezer evaporator clear. Six Midea refrigerator manual families list it identically, with wording that varies only by family.

How is E5 different from E2?

E2 is the freezer temperature sensor (wrong temperature reading); E5 is the freezer defrost sensor (failed melt cycle). E2 misreads the temperature; E5 lets frost build on the evaporator until airflow chokes and the compartments warm gradually. They are different parts — replacing one will not fix the other.

Why is there frost in my freezer with E5?

Because the defrost cycle is no longer being timed correctly: frost that should be melted away on schedule stays on the evaporator, thickens, and blocks airflow. Heavy frost on the freezer back panel is the classic E5 symptom — do not chip it off with tools.

How do I clear Midea refrigerator E5?

Power-cycle once — unplug, wait a few minutes, plug back in. If E5 was a transient detection glitch it clears. If it returns, the fault is genuine: the sensor, its circuit and the PCB are technician checks. There is no deeper home fix.

Is Midea refrigerator E5 the same as air-conditioner E5?

No — same letter, different appliance. Midea air-conditioner E5 is an indoor pipe-sensor fault. Midea refrigerator E5 is the freezer defrost sensor. And Midea dishwasher E5 does not exist in any Midea dishwasher manual.
