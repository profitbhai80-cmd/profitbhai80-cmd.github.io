Midea Refrigerator Error Code CC

Quick answer

Error codeCC — Midea Refrigerator
What it meansCC on a Midea refrigerator means the defrosting sensor in the ice-making chamber fails. The chamber is the enclosed space where the ice maker lives; its defrost sensor tells the control when the chamber has been defrosted. CC is NOT C9 — C9 is the chamber's temperature sensor, a different sensor with a different job — and it is not E5 or E4, which are the freezer and fresh-food defrost sensors. No dedicated CC page existed anywhere before this one: the code appears only as a one-line manual row and a passing mention on aggregator pages.
Most common causeThe ice-making chamber's defrost sensor itself has failed, frost or ice buildup around the chamber is confusing the sensor, or its connection has been disturbed — so the control cannot confirm the chamber's defrost state.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home checks are: confirm the code is CC (not C9, E4 or E5), power-cycle once, check the ice box is seated, and look for heavy frost or ice buildup in the chamber to report to the technician. Sensor testing and replacement are technician-only — the manuals say to contact maintenance personnel.

Applies to — read this first

This page is for Midea French-door and 4-door refrigerators with an ice-making chamber. The chamber has two sensors, and CC is one of them: CC is the DEFROST sensor, C9 is the (temperature) sensor. The rest of the ladder matters too: F4 and EE are the fresh-food and freezer bottom sensors, E5 and E4 are the freezer and fresh-food defrost sensors — CC is none of those.

No competitor page has a CC section at all: both aggregators mention CC exactly once, as a one-line gloss inside their EE sections, with no steps, no FAQs, no models and no manuals. This page is the first dedicated CC page, and its core value is the CC-vs-C9 distinction that nobody states.

What the machine does when CC fires

The display shows CC and the control loses its defrost reading for the ice-making chamber: it cannot confirm the chamber's defrost state, so ice production from that chamber is no longer managed normally. The refrigerator itself keeps cooling and freezing — the manuals say so explicitly — but the chamber's ice making is out of reliable service until the sensor fault is fixed.

Common causes

  1. The ice-making chamber's defrost sensor itself has failed.
  2. Heavy frost or ice buildup around the chamber confusing or blocking the defrost sensor.
  3. The defrost sensor's connection has been disturbed or damaged.
  4. A fault in the ice-making board circuit that the control reads as a sensor failure.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the code letter: CC is the chamber DEFROST sensor. If your display shows C9, that is the chamber temperature sensor — a different page and a different part.
  2. Power-cycle once: switch the refrigerator off, wait a few minutes, switch it back on, and see whether CC returns.
  3. Remove the ice box and check it is seated correctly, then look for heavy frost or ice buildup in the chamber. Do not chip ice off with tools and do not use sharp objects — just note what you see for the technician, then reseat the box.
  4. If CC returns after the power-cycle, stop: sensor testing and replacement belongs to a qualified technician.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. CC has no deeper home diagnostic in any manual — code check, power-cycle, box seating, frost observation, then the technician. Never chip frost off with tools, never open the chamber's panels, and never test sensor resistance yourself.

When to call a technician

  • CC returns after a power-cycle with the ice box seated.
  • Heavy frost or ice buildup in the ice-making chamber, or ice production stopping while CC is showing.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or water leaking around the ice maker — switch off and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

CC involves a mains-powered refrigerator with an ice maker: never open the chamber's panels, never chip frost off with tools or sharp objects, and never test sensor circuits yourself. Switch the unit off before removing the ice box, keep hands dry, and leave all electrical and sealed-system work to a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does CC mean on a Midea refrigerator?

CC means the defrosting sensor in the ice-making chamber fails. Three French-door manual families (MRF29D3, MRF29D9BST, MRF29D6AST) list it identically, and the 4-door manual lists the same chamber fault family.

What is the difference between CC and C9?

CC is the chamber's DEFROST sensor — it tells the control about defrosting. C9 is the chamber's (temperature) SENSOR — it tells the control how cold the chamber is. Two sensors, two codes, two repairs.

Why does one French manual seem to call C9 a defrost sensor?

A French-language copy of the D9BST manual words C9 with a defrost term, which collides with CC's meaning. The English tables are consistent: CC is the defrost sensor, C9 is the (temperature) sensor. This page follows the English tables and says so, rather than hiding the conflict.

What is the difference between CC, E5 and E4?

CC is the ice-making chamber's defrost sensor. E5 is the freezer defrost sensor and E4 is the fresh-food defrost sensor. Different locations, different parts.

Can I chip the frost off myself to fix CC?

No. If you see heavy frost in the chamber, note it for the technician and do not chip it with tools or sharp objects — that risks damaging refrigerant lines and panels, and the sensor itself may still be the fault.
