Midea Refrigerator Error Code F5

Quick answer

Error codeF5 — Midea Refrigerator
What it meansF5 on a Midea refrigerator means the refrigerating (fresh-food) ice maker fails to turn over (release) its ice — a mechanical harvest failure, not a sensor failure. F5 exists ONLY on French-door models with a refrigerating-chamber ice maker. The fridge itself keeps refrigerating; only ice production stops. Check for jammed or bridged cubes first: cubes bridged across the mould are the commonest harvest block.
Most common causeThe ice maker's harvest step fails: cubes jammed or bridged across the mould block the turn-over, or the ice-maker motor cannot complete the release.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home checks are: power-cycle once, toggle the ice maker off and back on, and look for jammed or bridged cubes or an overfilled mould — clear a jam gently, never by forcing the mechanism. Motor or PCB work is technician work; the manuals say to contact maintenance personnel.
Checked againstMidea MRF29D3AST/MRF29D3AWW/MRF29D3ABB French Door User Manual (midea.com); Midea MRF29D9BST French Door Series User Manual; Midea MRF29D6AST French Door Refrigerator Freezer User Manual

Applies to — read this first

This page is only for French-door Midea refrigerators with a refrigerating-chamber (fresh-food) ice maker. F5 is a mechanical harvest failure — the maker cannot flip and release its ice — and that single distinction decides the repair: a jammed mould is a home fix, a dead motor is not. Do not let any page lump F5 with the sensor and initialization codes.

What the machine does when F5 fires

The display shows F5 and the fresh-food ice maker stops releasing ice: the harvest cycle runs freeze, then release (turn-over), then harvest — and F5 means the release step fails, so finished cubes never leave the mould. The refrigerator itself keeps refrigerating, so food stays safe; only ice production stops, and the mould may sit full of unreleased cubes.

Common causes

  1. Cubes jammed or bridged across the mould, blocking the turn-over — the commonest harvest block.
  2. An overfilled mould that the maker cannot flip.
  3. A failed ice-maker motor that cannot complete the release movement.
  4. A fault in the ice-maker drive circuit that the control reads as a harvest failure.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Power-cycle the refrigerator once: unplug it, wait a few minutes, plug it back in, and see whether F5 returns.
  2. Toggle the ice maker off and back on, then look into the mould for jammed or bridged cubes or an overfilled mould. Clear a jam gently, without forcing the mechanism — forcing a jammed maker can damage it.
  3. Confirm the rest of the fridge is still cooling normally — with F5 it should be, since only ice production is affected.
  4. If F5 returns with the mould clear, stop: the motor or drive circuit is the likely fault and the manuals say to contact maintenance personnel. Motor replacement is technician work.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. Never force the ice maker by hand, never run a forced test on a jammed maker, and never open the refrigerator's electrical covers. If the fridge itself stops cooling, that is a different fault — treat it as urgent and call a technician.

When to call a technician

  • F5 returns with the mould clear of jammed or bridged cubes.
  • The ice maker never starts its turn-over movement at all (as opposed to starting and jamming).
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or water near electrical parts — unplug immediately and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

F5 involves mains-powered refrigerator electronics: never open the refrigerator's electrical covers or touch motor or PCB terminals while investigating it. Unplug the refrigerator before any visible check, and never force the ice maker mechanism by hand — a forced harvest can damage it. Motor replacement belongs to a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F5 mean on a Midea refrigerator?

F5 means the refrigerating (fresh-food) ice maker fails to turn over (release) its ice — a mechanical harvest failure. Three French-door manual families — MRF29D3, MRF29D9BST and MRF29D6AST — list it identically.

What is the difference between Midea refrigerator F5 and F4?

F5 is mechanical: the maker cannot flip and release its ice. F4 is a sensor fault: the ice maker's bottom sensor has failed. Treating them as one fault sends F5 owners to replace a sensor and F4 owners to replace a motor.

What is the difference between F5, F6 and EP?

F5 is a harvest failure (the release step fails). F6 is an initialization failure (the maker never starts its cycle). EP is the freezer counterpart of F5 — the freezer ice maker's turn-over failure, a different maker.

Can I still use my fridge if it shows F5?

Yes, for food: the refrigerator keeps refrigerating and only ice production stops. Clear any jammed or bridged cubes gently — but never force the maker by hand, because a forced harvest can damage it.

How do I clear Midea refrigerator F5?

Power-cycle once, toggle the ice maker off and on, and clear any jammed or bridged cubes or an overfilled mould. If F5 returns with the mould clear, the motor or drive circuit is the likely fault and the manuals say to contact maintenance personnel.
