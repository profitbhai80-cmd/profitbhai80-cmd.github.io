Midea Refrigerator Error Code L5

Quick answer

Error codeL5 — Midea Refrigerator
What it meansL5 = condenser fan fault — the fan that cools the condenser near the compressor at the back/bottom of the fridge has failed. Not the freezer fan (that is L3/L4) and not the wrong-speed code (that is L6). L-codes appear only on the smart side-by-side families; French-door and bottom-freezer families use E-codes.
Most common causeA dead condenser fan motor, or loose connection terminals on the condenser fan.
Safe to check yourself?Limited: the manuals say the refrigerator may still provide cooling or freezing functions with L5 showing, but the user should contact maintenance personnel. The home scope is: confirm the code, check the back/bottom airflow is clear from the outside, restart once. The rest is technician work.
Checked againstMidea MDRS925CIE46M Side-by-Side User Manual fault table (midea.com official UK PDF); Midea MDRB600MIC46P User Manual fault table (midea.com official PDF); Midea MRS25I7BST Side-by-Side User Manual fault table (manuals.plus)

Applies to — read this first

The L-codes are the smart side-by-side families' fan codes — MDRS925CIE46M, MDRB600MIC46P and MRS25I7BST — and they do not appear on the French-door or bottom-freezer E-code families. Two different fans, four different codes: L3/L4 are the freezer fan (inside the freezer, circulating cold air), L5/L6 are the condenser fan (near the compressor at the back/bottom, cooling the condenser). The manuals list all four as separate rows — L5 is the condenser fan's fault row.

Midea MDRS925CIE46M Side-by-Side User Manual (midea.com official UK PDF)L5 = Condenser fan fault — in the L3/L4/L5/L6 fan-code block.
Midea MDRB600MIC46P User Manual (midea.com official PDF)L5 = Condenser fan fault, same L3–L6 block.
Midea MRS25I7BST Side-by-Side User Manual (manuals.plus)L5 = Condenser fan fault, same block. Fault preamble: the refrigerator may still provide cooling/freezing functions, but the user should contact maintenance personnel.
L3/L4 — the other fan pairL3 = freezer fan fault, L4 = freezer fan speed abnormal: the fan inside the freezer. L5/L6 are the condenser fan near the compressor — a different fan, a different row.
L6 — the paired code, not the sameL6 = condenser fan speed is abnormal: the condenser fan turns but at the wrong speed. L5 means it has faulted (not working). Two rows, two faults.

No ranking page explains any L-series code: the leading general guide has no L5 row and never maps any code to the condenser fan — its E9 section mentions checking the condenser fan in passing, but without an L5 mapping it is not actionable. Owners often confuse the two fans, so the manuals' own split is genuinely useful, and it is documented here on two official midea.com manuals.

What the machine does when L5 fires

The display shows L5 and the fault stays: it does not self-clear. With the condenser fan dead, the condenser sheds heat poorly — the compressor works harder and temperatures drift even though the fridge may keep running for a while.

Common causes

  1. The condenser fan motor has died and is not running at all.
  2. The fan's connection terminals are loose, corroded or unplugged.
  3. The fan is blocked by dust build-up or a foreign object at the back/bottom.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the exact code and your model: L5 is a smart side-by-side family code (MDRS925CIE46M, MDRB600MIC46P, MRS25I7BST).
  2. From the outside only, check the back/bottom area is clear of dust blankets and obstructions — a blocked condenser area is the one home-checkable contributor. Do not reach into the fan.
  3. Restart the fridge once and see whether L5 returns.
  4. Do not open the back panel or touch the fan or its wiring — motor and terminal work is technician territory.
  5. If L5 persists, contact maintenance personnel as the manuals instruct.

The manuals name no user-serviceable fix for L5 beyond the restart and clear outside airflow. The fan, its terminals and any motor replacement are qualified-person work that means opening the machine.

When to call a technician

  • L5 persists after one restart with clear outside airflow.
  • The compressor area runs hot or the fridge is struggling to hold temperature while L5 shows.
  • Any burning smell or damaged wiring — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

L5 involves mains-powered refrigerator electronics at the back of the unit: never open the back panel, remove the rear cover, or touch fan wiring while investigating it. Switch the fridge off at the outlet before any visible check, and leave fan and board work to a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does L5 mean on a Midea refrigerator?

L5 means the condenser fan has faulted — the fan near the compressor at the back/bottom that cools the condenser is not working. It is verified in three Midea manuals, two of them on midea.com itself (MDRS925CIE46M, MDRB600MIC46P), plus the MRS25I7BST manual.

Is the condenser fan the same as the freezer fan?

No. The condenser fan (L5/L6) sits near the compressor at the back/bottom and cools the condenser; the freezer fan (L3/L4) is inside the freezer circulating cold air. The manuals keep them as four separate rows — owners often confuse the two fans, so check which pair your code belongs to.

What is the difference between L5 and L6?

The manuals' own distinction, mirroring the L3/L4 pair: L5 = condenser fan fault (the fan is not working); L6 = condenser fan speed abnormal (the fan is turning but at the wrong speed).

Can the fridge keep running with L5 showing?

The manuals say the refrigerator may still provide cooling or freezing functions with L5 showing, but the user should contact maintenance personnel. With the condenser fan dead, the compressor works harder and temperatures drift — it is not a stable state.

Can I clean around the condenser fan myself?

You can clear dust and obstructions from the outside of the back/bottom area, which is the one home-checkable contributor. Do not open the rear cover, reach into the fan, or touch its wiring — that is technician work.
