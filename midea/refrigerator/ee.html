Midea Refrigerator Error Code EE

Quick answer

Error codeEE — Midea Refrigerator
What it meansEE on a Midea refrigerator means the sensor at the bottom of the freezer ice maker fails. It is the freezer counterpart of F4 (the fresh-food maker's bottom sensor). On single-ice-maker Midea families the same code is worded generically as 'ice maker sensor fault' — without the freezer qualifier. The manuals' own line applies: the refrigerator can still provide cooling and freezing functions, but you should contact maintenance personnel.
Most common causeThe freezer ice maker's bottom sensor itself has failed, or its connection has been disturbed — so the control gets no usable reading from the bottom of the freezer maker.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home checks are: confirm the code is EE (not F4, CC or C9) and that it is the freezer maker, power-cycle once, and check the freezer ice box is seated. Sensor resistance testing and sensor or board replacement are technician-only — the manuals say to contact maintenance personnel.

Applies to — read this first

This page is for Midea refrigerators, and the sensor's exact position is the whole point: EE is the BOTTOM sensor of the FREEZER ice maker. That separates it from F4 (bottom sensor of the fresh-food maker) and from CC and C9 (sensors in the ice-making chamber). On single-ice-maker families the manual wording drops the freezer qualifier and just says 'ice maker sensor fault' — same code, less specific wording.

EE vs F4 vs CC vs C9 — do not mix themEE = bottom sensor, freezer maker. F4 = bottom sensor, fresh-food maker. CC = defrost sensor, ice-making chamber. C9 = temperature sensor, ice-making chamber. Four sensors, four codes.

Both ranking aggregator pages give EE one vague paragraph ('a problem with a sensor specifically located within the freezer's ice maker unit' / 'the temperature sensor within the freezer ice maker') with no bottom-sensor specificity, no model families and no manual names — and then prescribe multimeter resistance testing and sensor/PCB replacement as home steps. This page names the exact sensor, separates it from its three neighbours, and keeps testing and replacement technician-only.

What the machine does when EE fires

The display shows EE and the freezer ice maker's bottom sensor gives the control no usable reading: the control cannot trust the freezer maker's sensor, so freezer ice production stops being managed normally. The refrigerator itself keeps cooling and freezing — the manuals say so explicitly — but the freezer maker is out of reliable service until the sensor fault is fixed.

Common causes

  1. The freezer ice maker's bottom sensor itself has failed.
  2. The sensor's connection has been disturbed or damaged, so its reading no longer reaches the control.
  3. A fault in the ice-making board circuit that the control reads as a sensor failure.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the code letter: EE is the freezer bottom sensor. If your display shows F4, that is the fresh-food bottom sensor; CC and C9 are chamber sensors, not bottom sensors.
  2. Confirm which maker is affected: on dual-ice-maker models EE is the freezer maker, not the fresh-food maker.
  3. Power-cycle once: switch the refrigerator off, wait a few minutes, switch it back on, and see whether EE returns.
  4. Remove the freezer ice box and check it is seated correctly, then reseat it. Do not remove panels, do not touch sensor wiring, and do not test anything with a meter.
  5. If EE returns after the power-cycle with the box seated, stop: sensor testing and replacement belongs to a qualified technician.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. EE has no deeper home diagnostic in any manual — code check, maker check, power-cycle, box seating, then the technician. The aggregators' resistance-testing steps are technician work, not home steps, and this page does not repeat them as instructions.

When to call a technician

  • EE returns after a power-cycle with the freezer ice box seated.
  • The freezer ice maker produces no ice, or produces it erratically, while EE is showing.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or water leaking around the ice maker — switch off and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

EE involves a mains-powered refrigerator with an ice maker: never open the ice maker housing, never touch sensor wiring, and never test sensor resistance yourself. Switch the unit off before removing the ice box, keep hands dry, and leave all electrical and sealed-system work to a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does EE mean on a Midea refrigerator?

EE means the sensor at the bottom of the freezer ice maker fails. French-door manuals (MRF29D9BST, MRF29D6AST) word it exactly that way; single-ice-maker manuals word the same code generically as 'ice maker sensor fault'.

What is the difference between EE and F4?

Only which maker: EE is the bottom sensor of the freezer ice maker, F4 is the bottom sensor of the fresh-food (refrigerating) ice maker. Same position, different maker.

What is the difference between EE, CC and C9?

EE is a bottom sensor on the freezer maker. CC is the defrost sensor in the ice-making chamber. C9 is the (temperature) sensor in the ice-making chamber. Bottom versus chamber is the distinction that matters.

Can I test the EE sensor myself with a multimeter?

No — not as a home step. The aggregator pages present resistance testing and sensor replacement as home solutions; on this page they are technician-only, because the manuals' only instruction beyond the fault listing is to contact maintenance personnel.

Does the refrigerator still cool with EE showing?

Yes. The manuals say the refrigerator can still provide cooling and freezing functions in this fault case, but that you should contact maintenance personnel to ensure normal use.
