Midea Dishwasher Error Code E7

Quick answer

Error codeE7 — Midea Dishwasher
What it meansE7 means a short-circuit failure of the thermistor — the temperature sensor that measures the water temperature in the tub has shorted, so the control board reads an impossible temperature and locks the machine out. The service manual detects it in the test program.
Most common causeA short-circuited NTC thermistor or shorted wiring in its circuit — the board reads an impossible temperature and locks out.
Safe to check yourself?Partially. You can safely power-cycle the dishwasher at the wall and run the manual's test program to confirm the code. The NTC resistance check and any board or wiring work are technician work.
Checked againstMidea Dishwasher Technical Service Manual — Troubleshooting and Repair Guide (PCB model 7601S platform); Shunde Midea Dishwasher Manufacturing Co., Ltd. maintenance manual (model WQP8-B9252)

Applies to — read this first

Good news for diagnosis: E7 is consistent across the Midea dishwasher families we checked — it is always the short-circuit failure of the thermistor (the water-temperature sensor). What you must NOT do is borrow meanings from lookalike sources: one ranking page calls E7 a 'wash pump issue' and another lists E7 as an 'outdoor ambient temperature sensor error' — a dishwasher has no outdoor ambient sensor and no wash-pump code at E7; both contradict the Midea service manuals. E7 is the short-circuit half of a matched pair with E6 (open-circuit); see our E6 page for the other half.

ManualExact wording for E7
Midea Dishwasher Technical Service Manual — Troubleshooting and Repair Guide (PCB model 7601S platform)"E7 Short-circuit failure of thermistor: In test program, if a short-circuit thermistor failure is detected, E7 occurs. The drain pump runs until the flowmeter is motionless for 2 mins, other components stop, and a buzzer sounds for 30s with 'Error 7' displayed." Inspection tree: "For E6 (Open-circuit thermistor failure) and E7 (Short-circuit thermistor failure), check NTC circuit. If incorrect, replace NTC." NTC resistance table (measured at the RE connector): 15 °C = 17.48 kΩ, 20 °C = 12.12 kΩ, 25 °C = 10 kΩ, 30 °C = 8.299 kΩ, 40 °C = 5.807 kΩ, 50 °C = 4.144 kΩ, 60 °C = 3.011 kΩ, 70 °C = 2.224 kΩ, 80 °C = 1.667 kΩ, 85 °C = 1.451 kΩ — a shorted thermistor reads near-zero against this table.
Shunde Midea Dishwasher Manufacturing Co., Ltd. maintenance manual (model WQP8-B9252)"If the thermocouple has been checked for a short circuit, the dishwasher will shut off all the loads, the alarm buzzer will ring for thirty seconds and the windows display E7, after alarming, the dishwasher pauses for three minutes, then drains water for sixty seconds." (Older-generation reaction variant.)

The service manual detects E7 in the test program ('In test program, E1, E3, E4, E6, E7, Ed are valid'). On the older WQP8 generation the machine's reaction differs — all loads shut off, 30-second buzzer, then a 3-minute pause followed by 60 seconds of draining — versus the current generation's drain-pump-until-flowmeter-still sequence. Both are documented in the series table.

What the machine does when E7 fires

The drain pump runs until the flowmeter is motionless for 2 minutes, all other components stop, and the buzzer alarms for 30 seconds with 'Error 7' displayed. (On the older WQP8 generation the reaction is: all loads shut off, the buzzer rings for 30 seconds, then the dishwasher pauses for three minutes and drains water for sixty seconds.)

Common causes

  1. The NTC thermistor has short-circuited internally — the service manual's primary definition of E7.
  2. Damaged insulation or pinched wiring shorting the NTC circuit (the RE circuit on the main PCB).
  3. A shorted connector in the thermistor circuit.
  4. A faulty main PCB — the service manual's E6/E7/Ed tree ends here: 'If problems persist after all inspections, a PCB malfunction is likely; replace the PCB.'

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the dishwasher off at the wall socket, wait a full minute, and switch it back on. If E7 was a transient detection glitch it clears; if it returns on the next cycle, the thermistor-circuit fault is genuine.
  2. Confirm it with the manual's test program: with the door open and within 60 seconds of power-on, hold down Start/Pause and press POWER until the machine enters test mode, then close the door to run it. E7 will re-display if the short-circuit fault is present. (Activation can vary slightly between models.)
  3. If E7 is confirmed, stop: the service manual's next step is unplugging the RE connector and measuring the NTC resistance against the manual's table — a shorted thermistor reads near-zero instead of the expected ~10 kΩ at room temperature — which means opening the protecting box and handling the PCB. That is technician work. Check the connection first; if the short is in the thermistor, replace the NTC; if everything checks good, the manual points at the PCB.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. If a check needs the bottom panel or protecting box opened, a connector unplugged, or the PCB touched, it is no longer a safe home check — skip to the technician section.

When to call a technician

  • E7 returns after the power cycle and in the test program — the short-circuit fault is genuine.
  • The NTC resistance reads near-zero at the RE connector — the thermistor has shorted internally.
  • Visible pinched or heat-damaged wiring in the sump area shorting the thermistor circuit.
  • Water overheats or the heating runs unchecked before E7 latched — the blinded temperature control is a safety issue, switch off and call promptly.
  • All NTC checks pass but E7 persists — the service manual points at the main PCB.

Safety first

Disconnect the dishwasher from power before any inspection — the service manual's own safety notice states it is 'only intended for qualified technicians who are aware of the respective safety regulations.' Mains-voltage wiring and capacitors that hold charge sit behind the bottom panel; water and electricity share the same cabinet. Never open the protecting box or touch the PCB yourself.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E7 mean on a Midea dishwasher?

E7 means a short-circuit failure of the thermistor — the temperature sensor that measures the water temperature in the tub has shorted (near-zero resistance), so the control board reads an impossible temperature and locks the machine out. The Midea service manual defines it exactly this way.

What is the difference between Midea dishwasher E7 and E6?

They are the two faces of one fault: E7 = the thermistor circuit is shorted (near-zero resistance), E6 = the thermistor circuit is open (infinite resistance). Both point at the same NTC thermistor integrated into the sump and share the same inspection tree.

How do I reset Midea dishwasher E7?

Switch the dishwasher off at the wall, wait a minute, and switch it back on. If E7 returns, it is a genuine thermistor-circuit fault. You can confirm it with the manual's test program (door open, within 60 seconds of power-on hold Start/Pause and press POWER until test mode, then close the door). Past that, the NTC resistance check is technician work.

Some sites say Midea E7 is a wash pump problem. Is that right?

No. The Midea service manuals define E7 strictly as a short-circuit failure of the thermistor. The wash pump has no E7 code — and neither does any 'outdoor ambient temperature sensor', which is a heat-pump part that doesn't exist on a dishwasher. Both claims contradict the manufacturer's own documentation.
