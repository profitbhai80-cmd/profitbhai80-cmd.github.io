Midea Dishwasher Error Code E1

Quick answer

Error codeE1 — Midea Dishwasher
What it meansLonger inlet time: the inlet valve has been open for about 4 minutes without enough water entering. The manuals define it this way across all model families — the US MDT24P4AST manual words it as "Not enough water flow in allotted time".
Most common causeThe supply tap is closed or only partly open, the inlet hose is kinked or restricted, the inlet mesh filter is clogged, or household water pressure is below 20 psi.
Safe to check yourself?Yes — for the basic external checks below. Do not open the machine's body or test valves with a multimeter.
Checked againstMidea MDT24P4AST user manual (US); Midea MDT24P6CST user manual; Midea Smart Dishwasher with WiFi (Model 340607) manual; Midea MDP18B10AWWC manual; Midea MFD60S120X-HR manual; Midea dishwasher technical service manual

Applies to — read this first

Good news on this code: unlike Hisense's E1, which means two different things on two model families (see our Hisense dishwasher E1 page), Midea's E1 is consistent — every Midea dishwasher manual we checked defines it as a water-fill timeout.

ManualExact wording for E1
MDT24P4AST (US) / MDT24P6CST"Not enough water flow in allotted time." Cause: low or no water flow. Solution: confirm water is turned on; verify pressure 20–80 psi.
Smart Dishwasher with WiFi (Model 340607) / MDP18B10AWWC"Longer inlet time." Cause: faucet not opened, water intake restricted, or water pressure too low.
MFD60S120X-HR (EU freestanding)Water filling time exceeded the preset limit — inlet valve open 4 minutes without reaching the desired water level.
Factory technical service manual"E1 Water filling exceed pre-set time: if inlet valve is open for 4 mins but water quantity is insufficient, E1 occurs."

Note: these are built-in models. Compact Midea countertop dishwashers may use different codes — if your machine is a countertop model, confirm in its own manual. Find your model number on the rating label — see how to find your model number.

What the machine does when E1 fires

From the factory technical service manual: when E1 occurs, the drain pump runs until the flowmeter is motionless (about 2 minutes), all other components stop, the buzzer sounds for 30 seconds and the display shows "Error 1". That pump running on its own is the machine's designed reaction, not a second fault — let it finish, then switch the power off and fix the water supply.

Common causes

  1. The water tap feeding the dishwasher is closed or only partly open.
  2. The inlet hose is kinked, trapped, or its connection is restricted.
  3. The small mesh filter at the inlet hose connection is clogged with grit or limescale.
  4. Household water pressure is below 20 psi — the US manual specifies 20–80 psi for sufficient flow.
  5. The AQUASTOP safety-hose device (on models with one) has shut the inlet — the service manual lists the AQUASTOP hose and tap first in its E1 inspection chain.
  6. Persistent E1 after all water checks: inlet valve coil or connector fault, flowmeter or pressure-switch fault, or control board fault — technician territory.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the dishwasher off. Confirm the supply tap is fully open, not just partly — a tap turned a quarter-turn can starve the fill.
  2. Straighten any kink or tight bend in the inlet hose. If the machine moves freely, look behind it; do not force a built-in unit.
  3. With the tap closed and a towel ready, disconnect the inlet hose at the tap end, rinse its small mesh filter under running water, and reconnect firmly.
  4. If your hose has an AQUASTOP box on the tap end, check the hose is not kinked right behind it; a tripped AQUASTOP is a sign of a leak, so also look for water under the machine.
  5. Run a short cycle and listen: you should hear water entering within the first few minutes. If the cycle completes without the code, the cause was in the steps above.
  6. If E1 returns immediately on the next fill, stop — the fault is in the valve, the flowmeter/pressure-switch sensing, or the board. Those tests need the body opened and a multimeter, which the manuals reserve for service staff.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. If a check needs a panel removed, a part tested, or a wire touched, it is no longer a safe home check — skip to the technician section.

When to call a technician

  • E1 returns on every cycle even with a known-good, fully open water supply and a clean inlet filter.
  • You hear no water entering at all, or water enters and then stops partway through the fill.
  • There is any sign of leaking under or behind the machine, or the AQUASTOP device has tripped.

Safety first

Always switch off and unplug the dishwasher before moving it or disconnecting hoses, and close the water tap first. Water and electricity are a dangerous mix — never work on a machine that is connected to power with wet hands or a wet floor.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. Inlet-valve electrical tests with a multimeter are service-staff work. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E1 mean on a Midea dishwasher?

E1 means the water inlet time was exceeded: the inlet valve stayed open for about 4 minutes without enough water entering. The manuals agree on this meaning across all Midea dishwasher model families — the US MDT24P4AST manual words it as "Not enough water flow in allotted time."

What does the dishwasher do when E1 fires?

Per the factory technical service manual, the drain pump runs until the flowmeter stops (about 2 minutes), all other components stop, the buzzer sounds for 30 seconds and the display shows "Error 1". That is the machine's designed reaction — switch the power off and fix the water supply before restarting.

How do I clear Midea dishwasher E1?

Fix the cause: open the supply tap fully, straighten the inlet hose, rinse the inlet mesh filter, and check water pressure is at least 20 psi (the US manual specifies 20–80 psi). Then run a short cycle. There is no special reset button sequence in the manuals.

Some websites say Midea E2 is the overflow error. Is that right?

No. No Midea built-in dishwasher manual lists an E2 code — overflow is E4. Sites inventing an E2 or F3 code for Midea dishwashers contradict every manual we checked. Also unlike Hisense, whose E1 means different things on different model families, Midea's E1 has the same meaning on every family.
