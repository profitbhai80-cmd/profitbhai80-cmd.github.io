Midea Dishwasher Error Code E3

Quick answer

Error codeE3 — Midea Dishwasher
What it meansE3 on a Midea dishwasher means heating exceeded the preset time: the heating element has run for 60 minutes but the water temperature detected by the NTC sensor has not reached the desired value. When E3 fires, the drain pump runs until the flowmeter has been motionless for 2 minutes, everything else stops, and the buzzer sounds for 30 seconds. Do not replace the heater first — the manual's inspection order starts with the filter and the washing pump, and a siphoning drain hose can cause E3 with a healthy heater.
Most common causeThe water never reaches temperature within 60 minutes — most often a jammed filter, a failing washing pump, or a drain hose that siphons water away, rather than a dead heater.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. Cleaning the filter, checking the drain hose and reducing excess detergent are safe home checks. Heater, pump, NTC and PCB work is technician work.
Checked againstMidea Technical Service Manual MDWEB1403MB-WA (midea.com, official) — E3: heating exceed preset time (60 minutes, NTC not reaching desired value); drain pump until flowmeter motionless 2 minutes, others stopped, buzzer 30 seconds; Midea Dishwasher Technical Service Manual, 7601S platform — identical E3 definition and reaction; inspection tree: water temperature, filter jam, heater circuit, washing-pump circuit, NTC circuit; Midea standard-version display-code user manuals — E3 tables: not reaching required temperature

Applies to — read this first

E3 settles an old flag in this library: it was once listed alongside E2 as possibly invented. The check splits them — E3 is real and defined in Midea's own official service manual, while E2 appears in no Midea dishwasher document at all (and neither does E5). The official manual's valid-code list runs E1, E3, E4, E6, E7, E8, Ed — there is no E2 or E5 to borrow.

The ranking pages contradict themselves on E3: one page calls it a water-heating problem, a temperature-sensor problem and a heater malfunction in different sections, and tells readers to 'reset the thermostat' — a step no Midea manual contains. The manual's trigger is time plus NTC reading, not a thermostat.

What the machine does when E3 fires

When E3 fires, the machine protects itself in a fixed sequence: the drain pump runs until the flowmeter has been motionless for 2 minutes, every other component stops immediately, the buzzer alarms for 30 seconds, and 'error 3' is shown. That drain-pump run is part of the fault reaction, not a second fault — do not read it as a drain code.

Common causes

  1. A jammed filter restricting circulation, so heated water never reaches the NTC at the expected rate — the first item in the service manual's inspection tree after the temperature check.
  2. Low hydraulic pressure or a washing-pump failure — water is not circulating past the heater properly.
  3. A drain hose that siphons water away during the wash, so the machine keeps losing the water it is trying to heat.
  4. A heater-circuit or NTC-circuit fault — the heater itself, or the sensor that reports the temperature, has failed.
  5. An anti-drying pressure-switch fault, excess detergent foaming, or (small probability) a PCB fault — all named in the service manual's troubleshooting table.
  6. Water that is already too hot when E3 fires — that points the tree at the over-heat proof switch instead of the heater.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the machine off and let it cool. Remove and clean the dishwasher filter, following your user manual — a jammed filter is the first and cheapest cause in the manual's tree.
  2. Check the drain hose: is it looped and routed so it cannot siphon water out of the tub during the wash? A hose lying flat on the floor can drain the machine as fast as it fills.
  3. Cut back detergent to the dose your manual specifies. Excess detergent foaming is a named E3 contributor in the troubleshooting table, and foam insulates the water from the heater.
  4. Run one normal cycle and watch for the point at which E3 returns. If it returns quickly with clean water and a clear filter, stop — the remaining checks (heater circuit, washing-pump circuit, NTC circuit, pressure switch, PCB) all involve the machine's internals and are technician work.
  5. Do not 'reset the thermostat' — no Midea manual contains that step, and the over-heat proof switch is a technician check, not a user reset.

Work through the home checks in order — filter, hose, detergent — and stop as soon as the code clears. If E3 returns with those clear, the fault is in the heater, pump, NTC or board, and guessing which one by replacing parts wastes money: the heater is only one of five tree items.

When to call a technician

  • E3 returns after the filter is clean, the drain hose is correctly routed and detergent is at the manual dose.
  • The water stays cold throughout the cycle — heater or NTC circuit suspect.
  • The machine drains continuously or the flowmeter never settles — pump or siphon fault beyond a hose re-route.
  • Any burning smell, scorching or water near electrics — switch off at the wall and call a technician.

Safety first

E3 involves a mains-powered heater and a water-filled tub. Never open the machine's panels, touch heater or pump electrics, or bypass the over-heat proof switch. Switch off at the wall before removing the filter if the manual requires it, and never run the machine with its panels removed. Heater, pump, NTC and PCB diagnosis belongs to a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E3 mean on a Midea dishwasher?

E3 means heating exceeded the preset time: the heating element ran for 60 minutes but the NTC sensor's water-temperature reading never reached the desired value. The official Midea service manual MDWEB1403MB-WA defines it exactly that way, and the 7601S service manual agrees.

Does E3 mean my heater is dead?

Not necessarily. The manual's inspection tree lists a jammed filter, low hydraulic pressure, a siphoning drain hose, a washing-pump failure and an NTC fault alongside the heater. Replacing the heater first wastes the repair if the real cause is a clogged filter or a siphoning hose.

What does the machine do when E3 fires?

The drain pump runs until the flowmeter has been motionless for 2 minutes, all other components stop immediately, the buzzer alarms for 30 seconds, and error 3 is shown. The drain-pump run is part of the E3 reaction, not a separate drain fault.

Is there a thermostat reset for Midea E3?

No. No Midea manual contains a user thermostat reset for E3. The manual's over-heat proof switch check is a technician step. Pages telling you to reset a thermostat are inventing a step.

Do Midea dishwashers have E2 or E5 codes?

No. Neither E2 nor E5 appears in any Midea dishwasher manual checked, and the official valid-code list jumps from E1 to E3. Pages listing Midea dishwasher E2 or E5 are inventing codes — do not trust their E3 row either.
