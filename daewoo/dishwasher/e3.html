Daewoo Dishwasher Error Code E3

Quick answer

Error codeE3 — Daewoo Dishwasher
What it meansE3 = heating exceeded the preset time ("Longer heating time") — the heating element ran for 60 minutes without the NTC-measured water temperature reaching its target. On EGSP-family models the buzzer sounds 30 seconds and the RAPID program LED flickers fast; on MQ-family models "E3" shows on the digital display. It is NOT a drain fault — that reading on some websites contradicts both Daewoo service manuals. Do not merge E3 with Daewoo washer E3 (overflow detection on DWM30-999PG / MINI Series — washer-only) or with Smeg DW E3 (different thresholds).
Most common causeThe heater coil is broken, the thermostat is active, or a connector is loose — the EGSP manual's own three heater failure points that "will cause the E3 error". A failed NTC can also trigger it; the manuals give a specific NTC-failure behavior (see steps).
Safe to check yourself?Limited: the manuals give no homeowner heater procedure — the heater test values (1800W, 29.265Ω ± 1.463Ω on EGSP; about 27–30Ω on MQ) are multimeter checks, i.e. technician work. What you can do: note the 60-minute/NTC threshold, power off/on, and check the obvious (programme actually calls for heat; machine is not behaving as in the 10/20-minute NTC-jump rule).

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E3 is a dishwasher heating-timeout code on two Daewoo DW platforms — the EGSP/GSP pressure-switch family (RAPID LED-signal models) and the MQ flowmeter family (digital-display models). The meaning is identical on both: the heating element ran 60 minutes without the NTC-measured water temperature reaching its target. The indication differs (RAPID LED vs digital "E3") — match your model family. Do NOT merge E3 with Daewoo washer E3 (DWM30-999PG / MINI Series = overflow detection, measured 3× above overflow — washer-only), with Smeg DW E3 (different thresholds: <1.5°C/20min), or with the DDWMJ1411W "Not reaching required temperature" row (code label unverified — not a gate family).

What the machine does when E3 fires

EGSP family: the buzzer alarms for 30 seconds, the RAPID program LED flickers quickly, and the drain pump runs until the pressure switch resets, then continues draining for 60 seconds. MQ family: the buzzer alarms for 30 seconds, "E3" shows on the digital display, all other components stop immediately, and the drain pump runs until the flowmeter is motionless for 2 minutes.

Common causes

  1. The heater coil is broken, so the heater cannot work — the EGSP manual's failure point 1.
  2. The thermostat is active, so the heater cannot work — failure point 2.
  3. A heater connector is loose, so the heater cannot work — failure point 3.
  4. The NTC has failed — the EGSP caution explains the behaviour: with NTC failure detected after heating starts, the program jumps to the next step after 10 minutes of heating; without NTC failure but temperature not reached, after 20 minutes.
  5. Limescale or low supply voltage reducing heat-up — plausible context; the manuals' own test values are the check, technician work.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Power off and on to clear the display; let the drain run finish (EGSP: until the pressure-switch reset + 60 seconds; MQ: until the flowmeter is motionless for 2 minutes).
  2. Note the NTC-jump rule from the EGSP manual: if the programme jumps forward after ~10 minutes of heating, the manual says an NTC failure was detected; after ~20 minutes with no NTC failure, the water simply did not reach temperature.
  3. Run the manuals' own heater checks via a qualified technician: EGSP — heater resistance 29.265Ω ± 1.463Ω (1800W, 230VAC), thermostats 98°C / 229°C; MQ — about 27–30Ω across the heater terminals with the door closed. A wrong reading means the heater coil or thermostat is broken (the manuals' own conclusion).
  4. If E3 returns, call a qualified technician — heater, thermostat, NTC and connector diagnosis is service work.

The heater test values are multimeter checks — the manuals' language (measure resistance, replace coil/thermostat) is service work, presented here as the technician's checklist, not homeowner DIY. There is no invented homeowner heater fix in any manual.

When to call a technician

  • E3 returns with programmes that clearly call for heat.
  • The programme jumps forward after ~10 minutes of heating (the manual's NTC-failure signature).
  • The heater resistance reads outside the manuals' values (EGSP: 29.265Ω ± 1.463Ω; MQ: about 27–30Ω).

Safety first

E3 involves the heater, mains voltage and water together: never open the machine, never probe terminals with power on, and never replace a heater, thermostat or sensor yourself — that is qualified-technician work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E3 mean on a Daewoo dishwasher?

E3 means heating exceeded the preset time — "Longer heating time". The heating element ran for 60 minutes without the NTC-measured water temperature reaching its target. It is verified verbatim in two Daewoo DW technical service manuals: EGSP-130E/13E/90E/9E (p.49) and DDW-MQ1214S (pp.28–30).

Why did my cycle jump forward after 10 or 20 minutes?

The EGSP manual explains it: if an NTC failure was detected after heating started, the program jumps to the next step after 10 minutes of heating; with no NTC failure but temperature not reached, after 20 minutes. It is the manual's own behaviour rule, not a new fault.

What is the difference between E3 and F8?

Different platforms, different codes. E3 is the EGSP/MQ-platform heating timeout — the manuals give heater test values for it. F8 is the UK Vestel-line "Heater error" — the manuals give no test values for F8 and route it to service. Never import E3's values into F8.

What does the flickering RAPID LED mean?

On EGSP/GSP pressure-switch models: the buzzer alarms 30 seconds and the RAPID program LED flickers fast while the drain pump runs — the E3 indication on that platform. On MQ digital models "E3" shows on the display instead.

Can I test the heater myself?

The manuals' tests (EGSP: 29.265Ω ± 1.463Ω; MQ: about 27–30Ω) are multimeter checks with the door closed — service work. A wrong reading means the heater coil or thermostat is broken, per the manuals themselves.
