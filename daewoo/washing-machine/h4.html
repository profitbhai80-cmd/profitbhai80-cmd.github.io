Daewoo Washing Machine Error Code H4

Quick answer

Error codeH4 — Daewoo Washing Machine
What it meansH4 on a Daewoo DWD/DWC drum washing machine means Wash Overheating: the wash temperature sensor reading is over 95 °C. The alarm sounds for 10 seconds every 10 minutes and the display clears when the power is turned off and on. The DWC-ED1213 causes are the wash heater working with no water supply (check the water level) and a broken wash temperature sensor (change the sensor temp.). This is a front-loader / washer-dryer code only — it does not exist on DWF top-loaders, and it is not a heater-failure code (that is H6).
Most common causeWash overheating — the wash temperature sensor reading has gone over 95 °C, often because the heater ran without enough water or the wash sensor itself is broken.
Safe to check yourself?Only in part. Switching off, letting the machine cool, switching on once, and checking that the water tap is open and the machine actually fills are safe user actions. Replacing the wash sensor or working on the heater is technician work inside a mains-powered machine.
Checked againstDaewoo Washing Machine Error Codes PDF, DD front-loader platform — H4 Overheating of the washing temperature sensor (95 °C), alarm and display-clear behaviour; Daewoo DWC-ED1213 Drum Washing Machine Service Manual (Dec 2005) — ERROR MODE table (H4 Wash Overheating over 95 °C); H4 troubleshooting table; Daewoo DWD-E1211R / DWD-E1211W Drum Washing Machine Service Manual — H4 Wash Overheating (Sensing Temp. over 95 °C); Daewoo Washing Machine Service Manual, DWD platform — H4 Thermistor Wash Overheating (heater without water; thermistor wash broken)

Applies to — read this first

H4 exists only on Daewoo drum (front-loader / washer-dryer) platforms — DWD and DWC models with wash-temperature sensing. What you must NOT do is borrow this meaning for a DWF top-loader: no DWF top-loader manual lists H4 (the DWF-9001Q series Auto Alarm table stops at E6). And do not confuse H4 with its neighbours: H4 is the wash-temperature sensor reading over 95 °C, H6 is a wash-heater anomaly, and H8 is heater overheating without water (temperature rise within 30 seconds).

Two manuals define H4 by the same threshold — sensing temperature over 95 °C — while the DWC-ED1213 troubleshooting table adds the two field causes: the wash heater working with no water supply (check the water level) and a broken wash temperature sensor (change the sensor temp.). The threshold is the definition; the causes explain how a machine gets there.

What the machine does when H4 fires

The alarm sounds for 10 seconds every 10 minutes and the error display is cleared when the power is turned off and on — the DD front-loader document's exact behaviour for H4. The wash is interrupted because the control has registered a wash temperature over 95 °C.

Common causes

  1. The wash temperature sensor reading exceeds 95 °C — the ERROR MODE definition in the DWC-ED1213 and DWD-E1211R/W manuals and the DD front-loader document: 'Wash Overheating (Sensing Temp. is over 95 °C)'.
  2. The wash heater worked without water in the tub — the DWC-ED1213 troubleshooting table's first H4 cause and the DWD service manual's wording ('The heater worked without the water in the tub. Check the water level.').
  3. The wash temperature sensor (thermistor wash) is broken or inferior — the DWC-ED1213 troubleshooting table's second H4 cause and the DWD service manual's remedy ('Change the thermistor wash.').

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the machine off and let it cool before touching anything — H4 means the wash sensor read over 95 °C, so the water and drum may be very hot.
  2. Check that the water tap is fully open and the machine actually fills on the next attempt. The DWC-ED1213 manual's first H4 cause is the wash heater working with no water supply, and its remedy is to check the water level.
  3. Switch the machine on once. The DD front-loader document states the error display is cleared when the power is turned off and on.
  4. If H4 returns, stop running hot washes and call a qualified technician. The manuals' remaining remedy is to change the wash temperature sensor (thermistor wash) and check the heater — internal repairs, not user work.

The 95 °C threshold is the H4 definition, not a guess — it appears in the DD document, the DWC-ED1213 ERROR MODE table, and the DWD-E1211R/W manual. A returning H4 after a power cycle and a confirmed water supply means a sensor or heater fault, not a setting to adjust.

When to call a technician

  • H4 returns after a power cycle with the water supply confirmed — the wash sensor or heater needs testing.
  • The machine fills poorly or not at all while showing H4 — the no-water heater cause needs checking before any sensor is replaced.
  • H4 appears together with H2 or H6 — the wash thermal system needs technician diagnosis as a whole.
  • Any burning smell, smoke, or water near electrical parts — switch off immediately and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

H4 means the wash water may be very hot — do not open the door immediately; let the machine cool first, and beware of scalding water if you must drain it. Never remove covers or touch the heater or sensor wiring. If you notice a burning smell, smoke, or water near electrical parts, switch the machine off and call a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does H4 mean on a Daewoo washing machine?

On Daewoo DWD/DWC drum (front-loader / washer-dryer) machines H4 means Wash Overheating: the wash temperature sensor reading is over 95 °C. The DWC-ED1213 service manual's causes are the wash heater working with no water supply (check the water level) and a broken wash temperature sensor (change the sensor temp.).

How do I clear the H4 code on my Daewoo washer?

The DD front-loader document states the alarm sounds for 10 seconds every 10 minutes and the error display is cleared when the power is turned off and on. Switch the machine off, let it cool, and switch it on once. If H4 returns, the sensor or heater needs a technician — do not keep running hot washes.

Is H4 the same as H6 or H8?

No. H4 is the wash sensor reading over 95 °C. H6 is an abnormal condition of the wash heater (the heater not raising temperature as expected). H8 is wash-heater overheating without water (temperature rising within 30 seconds of heater operation). Three different definitions — do not swap their fixes.

Does H4 apply to Daewoo top-loaders?

No. No DWF top-loader manual lists H4 — the DWF-9001Q series Auto Alarm table stops at E6 and the DWF-7002FL manual lists E1 to E6 only. H4 is a drum front-loader / washer-dryer code only.
