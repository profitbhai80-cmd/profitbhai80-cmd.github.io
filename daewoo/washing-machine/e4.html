Daewoo Washing Machine Error Code E4

Quick answer

Error codeE4 — Daewoo Washing Machine
What it meansE4 means the water sensor is abnormal on Daewoo DWF/DQ top-loaders. Both Daewoo manuals define it identically, and the manual's tactic is a single line: 'Contact our service department.' The competing definitions — overload, door lock, leakage — are either invented or borrowed from the DWD front-loader platform, where E4 genuinely means leakage.
Most common causeThe water-level sensor circuit is reporting abnormal readings; the manual's only prescribed action is to contact the service department.
Safe to check yourself?Partially. The power-cycle check is safe. Anything beyond that — opening the cabinet, touching the sensor or its wiring — is not: the manual says 'Do not dismantle or repair the washer by yourself.'
Checked againstDaewoo Washing Machine User Manual — DWF-9001Q, DWF-1401Q, DWF-1801Q, DWF-2001Q (Daewoo Electronics Sales & Services (M) Sdn Bhd); DAEMA DWF-2028ZD Fully Automatic Washing Machine User Manual (Model DWF-2028ZD)

Applies to — read this first

E4 belongs to Daewoo's DWF/DQ top-loader families — two manufacturer user manuals print the identical Auto Alarm table with E4 = 'The water sensor is abnormal'. Do NOT borrow meanings from other Daewoo platforms: on DWD front-loaders E4 is a leakage error — 'Water leaks from the tub or the hose drain' (drum service manual) — a completely different fault from the top-loader's sensor fault. Three competitor claims about E4 (overload, door lock, leakage) each describe something the manual does not say.

What the machine does when E4 fires

The machine shows E4 with its alarm and will not run a normal cycle — it cannot trust its water-level reading, so it stops rather than risk filling or spinning blind. This is a protection response; the fault is in the sensing circuit, not in anything you loaded.

Common causes

  1. The manual names no user-serviceable cause for E4 — its entire tactic is 'Contact our service department.' A water-sensor fault means the control cannot trust the water-level reading.
  2. Do not treat generic-list guesses (overloading, door lock, leaking hoses) as causes: none appear in the manual's E4 row. Overload on this platform is an E1 (load imbalance) matter; the door/lid is E2; leakage is the DWD front-loader E4 — each lives on its own code and platform. Acting on the wrong one means fixing the wrong fault.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the machine off with the power button, wait at least 3 minutes — the manual says 'Do not turn on again until 3 minutes after it is turned off' — then switch it back on and try a cycle. This rules out a transient sensor glitch.
  2. If E4 returns on the next cycle, stop: the manual's tactic is 'Contact our service department.' Note the exact code, the model number, and when in the cycle it appears, and give those to the service agent.
  3. Do not reduce the load (that is E1's fix, not E4's), do not inspect the door lock (E4 is not a lock code on this platform), and do not hunt for leaks as the primary check (leakage is the DWD front-loader E4) — and do not remove any panel; the manual forbids user disassembly.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. E4 has exactly one manual-prescribed outcome — the service department. The most common way searchers go wrong on E4 is applying the right fix to the wrong platform's meaning; the series table above exists to prevent exactly that. If a check needs a panel removed or wiring tested, it is no longer a safe home check — skip to the technician section.

When to call a technician

  • E4 returns immediately after the 3-minute power-cycle — the sensor fault is persistent, not transient.
  • E4 alternating with E3 (the manual lists both identically as water-sensor faults).
  • Any burning smell, smoke, or water near electrical parts with E4 displayed — switch off, unplug, and call for service without further checks.

Safety first

Never remove the top panel or any cover to reach the water sensor — it involves mains-voltage wiring and the manual explicitly forbids user disassembly. Unplug the machine before any external check. If water is leaking from the machine, switch it off, close the tap, and call the service department rather than running more cycles.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E4 mean on a Daewoo top-load washing machine?

E4 means the water sensor is abnormal. Both Daewoo DWF manuals define it identically — 'The water sensor is abnormal' — and the tactic is 'Contact our service department.' This applies to DWF/DQ top-loader families.

Some sites say Daewoo E4 means the washer is overloaded. Is that right?

No. Both Daewoo manuals define top-loader E4 as a water-sensor fault. Overload-type faults on this platform show as E1 (load imbalance) — the 'E4 = overload' claim comes from a generic list that cites no Daewoo manual.

Another site says E4 is a door-lock problem, and another says leakage. Which is it?

Neither — on the DWF top-loader. The door-lock claim is invented (lid matters are E2 on this platform), and the leakage definition belongs to Daewoo's DWD front-loader platform, whose service manual lists 'E4 LEAKAGE ERROR'. Same code, two platforms, two meanings — the manual for your machine decides.

Why do E3 and E4 say the same thing in the manual?

Because that is what Daewoo printed: both rows read 'The water sensor is abnormal' with the tactic 'Contact our service department.' We report the manual verbatim rather than inventing a distinction. Treat either code the same way.

Can I fix the water sensor myself?

No. The sensor and its wiring are internal parts, and the manual states 'Do not dismantle or repair the washer by yourself.' The only safe home check is a power-cycle to rule out a transient glitch; a returning E4 needs a qualified technician.
