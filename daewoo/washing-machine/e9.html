Daewoo Washing Machine Error Code E9

Quick answer

Error codeE9 — Daewoo Washing Machine
What it meansE9 means a water-level sensing fault: the water level sensor (pressure switch) signal is out of range. The Daewoo service document defines it precisely — the sensor frequency exceeds 30 kHz or drops below 15 kHz, or jumps by more than 1.44 kHz within 4 seconds after filling — and a second Daewoo user manual words it as "Water is not adjustable." Both Daewoo manuals agree: reset only, then contact the service center.
Most common causeA failed water-level sensor (pressure switch), its air hose disconnected from its position, or a wiring fault between the sensor and the control board.
Safe to check yourself?Only the basic reset. The Daewoo manuals give no user-serviceable procedure for E9 and instruct you to contact the service center. Do not open the cabinet.
Checked againstDaewoo Washing Machine Error Codes service document (Daewoo service material); Daewoo DWD-M301WP Wall-Mounted Front Load Washer User Manual (2025); Daewoo DWC-UD1222 Instruction Manual (supporting)

Applies to — read this first

E9 is series-specific on Daewoo: the service-document family (front-loaders using the IE/OE/UE/LE/DE/SE/QE code scheme) defines the exact frequency triggers below, while the DWD-M301WP wall-mounted mini series carries E9 with the user-facing wording "Water is not adjustable." Daewoo top-loader families (e.g. DWF series) use a different scheme entirely — water-level faults there are E5/E4, not E9. The DWC-UD1222 instruction manual also names E9 ("Error codes E8 and E9 require contacting the service center") without defining it — a third Daewoo source confirming E9 is a service-center code, not a user-fix code.

What the machine does when E9 fires

When the sensor signal goes out of range, the machine stops the cycle and displays E9. The water level cannot be regulated — that is what "Water is not adjustable" means — so the machine will not safely continue washing or spinning.

Common causes

  1. The water level sensor (pressure switch) has failed internally — the service document's frequency thresholds (above 30 kHz or below 15 kHz) describe the sensor's signal going out of range.
  2. The sensor's air hose is disconnected from its position — named explicitly in the service document as an E9 trigger.
  3. A wiring fault between the sensor and the control board (loose or broken cable).
  4. A sudden frequency jump of more than 1.44 kHz within 4 seconds after filling — the sensor signal behaving erratically mid-fill.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the machine off, wait a minute, and switch it back on. Run a short cycle and watch whether E9 returns immediately or only after the fill.
  2. Note when E9 appears: right after filling points to the sensor-signal fault the manual describes — the detail helps the technician.
  3. Stop here: both Daewoo manuals route E9 to the service center — the DWD-M301WP manual's only remedy is "Contact your local service center," and the DWC-UD1222 manual says E8/E9 "require contacting the service center." The manuals give no user procedure for opening the machine, and you should not.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. E9 is one of the codes Daewoo explicitly marks as service-center-only. Do not remove the top or rear panel, do not touch internal wiring, and do not replace the pressure switch yourself — unlike some competitor pages that recommend disassembly, the manufacturer's own manuals forbid it for this code.

When to call a technician

  • E9 appears on every cycle — the sensor, its hose, or its wiring needs professional diagnosis.
  • The machine fills to the wrong level or overfills before E9 appears — the level sensing is unreliable.
  • Any error alongside E9 (E8, IE) — combined sensor/drive faults need a technician's diagnosis.
  • Both Daewoo manuals say the same thing: contact the local service center. There is no user-fix step beyond the reset.

Safety first

Never remove the top or rear panel of a washing machine that is connected to power — internal components carry mains voltage even when the machine is "off" at the panel. The water-level sensor sits inside the cabinet; its hose, wiring, and the control board are not user-serviceable on this code. If the machine overfills, switch it off at the wall and close the water taps before calling the service center.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E9 mean on a Daewoo washing machine?

E9 is a water-level sensing fault: the water level sensor (pressure switch) signal is out of range — above 30 kHz or below 15 kHz, or jumping more than 1.44 kHz within 4 seconds after filling, per the Daewoo service document. The DWD-M301WP user manual words it as "Water is not adjustable."

Can I fix Daewoo E9 myself?

The manuals say no: the DWD-M301WP manual's only remedy is "Contact your local service center," and the DWC-UD1222 manual says E8/E9 "require contacting the service center." Some competitor pages tell you to disassemble the machine and clean terminals — the manufacturer's own manuals contradict that advice for this code.

Is Daewoo E9 a clogged pressure tube?

Not per the manufacturer. One ranking page blames a clogged tube, but the Daewoo service document names a DISCONNECTED sensor hose as the trigger, alongside the frequency thresholds. A clogged-tube fix and a disconnected-hose fix are different repairs — which is why the manual's wording matters.

Is Daewoo E9 the same on top-load models?

No — E9 is series-specific. Daewoo top-loader families like the DWF series use a different code scheme (water-level faults there show as E5/E4, not E9). Never read a code across Daewoo platforms.
