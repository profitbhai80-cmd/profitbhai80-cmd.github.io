Daewoo Refrigerator Error Code E9

Quick answer

Error codeE9 — Daewoo Refrigerator
What it meansE9 = water supply error — the icemaker/dispenser water-supply fault on Daewoo FRS-platform side-by-side refrigerators. Water is failing to reach the ice maker (interrupted or failed water input). It is NOT a temperature-sensor or compressor fault, despite what some AI-generated pages claim. Do not confuse this with Daewoo washer E9 (pressure-switch fault), Daewoo boiler E9 (water leakage), or "d1"/"D1" (defrost-sensor codes).
Most common causeThe ice maker's water input is interrupted — the supply line is not connected, the tap/valve is shut, air is trapped in the pipes, or a tube, connector or pipe is leaking.
Safe to check yourself?Limited: the manuals' own water-supply installation checks (run the WATER button 2–3 minutes to purge air; re-check tubes, connectors and pipes for leaks) are homeowner checks. There is no E9-specific diagnostic entry in either manual, so the page presents the manuals' water-supply checks — not an invented E9 fix flow.

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because E9 is an icemaker-platform code, not a general fridge code. It is documented on the Daewoo FRS icemaker-platform side-by-side refrigerators (FRS-2011/2031 and FRS-T20DA*), where E9 sits in the manual's icemaker E-series block (E1/EF/Et/E9/EA/Eu) — the water-input codes. The FRN/FRS-U20-era service manuals carry no error-code chapter for this platform block. Do NOT conflate E9 with "d1"/"D1" (defrost-sensor codes) or with "E1" (published here as EI). Do NOT import wrong-appliance E9s: the Daewoo washer E9 (pressure switch), the Daewoo gas-boiler E9 (water leakage), and the DWD-VF80M1 washer E-9 (motor triac / out-of-voltage) are different codes on different appliances.

What the machine does when E9 fires

The display shows E9 — the control has flagged the ice maker's water supply. The manual's own reset rule applies: "All the error codes are reset if they turn to be normal."

Common causes

  1. The water supply line is not connected or the supply valve is shut — no water reaching the ice maker.
  2. Air (bubbles) is trapped in the supply pipes after installation — the manuals' own named purge case.
  3. A tube, connector or pipe in the water supply system is leaking — the manuals' own re-check item.
  4. The ice maker assembly itself is not cycling water in — check with the ice-maker test switch.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Run the manuals' own air-purge check: "Plug the refrigerator, press the [WATER] button on the control panel for 2~3 minutes to remove any air (bubble) in the pipes and drain out the initial water."
  2. "Check the water leak again through the water supply system (tubes, connectors and pipes)."
  3. Push the ice maker ASS'Y TEST S/W (over 1 second) to cycle the ice maker and confirm water input is normal.
  4. The manual's reset rule: "All the error codes are reset if they turn to be normal" — E9 clears when the supply is genuinely restored.

Presenting these honestly: neither manual gives an E9-specific diagnostic or "control way" entry, so there is no invented E9 fix flow here. The steps above are the manuals' own water-supply installation checks and reset rule — the home scope is the supply line and the purge, everything deeper is service work.

When to call a technician

  • E9 persists with the water supply confirmed connected, purged and leak-free.
  • A tube, connector or pipe is leaking and you cannot see or reach the leak.
  • Other icemaker E-series codes (EI, EF, Eg, Eu, EA, Et) appear alongside E9 — the ice maker assembly needs service.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

E9 involves the water supply and mains-powered ice-maker electronics together: switch the fridge off before touching anything wet, never force connectors or valves, and leave panel-off ice-maker work to a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E9 mean on a Daewoo refrigerator?

E9 means a water supply error — the ice maker / dispenser water input is failing on Daewoo FRS-platform side-by-side refrigerators. It is verified verbatim in two Daewoo service manuals: S/M FRS2011000 for the FRS-2011/2031 and the FRS-T20DA* service manual (§5).

Is E9 the same as E1 or d1?

No. E1 (published here as EI) is the I-sensor disconnection fault and d1/D1 are defrost-sensor codes — E9 is the water-supply fault in the icemaker E-series block. Different rows, different causes.

How does E9 clear?

The manual's own rule: "All the error codes are reset if they turn to be normal." Fix the supply — connection, air purge, leak check — and E9 clears on its own.

Why do some sites say E9 is a temperature-sensor fault?

An AI-generated JustAnswer page guesses temperature-sensor or compressor-communication faults — it is not Daewoo-specific and contradicts both Daewoo manuals, which define E9 as a water supply error.

Does the Daewoo washer E9 mean the same thing?

No. Daewoo washer E9 is a water-level/pressure-switch fault, Daewoo boiler E9 is a water-leakage fault, and DWD-VF80M1 washer E-9 is a motor-triage/voltage code — all different appliances. For a refrigerator, E9 is the water supply error.
