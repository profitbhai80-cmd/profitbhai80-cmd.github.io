Daewoo Refrigerator Error Code EF

Quick answer

Error codeEF — Daewoo Refrigerator
What it meansEF on a Daewoo refrigerator means the flow sensor is in error: no pulse is received from the flow sensor for some time while water should be flowing. On FRS-2011/2031 models the numeric trigger is a pulse input below 10 during 1 second with the supply valve on, control falls back to timed supply of about 5.5 seconds, and termination is exchanging the flow sensor. The manual check point is the water supply line.
Most common causeThe flow sensor gives no pulses because the water supply is interrupted, the supply line is faulty, or the flow sensor itself has failed.
Safe to check yourself?Partly: check the external water supply is on and the supply line is not kinked or shut off. Anything beyond that — sensor testing or exchange — is technician work; do not open panels or the valve assembly.
Checked againstDaewoo FRSU21DFV User Manual section 5 (EF: flow-sensor error, no pulse during some time, check water supply line); Daewoo FRS-20 User Manual section 5 (EF table: flow sensor defective, same detailed item); Daewoo FRS-2011/2031 Service Manual section 6 (EF: pulse below 10 in 1 second with valve on, timed supply about 5.5 seconds, exchange flow sensor); Daewoo IWD/KAN58 repair manual section 7.1 (EF: flow sensor, solenoid-valve wording)

Applies to — read this first

A flow sensor counts pulses as water moves past it, and EF is what happens when the count stops. The fridge expects pulses while the supply valve is on; when it gets none for some time, it declares a flow-sensor error and falls back to a timed supply. That pulse-counting mechanism is the whole diagnosis, and it is what separates EF from a simple no-water complaint.

ManualExact wording for EF
Daewoo FRSU21DFV User Manual (section 5, item 10)EF ERROR. Cause: When flow-sensor error (there is no pulse during some time). Check point: Water supply line.
Daewoo FRS-20 User Manual (section 5, item 10)EF in the error table as Flow sensor: defective, with the identical detailed item and the same water-supply-line check point.
Daewoo FRS-2011/2031 Service Manual (section 6, item 10)EF Error. Cause: When flow-sensor is error (there is no pulse during some time). When the water supply valve is on, pulse input is below 10 during 1 second. Control: Time control (by vector time recorded in EEPROM; generally water is supplied about 5.5 seconds). Termination: Exchange flow sensor.
Daewoo IWD / KAN58 repair manual (section 7.1) — family wordingEF listed as Flow sensor, with the repair note to test the inlet solenoid valve and replace the complete solenoid valve on that family. The fault component is the same flow-sensing path; the FRS manuals exchange the flow sensor, the IWD wording replaces the valve assembly. Flagged here as a wording difference, not hidden.
Daewoo refrigerator Eg — do not mix themEg is the ice-maker I-sensor temperature not rising 5 minutes after water supply. EF is the flow sensor giving no pulses at all. Both sit on the water-supply path, but they test different parts.

EF is absent from the ranking all-codes lists entirely, and the manuals themselves state the pulse criterion only in service language. The plain-English pulse mechanism, the below-10-in-1-second trigger, and the 5.5-second timed fallback are what this page adds.

What the machine does when EF fires

The display shows EF and the water-supply logic changes: with no flow pulses arriving, the control cannot confirm water is flowing, so on FRS-2011/2031 models it falls back to timed supply of about 5.5 seconds from the recorded vector time. Ice and water output become unreliable until the sensor is exchanged.

Common causes

  1. The external water supply is off, or the supply line is kinked, blocked or shut off, so no water flows past the sensor.
  2. The flow sensor itself has failed and gives no pulses even when water flows.
  3. The supply valve or its sensing path is faulty, so pulses never reach the control.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the code is EF and that the fault is on the water-supply path: check the external water supply is on and the supply line is not kinked, crushed or shut off.
  2. Power-cycle once and see whether EF returns. A transient supply interruption can clear this way.
  3. If EF returns with the supply on and the line clear, stop. Flow-sensor testing and exchange, and any valve work, are technician work; do not open panels or the valve assembly.

An EF that clears after restoring the supply was a supply interruption, not a sensor failure. An EF that stays on with water available is a flow-sensor fault.

When to call a technician

  • EF returns after a power-cycle with the water supply on and the line clear.
  • Ice or water output stays unreliable, or the ice maker stops filling.
  • Any leak, burning smell, or visible damage near the supply valve — switch off the water and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

EF is a water-supply sensing fault, not a home repair: never dismantle the valve assembly or the sensor housing, and never bypass a sensor. Switch the unit off and shut the water supply before any handling, and leave sensor and valve work to a qualified technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does EF mean on a Daewoo refrigerator?

EF means the flow sensor is in error: no pulse is received from the flow sensor for some time while water should be flowing. The manual check point is the water supply line, and termination is exchanging the flow sensor.

What is a flow sensor?

It is a sensor that counts pulses as water flows past it, so the control can confirm water actually arrived. No pulses while the valve is on means the control cannot confirm flow, which is the EF trigger.

What is the numeric EF trigger?

On FRS-2011/2031 models, a pulse input below 10 during 1 second with the water supply valve on. Control then falls back to timed supply of about 5.5 seconds.

Is EF the same as Eg?

No. EF is the flow sensor giving no pulses. Eg is the ice-maker I-sensor temperature not rising 5 minutes after water supply. Both sit on the water path, but they test different parts.

Can I fix EF myself?

Only the external checks: supply on, line not kinked or shut off, one power-cycle. Sensor exchange and valve work are technician work.
