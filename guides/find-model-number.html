How to Find Your Appliance Model Number

Almost every error-code search goes wrong at the same place: the model number. Brands reuse the same code with different meanings across model families, so “Hisense E1” is not one question — it is several. The model number (and often the serial or product number next to it) is what pins a code to the right manual.

Where to look, appliance by appliance

Washing machines

Open the door: the label is usually on the rim of the door opening, on the door itself, or behind the pump-filter flap at the front. On top-loaders, check under the lid rim and at the back of the control panel.

Dishwashers

Open the door and look along its side edge or the top edge of the door — the label is commonly printed on the door's side trim. On some models it is on the side of the tub, visible with the door open.

Refrigerators & freezers

Look inside, on the left or right wall of the fridge compartment, often near the crisper drawers; on some models it is on the ceiling of the compartment or behind a drawer. Freezers often carry it on an inner wall or the inner side of the lid/door.

Ovens, ranges & microwaves

For ovens, open the door and check the frame around the cavity, or pull out the storage drawer and look at the frame behind it. Microwaves usually carry the label inside the door frame or on the back panel.

Air conditioners

Window and portable units have a label on the side or back of the casing. Split systems have one label on the indoor unit (often under the front panel edge) and another on the outdoor unit — for error codes, the indoor unit's model is usually the one that matters.

Model vs serial — which do you need?

The model number identifies the design and is what manuals and error-code lists are organised by. The serial number identifies your individual unit and matters for warranty and recalls. For looking up a code, start with the model; keep the serial handy for a technician.

Take a photo

Labels fade and machines get replaced. One clear photo of the rating label saves every future search — and it is exactly what a technician will ask for when you call.
