When an Appliance Error Requires Professional Service

Plenty of error codes clear with a cleaned filter, a straightened hose or a properly closed door. But some codes and symptoms mark the boundary of safe home troubleshooting. Crossing it risks injury, a bigger repair bill, or a voided warranty. Call a qualified technician when any of the following apply.

Always call — no exceptions

  • Gas appliances (gas ovens, ranges, water heaters, dryers): any ignition, burner or gas-supply fault. Never open gas lines or bypass flame safety devices.
  • Refrigerant codes on air conditioners or fridges: the system is sealed and regulated. Handling refrigerant needs certification and recovery equipment.
  • Burning smell, smoke, scorch marks, or a tripping breaker: switch off at the wall and keep it off until it has been inspected.
  • Water leaking near electrics, or water appearing under a machine repeatedly after the obvious external causes are fixed.
  • Microwave faults of any kind beyond the door and keypad: internal capacitors hold a dangerous charge even when the unit is unplugged. Never open a microwave cabinet.

Call when the code names an internal part

Codes for motors, Hall sensors, control boards, temperature sensors, inlet valves and heating elements describe parts inside the machine that need testing with a meter and fitting with the power isolated. If a guide for your code says “technician”, believe it.

Call when the safe checks are done and the code returns

If you have honestly worked the external checks (supply, hoses, filters, doors) and the same code comes straight back, the fault is internal by elimination. More restarts will not change that.

Make the visit count

Give the technician the brand, the full model number from the rating label, the exact code, and when in the cycle it appears. That information, ready in advance, is the difference between one visit and two.

A note on cost decisions

For an older machine, ask for the diagnosis and price before authorising the repair, and weigh it against the machine's age and replacement cost. A good technician will tell you honestly when a repair no longer makes economic sense.
