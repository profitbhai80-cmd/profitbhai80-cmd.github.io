Gree Air Conditioner Error Code E1

Quick answer

Error codeE1 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansE1 means compressor high pressure protection: the refrigerant pressure on the high side of the system has risen past the safe limit and the unit has shut itself down to protect the compressor. The Gree Vireo manual names the three causes: overcharged refrigerant, a blocked or dirty outdoor coil, and extreme outdoor ambient conditions.
Most common causeA blocked or dirty outdoor coil, or an overcharged refrigerant system — system pressure on the high side has risen past the safe limit.
Safe to check yourself?Partially. You can safely restart the unit, clear debris and blockages from around the outdoor unit, and clean or replace the indoor filters. Anything involving the refrigerant circuit or the high-pressure switch is technician work.

Applies to — read this first

Good news for diagnosis: within the Gree residential mini-split families we checked, E1 is consistent — it is always compressor high pressure protection. What you must NOT do is borrow meanings from lookalike sources: two top-ranking guides define Gree E1 wrongly (one says communication error, another says indoor sensor error — both contradict the Gree manuals), Gree E6 is the communication fault, and Midea mini-split E1 is a communication fault too — a different brand, a different meaning. Only the Gree mini-split indoor display's E1 is covered here.

E1 shows on the indoor unit's temperature display. The Vireo manual's outdoor-unit LED flash list covers other codes (E2/E4/E5/E6/E8/EE/F0) — no outdoor flash pattern is listed for E1, so diagnose from the indoor display. On Gree commercial/VRF lines the code map can shift; check your model's own manual.

What the machine does when E1 fires

The unit stops normal cooling and heating and displays E1 — a protection shutdown, not a second fault. Per the Gree service-manual behavior for this code, in Cooling or Dry mode all operations cease except the indoor fan; in Heat mode all operations cease. The compressor will not restart until the high-pressure condition is resolved.

Common causes

  1. Overcharged with refrigerant — the Vireo manual lists this cause first.
  2. A blocked or dirty outdoor (condenser) coil restricting heat rejection.
  3. Extreme outdoor ambient conditions driving system pressure up.
  4. A faulty high-pressure switch.
  5. Restricted refrigerant flow.
  6. Dirty indoor filters and coils reducing airflow — the most common preventable trigger.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Turn the unit off and restart it — the Vireo GEN3 owner's manual's own guidance for the E1 group: 'Try restarting the unit. If the problem persists, contact a qualified HVAC technician.' If E1 clears and stays cleared, it was a transient pressure spike.
  2. With the power off, inspect the outdoor unit: clear leaves, debris, and anything blocking airflow around the condenser coil, and make sure nothing is stacked against it. Check and clean the indoor filters — dirty filters and coils are the most common preventable trigger for high-pressure trips.
  3. If E1 returns after the restart and the airflow checks, stop: the remaining causes are an overcharged system, a faulty high-pressure switch, or a refrigerant-flow restriction. All three need gauges, recovery equipment, and certification — call a qualified HVAC technician.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. If a check needs an electrical cover removed, the refrigerant circuit opened, or a pressure switch tested, it is no longer a safe home check — skip to the technician section.

When to call a technician

  • E1 returns immediately after the restart and airflow checks — the charge or the high-pressure switch needs testing.
  • The outdoor fan is not running or runs slowly while E1 is active — a failing fan motor or capacitor drives head pressure up.
  • The unit was recently serviced or recharged and E1 started after — an overcharge is the prime suspect.
  • Ice on the pipework, hissing sounds, or oil stains — signs of a refrigerant leak feeding the pressure fault.
  • Any burning smell or visibly damaged wiring at either unit.

Safety first

Mini-split indoor and outdoor units contain mains-voltage wiring and capacitors that hold charge after switch-off — never remove electrical covers yourself. The refrigerant circuit must only be opened by certified technicians: it is illegal to vent refrigerant in most jurisdictions, and the R32 used in newer Gree units is a mildly flammable (A2L) refrigerant. The Gree manual's own wording stands: 'The air conditioner is not expected to be serviced by users. Incorrect repair may cause electric shock or fire, so please contact an authorized service center for professional service.'

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E1 mean on a Gree mini split?

E1 means compressor high pressure protection: the refrigerant pressure on the high side of the system has risen past the safe limit, and the unit has shut itself down to protect the compressor. Every Gree mini-split manual we checked defines E1 this way.

How do I reset Gree mini split E1?

Turn the unit off and restart it — that is the Gree owner's manual's own guidance for the E1 group ('Try restarting the unit. If the problem persists, contact a qualified HVAC technician'). Then check the outdoor unit for blocked airflow and clean dirty filters. If E1 comes back, the refrigerant charge or the high-pressure switch needs a technician.

Some sites say Gree E1 is a communication error. Is that right?

No. The Gree manuals define E1 strictly as compressor high pressure protection. Communication faults live at E6, not E1. Pages claiming E1 = communication are contradicting the manufacturer's own manuals.

Is Gree E1 the same as Midea mini-split E1?

No — and this is the easiest mix-up to make. Midea mini-split E1 is a communication fault between the indoor and outdoor units; Gree mini-split E1 is compressor high pressure protection. Never borrow a meaning across brands.
