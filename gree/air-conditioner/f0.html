Gree Air Conditioner Error Code F0

Quick answer

Error codeF0 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansF0 means two different faults depending on platform: on wall-split, mini-split and window units it is refrigerant leakage protection (the complete unit stops); on U-Match units it is an indoor ambient temperature sensor error. F0 (zero) is not Fo (letter o) — Fo is a maintenance status, 'gathering refrigerant'.
Most common causeWall-split: refrigerant leakage, an abnormal indoor evaporator temperature sensor, or the unit plugged up somewhere (Soyal §9.1 row 3). U-Match: open or short circuit of the indoor ambient sensor for 5 seconds continuously (R410A §1.1).
Safe to check yourself?Only the checks on this page: power-cycle once. Refrigerant leak detection, charge checks and sensor testing are all technician work. Never top up refrigerant yourself.

Applies to — read this first

F0 means TWO different faults depending on platform, and the two must never be merged. On wall-split, mini-split and window units F0 is refrigerant leakage protection: the complete unit stops. On U-Match units F0 is an indoor ambient temperature sensor error: the indoor temperature is set compulsively at 24°C and the system takes no other measure. Check the platform first. The U-Match numbering is shifted by one relative to wall-split: U-Match F1 is the evaporator sensor, F2 the condenser sensor, F3 the outdoor ambient sensor, F4 the discharge sensor.

F0 is also not Fo. Fo (lowercase o) is 'Gathering refrigerant' / 'Refrigerant recovery mode' — a maintenance STATUS, not a fault (Soyal §9.1 row 11; Mini flash once). Do not confuse F0 (zero) with Fo (letter o). The ranking pages each carry only half the story: the official Gree Comfort guide covers wall-split only (no U-Match split, no F0/Fo distinction) and has sloppy bits of its own — its download link is mislabelled 'Download E5 Troubleshooting Guide (PDF)' and Step 4/FAQ say 'FO persists' (letter O for zero). Arlington and sidzcoolcare present the U-Match ambient-sensor meaning as the universal F0 with no scope — wrong on wall-split numbering — and airconditioningmanuals says F0 means 'the discharge sensor is broken', which is F4 on wall-split numbering, not F0. Do not quote the official guide's procedure (gauges + PT chart, 10-minute hard reset, 100 PSI post-reset threshold, 500 PSI nitrogen / 500-micron evacuation) as manual text — it is an official guide, not a service manual.

What the machine does when F0 fires

Wall-split: the dual-8 shows F0 and the complete unit stops operation (Soyal §9.1 row 3). On Vireo mini-splits the outdoor red light shows 9 flashes and 1 second off. U-Match: an open or short circuit of the indoor ambient thermal bulb detected for continuous 5 seconds triggers F0; the indoor temperature is set compulsively at 24°C and the system takes no other measure; after the fault is removed the system restores operation by itself. Under air-supply mode only the fault is displayed and the indoor fan runs normally (U-Match R410A §1.1).

Common causes

  1. Wall-split: refrigerant leakage of unit (Soyal §9.1 row 3).
  2. Wall-split: indoor evaporator temperature sensor is abnormal (Soyal §9.1 row 3).
  3. Wall-split: unit is plugged up somewhere (Soyal §9.1 row 3).
  4. U-Match: open or short circuit of the indoor ambient thermal bulb detected for continuous 5 seconds (U-Match R410A §1.1).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the platform first: wall-split, mini-split or window (refrigerant leakage protection) versus U-Match (indoor ambient sensor error). The fix is different for each.
  2. Power-cycle once: switch the unit off and re-energize. On U-Match units the system restores itself once the sensor fault clears.
  3. Wall-split: if F0 returns, call a technician — refrigerant leak detection, charge check, evaporator-sensor testing and blockage checks are all technician work. Do not top up refrigerant yourself.
  4. U-Match: if F0 returns, a technician checks the indoor ambient sensor (thermal bulb) and its wiring.
  5. On a Vireo mini-split, the outdoor red light showing 9 flashes and 1 second off confirms the F0 refrigerant-leakage fault — report that pattern to the technician.

Branch A has causes but no manual fix procedure; Branch B has the manual's own behaviour. The official Gree Comfort F0 guide's procedure (gauges + PT chart, 10-minute hard reset, 100 PSI threshold, 500 PSI nitrogen / 500-micron evacuation) is an official guide, not a service manual — this page does not quote it as manual text.

When to call a technician

  • F0 returns after one power-cycle on a wall-split unit — most F0s are charge-related (leak or undercharge) and need pressure testing by a technician.
  • F0 returns on a U-Match unit after the sensor fault should have cleared.
  • Any refrigerant smell, hissing, or oil stains near the unit — switch off and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

F0 on wall-split units usually means a refrigerant leak: never attempt a DIY refrigerant top-up, never open the refrigerant circuit, and never run the unit with a suspected leak. Refrigerant work belongs to a qualified technician. On U-Match units the fault is a sensor, but sensor and wiring checks still belong to a technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F0 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

It depends on the platform: on wall-split, mini-split and window units F0 is refrigerant leakage protection (the unit stops); on U-Match units F0 is an indoor ambient temperature sensor error. Check your model family first.

Is F0 the same as Fo?

No. F0 (zero) is a fault. Fo (lowercase o) is 'Gathering refrigerant' / 'Refrigerant recovery mode' — a maintenance status, not a fault.

Can I reset F0 myself?

Power-cycle once. On wall-split units, if F0 returns the cause is usually a refrigerant leak, a faulty indoor coil/evaporator sensor, or a blockage — all technician work. On U-Match units the system restores itself once the sensor fault clears.

Why do some sites say F0 is an ambient temperature sensor fault?

That is the U-Match meaning. Sites that present it as the universal F0 are reading the U-Match table without scope. On wall-split units F0 is refrigerant leakage protection.

What does the official Gree F0 guide say?

Gree Comfort's guide covers the wall-split meaning (refrigerant protection lockout, charge-related in most cases). This page adds the U-Match branch and the F0/Fo distinction the guide does not carry.
