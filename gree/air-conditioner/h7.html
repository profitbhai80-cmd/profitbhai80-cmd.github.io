Gree Air Conditioner Error Code H7

Quick answer

Error codeH7 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansH7 = desynchronizing of compressor (compressor loss of step / loss of synchronism). The inverter drive has lost synchronism with the compressor motor. Blink pattern: OFF 3S and blink 7 times. H7 is a REAL fault - it is F7 (oil return, a running state) that does not exist as a fault, not H7.
Most common causeThe inverter drive has lost synchronism with the compressor - checked in the manuals by the AP1-compressor wiring and connection sequence, the L-N input voltage, the compressor coil resistance and insulation, the workload and radiating, and the refrigerant charge (Change 18/24 Service Manual, sections 9.4(3) and 9.4(6)).
Safe to check yourself?Partly - wait first: the manual checks whether the compressor has been stopped for more than 3 minutes, because restarting with unbalanced system pressure can cause a synchronism fault. Power the unit off, wait, and restart once. If H7 returns, the wiring, voltage, coil, radiating, and refrigerant checks are technician work.

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because Gree H7 is a real fault with one meaning across five manuals - and it is constantly confused with F7, which is not a fault at all. H7 = desynchronizing of compressor, also worded 'compressor loss step protection' and 'loss of synchronism': the inverter drive has lost synchronism with the compressor motor. 'Desynchronizing', 'loss of step', and 'loss of synchronism' are three wordings of the same fault. H7 is verified in the Change 18/24, evo, Livo Gen4, Soyal, and Mini After-sale manuals, and the U-Match duct/cassette manual agrees ('Drive desynchronizing protection'). GMV VRF H7 is a different fault - malfunction of the drive temperature sensor of the fan - do not merge it. Versati heat-pump H7 (compressor stalling / desynchronizing, section 4.1.6) is the same concept on a different appliance, corroboration only.

GMV VRF - DIFFERENT fault, do not mergeOn GMV VRF units H7 means 'Malfunction of drive temperature sensor of fan' - a different fault. Correct for VRF; wrong to apply to a wall-split.
H7 vs F7 - must not be confusedF7 is NOT a fault - it is the 'Oil returning' running state (Free Match p.122, Multi21, Livo Gen3). H7 IS a fault (desynchronizing). The two codes are one letter apart and competitors constantly confuse them: arlington invents an F7 'tube-exit sensor' while giving H7 the H5 fixes; appongtho lists F7 as 'compressor oil-carry protection' as if it were a fault.
H7 vs H5/P5 - must not be confusedH5 = IPM protection, P5 = over-phase-current protection - the manuals group all three in one diagnosis section (section 9.4(3)), but they are three different codes with different blink counts (H5 blink 5, H7 blink 7, P5 blink 15).

What the machine does when H7 fires

During cooling and drying operation the compressor stops while the indoor fan keeps running; during heating operation the complete unit stops. The display pattern is OFF 3 seconds, then blink 7 times.

Common causes

  1. Compressor stopped for less than 3 minutes before restart - the manual's first check: a short stop time with unbalanced system pressure can cause a synchronism fault on restart.
  2. AP1-compressor wiring fault - the control board AP1 and compressor COMP are poorly connected, loosened, or in the wrong connection sequence.
  3. Input voltage out of the normal range - tested as the voltage between L-N of the wiring board XT.
  4. Compressor coil fault - abnormal coil resistance, or the compressor coil not insulating well to the copper pipe.
  5. Heavy workload, poor radiating, or wrong refrigerant charge - the manual's remaining detection points before any board or compressor replacement.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Wait first: power the unit off and wait, because the manual checks whether the compressor has been stopped for more than 3 minutes - restarting with unbalanced system pressure can cause a synchronism fault. Restart once.
  2. If H7 returns, a technician must check in the manual's order: the AP1-compressor wiring and connection sequence, the L-N input voltage, the compressor coil resistance and insulation to the copper pipe, the workload, the radiating, and the refrigerant charge.
  3. The manual replaces the control board AP1 and then the compressor only as the last steps, and only if the malfunction remains after all the checks above.
  4. Do NOT use arlington's 'same as H5' fixes (tighten terminals, adjust phase sequence, replace mainboard/compressor) as the H7 diagnosis, and do NOT present aysonline's 'Replace compressor or control board' as a first step - the manual checks wiring, voltage, coil, radiating, and refrigerant first, and replaces AP1/compressor last.

The steps above follow the manual's section 9.4(3)/(6) diagnosis order (connection, voltage, coil, workload/radiating, refrigerant, AP1, compressor). The section 9.4(6) flowchart text is partly scrambled OCR, so only the detection points are quoted.

When to call a technician

  • H7 returns after one restart with a 3-minute wait - the AP1-compressor wiring and sequence, input voltage, coil resistance and insulation, radiating, and refrigerant charge need a technician.
  • Any burning smell, visibly damaged wiring, or other fault codes alongside H7 - switch off and call a qualified technician.
  • Your unit is a VRF unit showing H7 - VRF H7 is a fan drive temperature sensor fault, a different fault; use the VRF manual, not this page.

Safety first

H7 involves mains-voltage and compressor-coil checks at the outdoor control board: never open the outdoor unit or probe the AP1 board or compressor terminals yourself. Voltage, coil-resistance, and refrigerant work are technician jobs.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does H7 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

Desynchronizing of compressor - also worded 'compressor loss step protection' / 'loss of synchronism'. The inverter drive has lost synchronism with the compressor motor. Verified in five Gree manuals (Change 18/24 section 9.3, evo Malfunction Tab, Livo Gen4 section 9.1, Soyal section 9.1, Mini After-sale Error Code List).

What is the blink pattern for H7?

OFF 3 seconds, then blink 7 times (Change 18/24 section 9.3 row 27, evo Malfunction Tab, Mini After-sale manual). In cooling/drying the compressor stops while the indoor fan runs; in heating the whole unit stops.

How do I reset a Gree H7 error?

First wait: the manual checks whether the compressor has been stopped for more than 3 minutes, because restarting with unbalanced system pressure can cause a synchronism fault. Power the unit off, wait, and restart once. If H7 returns, a technician must check the AP1-compressor wiring and sequence, the input voltage, the compressor coil resistance and insulation, the radiating, and the refrigerant charge - in that order.

Is H7 the same as F7?

No - and this is the most common confusion. F7 is not a fault at all: it is the 'Oil returning' running state. H7 is a real fault (compressor desynchronizing). Do not apply F7 oil-return explanations - or invented F7 sensor fixes - to H7.

Does H7 mean the compressor must be replaced?

Not first. The manual replaces the control board AP1 and then the compressor only as last steps, after wiring, voltage, coil, radiating, and refrigerant checks. Sites that open with 'replace compressor or control board' skip the entire manual diagnosis.
