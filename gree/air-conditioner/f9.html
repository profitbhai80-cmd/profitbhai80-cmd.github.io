Gree Air Conditioner Error Code F9

Quick answer

Error codeF9 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansF9 on a Gree wall-split air conditioner means decrease frequency due to high air discharge (high exhaust/discharge temperature limit): the unit keeps running at reduced compressor frequency because of overload or excessive temperature, insufficient refrigerant, or a malfunctioning electric expansion valve (EKV). F9 is NOT an exhaust-sensor fault (that is F5), NOT the E4 high-discharge protection trip, and it does NOT stop the unit. On U-Match families F9 does not exist, and on GMV VRF platforms F9 is a compressor-5 discharge-sensor fault.
Most common causeHigh discharge temperature driving the frequency limit: overload or excessive temperature, insufficient refrigerant, or a malfunctioning electric expansion valve (EKV).
Safe to check yourself?Yes, for the checks on this page: confirm the code and platform, clear outdoor-coil airflow, clean indoor filters, and restart once. Refrigerant, expansion-valve and outdoor-board work is technician-only.
Checked againstGree GWH Series Service Manual, Display Method of Indoor/Outdoor Unit, entry 19 (F9: decrease frequency due to high air discharge; causes: overload or temperature too high, refrigerant insufficient, malfunction of electric expansion valve EKV; outdoor blink OFF 3s / OFF 1s, 9 times / twice); Gree Soyal Service Manual, same display table entry 19; GREE Inverter AC Service Manual (Bora inverter), same display table entry 19; Gree Livo+ LIVS12HP115V1A Service Manual §9.1 (F9: High exhaust temperature limit / drop frequency)

Applies to — read this first

F9 completes Gree's frequency-limit trio (F6 overload, F8 overcurrent, F9 high discharge temperature), and it is the one most often confused with its neighbours: F5 is the discharge SENSOR fault, E4 is the high-discharge PROTECTION trip, and F9 is the frequency-LIMIT response to high discharge temperature. Three codes, three different faults — the page exists to keep them apart.

F9 vs F5 vs E4 — do not mix themF9 = frequency-limit response to high discharge temperature (unit keeps running slower). F5 = discharge temperature SENSOR open/short (a sensor fault). E4 = high discharge temperature PROTECTION trip (a stop fault). The three codes are routinely conflated by competitors.

The ranking pages give F9's label at best and never make the F9/F5/E4 distinction — yet that distinction decides the repair: a sensor replacement (F5), a protection investigation (E4), or a refrigerant/expansion-valve investigation (F9). A reader who cannot tell them apart will buy the wrong repair every time.

What the machine does when F9 fires

The unit keeps running with all loads operating normally, but the compressor's operating frequency is decreased because the discharge temperature is too high. The control is protecting the compressor by slowing it down rather than stopping it — and the outdoor unit signals F9 with its blink pattern (OFF 3s / OFF 1s, 9 times / twice on the GWH display method).

Common causes

  1. Overload or excessively high temperature in the system.
  2. Insufficient refrigerant — low charge drives discharge temperature up.
  3. A malfunctioning electric expansion valve (EKV) — the valve that meters refrigerant into the evaporator.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the code and the platform: F9 on a wall-split unit is this page. If your display shows F5, that is the discharge-sensor fault; if it shows E4, that is the high-discharge protection trip. If your unit is a U-Match unit, stop — U-Match tables have no F9. If it is a GMV VRF unit, stop — its F9 is a compressor-5 discharge-sensor fault.
  2. Clear blocked airflow at the outdoor coil and clean the indoor filters: restricted airflow drives temperatures up and feeds the overload side of F9.
  3. Restart the unit once and watch whether it settles back to normal running. If F9 keeps returning, stop: refrigerant charge and expansion-valve work belong to a qualified technician. Do not open the outdoor unit yourself, and never attempt refrigerant work.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the unit runs normally. F9 has no home refrigerant or valve step in any manual — the home scope is code/platform confirmation, airflow, filters, and one restart. Never open the outdoor unit's covers or touch sensor wiring.

When to call a technician

  • F9 returns repeatedly after airflow and filter checks and one restart.
  • Cooling is weak or absent alongside F9 — consistent with low refrigerant or an expansion-valve fault.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or refrigerant smell near the outdoor unit — switch off and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

F9 involves mains-powered air-conditioner electronics and an outdoor unit: never open the outdoor unit's covers or touch sensor terminals while investigating it, and never attempt refrigerant work — refrigerant handling belongs to a qualified technician. Switch the unit off at the isolator before any visible check.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F9 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

On wall-splits F9 means decrease frequency due to high air discharge: all loads keep operating normally while the compressor's operating frequency is decreased, because of overload or excessive temperature, insufficient refrigerant, or a malfunctioning electric expansion valve (EKV). Four wall-split-family manuals agree.

What is the difference between Gree F9, F5 and E4?

F9 is the frequency-limit response to high discharge temperature (unit keeps running slower). F5 is the discharge SENSOR fault (open/short circuit). E4 is the high-discharge PROTECTION trip (a stop fault). Three codes, three different repairs.

Is Gree F9 a refrigerant leak?

Not necessarily — low refrigerant is one of three manual causes, alongside overload/excessive temperature and an EKV malfunction. The manual does not let you pick one without a technician's checks; do not assume a leak.

My unit is a U-Match — does F9 apply?

No. U-Match error tables run F0–F5 only and have no F9. On GMV VRF platforms F9 is a compressor-5 discharge-sensor fault instead. This page is wall-split only.

How do I clear Gree air conditioner F9?

Confirm the code and platform, clear outdoor-coil airflow, clean the indoor filters, and restart once. If F9 keeps returning, refrigerant or expansion-valve work belongs to a technician.
