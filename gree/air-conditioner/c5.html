Gree Air Conditioner Error Code C5

Quick answer

Error codeC5 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansC5 means a jumper cap malfunction on the indoor unit's control board: the small configuration jumper that tells the board the unit's model and capacity settings is missing, wrong, damaged, or not seated. It most often appears after a control-board replacement where the jumper was not transferred from the old board.
Most common causeA missing, wrong, damaged, or badly seated jumper cap on the indoor unit's main control board — most often after a control-board replacement where the jumper was not moved across from the old board.
Safe to check yourself?Partially. You can safely power-cycle the unit once at the breaker to clear a transient latch. If C5 appeared right after a board replacement, tell the installer the jumper must be transferred. Anything that touches the indoor unit's main control board is not a home check.

Applies to — read this first

Good news for diagnosis: within the Gree residential mini-split families we checked, C5 is consistent — it is always the indoor unit's jumper cap malfunction protection. What you must NOT do is borrow meanings from other Gree lines or other brands: on Gree's GMV VRF commercial systems C5 means something entirely different (an engineering serial-number conflict), some Gree families use C4 for the outdoor-unit jumper fault while C5 is the indoor one, and other brands' C5 codes are unrelated. This page covers the mini-split indoor-display C5 only.

On Gree's GMV VRF/commercial line (Ultra Heat GMV6 service manual), C5 = 'Indoor Unit Engineering SN Conflict' — two indoor units sharing the same engineering serial number, cleared through commissioning software. That is a different fault on a different product line; this page covers the residential mini-split meaning only. Also note: in the Gree Dry Contact & 24V Adaptor manual family, the outdoor-unit jumper fault is C4 ('ODU jumper cap error') while C5 is the indoor-unit one — if your display shows C4, that is a different code.

What the machine does when C5 fires

The indoor unit displays C5 and stops normal operation — this is a configuration protection, not a running fault. The Gree Fairy/Comfort Pro service manual's C5 procedure starts from 'Display shows C5' and works through the jumper cap before anything else, which tells you the unit will not run until the configuration fault is cleared.

Common causes

  1. No jumper cap inserted on the control board — the Vireo manual lists this cause first.
  2. An incorrect or damaged jumper cap on the control board.
  3. A control-board replacement where the original jumper was not transferred to the new board — the most common real-world trigger.
  4. The jumper cap not seated tightly on the board pins.
  5. Indoor and outdoor units that are not compatible with each other (Vireo manual's third listed cause).
  6. A faulty indoor main control board itself — the service manual's last resort after the jumper cap checks.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off at the breaker, wait a few minutes, then switch it back on. A transient latch can clear on a power cycle; if C5 comes straight back, the configuration fault is genuine.
  2. Ask when C5 first appeared. If it showed up right after a control-board replacement, the cause is almost certainly the jumper: the new board needs the jumper transferred from the old board. That work belongs to the installer or technician who fitted the board — do not open the unit yourself to do it.
  3. If there was no recent board work and C5 persists, stop: the Gree service manual's C5 checks — confirming the jumper cap is present, reseating it tightly, replacing the cap, and finally replacing the main board — all happen on the indoor unit's main control board behind the electrical cover. Switch the unit off and call a qualified HVAC technician.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. If a check needs an electrical cover removed or a control board touched, it is no longer a safe home check — skip to the technician section.

When to call a technician

  • C5 returns immediately after the power-cycle reset — the jumper cap is missing, wrong, damaged, or loose.
  • C5 appeared right after a control-board replacement — the jumper was not transferred from the old board.
  • The unit is a Gree GMV/VRF commercial system showing C5 — that is the engineering SN-conflict fault, a different procedure entirely.
  • Any sign of burnt, damaged, or water-affected board components behind the indoor unit's electrical cover.

Safety first

The indoor unit's main control board sits behind the electrical cover with mains-voltage wiring and capacitors that hold charge after switch-off — never remove electrical covers or touch the board yourself. The Gree R32 Slim Duct manual's own wording stands: 'The air conditioner is not expected to be serviced by users. Incorrect repair may cause electric shock or fire, so please contact an authorized service center for professional service.' Note the R32 units use A2L mildly-flammable refrigerant, another reason board and sealed-system work stays with certified technicians.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does C5 mean on a Gree mini split?

C5 means a jumper cap malfunction on the indoor unit's control board. A jumper cap is a small configuration plug on the board that tells it the unit's model and capacity settings — if it is missing, wrong, damaged, or not seated, the board refuses to run and shows C5. Every Gree mini-split manual we checked defines it this way.

Can I fix Gree C5 myself?

Only the first step: power-cycle the unit once at the breaker. If C5 returns, it is a genuine configuration fault. If the code appeared right after a control-board replacement, the installer must transfer the jumper from the old board to the new one. Everything else — checking, reseating, or replacing the jumper cap — happens on the indoor unit's main control board behind the electrical cover, and that is technician work.

Is Gree C5 the same as EE?

No. C5 is a jumper cap (configuration) fault; EE is a memory-chip (EEPROM) malfunction. They point at different parts of the control board and have different fixes — do not treat them as the same code.

My Gree GMV system shows C5. Is this the same fault?

No. On Gree's GMV VRF commercial line, C5 means 'Indoor Unit Engineering SN Conflict' — a commissioning/software issue, not a jumper cap. This page covers the residential mini-split C5 only; check your GMV service manual for the SN-conflict procedure.
