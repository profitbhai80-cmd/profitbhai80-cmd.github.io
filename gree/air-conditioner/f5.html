Gree Air Conditioner Error Code F5

Quick answer

Error codeF5 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansF5 on a Gree wall-split air conditioner means the compressor discharge (exhaust) temperature sensor is open circuit or short circuit — a bad connection or a failed sensor. Do not confuse it with E4: E4 is a high discharge temperature protection trip, while F5 is a sensor fault. On U-Match families F5 instead means the wired-controller temperature sensor — read the split before you act, because a wall-controller fault and a discharge-sensor fault are entirely different repairs.
Most common causeThe compressor discharge temperature sensor has failed, or its connection to the outdoor unit's control is loose or damaged — the control gets no usable discharge-temperature reading.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home checks are: confirm your unit is a wall-split and not a U-Match, confirm the code is F5 and not E4, and power-cycle once. The discharge sensor lives in the outdoor unit and any refrigerant-adjacent work is strictly technician-only — never open the outdoor unit yourself.
Checked againstGree NEO09AC115V1A Neo Ductless Instruction Manual (F5: Compressor Discharge Temp. Thermistor, Bad Connection or Discharge Sensor Failure); Gree One-Way Cassette Owner's Manual section 7 (F5: outdoor discharge temperature sensor open/short circuited); Gree UMA30HP230V1ACS U-Match Table 6.1 (split: F5 = temperature sensor error of wired controller); Gree ME33-42/G Owner's Manual p.30 (split: F5 = wired controller temperature sensor error)

Applies to — read this first

This page is for Gree wall-split air conditioners, where F5 is the compressor discharge sensor — last in the wall-split ladder (F1 indoor air, F2 indoor coil, F3 outdoor air, F4 outdoor coil, F5 discharge). Two confusions must be cleared first: F5 is not E4, and on U-Match units F5 is not a discharge sensor at all.

ManualExact wording for F5
Gree NEO09AC115V1A Neo Ductless Instruction Manual (greecomfort.com)'Compressor Discharge Temp. Thermistor | F5 | Bad Connection, or Discharge Sensor Failure.'
Gree One-Way Cassette Owner's Manual (§7)'F5 | Outdoor discharge temperature sensor is open/short circuited.'
Gree UMA30HP230V1ACS U-Match Table 6.1 — PLATFORM SPLIT'F5 | Temperature sensor error of wired controller.' On U-Match families F5 is the wall-controller sensor, not the discharge sensor.
Gree ME33-42/G Owner's Manual (p.30) — split corroboration'F5 | Wired controller temperature sensor error.' Confirms the U-Match F5 assignment on a second manual.
F5 vs E4 — do not mix themF5 = compressor discharge SENSOR fault (open/short circuit). E4 = high discharge TEMPERATURE protection trip — a protection event, not a sensor fault. Different faults, different repairs.
Wall-split ladder vs U-Match ladderWall-split: F1 indoor air, F2 indoor coil, F3 outdoor air, F4 outdoor coil, F5 discharge. U-Match (shifted): F2 condenser, F4 discharge, F5 wired-controller sensor.

Two ranking pages present the U-Match F5 (wired-controller sensor) as universal — a wall-split owner following them replaces a wall controller for a discharge-sensor fault. The bare-table pages get F5 right for wall-splits in one row but never make the F5-versus-E4 distinction, so a reader cannot tell a sensor fault from a high-temperature protection trip.

What the machine does when F5 fires

The unit reports F5 and the control loses its compressor discharge-temperature reading: an open or shorted sensor gives the control no usable discharge temperature, which is why the unit reports the fault instead of running normally. The discharge sensor lives in the outdoor unit, so everything past the code reading is outdoor-unit work — and a U-Match owner with a faulty wall-controller sensor who reads a wall-split F5 page would be sent to test that same outdoor sensor for a wall-controller fault.

Common causes

  1. The compressor discharge (exhaust) temperature sensor itself has failed.
  2. A bad connection between the sensor and the outdoor unit's control board.
  3. Damage to the sensor wiring that reads as an open or short circuit.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the code letter: F5 is a sensor fault; E4 is a high discharge temperature protection trip. If your display shows E4, this page is not your fault.
  2. Confirm your platform: if your unit is a U-Match unit, stop — your F5 is the wired-controller temperature sensor, not the discharge sensor on this page.
  3. Power-cycle the unit once: switch it off at the remote and the isolator, wait a few minutes, switch it back on, and see whether F5 returns.
  4. If F5 returns, stop: the sensor lives in the outdoor unit and its circuit checks — plus any refrigerant-adjacent work — belong to a qualified technician. Do not open the outdoor unit yourself.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. F5 has no deeper home diagnostic in any manual — code check, platform check, power-cycle, then the technician. Never open the outdoor unit's covers or touch sensor wiring.

When to call a technician

  • F5 returns after a power-cycle on a confirmed wall-split unit.
  • The discharge sensor circuit reads open or shorted at the terminals.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or refrigerant smell near the outdoor unit — switch off and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

F5 involves mains-powered air-conditioner electronics and an outdoor unit: never open the outdoor unit's covers or touch sensor terminals while investigating it, and never attempt refrigerant work — refrigerant handling belongs to a qualified technician. Switch the unit off at the isolator before any visible check.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F5 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

On wall-splits F5 means the compressor discharge (exhaust) temperature sensor is open circuit or short circuit — a bad connection or a failed sensor. Two wall-split manuals (NEO09, One-Way Cassette) agree on this meaning.

What is the difference between Gree F5 and E4?

F5 is a sensor fault: the discharge sensor circuit is open or shorted. E4 is a high discharge temperature protection trip: the protection event, not a sensor fault. Different faults, different repairs.

My Gree unit is a U-Match — is F5 still the discharge sensor?

No. On U-Match families F5 means the wired-controller temperature sensor. A U-Match owner with a faulty wall-controller sensor who reads a wall-split F5 page would test the compressor discharge sensor instead — wrong part, outdoor unit, refrigerant-adjacent work for a wall-controller fault.

Why do some websites say Gree F5 is the wall-controller sensor?

Because they present the U-Match numbering as their main table without naming the shift. That wired-controller meaning is correct for U-Match units and wrong for wall-splits.

How do I clear Gree air conditioner F5?

Confirm the code is F5 (not E4) and your platform is a wall-split, then power-cycle once. If F5 returns, the outdoor discharge-sensor circuit fault is genuine and belongs to a technician.
