Gree Air Conditioner Error Code E0

Quick answer

Error codeE0 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansE0 = pump failure (the condensate water pump). When full-water protection has not recovered for a continuous 2 hours, the unit concludes the water pump is at fault, shuts off all loads, and cannot recover automatically. U-Match duct/cassette ONLY - E0 is absent from every wall-split manual checked, so a wall-split owner will never see E0.
Most common causeThe condensate water pump has failed - full-water protection has continued for 2 hours without recovering, so the unit declares the water pump at fault (Gree U-Match Service Manuals GUHN09NK3AO and GUHN09NK1AO, section 1).
Safe to check yourself?No - there is no manual reset for E0. Switch the unit off, check that the condensate drain/pump path is not blocked, and call a technician - the pump itself is the declared fault, and a power cycle will not clear it.
Checked againstGree U-Match Service Manual, GUHN09NK3AO, section 1 Trouble Table + Water Pump origin row + Gree U-Match Service Manual, GUHN09NK1AO, section 1 Trouble Table + Water Pump origin row

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because Gree E0 is a U-Match duct/cassette code ONLY - never a general wall-split code. E0 = pump failure (the condensate water pump), verified in two Gree U-Match service manuals (GUHN09NK3AO and GUHN09NK1AO, section 1 Trouble Table). E0 is ABSENT from every wall-split table checked first-hand 2026-11-07: Change 18/24 section 9.3 (rows 1-40, E1 to FH, no E0), evo Malfunction Tab (E1 to FH, no E0), Livo Gen4 section 9.1 (rows 1-49, no E0), Livo+ section 9.1 (p.40, no E0), Soyal section 9.1 (rows 1-53, no E0). Do NOT present E0 as a general wall-split code - same framing as the PA/PC specs (U-Match-only). If your wall-split shows a code, check your own model's manual - the code you have is almost certainly not E0, or your unit is a U-Match duct/cassette system.

What the machine does when E0 fires

All loads are shut off and the fault cannot be restored automatically - the unit does not recover by itself, and a power cycle does not clear it. E0 is not a flash-count code like the wall-split E-codes: U-Match shows the code on the wired controller / dual-8 display.

Common causes

  1. Condensate water pump failure - full-water protection has continued for 2 hours without recovering, so the unit declares the water pump at fault (both U-Match manuals, Water Pump origin row).
  2. Blocked condensate drain/pump path - the only drain check the manuals give is the E9 step (condensate overflow switch, 8-second detection); a blocked drain path can keep the full-water state from recovering.
  3. Wrong-code reading - errors.codes and hundred-worries claim E0 means 'insufficient voltage' and advise checking the 220V supply or buying a voltage stabilizer; no Gree manual ties E0 to supply voltage, and no manual prescribes any of that for E0.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off - the fault 'cannot be automatically recovered', so do not wait for it to clear by itself.
  2. Check that the condensate drain/pump path is not blocked (the E9 step - condensate overflow switch, 8-second detection - is the only drain check the manuals give).
  3. Call a technician - the water pump is the declared fault, and a power cycle will not clear E0. There is NO manual reset ritual for E0 in either manual.
  4. Do NOT follow errors.codes' steps ('check the 220V supply / home wiring / internal wiring / replace the PCB') - that list is generic invented advice for its (wrong) 'insufficient voltage' meaning, and no U-Match manual prescribes any of it for E0. Do NOT buy a voltage stabilizer for E0 - no manual ties E0 to supply voltage.

The steps above are the manuals' full remedy: the fault does not clear by itself and is not cleared by a power cycle in either manual (contrast E9's water-full protection, which is a level event). No manual gives a reset ritual for E0.

When to call a technician

  • E0 is displayed at all - the pump is the declared fault and the unit cannot recover automatically, so a technician is needed.
  • Water is visibly overflowing or the drain pan is full - switch off and call a technician; do not run the unit with standing water.
  • Your unit is a wall-split showing E0 - E0 is not a wall-split code, so what you are seeing needs professional diagnosis of the real fault.

Safety first

E0 involves standing condensate water and a mains-powered pump: switch the unit off before any visible drain check, never open the indoor unit or probe the pump or control board yourself, and call a technician for the pump itself.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E0 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

Pump failure - the condensate water pump. In the U-Match duct/cassette manuals, E0 means the water pump is at fault after full-water protection has not recovered for 2 continuous hours. Verified in two Gree U-Match service manuals (GUHN09NK3AO section 1, GUHN09NK1AO section 1).

Is E0 a low-voltage error?

No. Several websites claim E0 means 'insufficient voltage' and tell you to buy a stabilizer - no Gree manual says that. E0 is a pump failure on U-Match duct/cassette units. Do not buy a stabilizer for E0.

Does E0 appear on Gree wall-split / mini-split units?

No - E0 is not listed in any wall-split table checked (Change 18/24, evo, Livo Gen4, Livo+, Soyal). If your wall-split shows a code, check your own model's manual - the code you have is almost certainly not E0, or your unit is a U-Match duct/cassette system.

What is the difference between E0 and E9?

E9 is full-water protection: water detected for a continuous 8 seconds after power-on. E0 is the verdict that follows when that full-water state has not recovered for 2 continuous hours - the pump itself is declared at fault, all loads shut off, and it cannot recover automatically.

How do I reset a Gree E0 error?

There is no manual reset: the fault 'cannot be automatically recovered'. Switch the unit off, check the condensate drain path is not blocked, and call a technician - the water pump is the declared fault, and a power cycle will not clear it.
