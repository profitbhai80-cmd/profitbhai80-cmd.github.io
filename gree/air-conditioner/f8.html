Gree Air Conditioner Error Code F8

Quick answer

Error codeF8 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansF8 on a Gree wall-split air conditioner means decrease frequency due to overcurrent: the unit keeps running at reduced compressor frequency because the input supply voltage is too low, or the system pressure is too high and the system is overloaded. F8 is NOT an exhaust-sensor fault, NOT a system-overload stop, and it does NOT stop the unit. On U-Match families F8 does not exist, and on GMV VRF platforms F8 is a discharge-sensor fault instead.
Most common causeLow input supply voltage, or high system pressure with system overload — the two manual causes for the F8 frequency limit.
Safe to check yourself?Yes, for the checks on this page: confirm the code and platform, check the supply (other appliances dimming or struggling points at a supply problem — call an electrician, not an AC technician, for that), clear outdoor-coil airflow, clean indoor filters, and restart once. Electrical-board and refrigerant work is technician-only.
Checked againstGree GWH Series Service Manual, Display Method of Indoor/Outdoor Unit, entry 18 (F8: decrease frequency due to overcurrent; causes: input supply voltage too low, system pressure too high and overload; outdoor blink OFF 3s / OFF 1s, 8 times / once); Gree Soyal Service Manual, same display table entry 18; GREE Inverter AC Service Manual (Bora inverter), same display table entry 18; Gree Livo+ LIVS12HP115V1A Service Manual §9.1 (F8: Over current limit / drop frequency)

Applies to — read this first

F8 is the second of Gree's frequency-limit codes, and the same warning applies as for F6: not every F code is a sensor. F8 slows the compressor and keeps the unit running. Two more distinctions matter before you act: F8 is not E5 (the over-current PROTECTION stop fault), and F8's manual causes are specific — low supply voltage, or high pressure plus overload — not a generic 'system overload'.

GMV VRF platforms — DIFFERENT F8On GMV VRF platforms F8 = discharge temperature sensor fault of a compressor (GMV6 service manual §2.76 says compressor 4; the GMV chapter list says compressor 3 — the two GMV documents disagree on the compressor number, but both agree F8 is a VRF discharge-sensor fault, not a frequency limit). VRF is out of scope for this page.
F8 vs E5 vs F6 — do not mix themF8 = overcurrent frequency LIMIT (unit keeps running slower). E5 = AC over-current PROTECTION (a stop fault). F6 = overload frequency limit (different trigger: overload / high-temperature resistance). Three codes, three responses.

The ranking pages fail F8 the same way they fail F6: bare-table rows with the right label and no behaviour, no blink pattern, no scope — plus one specific theft, where a translated guide's F8 row ('system overload') steals F6's overload meaning and mislabels F8. The two manual causes (low supply voltage; high pressure + overload) are the diagnosis, and neither appears on the ranking pages.

What the machine does when F8 fires

The unit keeps running with all loads operating normally, but the compressor's operating frequency is decreased — either because the input supply voltage is too low to sustain full frequency, or because system pressure is too high and the system is overloaded. The outdoor unit signals F8 with its blink pattern (OFF 3s / OFF 1s, 8 times / once on the GWH display method).

Common causes

  1. Input supply voltage too low — the first manual cause. If lights dim or other appliances struggle when the AC runs, the supply is the suspect, not the AC.
  2. System pressure too high combined with system overload — the second manual cause.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the code and the platform: F8 on a wall-split unit is this page. If your unit is a U-Match unit, stop — U-Match tables have no F8. If your unit is a GMV VRF unit, stop — its F8 is a discharge-sensor fault.
  2. Check the supply picture: are lights dimming, or do other large appliances struggle while the AC runs? If so, note it for an electrician — a supply-voltage problem is not an AC fault to fix at the unit.
  3. Clear blocked airflow at the outdoor coil and clean the indoor filters: restricted airflow drives pressure up and feeds the overload side of F8.
  4. Restart the unit once and watch whether it settles back to normal running. If F8 keeps returning, stop: pressure, refrigerant and outdoor-board work belong to a qualified technician. Do not open the outdoor unit yourself.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the unit runs normally. F8 has no sensor to replace — the home scope is supply observation, airflow, filters, and one restart. Never open the outdoor unit's covers or touch sensor wiring.

When to call a technician

  • F8 returns repeatedly after airflow and filter checks and one restart.
  • Lights dimming or other appliances struggling whenever the AC runs — a supply-voltage problem for an electrician.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or refrigerant smell near the outdoor unit — switch off and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

F8 involves mains-powered air-conditioner electronics and an outdoor unit: never open the outdoor unit's covers or touch sensor terminals while investigating it, and never attempt refrigerant or supply-electrical work — both belong to qualified technicians. Switch the unit off at the isolator before any visible check.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F8 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

On wall-splits F8 means decrease frequency due to overcurrent: all loads keep operating normally while the compressor's operating frequency is decreased, because the input supply voltage is too low or the system pressure is too high and the system is overloaded. Four wall-split-family manuals agree.

Is Gree F8 a sensor fault?

No. F8 is a frequency-limit protection, not a sensor fault. The 'every F code is a sensor' framing on some ranking pages is false for F6, F8 and F9.

What is the difference between Gree F8 and E5?

F8 is an overcurrent frequency LIMIT — the unit keeps running slower. E5 is the AC over-current PROTECTION — a stop fault. Same overcurrent family, different response.

My unit is a U-Match — does F8 apply?

No. U-Match error tables run F0–F5 only and have no F8. On GMV VRF platforms F8 is a discharge-sensor fault instead. This page is wall-split only.

How do I clear Gree air conditioner F8?

Confirm the platform, check whether the supply is struggling (lights dimming = electrician), clear outdoor-coil airflow, clean the indoor filters, and restart once. If F8 keeps returning, pressure or supply work belongs to a technician.
