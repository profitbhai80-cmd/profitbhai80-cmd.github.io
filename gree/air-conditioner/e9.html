Gree Air Conditioner Error Code E9

Quick answer

Error codeE9 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansE9 on a Gree air conditioner (duct / cassette families) means FULL WATER PROTECTION — the indoor unit's condensate level has reached the full-water state (drain tray / water-level device). Series split: the GREE Vireo GEN3 wall-split owner's manual states E2 and E9 are operational status codes, not error codes — so a Vireo wall-split E9 is not this fault. On duct/cassette units check the drain line, tray and pump; on a Vireo wall-split, do not chase a drain fault from this code.
Most common causeOn duct / cassette families: condensate level rising to the full-water state — blocked or kinked drain line, a full or stuck drain tray, a failed condensate pump, or a tripped water-level device.
Safe to check yourself?Yes for the external drain-side checks on duct/cassette units — drain line, tray and pump access points the manual's meaning points to. On a Vireo wall-split there is nothing to fix from this code at all.

Applies to — read this first

Gree E9 splits the families cleanly. On duct / cassette units the manuals agree: full water protection — condensate has reached the full-water state. But the GREE Vireo GEN3 wall-split owner's manual says E2 and E9 are operational status codes, not error codes — so on a Vireo wall-split, E9 is not a drain fault at all. Every blog list misses this split, and that is where Vireo owners waste an afternoon hunting a drain problem that does not exist.

The difference this page wins on is the Vireo split. No ranking page distinguishes the Vireo status use from the duct/cassette fault — they apply the drain meaning to every Gree unit, including the one manual that says it is not an error.

What the machine does when E9 fires

On duct / cassette units the system enters full-water protection and the display shows E9 (or the LED blinks): the indoor unit's condensate has reached the full-water state, so cooling and dehumidifying operation stops until the water is cleared.

Common causes

  1. A blocked or kinked condensate drain line — the most common path to a full tray.
  2. The drain tray full or the float / water-level device stuck at the full position.
  3. A condensate pump that has stopped moving water (on units fitted with one).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Power the unit off and place a towel or tray under the indoor unit's drain area.
  2. Check the visible condensate drain line for kinks, disconnections or blockage at the outlet.
  3. Check the drain tray is not full and that the float / water-level device moves freely and is not stuck up.
  4. On units with a condensate pump, check the pump is actually lifting water when powered; a silent pump with a full tray points to pump failure.
  5. If your unit is a GREE Vireo wall-split, skip all of this: E9 there is a status code, not a drain fault.
  6. If the code persists with the drain path clear, call a qualified technician — the water-level device or its wiring needs professional diagnosis.

Work the drain path in order and stop when it clears. The critical fork is the family check: duct/cassette E9 is a real water fault, Vireo wall-split E9 is not — make sure you know which you own.

When to call a technician

  • E9 persists with the drain line, tray and pump all clear.
  • The float / water-level device is damaged or its wiring is broken.
  • A Vireo wall-split E9 you cannot identify as a normal status display — have a technician confirm rather than draining anything.
  • Water leaking from the indoor unit housing itself, not the drain outlet — switch off and call a technician.

Safety first

E9 is a water fault in a mains appliance. Power off before touching the drain tray or pump, mop up spills, and never work on a powered machine with wet hands. Never open the unit's panels or touch internal wiring.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E9 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

On duct / cassette families E9 means full water protection — the indoor unit's condensate has reached the full-water state, per the Slim Duct and One-Way Cassette owner's manuals, the R410A service manual and the Free Match indoor-unit table.

My Gree Vireo wall-split shows E9 — is my drain blocked?

No — on the Vireo GEN3 wall-split the owner's manual states E9 is an operational status code, not an error code. Do not chase a drain fault from it; it is not the duct/cassette fault.

How do I clear Gree E9 on a duct or cassette unit?

Power off, then work the drain path: drain line for kinks or blockage, drain tray not full, float moving freely, and the condensate pump lifting water if one is fitted. Clear water stops the protection.

What does 'full water protection' actually do?

The system detects the full-water state at the indoor unit's water-level device and shows E9 (or blinks the LED), stopping cooling and dehumidifying operation until the condensate is cleared.

E9 is still there with the drain clear — what next?

Stop and call a qualified technician. With the drain path clear, the fault is in the water-level device, its wiring or the control — professional diagnosis, not more drain work.
