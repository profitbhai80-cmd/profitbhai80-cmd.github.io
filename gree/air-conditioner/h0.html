Gree Air Conditioner Error Code H0

Quick answer

Error codeH0 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansH0 = heating anti-high temperature drop frequency (anti-high temperature protection in heating). When the temperature gets too high in heating, the unit does NOT stop - all loads keep operating while the compressor frequency is decreased. Blink pattern: OFF 3S and blink 10 times. H0 is a frequency-LIMIT protection, not a stop fault and not a sensor fault.
Most common causeHigh temperature in heating operation - the outdoor ambient temperature is too high (above 53 degC) or the indoor/outdoor radiating environment and airflow are poor, so the unit protects itself by slowing the compressor (Change 18/24 Service Manual, section 9.4(4)).
Safe to check yourself?Yes - H0 is a normal protection. Check whether the outdoor ambient temperature is above 53 degC and whether the indoor/outdoor airflow and radiating environment are clear; if the cause clears, the unit resumes normal frequency by itself. Do not replace a sensor for H0 - no sensor appears in the manual's H0 diagnosis.
Checked againstGree Change 18/24 Service Manual, section 9.3 row 28 + section 9.4(4) + GREE evo Split AC Owner's Manual, Malfunction Tab + Gree Soyal Service Manual, section 9.1 row 31

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because Gree H0 is a heating-mode frequency-drop protection - not a failed start, and not a stop fault. H0 is verified with an identical meaning in three Gree factory manuals: the Change 18/24 Service Manual (section 9.3 row 28), the evo Split AC Owner's Manual (Malfunction Tab), and the Soyal Service Manual (section 9.1 row 31). When the temperature gets too high in heating, all loads keep operating while the compressor frequency is decreased. H0 is present in Change 18/24, evo, and Soyal - but ABSENT from the R32-era tables checked: Livo Gen4 section 9.1 (rows 1-49), Livo+ section 9.1 (p.40), GWH18QD section 9.1, and Fairy/R32 GWH12ACC section 9.1. H0 does not exist on every Gree range, so check your own manual. VRF H0 is a different fault - fan drive board fault on GMV VRF - do not merge it with this wall-split H0.

What the machine does when H0 fires

All loads operate normally, while the operation frequency for the compressor is decreased during heating operation. The display pattern is OFF 3 seconds, then blink 10 times. The unit does not stop.

Common causes

  1. Outdoor ambient temperature too high - the manual's first detection point is whether the outdoor ambient temperature is in the normal range; above 53 degC the protection trips.
  2. Poor radiating environment of the indoor or outdoor unit - blocked airflow or heat build-up around either unit.
  3. Indoor or outdoor fan not running normally - the manual checks whether the indoor and outdoor fan are running normal.
  4. Wrong-code reading - onlyerrorcodes.com's dedicated H0 page describes Lc (startup failure), not H0; aysonline's 'replace sensor if faulty' is invented, as no sensor appears in the H0 diagnosis.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Check whether the outdoor ambient temperature is above 53 degC. If it is, improve the outdoor ambient temperature - H0 is a normal protection, and the unit resumes normal frequency by itself once the cause clears.
  2. Check the radiating environment of the indoor and outdoor unit and clear any airflow obstruction around either unit.
  3. The manual's diagnosis (Change 18/24 section 9.4(4)) says: after the unit is de-energized for 20 minutes, check whether the radiating of the outdoor and indoor unit is well.
  4. Do NOT replace a sensor for H0 - no sensor appears in the manual's H0 diagnosis. The OFAN terminal, fan resistance (less than 1k ohm between any two terminals), fan motor, capacitor C1, and control board AP1 checks are technician work, not DIY.

The steps above are the manual's section 9.4(4) diagnosis in homeowner-safe framing. The OFAN/resistance/AP1 steps are technician work; the flowchart text in the manual is partly scrambled OCR, so only the detection points are quoted.

When to call a technician

  • H0 keeps returning after the ambient temperature and airflow are normal - the OFAN terminal, fan motor, capacitor C1, or control board AP1 needs a technician.
  • Any burning smell, visibly damaged wiring, or other fault codes alongside H0 - switch off and call a qualified technician.
  • Your unit is a VRF unit showing H0 - VRF H0 is a fan drive board fault, a different fault; use the VRF manual, not this page.

Safety first

H0 is a normal protection and the safe checks are all from outside the unit - ambient temperature and airflow. Never open the outdoor unit or probe the control board (AP1) or fan terminals yourself; those checks are technician work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does H0 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

Heating anti-high temperature drop frequency. In heating mode, when the temperature gets too high, the unit slows the compressor down instead of stopping - all loads keep operating at reduced frequency. Verified in three Gree manuals (Change 18/24 section 9.3, evo Malfunction Tab, Soyal section 9.1).

What is the blink pattern for H0?

OFF 3 seconds, then blink 10 times (Change 18/24 section 9.3 row 28, evo Malfunction Tab). In heating the compressor frequency is decreased; the unit does not stop.

Will my AC keep running with H0?

Yes - that is the point of H0. Unlike E8 (which stops the compressor), H0 only reduces compressor frequency while all loads keep operating. If the outdoor ambient temperature is above 53 degC or airflow is blocked, fix that and the unit returns to normal frequency by itself.

Is H0 a sensor fault?

No. Some sites tell you to replace a sensor for H0 - no sensor appears in the manual's H0 diagnosis. The manual checks outdoor ambient temperature, indoor/outdoor fan operation, and the radiating environment, then the OFAN terminal and fan (technician steps).

Is H0 the same as E8 or Lc?

No. E8 is the anti-high temperature protection that STOPS the compressor (blink 8). Lc is startup failure - the compressor failed to start (a dedicated H0 page online actually describes Lc, not H0). H0 is the heating-mode frequency-drop protection (blink 10).
