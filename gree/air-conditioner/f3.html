Gree Air Conditioner Error Code F3

Quick answer

Error codeF3 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansF3 on a Gree air conditioner means the outdoor ambient temperature sensor is open circuit or short circuit — a bad connection or a failed sensor at the outdoor unit. F3 is the rare Gree F-code where wall-split and U-Match numbering agree, so there is no platform split to worry about on this code. It is not the outdoor coil sensor (F4 on wall-splits, F2 on U-Match) and not the discharge sensor. Power-cycle once; outdoor-unit sensor and board work is technician-only, and one ranking page's bare '20K' resistance value is not a manual-sourced diagnostic.
Most common causeThe outdoor ambient sensor has gone open or short circuit, or its connection to the outdoor controller is loose — the control can no longer read the outside air temperature.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home check is a single power-cycle and noting the display pattern. Anything involving the outdoor unit's electronics is technician work.
Checked againstGree NEO09AC115V1A Neo Ductless Instruction Manual (greecomfort.com) — F3: outdoor air temp. thermistor, bad connection or outdoor ambient sensor failure; Gree One-Way Cassette Air Conditioner Owner's Manual (§7) — F3: outdoor ambient temperature sensor is open/short circuited; Gree UMA30HP230V1ACS U-Match Installation Manual (Table 6.1) — F3: outdoor ambient temperature sensor error; Gree ME33-42/G Owner's Manual (p.30) — F3: outdoor ambient temperature sensor error

Applies to — read this first

F3 is the exception that proves the Gree numbering rule: on F1, F2, F4 and F5 the wall-split and U-Match numberings shift by one, and readers must check their platform first — but on F3 both numberings land on the outdoor ambient sensor. Four manuals prove it, from both sides of the shift. That agreement is itself the page's differentiator, because the ranking tables either never say it or get the neighbouring codes wrong while accidentally getting F3 right.

U-Match: F2 outdoor condenser, F3 outdoor ambient, F4 discharge, F5 wired controller. F3 is outdoor ambient on both; the coil and discharge sensors are the ones that move.

One ranking page tells readers to check that the outdoor ambient sensor 'is 20K' — a bare resistance value with no manual citation and no temperature attached. Thermistor resistance is temperature-dependent, so a bare '20K' is not a diagnostic; do not use it. Another ranking table is only accidentally right on F3 while misdiagnosing F1, F2, F4 and F5 — do not trust its whole table because this one row agrees.

What the machine does when F3 fires

The indoor unit displays F3 and the control loses its outside-air reading. Without a trustworthy outdoor ambient temperature, the control cannot choose the right operating behaviour for the conditions outside — it is flying blind on the weather side of the system, while the indoor sensors keep reporting normally. The manuals describe the sensor fault and its display, not a separate shutdown sequence; the symptom is lost outdoor-temperature control, not a compressor protection event like E1, E3 or H3.

Common causes

  1. A bad connection — the outdoor ambient sensor's wiring terminal to the outdoor controller loosened or poorly contacted.
  2. A faulty outdoor ambient sensor itself — the thermistor gone open circuit or short circuit.
  3. A damaged sensor cable between the sensor and the outdoor controller, exposed to weather at the outdoor unit.
  4. A faulty outdoor mainboard — the final verdict when the connection is sound and the sensor itself tests good.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off, wait a minute, and switch it back on. If F3 clears, it was a transient detection glitch — keep an eye on it, because a repeat points to the outdoor sensor circuit.
  2. Note exactly what the display shows: F3 on a display model. If the code is F4 on a wall-split (outdoor coil) or F2 on a U-Match (outdoor condenser), you have a different sensor — check the neighbour ladder in the series table before deciding anything.
  3. Confirm the fault is at the outdoor unit: F3 is the outdoor ambient sensor, not an indoor sensor. Do not open the outdoor unit's electrical covers or test its terminals yourself.
  4. If F3 returns after the restart, stop: the next steps are checking the sensor's connection, testing the sensor against its resistance table at a known temperature, and then the outdoor mainboard — all of which mean opening the outdoor unit and handling its electronics. That is technician work.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. F3 has no deeper home diagnostic in any manual — power-cycle, identify the code against the ladder, then the technician's connection, sensor and mainboard checks. Never open the outdoor unit's electrical covers or test a live board.

When to call a technician

  • F3 returns after a power-cycle — the outdoor sensor circuit fault is genuine, not a glitch.
  • The sensor connection is loose, corroded or weather-damaged at the outdoor unit.
  • The sensor fails its resistance check at a known temperature — a faulty sensor, replaced with the same model per the manual.
  • Connection sound and sensor good but F3 persists — the outdoor mainboard is suspect.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or scorching near either unit — switch off immediately and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

F3 involves mains-powered outdoor electronics and weather-exposed wiring: never open the outdoor unit's electrical covers or touch sensor or mainboard terminals while investigating it. Switch the power off before any visible check, and never work on a live board. The connection, resistance and mainboard verdicts in the service manuals belong to a qualified technician. If F3 persists after a power-cycle, switch the unit off and call a qualified technician — never attempt refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F3 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

F3 means the outdoor ambient temperature sensor is open circuit or short circuit — a bad connection or a failed sensor at the outdoor unit. The NEO09 manual calls it 'Outdoor Air Temp. Thermistor | F3 | Bad Connection, or Outdoor Ambient Sensor Failure', and the cassette, U-Match and ME33-42/G manuals agree.

Does the wall-split / U-Match numbering shift affect F3?

No — F3 is the rare code where the two numberings agree. Wall-split F3 and U-Match F3 are both the outdoor ambient sensor. The sensors that move between numberings are F1, F2, F4 and F5 (for example, F4 is the outdoor coil sensor on wall-splits but the discharge sensor on U-Match).

Is Gree F3 the outdoor coil sensor?

No. On wall-splits the outdoor coil (condenser) sensor is F4, and on U-Match it is F2. F3 is the outdoor ambient (outside-air) sensor on both numberings. Confusing F3 with the coil sensor means replacing the wrong part.

How do I clear Gree F3?

Power-cycle the unit once — if F3 was a transient detection glitch it clears. If it returns, the fault is genuine: the service tree is the sensor connection, then the sensor against its resistance table at a known temperature, then the outdoor mainboard. All of those steps involve the outdoor unit's electronics and are technician work.

Should I check that the sensor reads 20K?

No — not on the evidence available. One ranking page states a bare '20K' value with no manual citation and no temperature attached, and thermistor resistance changes with temperature, so a bare value is not a diagnostic. Use the manual's resistance table at a known temperature, through a technician.
