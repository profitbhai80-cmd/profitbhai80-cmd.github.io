Gree Air Conditioner Error Code F6

Quick answer

Error codeF6 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansF6 on a Gree wall-split air conditioner means limit / decrease frequency due to overload: the unit keeps running, but the compressor runs at reduced frequency because of overload / high-temperature resistance. F6 is a frequency-limit state, NOT a sensor fault and NOT a stop fault — nothing stops, nothing is declared broken. On U-Match families F6 does not exist at all, and on GMV VRF platforms F6 means something entirely different (a compressor-2 discharge-sensor fault).
Most common causeOverload / high-temperature resistance: the system limits compressor frequency to protect itself while every load keeps operating.
Safe to check yourself?Yes, for the checks on this page: confirm the code is F6 and your unit is a wall-split, clear blocked airflow at the outdoor coil, clean the indoor filters, and restart once. Anything involving refrigerant, sensors, or the outdoor electrical box is technician work.
Checked againstGree GWH Series Service Manual, Display Method of Indoor/Outdoor Unit, entry 17 (F6: overload, high temperature resistant; all loads normal, frequency decreased; outdoor blink OFF 3s / OFF 1s, 6 times / 3 times); Gree Soyal Service Manual, same display table entry 17; GREE Inverter AC Service Manual (Bora inverter), same display table entry 17; Gree Livo+ LIVS12HP115V1A Service Manual §9.1 (F6: Overload limit / drop frequency)

Applies to — read this first

F6 breaks the pattern most Gree lists teach you: not every F code is a sensor. F6, F8 and F9 are frequency-limit protections — the unit deliberately slows the compressor and keeps running. If you go hunting for a sensor on an F6 unit, you are chasing a part that has not failed. Read the scope notes before anything else, because F6's meaning changes completely off wall-splits.

Gree Soyal Service Manual (same display table, entry 17)Identical F6 row — second family corroboration.

U-Match families — F6 DOES NOT EXISTU-Match error tables (GUD50P duct manual, UMA manual) run F0–F5, then C5/EE/PF/H3 — no F6, F8 or F9. A U-Match owner will never see F6; do not present this wall-split page as universal.
GMV VRF platforms — DIFFERENT F6On GMV VRF platforms F6 = discharge/exit-tube temperature sensor fault of compressor 2 (GMV6 service manual §2.74: AD value out of limits for 30 s; causes: poor sensor contact, abnormal sensor, abnormal detection circuit). VRF is out of scope for this page.
F6 vs H3 vs H4 — do not mix themF6 = frequency limit: everything keeps running at lower frequency. H3 = compressor overload PROTECTION (a stop fault: compressor stops, indoor fan runs in cooling). H4 = system overload protection (whole-unit stop behaviour in heating). Same overload family, different responses.

The ranking pages fail F6 in three different ways: luce and pickhvac give the correct label and nothing else (no behaviour, no blink pattern, no scope); aysonline invents 'faulty components' and 'reduce load on system' (the manual says frequency is reduced automatically — the user does not reduce load); arlington calls F6 a tube-middle sensor fault and presents a shifted U-Match-style ladder as universal (its F-series is shifted by one versus wall-split). The behaviour sentence — all loads keep running, frequency decreased — is the fact that separates F6 from every sensor fault.

What the machine does when F6 fires

The unit keeps running with all loads operating normally, but the compressor's operating frequency is decreased. That is the protection working as designed: faced with overload / high-temperature resistance, the control slows the compressor instead of stopping the unit or declaring a sensor fault. The outdoor unit signals F6 with its blink pattern (OFF 3s / OFF 1s, 6 times / 3 times on the GWH display method).

Common causes

  1. Overload on the system — the condition the GWH display table names for F6 (overload, high-temperature resistance).
  2. High-temperature resistance: the system is running hot enough that the control limits frequency to protect itself.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the code and the platform: F6 on a wall-split unit is this page. If your unit is a U-Match unit, stop — U-Match tables have no F6. If your unit is a GMV VRF unit, stop — its F6 is a compressor-2 discharge-sensor fault.
  2. Clear blocked airflow at the outdoor coil and clean the indoor filters: restricted airflow is the home-checkable contributor to an overloaded, hot-running system.
  3. Restart the unit once and watch whether it settles back to normal running. F6 is a limit state, not a latched fault — if the overload condition passes, normal frequency returns.
  4. If F6 keeps returning, or the unit runs hot, makes unusual noises, or stops cooling, stop: refrigerant charge, sensor circuits and outdoor-board work belong to a qualified technician. Do not open the outdoor unit yourself.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the unit runs normally. F6 has no sensor to replace and no component the manual names for the user to change — the home scope is airflow, filters, and one restart. Never open the outdoor unit's covers or touch sensor wiring.

When to call a technician

  • F6 returns repeatedly after airflow and filter checks and one restart.
  • The unit stops cooling, runs excessively hot, or shows other fault codes alongside F6.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or refrigerant smell near the outdoor unit — switch off and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

F6 involves mains-powered air-conditioner electronics and an outdoor unit: never open the outdoor unit's covers or touch sensor terminals while investigating it, and never attempt refrigerant work — refrigerant handling belongs to a qualified technician. Switch the unit off at the isolator before any visible check.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F6 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

On wall-splits F6 means limit / decrease frequency due to overload: all loads keep operating normally while the compressor's operating frequency is decreased. Four wall-split-family manuals agree on this row, including the GWH display-table entry 17.

Is Gree F6 a sensor fault?

No. F6 is a frequency-limit protection, not a sensor open/short fault. The arlington page's 'tube-middle sensor' claim is wrong for wall-splits — on the wall-split ladder the tube sensors are F2/F4, and F6 is the overload limit.

Does the unit stop when F6 shows?

No. That is the point of a frequency limit: everything keeps running at reduced compressor frequency. The stop faults in this family are H3 (compressor stops) and H4 (whole-unit stop in heating).

My unit is a U-Match — does F6 apply?

No. U-Match error tables run F0–F5 only and have no F6, F8 or F9. And on GMV VRF platforms F6 is a compressor-2 discharge-sensor fault — a different fault entirely. This page is wall-split only.

How do I clear Gree air conditioner F6?

Confirm the platform, clear outdoor-coil airflow, clean the indoor filters, and restart once. If F6 keeps returning, the overload condition is genuine and belongs to a technician — there is no user-replaceable sensor for F6.
