Gree Air Conditioner Error Code P5

Quick answer

Error codeP5 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansP5 = overcurrent protection of phase current for compressor. The compressor's phase current has gone too high, so the unit stops the compressor (indoor fan keeps running in cooling/drying; the whole unit stops in heating). Blink pattern: OFF 3S and blink 15 times. Compressor replacement is the LAST step, not the first.
Most common causeThe compressor phase current went too high. The manual's diagnosis checks the compressor terminals, supply voltage (210-250VAC), compressor resistance, refrigerant charge, and airflow before any part is replaced.
Safe to check yourself?No manual reset for P5. Turn the unit off and on once; if P5 comes back, it needs a technician - compressor/inverter diagnosis (terminals, voltage, resistance, refrigerant, airflow).
Checked againstGree Change 18/24 Service Manual (section 9.3 row 20) + Gree Livo Gen4 Service Manual (section 9.1 row 27 + section 9.2(2)) + Gree GWH18QD-K3DNA1G / GWH24QE-K3DNA1G Service Manual (section 9.1) + GREE evo Split AC Owner's Manual (Malfunction Tab)

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because P5 is a protection fault with a fixed diagnosis order: the compressor's phase current went too high, so the unit stops the compressor. The Livo Gen4 manual's diagnosis order is wiring/terminals, then supply voltage, then compressor resistance, then refrigerant charge, then airflow, then control board - and only then the compressor. Compressor replacement is the LAST step, not the first. VRF wording ("Over-current protection of inverter compressor", GMV manuals) is the same concept in different words; do not merge VRF diagnosis into this wall-split page.

What the machine does when P5 fires

The display shows P5 and the unit stops the compressor: during cooling and drying the indoor fan keeps running while the compressor stops; during heating the complete unit stops. Blink pattern: OFF 3 seconds, then blink 15 times.

Common causes

  1. Compressor phase current too high - the manuals' own fault definition; the current protection tripped.
  2. Supply voltage outside 210-250VAC at the XT L-N terminals (Livo Gen4 section 9.2(2) detection point).
  3. Compressor fault - UVW resistance not roughly equal, or resistance vs the copper pipe not over 500 ohms (section 9.2(2)); compressor replacement is the LAST step.
  4. Refrigerant charge or airflow problem - both are section 9.2(2) detection points before any part is replaced.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Turn the unit off and on once. There is no manual reset for P5; if it returns after a restart, it needs a technician.
  2. A technician follows the Livo Gen4 section 9.2(2) order: compressor COMP terminal, power supply voltage (210-250VAC), compressor UVW resistance, refrigerant charge, air inlet/outlet - then control board, then compressor last.
  3. Do not invent a breaker-reset ritual or a "replace compressor" first step. No manual gives a user reset for P5.

The steps above are the manuals' own diagnosis order. This page does not invent a breaker-reset ritual or a compressor-first fix - the manual lists compressor replacement last.

When to call a technician

  • P5 returns after one restart - the compressor terminals, voltage, resistance, refrigerant charge, and airflow need a technician.
  • The unit stops the compressor in cooling/drying or the whole unit in heating - that is the P5 protection behaviour, not a separate fault.
  • Any burning smell, visibly damaged wiring, or other fault codes alongside P5 - switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

P5 involves mains-powered compressor and inverter electronics in the outdoor unit: never open the outdoor unit or touch compressor terminals yourself. The manual's diagnosis (COMP terminal, UVW resistance, refrigerant charge) is technician work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does P5 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

Overcurrent protection of phase current for compressor - the compressor's phase current went too high, so the unit stopped the compressor. Verified in four Gree service/owner's manuals (Livo Gen4 section 9.1, Change 18/24 section 9.3, GWH18QD section 9.1, evo Malfunction Tab).

How do I reset a Gree P5 error?

There is no manual reset for P5. Turn the unit off and on once; if P5 comes back, it needs a technician - the manual's diagnosis checks the compressor terminals, supply voltage (210-250VAC), compressor resistance, refrigerant charge, and airflow before any part is replaced.

Does P5 mean I need a new compressor?

Not necessarily - and not first. The manual lists compressor replacement as the last step, after the wiring, voltage, resistance, refrigerant, and airflow checks. Many P5 cases are voltage, refrigerant, or airflow problems.

What is the blink pattern for P5?

OFF 3 seconds, then blink 15 times (Change 18/24 section 9.3, evo Malfunction Tab). The indoor fan keeps running in cooling/drying; the whole unit stops in heating.

Is P5 the same as P0, P1, P2 or P3?

No. P0-P3 are compressor test states (minimum, nominal, maximum, intermediate frequency) shown during an engineer's test run - not faults. P5 is a real protection fault. (And P4 is VRF-only; no wall-split manual lists it.)
