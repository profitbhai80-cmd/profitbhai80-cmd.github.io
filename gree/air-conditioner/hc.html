Gree Air Conditioner Error Code HC

Quick answer

Error codeHC — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansHC means PFC (Power Factor Correction) protection: the outdoor unit's PFC circuit has tripped. The indoor unit flashes 6 times. In cooling the outdoor fan and compressor stop; in heating the outdoor fan, compressor and indoor fan stop.
Most common causeBad power-grid quality or sharply fluctuating AC input voltage; power plug / wiring board / reactor not connected reliably; dirty heat exchangers or blocked air inlets/outlets; damaged outdoor main board (Mini manual's four causes).
Safe to check yourself?Only the cleaning step: check the indoor/outdoor heat exchangers and air inlets/outlets are clean and unblocked. Grid voltage, the reactor, the PFC module and the main board are technician checks.

Applies to — read this first

HC is the PFC-circuit protection, and nine manufacturer manuals agree on it with no series split. The meaning is the easy part; the traps are the manual itself and the decoder pages. The U-Match R32 manual copy-pastes H5's judgment text into its HC section (§3.4.22) — a copy-paste error inside the Gree manual — so that section must never be used to define HC. And the Mini table lists E6 (communication) as flash 6 as well, so flash 6 is not a unique identifier for HC.

The ranking pages invent HC's fix. Aysonline gives one row — 'Faulty PFC module or sensor — Replace module or sensor' — but no manual says that: the Soyal fix is to replace the outdoor control panel AP1 or the reactor, and the Mini causes are grid quality, wiring/reactor connection, dirt, and the main board. Arlington invents two restrictions: HC 'can only happen with the 42K, 48K, or 60K sensors' and 'ensure that the PFC module is tightened perfectly' plus 'replace the PFC sensor' — no manual limits HC by capacity (HC is listed for 9K-60K wall-splits and U-Match), no manual has a 'PFC sensor', and no manual has a 'tighten the PFC module' step. Luce gives one correct row and nothing else. The same pages carry errors elsewhere — arlington's L9 'not an error' framing, its H6 outdoor-fan claim, its PL 185VAC figure (150VAC per the manuals) and its self-styled 'Official' label (it is a dealer page).

What the machine does when HC fires

In cooling the outdoor fan and compressor stop; in heating the outdoor fan, compressor and indoor fan stop. The indoor unit flashes 6 times (Mini after-sale manual) — with a caveat: the same Mini table lists E6 (communication) as flash 6 as well, so flash 6 alone does not identify HC.

Common causes

  1. Bad quality of power grid / AC input voltage fluctuates sharply (Mini manual, cause 1).
  2. Power plug / wiring board / reactor not connected reliably (Mini manual, cause 2).
  3. Indoor/outdoor heat exchanger dirty or air inlet/outlet blocked (Mini manual, cause 3 — the only owner-actionable cause).
  4. Outdoor main board damaged (Mini manual, cause 4).
  5. Grid voltage abnormal; PFC module damaged; 15V supply voltage of IPM module lower than 13.5V; PFC PWM signal abnormal; PFC current sampling abnormal (U-Match R32 §3.4.22 causes).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Check that the indoor and outdoor heat exchangers and air inlets/outlets are clean and unblocked — the Mini manual's cause 3, and the only owner-actionable cause.
  2. Switch the unit off and re-energize once. If HC clears, the trip was transient.
  3. If HC returns, stop: grid voltage, the reactor, the wiring board, the PFC module and the main board are all technician checks. The Soyal fix is to replace the outdoor control panel AP1 or the reactor — technician work inside the outdoor unit.

Do not invent an owner-level voltage test, reactor test, or board replacement for HC — no manual gives one. Do not use arlington's 'tighten the PFC module' step or its 'PFC sensor' replacement; neither exists in any manual.

When to call a technician

  • HC returns after cleaning and one restart.
  • The unit trips again as soon as it restarts, or other fault codes appear alongside HC.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or scorch marks near the outdoor unit — switch off and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

HC involves the outdoor unit's power electronics — grid voltage, the reactor, the PFC module and the main board. Never open the outdoor unit's covers or touch board terminals while investigating it, and never measure mains voltage yourself.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does HC mean on a Gree air conditioner?

PFC (Power Factor Correction) protection: the outdoor unit's PFC circuit has tripped. Verified in nine Gree manuals. The indoor unit flashes 6 times.

What causes HC?

The Mini manual names four causes: bad power-grid quality or sharply fluctuating AC input voltage; power plug / wiring board / reactor not connected reliably; dirty indoor/outdoor heat exchangers or blocked air inlets/outlets; damaged outdoor main board.

Can I fix HC myself?

Only the cleaning step: check that the indoor and outdoor heat exchangers and air inlets/outlets are clean and unblocked. Grid voltage, the reactor, the PFC module, and the main board are all technician checks. The Soyal fix is to replace the outdoor control panel AP1 or the reactor.

Is HC only a problem on big 42K/48K/60K units?

No. One popular decoder page claims that, but no manual limits HC by capacity — HC is listed for 9K-60K wall-splits and U-Match units.

Is HC the same as H5?

No. H5 is IPM protection (the IPM module itself). HC is PFC-circuit protection. The U-Match R32 manual accidentally copy-pastes H5's judgment text into its HC section — do not use that section to define HC.
