Gree Air Conditioner Error Code F2

Quick answer

Error codeF2 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansF2 on a Gree wall-split air conditioner means the indoor evaporator (indoor coil/tube) temperature sensor is open circuit or short circuit — a bad connection or a failed sensor. The flash test tells it apart from F1: F2 flashes twice every 3 seconds (STATUS/cool indicator), F1 flashes once. On U-Match and fan-coil families F2 instead means the outdoor condenser temperature sensor — read the split before you act, because the two platforms shift the F-numbers by one.
Most common causeThe indoor evaporator temperature sensor has failed, or its connection to the indoor unit's control is loose or damaged — the control gets no usable coil-temperature reading.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home checks are: confirm the flash pattern (twice every 3 seconds), confirm your unit is a wall-split and not a U-Match, and power-cycle once. Sensor, terminal and mainboard work is technician work; the outdoor unit and any refrigerant-adjacent work are strictly technician-only.

Thermistor, Bad Connection or Indoor Coil Sensor Failure); Gree One-Way Cassette Owner's Manual section 7 (F2: indoor evaporator temperature sensor open/short circuited); Gree GJC08BU-A6NRNJ2A Window Instruction Manual (F2: indoor evaporator temperature sensor malfunction); Gree ETAC3-07HC230VA Terminal Manual (F2: indoor tube sensor, STATUS flash twice every 3s); Gree UMA30HP230V1ACS U-Match Table 6.1 (split: F2 = condenser temperature sensor error)

Applies to — read this first

This page is for Gree wall-split air conditioners. The wall-split F-ladder is F1 indoor air, F2 indoor coil, F3 outdoor air, F4 outdoor coil, F5 discharge — and F2 sits second in that ladder. But Gree's U-Match and fan-coil families shift the whole ladder by one, so a U-Match owner reading a wall-split page without the split would replace the wrong sensor. Check your platform first.

Gree One-Way Cassette Owner's Manual (§7)'F2 | Indoor evaporator temperature sensor is open/short circuited.' Same ladder as NEO (F1 ambient, F2 evaporator, F3 outdoor ambient, F4 outdoor condenser, F5 outdoor discharge).

Two ranking pages present the shifted U-Match numbering as the main table with only a vague aside about wall-mounted splits — a wall-split owner following them replaces the outdoor condenser sensor for an indoor evaporator fault. One of them also states a bare 50K sensor value with no manual citation and no platform note; a wall-split indoor coil sensor checked against that value would be misdiagnosed. Trust the flash count and the platform, not the list.

What the machine does when F2 fires

The unit reports F2 and the control loses its indoor coil-temperature reading: an open or shorted sensor gives the control no usable temperature for the evaporator, which is why the unit reports the fault instead of running normally. On terminal units the STATUS (cool) indicator flashes twice every 3 seconds while F2 is active.

Common causes

  1. The indoor evaporator (coil/tube) temperature sensor itself has failed.
  2. A bad connection between the sensor and the indoor unit's control board.
  3. Damage to the sensor wiring that reads as an open or short circuit.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the flash pattern: on terminal units F2 is STATUS flashing twice every 3 seconds. One flash every 3 seconds is F1 (indoor air sensor), not F2.
  2. Confirm your platform: if your unit is a U-Match or fan-coil unit, stop — your F2 is the outdoor condenser temperature sensor, not the indoor evaporator sensor on this page.
  3. Power-cycle the unit once: switch it off at the remote and the isolator, wait a few minutes, switch it back on, and see whether F2 returns.
  4. If F2 returns, stop: the service tree runs terminal, then short, then resistance table, then mainboard — all technician work. Do not open the indoor unit's covers or test sensor circuits yourself.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. F2 has no deeper home diagnostic in any manual — flash count, platform check, power-cycle, then the technician's terminal and mainboard tree. Never open the unit's covers or touch sensor wiring.

When to call a technician

  • F2 returns after a power-cycle on a confirmed wall-split unit.
  • The indoor coil sensor circuit reads open or shorted at the terminals.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or water near electrical parts — switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

F2 involves mains-powered air-conditioner electronics: never open the indoor unit's covers or touch sensor terminals while investigating it, and never attempt refrigerant work — refrigerant handling belongs to a qualified technician. Switch the unit off at the isolator before any visible check.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F2 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

On wall-splits F2 means the indoor evaporator (indoor coil/tube) temperature sensor is open circuit or short circuit — a bad connection or a failed sensor. Three wall-split manuals (NEO09, One-Way Cassette, GJC window) agree on this meaning.

How do I tell Gree F2 from F1?

By the flash count: on terminal units F2 flashes twice every 3 seconds (STATUS/cool indicator) and F1 flashes once every 3 seconds. F1 is the indoor air (ambient) sensor; F2 is the indoor coil sensor.

My Gree unit is a U-Match — is F2 still the indoor coil sensor?

No. On U-Match and fan-coil families the F-numbers shift by one, so F2 there means the outdoor condenser temperature sensor. A U-Match owner following a wall-split page would replace the wrong sensor.

Why do some websites say Gree F2 is the condenser sensor?

Because they present the shifted U-Match numbering as their main table without naming the shift. That condenser meaning is correct for U-Match units and wrong for wall-splits — which is why this page leads with the platform split.

How do I clear Gree air conditioner F2?

Confirm the flash pattern and your platform, then power-cycle once. If F2 returns, the sensor circuit fault is genuine and the service tree (terminal, short, resistance table, mainboard) belongs to a technician.
