Gree Air Conditioner Error Code P8

Quick answer

Error codeP8 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansP8 = module high temperature protection (the IPM module is actually too hot). Blink pattern: OFF 3S and blink 19 times. Thresholds: compressor frequency drops at module temperature 80 C or higher; the unit stops at 95 C or higher; it resumes when the module temperature falls below 188.6 F (86 C) and the compressor has been stopped for 3 minutes. P8 is NOT the same as P7.
Most common causeThe IPM module in the outdoor unit overheated - radiating grease not enough, screws not fixed tightly, or hardware malfunction of the outdoor main board (Livo Gen4 display-table row 33).
Safe to check yourself?Switch the complete unit off and leave it de-energized for 20 minutes so the module cools, then restart. If P8 comes back, the thermal grease / radiator / AP1 checks are technician work.
Checked againstGree Change 18/24 Service Manual (section 9.3 row 34) + Gree Livo Gen4 Service Manual (section 9.1 rows 30 + 33) + Gree GWH18QD-K3DNA1G / GWH24QE-K3DNA1G Service Manual (section 9.1) + GREE evo Split AC Owner's Manual (Malfunction Tab) + Gree GWC18MC-D3DNA3F/I Service Manual (section 5(8))

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because P8 is routinely conflated with P7: P8 = the module is actually too hot (real over-temperature); P7 = the module temperature SENSOR CIRCUIT is faulty (open/short). Different faults: P8 -> 20-min de-energize, thermal-grease/radiator check, then AP1; P7 -> replace outdoor AP1. VRF wording ("Drive IPM high temperature protection of compressor", GMV manuals) is the same concept; Versati heat-pump P8 is the same concept on a different appliance - note in one line each, do not borrow their thresholds.

What the machine does when P8 fires

The display shows P8 and the module is too hot: at module temperature 80 C or higher the compressor frequency drops or stops increasing; at 95 C or higher the unit stops for module overheating protection. It resumes when the module temperature falls below 188.6 F (86 C) and the compressor has been stopped for 3 minutes. Blink pattern: OFF 3 seconds, then blink 19 times. In cooling/drying the compressor stops while the indoor fan runs; in heating the whole unit stops.

Common causes

  1. Module high temperature protection - the IPM module in the outdoor unit is too hot (the manuals' own fault definition).
  2. Radiating grease not enough on the IPM module of outdoor control panel AP1 (Livo Gen4 row 33).
  3. Radiator not inserted tightly / screws not fixed tightly (Livo Gen4 row 33; Change 18/24 cause column).
  4. Hardware malfunction of the outdoor main board (Livo Gen4 row 33) - technician diagnosis.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the complete unit off and leave it de-energized for 20 minutes so the module cools, then restart - the manual's own first step.
  2. If P8 comes back, a technician needs to check the thermal grease on the IPM module, whether the radiator is inserted tightly, and the outdoor control panel AP1 - quoted verbatim: "After the complete unit is de-energized for 20min, check whether the thermal grease on IPM module of outdoor control panel AP1 is sufficient and whether the radiator is inserted tightly. If the problem cannot be solved, please replace the control panel AP1."
  3. Do not invent a "reduce load on IPM" step (aysonline) or a sensor check (arlington) - neither is in any manual.

The steps above are the manuals' own fix sequence, in order. This page does not invent a "reduce load on IPM" step or a sensor check - neither is in any manual for P8.

When to call a technician

  • P8 returns after a 20-minute de-energize and restart - the thermal grease, radiator, and AP1 need a technician.
  • Any burning smell, visibly damaged wiring, or other fault codes alongside P8 - switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

P8 involves mains-powered electronics in the outdoor unit: never open the outdoor unit or touch the IPM module, thermal grease, or control panel AP1 yourself. The grease/radiator/AP1 checks are technician work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does P8 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

Module high temperature protection - the IPM module in the outdoor unit is too hot. Verified in five Gree manuals (Change 18/24 section 9.3, Livo Gen4 section 9.1, GWH18QD section 9.1, evo Malfunction Tab, GWC18MC section 5(8)).

Is P8 the same as P7?

No. P8 = the module is actually too hot; P7 = the module temperature sensor CIRCUIT is faulty (open/short). P8 is fixed by cooling the module and checking thermal grease / the radiator; P7 is fixed by replacing outdoor control panel AP1.

How do I reset a Gree P8 error?

Switch the complete unit off and leave it de-energized for 20 minutes so the module cools, then restart. If P8 comes back, a technician needs to check the thermal grease on the IPM module, whether the radiator is inserted tightly, and the outdoor control panel AP1.

At what temperature does P8 trigger?

Per the GWC18MC service manual section 5(8): the compressor frequency drops at module temperature 80 C or higher, and the unit stops at 95 C or higher. It resumes when the module temperature falls below 188.6 F (86 C) and the compressor has stopped for 3 minutes.

What is the blink pattern for P8?

OFF 3 seconds, then blink 19 times (Change 18/24 section 9.3, evo Malfunction Tab). In cooling/drying the compressor stops while the indoor fan runs; in heating the whole unit stops.
