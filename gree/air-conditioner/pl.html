Gree Air Conditioner Error Code PL

Quick answer

Error codePL — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansPL = voltage of DC bus-bar is too low (low PN voltage protection). The DC bus voltage has fallen too low, so the unit stops the compressor (indoor fan keeps running in cooling/drying; the whole unit stops in heating). Blink pattern: OFF 3S and blink 21 times. The manual rule is L-N below 150VAC - NOT the 185VAC figure other sites quote.
Most common causeLow incoming supply voltage (L-N below 150VAC on the wiring board), or a fault in the outdoor control panel AP1 circuit (electrolytic capacitor C / control board).
Safe to check yourself?Partly - check whether the house supply itself is low and wait for it to return to normal before restarting. The capacitor-C voltage check and any board replacement are technician work (live-voltage hazard).
Checked againstGree Change 18/24 Service Manual, section 9.3 row 36 + Gree Mini After-sale Service Manual, Error Code List + Gree Livo+ Service Manual, section 9.1 p.40 + Gree Rooftop Packaged Service Manual, section 5.1 + section 1.2

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because Gree PL is one fault with one meaning across four manuals - but the number other sites quote for it is wrong. PL = voltage of DC bus-bar is too low (low PN voltage protection), blink 21 in the Change 18/24, Mini After-sale and Livo+ manuals, and the Rooftop Packaged manual agrees. The manual rule is: measure the voltage between L and N on the wiring board (XT) - if it is LOWER than 150VAC, wait until the power voltage is increased to the normal range, then turn the unit on. One ranking site says PL triggers below 185VAC - that figure is the Versati heat-pump input range (185-264VAC), the wrong appliance, and no Gree wall-split manual says it.

What the machine does when PL fires

During cooling and drying operation the compressor stops while the indoor fan keeps running; during heating operation the complete unit stops. The display pattern is OFF 3 seconds, then blink 21 times.

Common causes

  1. Low incoming supply voltage - the L-N voltage on the wiring board (XT) is below 150VAC, so the DC bus cannot hold its voltage.
  2. Outdoor control panel AP1 circuit fault - if the AC input is normal but PL persists, the electrolytic capacitor C voltage check points to the control panel circuit.
  3. Rooftop packaged units: input voltage below 90% of rated - the Rooftop manual's own trigger.
  4. Wrong-appliance reading - the 185VAC figure is the Versati heat-pump input range (185-264VAC), not a Gree wall-split threshold; JustAnswer's AI blurb wrongly calls PL a communication error (that is E6).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Check whether the house supply itself is low - if other appliances are also affected, wait for the supply to return to its normal range before restarting the unit.
  2. Measure the voltage between L and N on the wiring board (XT). If it is lower than 150VAC, wait until the power voltage is increased to the normal range, then turn the unit on (Mini manual, clean text - the Change 18/24 Scribd text layer wrongly renders this as 'higher than 150VAC').
  3. If the AC input is normal, a technician measures the voltage of the electrolytic capacitor C on the outdoor control panel (AP1). If that voltage is normal, the circuit is malfunctioning - replace the control panel AP1 (Mini manual: replace the outdoor control board).
  4. Rooftop packaged units: if the input voltage is below 90% of rated, adjust it to rated voltage; if the voltage is normal, the compressor drive board may be operating improperly and needs replacement.
  5. Do not use the 185VAC trigger other sites quote, do not inspect the compressor for PL (no manual lists the compressor), and do not treat the JustAnswer 110-125V / 210-245V figures as manual steps - they are a forum answer, not a manual quote.

The 150VAC threshold is identical in the Change 18/24 and Mini manuals; only the Scribd OCR wording differs. The capacitor-C voltage check and any board replacement are live-voltage technician work - not homeowner steps.

When to call a technician

  • PL returns with a normal supply voltage - the electrolytic capacitor C voltage and the outdoor control panel AP1 need a technician.
  • The supply voltage is low across the house - an electrician should check the incoming supply before the unit is run again.
  • Any burning smell, visibly damaged wiring, or other fault codes alongside PL - switch off and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

PL involves mains-voltage measurement at the wiring board (XT) and the outdoor control panel: never measure live L-N voltage or open the outdoor unit yourself. Voltage checks are technician work with the proper meter and qualifications.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does PL mean on a Gree air conditioner?

Voltage of DC bus-bar is too low - low PN voltage protection. The DC bus voltage has fallen too low, so the unit stops the compressor to protect itself. Verified in three Gree manuals (Change 18/24 section 9.3, Mini After-sale Error Code List, Livo+ section 9.1).

How do I reset a Gree PL error?

First check whether the house supply itself is low. If the L-N voltage at the wiring board is below 150VAC, wait until the supply returns to its normal range, then turn the unit back on. If the supply is normal and PL comes back, a technician needs to check the electrolytic capacitor C voltage on the outdoor control panel AP1 - if that is normal, the control panel needs replacing.

What is the blink pattern for PL?

OFF 3 seconds, then blink 21 times (Change 18/24 section 9.3, Mini After-sale manual, Livo+ section 9.1). In cooling/drying the compressor stops while the indoor fan runs; in heating the whole unit stops.

Is PL the same as PH or U3?

No. PH = DC bus voltage TOO HIGH (blink 11, above 265VAC). U3 = a DC bus-bar voltage-DROP event (blink 20, 'supply voltage is unstable'). PL is the low-voltage protection state - the bus voltage itself is too low.

Does PL mean the compressor is faulty?

No - no manual lists the compressor for PL. It is a supply-voltage / DC-bus problem: low incoming voltage, or a fault in the outdoor control panel AP1 circuit. Do not replace the compressor for PL.
