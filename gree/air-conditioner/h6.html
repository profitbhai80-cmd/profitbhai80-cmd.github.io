Gree Air Conditioner Error Code H6

Quick answer

Error codeH6 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansH6 means no feedback from the indoor fan motor: the control board is not receiving the signal that tells it the indoor fan is running. The GWH18TC service manual gives the mechanism — if the indoor fan motor keeps a low rotation speed for a continuous period after startup, the unit stops operation and displays H6. The split manual's cause pair is 'fan motor or board error', with a connector check.
Most common causeThe indoor fan motor is not sending a feedback signal — blocked, failed, or disconnected — so the board stops the unit and shows H6.
Safe to check yourself?Partially. You can safely power the unit off, check that nothing is blocking the indoor fan, and restart it once — the GEN3 manual's own step. Anything involving the fan motor connector, motor replacement, or the control board is technician work and involves covers and mains wiring.

Applies to — read this first

Good news for diagnosis: within the Gree mini-split families we checked, H6 is consistent — it is the indoor fan motor giving no feedback to the control board. What you must NOT do is blur it with the U-Match wording: U-Match manuals phrase H6 broadly as 'DC fan error', while wall-split and duct manuals phrase it specifically as no feedback from the indoor fan motor. This page leads with the wall-split meaning and carries the U-Match variant as a note. H6 is also not E8: on wall splits E8 is anti-high-temperature protection, while on U-Match E8 is the indoor fan error — the two families swap those labels, and H6 must not be confused with either.

H6 shows on the indoor unit's display. The Gree Split AC User Manual's power-indicator pattern for H6 is 11 flashes every 3 seconds. The GWH18TC service manual gives the mechanism: if the indoor fan motor keeps a low rotation speed for a continuous period after startup, the unit stops operation and displays H6. On Gree commercial/VRF lines the code map can shift; check your model's own manual.

What the machine does when H6 fires

The unit stops operation and displays H6 (GWH18TC service manual). The indoor fan is not running normally — it may be stopped, crawling at low speed, or spinning without the board registering it. The unit will not resume normal operation while the feedback signal is missing.

Common causes

  1. Indoor fan is blocked or obstructed, so it cannot spin up to speed.
  2. Indoor fan motor has failed — the board gets no feedback signal from the motor.
  3. The fan motor connector is loose or not properly connected — the split manual lists the connector check alongside motor and board faults.
  4. The control board is faulty and cannot read the fan feedback signal.
  5. The fan motor holds a low rotation speed continuously after startup, triggering the locked-protection stop (GWH18TC service manual).

Safe checks, in order

  1. Power the unit off. H6 means the indoor fan is not running normally — do not keep running the unit with a dead indoor fan.
  2. Without removing any covers, check that nothing is blocking the indoor fan or its outlet — dust buildup, a foreign object, or a closed louvre can stall it.
  3. Try restarting the unit — the Vireo GEN3 owner's manual's own guidance for the H6 group: 'Try restarting the unit. If the problem persists, contact a qualified HVAC technician.'
  4. If H6 returns, stop: the remaining causes are a loose fan-motor connector, a failed fan motor, or a faulty control board. All three need the unit opened and tested by a qualified HVAC technician.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. The connector, motor, and board steps in the split manual are technician steps — the manual lists them as the repair path, not as homeowner checks.

When to call a technician

  • H6 returns on the first restart after the blockage check.
  • The indoor fan does not spin, spins only slowly, or makes grinding, buzzing, or clicking noises.
  • The fan spins but H6 still shows — the feedback signal itself is missing (connector, motor sensor, or board).
  • Any burning smell, sparking, or visibly damaged wiring at the indoor unit.

Safety first

Mini-split indoor and outdoor units contain mains-voltage wiring and capacitors that hold charge after switch-off — never remove electrical covers yourself to reach the fan motor, its connector, or the board. The split manual's connector and motor/board steps are technician steps, not homeowner steps. The Gree manual's own wording stands: 'The air conditioner is not expected to be serviced by users. Incorrect repair may cause electric shock or fire, so please contact an authorized service center for professional service.'

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does H6 mean on a Gree mini split?

H6 means no feedback from the indoor fan motor: the control board is not receiving the feedback signal that tells it the indoor fan is running. The R32 Slim Duct manual phrases it exactly that way ('No feedback of indoor fan motor'), and the GWH18TC service manual explains the mechanism — if the fan motor holds a low rotation speed continuously after startup, the unit stops and displays H6.

How do I reset Gree mini split H6?

Try restarting the unit — that is the Gree Vireo GEN3 owner's manual's own guidance for the H6 group ('Try restarting the unit. If the problem persists, contact a qualified HVAC technician'). Before restarting, power off and check nothing is blocking the indoor fan. If H6 returns, the motor, its connector, or the control board needs a technician.

Is Gree H6 an indoor fan fault or an outdoor fan fault?

On wall-split and duct mini-splits H6 is specifically the indoor fan motor (R32 Slim Duct manual). U-Match manuals phrase H6 more broadly as 'DC fan error'. One ranking page hedges 'indoor or outdoor, depending on the model' without naming the split — on a wall-split indoor display, treat H6 as the indoor fan.

Is Gree H6 the same as E8?

No. On wall splits E8 is anti-high-temperature protection and H6 is the fan fault. On U-Match systems E8 is the indoor fan error instead — the families use different labels for the fan fault. Match the code to your own model's manual; do not borrow the meaning across families.
