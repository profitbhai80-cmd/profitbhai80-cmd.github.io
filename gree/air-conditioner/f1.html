Gree Air Conditioner Error Code F1

Quick answer

Error codeF1 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansF1 on a Gree wall-split means the indoor ambient (room) temperature sensor is open circuit or short circuit: the sensor that measures room air at the indoor unit has failed or lost its connection. The Gree manuals name the causes as a bad connection, a faulty sensor, a short from tripped parts, or a faulty mainboard, and the service manual's tree checks the terminal, then shorts, then sensor resistance, then the mainboard. Power-cycle once; if F1 returns, the room sensor circuit needs a qualified technician.
Most common causeThe indoor ambient (room) temperature sensor has gone open or short circuit, or its connection to the indoor controller is loose — the control can no longer read room temperature.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home check is a single power-cycle: switch the unit off, wait, and switch it back on — if F1 clears it was a transient detection glitch. Anything beyond that (checking the sensor's terminal, testing for shorts, measuring resistance, or testing the mainboard) is technician work under the service manual's own tree; never open the indoor unit's electrical covers yourself.

Applies to — read this first

This page is for Gree WALL-SPLIT units only. On wall-splits, F1 is always the indoor ambient (room) temperature sensor, open or short circuit. The platform split matters more here than on any other Gree code: on Gree U-Match and fan-coil (FCU) families the F-numbers shift by one, so there F1 means the evaporator tube sensor instead. Check which family you own before deciding which sensor is at fault — the wall-split test below (cool/STATUS indicator flashing once every 3 seconds) is how you confirm you are on wall-split numbering.

The service manual's F1/F2 tree is a technician tree: check the sensor–controller terminal for looseness or poor contact, check for a short from tripped parts, check the sensor against the resistance table, then the mainboard. Those steps are quoted here only so you know what the technician will test — opening the indoor unit's electronics is not a home check.

What the machine does when F1 fires

The indoor unit displays F1 — on lamp models the cool (or STATUS) indicator flashes once every 3 seconds. With its room-temperature reading gone, the control cannot regulate the room to the setpoint. The manuals describe the display and the sensor fault, not a separate shutdown sequence; do not expect the unit to behave like a protection code (E1/E5/H3) — the symptom is lost temperature control, not a pressure or compressor event.

Common causes

  1. A bad connection — the indoor ambient sensor's wiring terminal to the controller loosened or poorly contacted, the first check in the Gree service manual's F1/F2 tree.
  2. A faulty indoor ambient sensor itself — the room-temperature thermistor gone open circuit or short circuit.
  3. A short circuit caused by tripped (dislodged) parts inside the indoor unit — the service manual's second check.
  4. A faulty mainboard — the service manual's final verdict when the terminal is tight, no short exists, and the sensor still fails the resistance table.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off, wait a minute, and switch it back on. If F1 clears, it was a transient detection glitch — keep an eye on it, because a repeat points to the room sensor circuit.
  2. Note exactly what the display shows: F1 on a display model, or the cool/STATUS indicator flashing once every 3 seconds on a lamp model. If it flashes twice, you have F2 (indoor coil sensor), not F1.
  3. Confirm you own a wall-split, not a U-Match or fan-coil unit: if yours is a U-Match/FCU unit, stop here — on those families F1 is the evaporator tube sensor and this page's sensor assignment does not apply.
  4. If F1 returns after the restart on a wall-split, stop: the service manual's next steps are checking the sensor terminal's tightness, checking for a short from tripped parts, then measuring the sensor against the resistance table — all of which mean opening the indoor unit and handling its electronics. That is technician work. If the terminal is tight and no short exists but the sensor fails the table, the sensor gets replaced with the same model; if the sensor passes, the manual condemns the mainboard.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. F1 has no deeper home diagnostic in any manual — power-cycle, identify the flash pattern, confirm the platform, then the technician's terminal/short/resistance/mainboard tree. Never open the indoor unit's electrical covers or test a live board.

When to call a technician

  • F1 returns after a power-cycle — the room sensor circuit fault is genuine, not a glitch.
  • The sensor terminal is loose or poorly contacted, or a part has tripped and shorted — the service manual's first two checks.
  • The sensor fails the resistance table — faulty sensor, replaced with the same model per the manual.
  • Terminal tight, no short, sensor passes the table, but F1 persists — the service manual condemns the mainboard.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or scorching near the indoor unit — switch off immediately and call a qualified technician.

Safety first

F1 involves mains-powered indoor electronics: never open the indoor unit's electrical covers or touch sensor or mainboard terminals while investigating it. Switch the power off before any visible check, and never work on a live board. The terminal, short-circuit, resistance and mainboard verdicts in the service manual belong to a qualified technician. If F1 persists after a power-cycle, switch the unit off and call a qualified technician — never attempt refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F1 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

On a Gree wall-split, F1 means the indoor ambient (room) temperature sensor is open circuit or short circuit: the sensor that measures room air at the indoor unit has failed or lost its connection. The Gree Split Air Conditioner User Manual lists it as 'F1 (Cool indicator flashes once every 3 sec.): Indoor temperature sensor error', the ETAC3 terminal manual as indoor ambient sensor open/short with the STATUS indicator flashing once every 3 seconds, and the NEO09 manual as 'Indoor Air Temp. Thermistor | F1 | Bad Connection, or Indoor Air Sensor Failure'.

Is Gree F1 the evaporator coil sensor?

Not on a wall-split — that is F2 on wall-splits. F1 = indoor ambient sensor, F2 = indoor (evaporator) tube sensor, F3 = outdoor ambient sensor, F4 = outdoor tube sensor, F5 = discharge sensor. The confusion comes from Gree U-Match / fan-coil families, where the numbers shift by one and F1 is the evaporator tube sensor. If your unit is a U-Match or fan-coil unit, do not use this page's sensor assignment.

How do I confirm F1 on a lamp-only model?

On wall-splits the cool indicator flashes once every 3 seconds for F1 (ETAC3: STATUS indicator flashes once and off 3 seconds, circularly). F2 flashes twice in the same pattern. If your indicator flashes twice, you have F2, not F1 — different sensor, different part.

How do I clear Gree F1?

Power-cycle the unit once — if F1 was a transient detection glitch it clears. If it returns, the fault is genuine: the service manual's tree is terminal tightness, then short-circuit check, then sensor resistance table, then mainboard. All of those steps involve the indoor unit's electronics and are technician work; there is no deeper home fix.

Why can't my air conditioner control the room temperature with F1?

Because the control has lost its room-temperature reading: the indoor ambient sensor is what tells the unit how warm the room actually is. With F1 the unit cannot regulate to your setpoint — it is flying blind on room temperature, which is why the fault needs a technician rather than a setting change.
