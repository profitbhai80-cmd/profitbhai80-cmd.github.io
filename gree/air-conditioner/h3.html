Gree Air Conditioner Error Code H3

Quick answer

Error codeH3 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansH3 means compressor overload protection has tripped on Gree mini-split families: the compressor drew too much current or ran too hot, and the protection cut in. On Gree VRF platforms (GMV series) H3 means something different — fan drive module reset protection — so identifying your platform is the first step.
Most common causeLow refrigerant charge, a blocked capillary, a failing compressor motor — or a loose OVC-COMP wiring terminal (per the Livo and ceiling-cassette manuals).
Safe to check yourself?Only the manual's own restart and airflow checks. Anything electrical or refrigerant-related is technician work — and repeated resetting against the protection is forbidden.
Checked againstGree NEO series Instruction Manual (NEO09AC115V1A, Cat DFS-NEO-CO-2IN, 2014); Gree Livo series LIVS18HP230V1BH manual; Gree Ceiling Cassette Owner's Manual; Gree ME33-42/G Dry Contact & 24 Volt Adaptor Owner's Manual; Gree U-Match UMAT30HP230V1AC User Manual; Gree GMV6 Mini DC Inverter VRF Service Manual & Troubleshooting Guide

Applies to — read this first

H3 is one of Gree's most mis-explained codes because the code map shifts between platforms. On wall-split mini-split families (NEO, Livo, ceiling cassette, U-Match) H3 is compressor overload protection — consistent across all of them. But on Gree's VRF platforms (GMV5E, GMV6) H3 is the fan drive module's reset protection, and on the R32 Slim Duct family H3 does not exist at all. Check your model family on the rating label before reading any meaning: a GMV unit diagnosed with the mini-split meaning would send a technician chasing the wrong fault.

If this does not correct the problem contact a qualified HVAC technician") without defining a meaning — the family table above gives the defined meanings.

What the machine does when H3 fires

When the overload protection trips, the compressor stops and the indoor unit displays H3. On Livo and ceiling-cassette families you can confirm the fault at the outdoor unit: its indicator flashes 8 times, then stays off for 1 second, repeating. The unit will not restart the compressor until the protection resets — repeated trips mean the underlying cause is still present.

Common causes

  1. Low refrigerant charge — the NEO manual's first listed cause.
  2. A blocked capillary or restricted refrigerant circuit — the NEO manual's second cause.
  3. A failing compressor motor — the NEO manual's third cause.
  4. A loose wiring terminal OVC-COMP between the outdoor drive board and the compressor — the Livo and ceiling-cassette manuals' specific electrical cause.
  5. Extreme outdoor heat or blocked airflow around the outdoor unit, making the compressor work harder — check airflow as a first step.
  6. On GMV VRF platforms only: a fan drive board fault — the family-shift meaning, not compressor overload.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off and wait at least 30 minutes for the compressor to cool — the portable-AC manual uses this exact wait, and it matches the protection-reset logic generally.
  2. Clear any blockage around the outdoor unit: leaves, covers, or objects against the condenser. Make sure air can flow freely around both units.
  3. On a very hot day (above about 95°F / 35°C), wait for the temperature to drop before restarting — heat alone can trip the overload.
  4. Restart once. If H3 returns, stop: the remaining causes — refrigerant charge, capillary blockage, compressor motor, OVC-COMP terminal — all need gauges, electrical tests, or the unit opened, which the manuals reserve for qualified HVAC technicians.

Work through the checks in order and give the unit only one restart after the checks. Repeatedly resetting against a compressor protection trip can damage the compressor — that is why the manuals' own boundary is one restart, then a technician. If a check needs a cover removed, a terminal touched, or refrigerant handled, it is no longer a safe home check — skip to the technician section.

When to call a technician

  • H3 returns after one restart and the cool-down wait — the cause is in the refrigerant circuit, the compressor, or the wiring.
  • You can confirm 8 flashes / 1 sec off at the outdoor unit — note this for the technician; it confirms the overload trip.
  • Any burning smell, buzzing, or the compressor failing to start at all.
  • Refrigerant work (charge check, leak repair), capillary or compressor electrical tests, and OVC-COMP terminal work are licensed-technician-only (US EPA 608 / UK F-Gas rules apply).

Safety first

Compressor overload protection exists to prevent compressor damage and electrical faults — never bypass it and never reset the unit repeatedly to force it to run. Switch the unit off between checks and leave electrical covers closed. Refrigerant and compressor electrical work needs a certified technician; the outdoor unit carries mains voltage and the compressor circuit stores charge. If the overload keeps tripping, keep the unit off and call a qualified HVAC technician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does H3 mean on a Gree mini split?

H3 is compressor overload protection: the compressor drew too much current or overheated and the protection tripped. The NEO manual lists the causes as low refrigerant charge, blocked capillary, or compressor motor failure; Livo and ceiling-cassette manuals add a loose OVC-COMP wiring terminal. You can confirm it at the outdoor unit — 8 flashes, 1 second off.

Is H3 a sensor fault or defrost on Gree?

No. Sensor faults are F-codes and defrost status is H1 on Gree. AI-generated blocks on two JustAnswer threads wrongly call H3 a sensor or defrost issue — the Gree manuals define H3 as compressor overload protection on mini-splits.

Why does my Gree GMV unit's H3 mean something different?

Gree's VRF platforms use a different code map: on GMV5E/GMV6, H3 is the fan drive module's reset protection, not compressor overload. This family shift is exactly why our page carries a per-family table — generic lists that map H3 to compressor overload for all Gree units will misdiagnose a GMV system.

How many times can I reset Gree H3?

Once, after the airflow and cool-down checks. The manuals' own boundary is one restart — then a technician. Repeatedly resetting against a compressor overload trip risks destroying the compressor.

Does H3 mean the outdoor unit is overheating?

Heat can be a trigger — high outdoor temperature or blocked airflow makes the compressor work harder — but H3 is the compressor overload protection, and the manual's causes include low refrigerant charge, blocked capillary, compressor motor failure, and a loose OVC-COMP terminal. Cleaning the coils alone cannot fix a low charge or a loose terminal.
