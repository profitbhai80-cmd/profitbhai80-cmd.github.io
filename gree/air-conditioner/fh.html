Gree Air Conditioner Error Code FH

Quick answer

Error codeFH — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansFH on a Gree wall-split air conditioner means limit or decrease frequency due to antifreezing: the unit keeps running, but the compressor runs at reduced frequency because of antifreezing protection. It is a frequency-limit state, not a sensor fault and not a stop fault. The manuals name two causes: poor air-return in the indoor unit, or fan speed too low. On GMV VRF platforms FH means something entirely different (an abnormal current sensor of compressor 1) and is out of scope here.
Most common causePoor air-return in the indoor unit, or indoor fan speed too low, so the control limits compressor frequency to protect against freezing.
Safe to check yourself?Yes, for the checks on this page: confirm the code is FH and your unit is a wall-split, clear blocked indoor airflow, check the indoor fan is running normally, clean the indoor filters, and restart once. Anything involving refrigerant, sensors, or the outdoor electrical box is technician work.
Checked againstGree GWH Series Service Manual, display table entry 20 (FH: limit/decrease frequency due to antifreezing, all loads normal, frequency decreased, outdoor blink OFF 3 seconds / blink 2 times); Gree Soyal Service Manual, same display table entry 20; GREE Inverter AC Service Manual (Bora inverter), same display table entry 20

Applies to — read this first

FH belongs to the frequency-limit family, and that family breaks the pattern most lists teach you: not every F code is a sensor, and not every protection stops the unit. F6, F8, F9 and FH are limits: the unit deliberately slows the compressor and keeps running. If you go hunting for a sensor on an FH unit, you are chasing a part that has not failed.

The ranking pages fail FH in three ways: some give the correct label and nothing else (no causes, no blink pattern, no behaviour); one invents a faulty sensor cause the manuals never name; others spin a generic freeze narrative about leaks or valve settings that contradicts the manuals. The keeps-running-slower behaviour, the blink pattern, and the entry number are what this page adds.

What the machine does when FH fires

The unit keeps running with all loads operating normally, but the compressor's operating frequency is decreased. That is the protection working as designed: faced with an antifreezing condition, the control slows the compressor instead of stopping the unit or declaring a sensor fault. The outdoor unit signals FH with its blink pattern (OFF 3 seconds / blink 2 times on the GWH display method).

Common causes

  1. Poor air-return in the indoor unit, so the indoor coil runs cold enough to trigger the antifreezing limit.
  2. Indoor fan speed too low, so air movement across the indoor coil is insufficient and the limit engages.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the code and the platform: FH on a wall-split unit is this page. If your unit is a GMV VRF unit, stop: its FH is a compressor-1 current-sensor fault, a different diagnosis.
  2. Clear blocked indoor airflow and clean the indoor filters: restricted air-return is the home-checkable contributor the manuals name.
  3. Check the indoor fan is running normally at its set speed, then restart the unit once and watch whether it settles back to normal running. FH is a limit state, not a latched fault: if the antifreezing condition passes, normal frequency returns.
  4. If FH keeps returning, or the unit stops cooling, makes unusual noises, or shows other fault codes alongside FH, stop: refrigerant charge, sensor circuits and outdoor-board work belong to a qualified technician. Do not open the outdoor unit yourself.

FH has no sensor to replace and no component the manuals name for the user to change: the home scope is indoor airflow, filters, fan speed, and one restart. Never open the outdoor unit's covers or touch sensor wiring.

When to call a technician

  • FH returns repeatedly after airflow and filter checks and one restart.
  • The unit stops cooling, runs excessively cold at the indoor coil, or shows other fault codes alongside FH.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or refrigerant smell near the outdoor unit — switch off and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

FH involves mains-powered air-conditioner electronics and an outdoor unit: never open the outdoor unit's covers or touch sensor terminals while investigating it, and never attempt refrigerant work — refrigerant handling belongs to a qualified technician. Switch the unit off at the isolator before any visible check.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does FH mean on a Gree air conditioner?

On wall-splits FH means limit / decrease frequency due to antifreezing: all loads keep operating normally while the compressor's operating frequency is decreased. Three wall-split-family manuals agree on this row, entry 20 of the GWH display table.

Is Gree FH a sensor fault?

No. FH is a frequency-limit protection, not a sensor open/short fault. The manuals name poor indoor air-return and low fan speed as the causes, not a faulty sensor.

Does the unit stop when FH shows?

No. That is the point of a frequency limit: everything keeps running at reduced compressor frequency. The stop fault in this family is different: E2 is the antifreezing protection stop.

Is FH the same on VRF units?

No. On GMV VRF platforms FH means an abnormal current sensor of compressor 1. This page is wall-split only; a VRF owner must not follow the wall-split limit diagnosis.

How do I clear Gree air conditioner FH?

Confirm the platform, clear indoor airflow, clean the indoor filters, check the indoor fan speed, and restart once. If FH keeps returning, the antifreezing condition is genuine and belongs to a technician.
