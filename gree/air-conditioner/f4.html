Gree Air Conditioner Error Code F4

Quick answer

Error codeF4 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansF4 on a Gree wall-split air conditioner means the outdoor condenser (outdoor coil/tube) temperature sensor is open circuit or short circuit — a bad connection or a failed sensor. On U-Match families F4 instead means the compressor discharge temperature sensor — read the split before you act, because the two platforms shift the F-numbers and the wrong sensor means outdoor-unit work for the wrong fault.
Most common causeThe outdoor condenser temperature sensor has failed, or its connection to the outdoor unit's control is loose or damaged — the control gets no usable condenser-temperature reading.
Safe to check yourself?Partly. The safe home checks are: confirm your unit is a wall-split and not a U-Match, and power-cycle once. The outdoor unit, its sensor circuit and any refrigerant-adjacent work are strictly technician-only — never open the outdoor unit yourself.
Checked againstGree NEO09AC115V1A Neo Ductless Instruction Manual (F4: Outdoor Coil Temp. Thermistor, Bad Connection or Outdoor Coil Sensor Failure); Gree One-Way Cassette Owner's Manual section 7 (F4: outdoor condenser temperature sensor open/short circuited); Gree GJC08BU-A6NRNJ2A Window Instruction Manual (F4: outdoor condenser temperature sensor malfunction); Gree UMA30HP230V1ACS U-Match Table 6.1 (split: F4 = discharge temperature sensor error)

Applies to — read this first

This page is for Gree wall-split air conditioners, where F4 is the outdoor condenser sensor — fourth in the wall-split ladder (F1 indoor air, F2 indoor coil, F3 outdoor air, F4 outdoor coil, F5 discharge). U-Match families shift the ladder, so their F4 is the discharge sensor instead. The two ladders side by side are the page's core value: the shift is visible, not just asserted.

Wall-split ladder vs U-Match ladderWall-split: F1 indoor air, F2 indoor coil, F3 outdoor air, F4 outdoor coil, F5 discharge. U-Match (shifted): F2 condenser, F4 discharge, F5 wired-controller sensor. Read your platform's ladder, not just the code letter.

Two ranking pages present the U-Match F4 (discharge sensor) as universal, with no warning that it is only right for U-Match and fan-coil units — a wall-split owner following them tests the discharge sensor for a condenser fault. One of them also states a bare 25K value with no manual citation and no temperature; bare resistance figures without a manual's table are not a diagnosis.

What the machine does when F4 fires

The unit reports F4 and the control loses its outdoor condenser-temperature reading: an open or shorted sensor gives the control no usable temperature for the outdoor coil, which is why the unit reports the fault instead of running normally. The condenser sensor lives in the outdoor unit, so everything past the code reading is outdoor-unit work.

Common causes

  1. The outdoor condenser (coil/tube) temperature sensor itself has failed.
  2. A bad connection between the sensor and the outdoor unit's control board.
  3. Damage to the sensor wiring that reads as an open or short circuit.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm your platform: if your unit is a U-Match or fan-coil unit, stop — your F4 is the compressor discharge temperature sensor, not the outdoor condenser sensor on this page.
  2. Power-cycle the unit once: switch it off at the remote and the isolator, wait a few minutes, switch it back on, and see whether F4 returns.
  3. If F4 returns, stop: the sensor lives in the outdoor unit and its circuit checks — plus any refrigerant-adjacent work — belong to a qualified technician. Do not open the outdoor unit yourself.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. F4 has no deeper home diagnostic in any manual — platform check, power-cycle, then the technician. Never open the outdoor unit's covers or touch sensor wiring.

When to call a technician

  • F4 returns after a power-cycle on a confirmed wall-split unit.
  • The outdoor condenser sensor circuit reads open or shorted at the terminals.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or refrigerant smell near the outdoor unit — switch off and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

F4 involves mains-powered air-conditioner electronics and an outdoor unit: never open the outdoor unit's covers or touch sensor terminals while investigating it, and never attempt refrigerant work — refrigerant handling belongs to a qualified technician. Switch the unit off at the isolator before any visible check.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does F4 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

On wall-splits F4 means the outdoor condenser (outdoor coil/tube) temperature sensor is open circuit or short circuit — a bad connection or a failed sensor. Three wall-split manuals (NEO09, One-Way Cassette, GJC window) agree on this meaning.

What is the difference between Gree F4 and F5?

On wall-splits F4 is the outdoor condenser sensor and F5 is the compressor discharge sensor — two different sensors in the outdoor unit. On U-Match units the ladder shifts, so F4 there is the discharge sensor and F5 is the wired-controller sensor.

My Gree unit is a U-Match — is F4 still the condenser sensor?

No. On U-Match families F4 means the compressor discharge temperature sensor. A U-Match owner following a wall-split page would test the discharge sensor for a condenser fault, and vice versa.

Why do some websites say Gree F4 is the discharge sensor?

Because they present the U-Match numbering as their main table without naming the shift. That discharge meaning is correct for U-Match units and wrong for wall-splits.

How do I clear Gree air conditioner F4?

Confirm your platform, then power-cycle once. If F4 returns, the outdoor sensor circuit fault is genuine and belongs to a technician — the sensor lives in the outdoor unit.
