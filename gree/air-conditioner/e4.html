Gree Air Conditioner Error Code E4

Quick answer

Error codeE4 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansE4 means compressor high discharge temperature protection: the compressor discharge temperature has risen past the safe limit and the unit has stopped to protect the compressor. The duct service manual measures the trip at over 118°C and lists seven possible reasons, from an unopened outdoor cutoff valve to insufficient refrigerant charge.
Most common causeThe compressor discharge temperature has risen past the protection limit (over 118°C per the duct service manual) — most often a fan, airflow, charge, or valve problem that only a technician can separate.
Safe to check yourself?Partially. You can safely switch the unit off, let it cool, clean or replace the indoor filters, and clear debris and blockages from around the outdoor unit. Anything involving valves, the expansion valve, the refrigerant charge, fan motors, or sensors is technician work.

Applies to — read this first

Good news for diagnosis: within the Gree mini-split families we checked, E4 is consistent — it is always compressor high discharge temperature protection. What you must NOT do is borrow the meaning from a top-ranking table that maps E4 to refrigerant leakage: a leak has its own code (F0), and E4 is the compressor running too hot. E4 is also not a sensor fault: the discharge sensor fault is F5, a different code from the discharge temperature protection trip.

E4 shows on the indoor unit's display. The Vireo manual's outdoor-unit yellow light pattern for E4 is 7 flashes and 1 second off. The duct service manual's trip condition is a discharge temperature over 118°C measured at the compressor exhaust pipe and shell sensor. On Gree commercial/VRF lines the code map can shift; check your model's own manual.

What the machine does when E4 fires

In cooling or drying operation the compressor and the outdoor fan stop while the indoor fan operates; in heating operation all loads stop (Soyal service manual). The unit stays stopped until the discharge temperature falls and the protection resets — repeated immediate trips mean the underlying cause is still present.

Common causes

  1. Cutoff valve of the outdoor unit is not opened — the duct service manual lists this cause first.
  2. Operation of the electronic expansion valve is abnormal.
  3. Outdoor or indoor fan is abnormal — a failing fan stops heat rejection and drives discharge temperature up.
  4. Filter screen or air duct of the indoor unit is blocked (cooling mode).
  5. Ambient operation temperature exceeds the operation range.
  6. Refrigerant charge is insufficient — low charge starves the compressor of cooling.
  7. System pipeline is blocked.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Switch the unit off and let it cool down completely. E4 means the compressor overheated — restarting a hot compressor into the same trip risks damage.
  2. Clean or replace the indoor filters, and clear leaves, debris, and anything blocking airflow around the outdoor unit. A blocked indoor filter/duct in cooling mode is one of the service manual's seven causes, and it is the only one you can fix yourself.
  3. Restart once. If E4 clears and stays cleared, it was a transient airflow or ambient-temperature trip.
  4. If E4 returns, stop: the remaining causes are an unopened outdoor cutoff valve, an abnormal expansion valve, an abnormal indoor or outdoor fan, out-of-range ambient operation, insufficient refrigerant charge, or a blocked pipeline. All six need gauges, tools, and certification — call a qualified HVAC technician.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. Do not restart the unit repeatedly to 'see if it goes away' — repeated discharge-temperature trips are exactly what the protection exists to prevent.

When to call a technician

  • E4 returns on the first restart after the cool-down and airflow checks.
  • The outdoor fan is not running, runs slowly, or stops and starts while E4 is active.
  • The unit was recently serviced, recharged, or had a valve worked on and E4 started after — a closed valve or wrong charge is the prime suspect.
  • Ice on the pipework, hissing sounds, or oil stains — signs of a refrigerant problem feeding the temperature fault.
  • Any burning smell or visibly damaged wiring at either unit.

Safety first

Mini-split indoor and outdoor units contain mains-voltage wiring and capacitors that hold charge after switch-off — never remove electrical covers yourself. The refrigerant circuit must only be opened by certified technicians: it is illegal to vent refrigerant in most jurisdictions, and the R32 used in newer Gree units is a mildly flammable (A2L) refrigerant. The Gree manual's own wording stands: 'The air conditioner is not expected to be serviced by users. Incorrect repair may cause electric shock or fire, so please contact an authorized service center for professional service.'

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E4 mean on a Gree mini split?

E4 means compressor high discharge temperature protection: the temperature at the compressor discharge has risen past the safe limit, so the unit has stopped to protect the compressor. The duct service manual's trip condition is a measured discharge temperature over 118°C.

Is Gree E4 dangerous? Should I keep running the unit?

Stop using it and let it cool. E4 is the compressor overheating — running through repeated trips risks compressor damage. Switch the unit off, do the airflow checks below, and if E4 returns, call a qualified HVAC technician rather than restarting it over and over.

How do I reset Gree mini split E4?

Switch the unit off and let it cool down, clean the indoor filters, and clear anything blocking airflow at the outdoor unit. Then restart once. E4 has no manual button-reset sequence; if it comes back, the cause is one of the service-manual items (valve, expansion valve, fan, charge, or blocked pipeline) and it needs a technician.

Is Gree E4 the same as a refrigerant leak or an F5 sensor fault?

No, and this is the easiest mix-up to make. Refrigerant leakage protection is F0, and the discharge temperature sensor fault is F5. E4 is the protection trip itself — the discharge temperature actually got too high — while F5 means the sensor measuring it is open or short-circuited. One ranking table maps E4 to refrigerant leakage; that contradicts the Gree manuals.
