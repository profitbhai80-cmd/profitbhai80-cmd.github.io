Gree Air Conditioner Error Code E5

Quick answer

Error codeE5 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansE5 means AC over-current protection: the unit has detected current above the safe limit and stopped to protect itself. The Gree Vireo manual names the three causes: supply voltage is unstable, supply voltage is too low and system load is too high, and the indoor coil is blocked or dirty.
Most common causeUnstable or sagging supply voltage under load — or a blocked/dirty indoor coil making the system draw excess current.
Safe to check yourself?Partially. You can safely restart the unit once (the manual's own step), clean or replace the indoor filters, and note whether E5 trips when other large appliances run at the same time. Anything involving supply-voltage testing, wiring, current-draw measurement, or the compressor and control boards is electrician/technician work.

Applies to — read this first

Good news for diagnosis: within the Gree mini-split families we checked, E5 is consistent — it is always AC over-current protection. What you must NOT do is merge E5 with its neighbors: H5 is IPM protection and P5 is phase over-current protection — three different protections that one top-ranking table merges into a single 'H5/P5' row. The Vireo manual's third cause is also routinely dropped by ranking pages: a blocked or dirty indoor coil can trip E5, not just voltage problems.

E5 shows on the indoor unit's display. The Vireo manual's outdoor-unit yellow light pattern for E5 is 5 flashes and 1 second off, and the Gree Split AC User Manual confirms the same 5-flash pattern on its power indicator (5 flashes every 3 seconds). On U-Match owner manuals there is no E5 row at all — U-Match overcurrent lives at P5 — so if your U-Match manual has no E5, use P5, not this page. On Gree commercial/VRF lines the code map can shift; check your model's own manual.

What the machine does when E5 fires

In cooling or drying operation the compressor and the outdoor fan stop while the indoor fan operates; in heating operation all loads stop (Soyal service manual). The unit stays stopped until the overcurrent condition clears — repeated immediate trips mean the supply or load problem is still present.

Common causes

  1. Supply voltage is unstable — the Vireo manual lists this cause first.
  2. Supply voltage is too low and system load is too high — the unit draws excess current when the supply sags under load.
  3. Indoor coil is blocked or dirty — restricted airflow forces the system to work harder and draw more current.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Try restarting the unit — the Vireo GEN3 owner's manual's own guidance for the E5 group: 'Try restarting the unit. If the problem persists, contact a qualified HVAC technician.' If E5 clears and stays cleared, it was a transient supply dip.
  2. Clean or replace the indoor filters. A blocked or dirty indoor coil is the Vireo manual's third E5 cause, and it is the one cause you can fix yourself.
  3. Note when E5 trips: if it trips when other large appliances start or run at the same time, that points to the Vireo manual's second cause (supply too low, load too high). Pass that observation to an electrician — do not test the supply yourself.
  4. If E5 returns after the restart and the filter clean, stop: the remaining diagnosis is supply-voltage testing and current-draw measurement on the compressor and boards. Call a qualified HVAC technician or an electrician.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the code clears. E5 is an electrical protection trip — if the restart and filter clean do not clear it, the next steps involve live mains testing and are not home checks.

When to call a technician

  • E5 returns on the first restart after the filter clean.
  • Lights dim or flicker in the house when the unit starts, or the breaker trips with E5 — a supply problem for an electrician.
  • E5 trips whenever other large appliances run — supply too low under load.
  • Any burning smell, scorch marks, or buzzing from either unit — leave the unit off and call an electrician.
  • The outdoor fan or compressor makes straining noises before E5 trips — a failing component drawing excess current.

Safety first

E5 is an electrical protection trip — never remove electrical covers or touch wiring, terminals, or boards yourself. Mini-split units contain mains-voltage wiring and capacitors that hold charge after switch-off. The Gree manual's own wording stands: 'The air conditioner is not expected to be serviced by users. Incorrect repair may cause electric shock or fire, so please contact an authorized service center for professional service.' If you smell burning, see scorching, or the breaker trips with E5, leave the unit off and call an electrician.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does E5 mean on a Gree mini split?

E5 means AC over-current protection: the unit has detected current above the safe limit and stopped to protect itself. The Gree Vireo manual names three causes: unstable supply voltage, supply voltage too low while system load is too high, and a blocked or dirty indoor coil.

How do I reset Gree mini split E5?

Try restarting the unit — that is the Gree Vireo GEN3 owner's manual's own guidance for the E5 group ('Try restarting the unit. If the problem persists, contact a qualified HVAC technician'). Then clean the indoor filters and check whether E5 trips when other large appliances run at the same time. If E5 returns, the supply or the current draw needs an electrician or HVAC technician.

Is Gree E5 the same as H5 or P5?

No. E5 is AC over-current protection on the mini-split indoor display. H5 is IPM (intelligent power module) protection and P5 is phase over-current protection — both live on different parts of the system and are listed separately in the U-Match manuals. One ranking table merges H5 and P5 into a single row; do not merge E5 into that row either.

Can a dirty coil really cause E5?

Yes — the Vireo and Soyal manuals both list a blocked or dirty indoor coil (evaporator) as the third E5 cause. Most ranking pages omit it and blame voltage alone, which sends owners to an electrician for a fault a filter clean would have fixed. Clean the filters first, then judge the voltage.
