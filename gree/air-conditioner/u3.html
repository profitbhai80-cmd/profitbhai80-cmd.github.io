Gree Air Conditioner Error Code U3

Quick answer

Error codeU3 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansU3 = PN / DC bus-bar voltage drop protection. The DC bus voltage dropped suddenly; the cause in every manual is unstable supply voltage. The indoor unit blinks 20 times. In cooling/drying the compressor stops while the indoor fan keeps running; in heating the complete unit stops.
Most common causeSupply voltage is unstable - the cause in every manual, quoted verbatim. No manual names any other cause for U3.

If U3 keeps returning, have an electrician check the supply and a technician check the unit.

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because U3 is one fault with one meaning across six manufacturer manuals - no series split. U3 = PN / DC bus-bar voltage drop protection, blink 20 in the evo, Change 18/24, Viola, Soyal and Mini after-sale manuals. Distinguish it from its neighbours, never merge: PL = low PN voltage protection, blink 21 - a sustained low-voltage state, not a drop event. PH = high DC bus voltage protection. E5 = overcurrent protection. H5 = IPM protection. Rooftop GK-H U7 = "DC Bus Voltage Drop Error" (rooftop numbering is shifted by one - wall-split U3 = rooftop U7). GMV VRF U3 = "Power phase-sequence protection" (different fault, out of scope).

What the machine does when U3 fires

During cooling and drying operation the compressor stops while the indoor fan keeps running; during heating operation the complete unit stops.

Common causes

  1. Supply voltage is unstable - the cause in every service manual, quoted verbatim. No manual names any other cause for U3.
  2. Wrong fix - arlington's "replace the main board of outdoor" step is invented: every manual gives the cause as "Supply voltage is unstable" and names no board replacement for U3.
  3. Wrong reading - "internal component malfunction" (pickhvac) is a guess; the manuals name only unstable supply voltage.
  4. Wrong-code reading - U3 is NOT a current-detection malfunction. That is U5's meaning; the f-static PDF's U3 row carries U5's meaning because its U-rows are shifted by one.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Wait for the supply voltage to stabilize: the GWC18MC manual says when the voltage resumes normal, the malfunction is eliminated automatically, and if the compressor has stopped for 3 minutes, the unit resumes its previous running status.
  2. If U3 keeps returning, have an electrician check the supply and a technician check the unit - persistent U3 is a supply or board problem a homeowner cannot diagnose from the display alone.
  3. Do not replace the outdoor main board for U3 - no manual gives a board-replacement step for U3; the manuals' cause is unstable supply voltage.

The manuals name NO board replacement for U3 (arlington's "replace the main board of outdoor" step is invented - the page must not repeat it). The page's owner guidance is only what the manuals support: wait for the supply voltage to stabilize; if U3 keeps returning, have an electrician check the supply and a technician check the unit.

When to call a technician

  • U3 keeps returning - the supply itself is unstable; get an electrician to check it, and a technician to check the unit.
  • U3 appears together with other supply-related symptoms (lights dimming, other appliances tripping) - the incoming supply needs an electrician, not an AC repair.
  • Your unit is a GMV VRF or Rooftop GK-H unit showing U3 - VRF U3 is phase-sequence protection and rooftop U3 does not carry this meaning (rooftop uses U7); use that platform's manual, not this page.

Safety first

U3 is a supply-voltage fault: never open the outdoor unit or test mains voltage yourself. Supply checks are electrician work; any unit check is technician work.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does U3 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

PN / DC bus-bar voltage drop protection: the voltage on the inverter's DC bus dropped suddenly. Verified in six Gree manuals (blink 20). The cause in every manual is the same: "Supply voltage is unstable."

Will U3 clear itself?

Yes, if the voltage recovers. The GWC18MC manual says: when the voltage resumes normal, the malfunction is eliminated automatically; if the compressor has stopped for 3 minutes, the unit resumes its previous running status. If U3 keeps coming back, the supply itself is unstable - get an electrician to check it.

Is U3 the same as PL?

No. U3 is a voltage drop event (blink 20); PL is low PN voltage protection (blink 21) - a sustained low-voltage state. PH is high DC-bus voltage. Three different codes, three different conditions; the manuals define each separately.

Is U3 a board fault?

Not according to the manuals. Every service manual names only unstable supply voltage as the cause and gives no board-replacement step for U3. (One popular site tells you to replace the outdoor main board for U3 - that step is invented; it appears in no Gree manual.)

U3 vs E5 vs H5 - what's the difference?

U3 is a DC-bus voltage drop (supply unstable). E5 is overcurrent protection (too much current). H5 is IPM module protection. All three can be triggered by bad power, but they are different protections on different circuits - the display tells you which one tripped.
