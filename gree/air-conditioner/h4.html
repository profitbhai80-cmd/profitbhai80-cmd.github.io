Gree Air Conditioner Error Code H4

Quick answer

Error codeH4 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansH4 on a Gree wall-split air conditioner means system overload protection: when the tube temperature gets too high — outdoor heat-exchanger temperature in cooling, indoor heat-exchanger temperature in heating — the protection activates. In cooling or drying the compressor stops while the indoor fan keeps running; in heating the whole unit stops. The manual's three causes are: outdoor temperature too high when cooling, insufficient outdoor air circulation, and refrigerant flow malfunction. On GMV VRF platforms H4 instead means fan drive PFC protection.
Most common causeTube temperature too high: outdoor heat too high in cooling, poor outdoor air circulation, or a refrigerant flow malfunction.
Safe to check yourself?Yes, for the checks on this page: confirm the code and platform, clear blocked airflow at the outdoor coil, clean indoor filters, and restart once. Refrigerant-flow and outdoor-board work is technician-only.
Checked againstGree GWH Series Service Manual, Display Method table entry 34 (H4: overload, high temperature resistant; cooling: compressor stops, indoor fan runs; heating: whole unit stops; outdoor blink OFF 3s / OFF 1s, 4 times / 6 times); Gree Soyal Service Manual, same display table; Gree GWHD Linge Wind Series Service Manual (GWHD12A6ND31A), malfunction-analysis item 6, System malfunction (H4), with the three causes; Gree UMA30HP230V1ACS U-Match Table 6.1 (H4: Overloading); Gree ME33-42/G Owner's Manual p.30 (H4: Overload)

Applies to — read this first

H4 is a stop protection, not a frequency limit — that is the first distinction. F6/F8/F9 slow the compressor and keep running; H4 stops things. The second distinction is against H3: H3 is compressor overload protection with its own cause tree (refrigerant amount, capillary blockage, compressor faults, protector malfunction), while H4 is system/tube-temperature overload protection with three causes. The manuals do not give a sharper H3-versus-H4 test than that, and this page does not invent one.

What the machine does when H4 fires

When the tube temperature gets too high — the outdoor heat-exchanger temperature in cooling, the indoor heat-exchanger temperature in heating — the H4 protection activates. In cooling or drying the compressor stops while the indoor fan keeps running; in heating the whole unit stops. The unit is protecting itself from an overloaded, overheated system, not reporting a broken sensor.

Common causes

  1. Outdoor temperature too high when cooling — the first manual cause.
  2. Insufficient outdoor air circulation — blocked coil, blocked clearance, or poor ventilation around the outdoor unit.
  3. Refrigerant flow malfunction — the third manual cause, and strictly technician territory.

Safe checks, in order

  1. Confirm the code and the platform: H4 on a wall-split unit is this page. If your unit is a GMV VRF unit, stop — its H4 is a fan drive PFC protection, a different fault.
  2. Clear blocked airflow at the outdoor coil: leaves, debris, and anything choking the coil or its clearance. Clean the indoor filters too.
  3. Note the mode: if H4 struck in cooling on a very hot day with a choked outdoor unit, the first two manual causes fit. Restart the unit once after clearing airflow and see whether H4 returns.
  4. If H4 returns, or the unit stops in heating, or cooling stays weak, stop: refrigerant-flow checks and outdoor-board work belong to a qualified technician. Do not open the outdoor unit yourself.

Work through the checks in order and stop as soon as the unit runs normally. H4's manual cause tree is short by design — outdoor heat, outdoor airflow, refrigerant flow — and only the first two have home checks. Never open the outdoor unit's covers or touch sensor wiring.

When to call a technician

  • H4 returns repeatedly after airflow and filter checks and one restart.
  • H4 in heating with the whole unit stopping, or H4 with weak cooling — consistent with a refrigerant-flow fault.
  • Any burning smell, damaged wiring, or refrigerant smell near the outdoor unit — switch off and call a qualified technician immediately.

Safety first

H4 involves mains-powered air-conditioner electronics and an outdoor unit: never open the outdoor unit's covers or touch sensor terminals while investigating it, and never attempt refrigerant work — refrigerant handling belongs to a qualified technician. Switch the unit off at the isolator before any visible check.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does H4 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

H4 means system overload protection: when the tube temperature gets too high, the protection activates — in cooling/drying the compressor stops while the indoor fan keeps running, and in heating the whole unit stops. The GWH display table (entry 34) and the GWHD malfunction-analysis item 6 agree.

What causes Gree H4?

Three manual causes: outdoor temperature too high when cooling, insufficient outdoor air circulation, and refrigerant flow malfunction. Only the first two have home checks (airflow, filters); refrigerant flow is technician work.

What is the difference between Gree H4 and H3?

H3 is compressor overload protection with its own cause tree (refrigerant amount, capillary blockage, compressor faults, protector malfunction). H4 is system/tube-temperature overload protection with the three causes above. The manuals give no sharper distinguishing test.

Is Gree H4 a sensor fault?

No. H4 is a protection response to high tube temperature, not a sensor open/short fault. The F-series codes (F1–F5) are the sensor faults; H4 stops the unit to protect it.

How do I clear Gree air conditioner H4?

Confirm the platform, clear outdoor-coil airflow, clean the indoor filters, and restart once. If H4 returns, the fault is genuine — refrigerant-flow or board work belongs to a technician.
