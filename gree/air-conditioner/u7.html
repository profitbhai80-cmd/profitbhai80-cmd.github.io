Gree Air Conditioner Error Code U7

Quick answer

Error codeU7 — Gree Air Conditioner
What it meansU7 = the four-way (reversing) valve is abnormal - the valve failed to change direction. The indoor unit blinks 20 times (Viola, Livo+). It is a heating-mode fault: in heating the complete unit stops.
Most common causeThe manuals name three causes: supply voltage is lower than AC175V; wiring terminal 4V is loosened or broken; 4V is damaged, replace 4V.
Safe to check yourself?Partly - if U7 appears once or twice it clears itself (U-Match manual). After lockout on the third occurrence, switch off and re-energize once to clear. If it returns, call a technician (voltage check, 4V terminal, 4V valve). The valve is a refrigerant-circuit part, not an owner replacement.

Applies to — read this first

Read this first, because U7 is one fault with one meaning across seven manufacturer manuals - no series split. U7 = the four-way (reversing) valve is abnormal, blink 20 (Viola, Livo+). It is a heating-mode fault: the detection rule only runs in heating, and in heating the complete unit stops. Distinguish it from its neighbours, never merge: U4 = compressor reverse protection (evo-only, blink 14) - a different fault; the f-static PDF assigns U7's fault and U7's below-175V cause to U4. Rooftop GK-H uses UL ("4-Way Valve Commutation Error"), not U7. GMV VRF has no U7 fault row in the tables checked. U-Match rows re-confirmed first-hand 2026-11-07 (manuals.plus conversion of the R410A service manual, section 1.1 row 32; R32 manual section 3.3 row 27).

What the machine does when U7 fires

During heating operation the complete unit stops.

Common causes

  1. Supply voltage is lower than AC175V - the manuals' first cause.
  2. Wiring terminal 4V is loosened or broken - the manuals' second cause.
  3. 4V is damaged, replace 4V - the manuals' third cause.
  4. Wrong-code reading - U4 is compressor reverse protection (evo-only, blink 14), not the 4-way valve. The f-static PDF gives U4 the reversing-valve fault and the below-175V cause, which are U7's.

Safe checks, in order

  1. If U7 appears once or twice, let it clear itself - the U-Match manual says the malfunction is eliminated automatically the first two times.
  2. On the third occurrence the unit locks out: switch it off and re-energize once to clear.
  3. If U7 comes back after that, call a technician - the voltage check, the 4V terminal check and the 4V valve are technician work.
  4. Do not replace the valve yourself: arlington's "you will have to replace it yourself" is unsafe DIY framing for a refrigerant-circuit part, and no manual gives an owner-level valve test, multimeter step, or DIY valve replacement.

No owner-level valve test, no multimeter step, and no DIY valve replacement exists in the manuals. The page's only owner actions are the manual's own: if U7 appears once or twice it clears itself; after lockout, switch off and re-energize once; if it returns, call a technician.

When to call a technician

  • U7 returns after a lockout clear - the valve or its supply needs a technician (voltage below AC175V, 4V terminal, 4V valve).
  • U7 appears in cooling - the detection rule only runs in heating, so a cooling-mode U7 needs professional diagnosis of the real fault.
  • Your unit is a Rooftop GK-H unit showing a 4-way valve fault - rooftop uses UL, not U7; use that platform's manual, not this page.

More guidance: when an appliance error requires professional service.

Safety first

U7 involves mains voltage (below AC175V check) and a refrigerant-circuit valve: never open the outdoor unit, test the 4V terminal, or replace the valve yourself. The valve is replaced by a technician after the three causes are checked.

Never open an appliance that is connected to power, never bypass a safety device, and never attempt gas, refrigerant or high-voltage work without the proper qualifications. If anything feels beyond a basic external check, stop and call a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

What does U7 mean on a Gree air conditioner?

The four-way (reversing) valve is abnormal: the valve failed to change direction. Verified in seven Gree manuals (blink 20). In heating mode the complete unit stops.

Why does U7 only appear in heating?

The detection rule only runs in heating: after the compressor starts in heating, the unit watches the difference between evaporator temperature and ambient temperature for 10 minutes. If it stays below the protective value, U7 is reported. The Change and Livo Gen4 manuals both say the complete unit stops "during heating operation".

Will U7 clear itself?

The first two times, yes - the U-Match manual says the malfunction is eliminated automatically. On the third occurrence the unit locks out: switch it off and re-energize to clear. If U7 comes back after that, the valve or its supply needs a technician.

What causes U7?

The manuals name three causes: supply voltage lower than AC175V; wiring terminal 4V loosened or broken; the 4V valve itself damaged (replace 4V). All three are technician checks - the valve is a refrigerant-circuit part, not an owner replacement.

Is U7 the same as U4?

No. U7 is the 4-way valve failing to reverse; U4 is compressor reverse protection (evo-only, blink 14). One popular PDF mixes them up - it gives U4 the reversing-valve fault and the below-175V cause, which are U7's. Check the code on your display, not the PDF.
